# Experimental conlangs at the extremes: Toki Pona (minimal), Ithkuil (maximal), Láadan (hypothesis-driven)

Research date: 2026-10-09. About 20 search and fetch calls. Source caveats: the New Yorker and web.archive.org could not be fetched, so the Foer article is cited through a blog reproduction/summary (flagged). Wikipedia is used as a secondary aggregator for several facts and is flagged where it's the only source. Elgin's own blog (ozarque.livejournal.com) and the SFWA-hosted Láadan FAQ (404) were not reachable directly; her quotes come through Lithub/Conversationalist reporting.

## Toki Pona: goals, lexicon, phonotactics, ambiguity, community, drift, studies

### Takeaway
Toki Pona started as a personal, therapeutic, Taoism-inspired artlang (2001). It has about 120–137 words and 14 phonemes. It turned out to be the most-used experimental conlang of the three: about 2,000 census respondents in each of 2022 and 2024, half of them using it weekly, mostly on Discord. Its lexicon is now partly set by community surveys (Lang's 2021 dictionary was compiled from Discord polls). Measurable usage drift follows natural-language sociolinguistic patterns.

### Cited Findings
**Goals and origin**
- Sonja Lang created Toki Pona in 2001 while "experiencing depression", as "a way to simplify her thoughts". It was "partly inspired by Taoist philosophy" and designed as "a philosophical language focused on minimalism", meant to "help users concentrate on basic things and to promote positive thinking", explicitly framed around Sapir–Whorf. — [Wikipedia: Toki Pona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)

**Lexicon (pu / ku)**
- 2001: early version online. 2014: *Toki Pona: The Language of Good* (*lipu pu*). July 2021: *Toki Pona Dictionary* (*lipu ku*). 2024: a Toki Pona adaptation of *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- pu has "120 main words, plus 3 words presented as synonyms" (often cited as 120 or 123). ku "lists 137 'essential' words and a small number of less-used ones". *nimi ku suli* = "16 new 'essential' words" beyond pu. *nimi ku lili* = "45 words given by 40% or less of respondents". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- ku was compiled from "more than 11,000 entries detailing the use of the language as she gathered from polls conducted in the *ma pona pi toki pona* Discord server over a few months". The dictionary is therefore descriptive of community usage, not prescriptive. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- Etymological sources of pu words (per Wikipedia's table): Finnish and Tok Pisin 16 each (13.2%), Esperanto and Serbo-Croatian 13 each (10.7%), Dutch and Acadian French 11 each (9.09%), Georgian and English 8 each (6.61%). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)

**Phonology / phonotactics**
- 14 phonemes: 9 consonants /p t k s m n l j w/ and 5 vowels /a e i o u/. There are "no diphthongs, vowel hiatus, contrasting vowel length, consonant clusters (except those starting with the nasal coda), or tones". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- Syllables: the first syllable is (C)V(N); later syllables require the onset consonant, i.e. CV(N). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- Side finding: the 2010 ROILA robot-language project found a "modified vocabulary of Toki Pona significantly outperformed English" in machine speech recognition. This is evidence that the sparse phonotactics aid recognition. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona) (secondary; original paper not checked)

**Meaning-building, names, ambiguity**
- Words are "polysemous" and each "covers a range of similar concepts". Compounds build meaning: *jan pona* = "good person", i.e. "friend". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- Names: "Toki Pona does not use isolated proper nouns; instead, they must modify a preceding noun" (a head noun such as *jan* "person" or *ma* "land"). Names are adapted to the phonology, e.g. English → *Inli*, John → *San*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- A 2017 academic description names four main sources of ambiguity: (1) the small vocabulary, (2) prepositions, (3) the absence of *li* after *mi*/*sina*, (4) incomplete sentences. It says syntactic ambiguity can often be resolved from word semantics and dictionary classes. It also built a Toki Pona WordNet linking the words to 4,027 synsets, which gives a rough measure of how many distinct senses the ~120 words must cover. — [Fabbri et al., arXiv 1712.09359](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.09359)

**Community size and activity**
- Lang in 2007: "at least 100 people speak Toki Pona fluently" and "a few hundred have a basic knowledge". Wikipedia's 2021 user estimate is "500–5000". The range is wide, so treat it as order-of-magnitude only. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- ISO 639-3 **tok**: two applications failed. A third was filed in August 2021 and the code was adopted in January 2022. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)
- The Toki Pona census (*lipu jan ale pi toki pona*) is run by community members jan Sonatan, jan Olipija and jan Tamalu. Censuses were held in April 2021, August 2022 and August 2024. — [sona pona wiki](https://sona.pona.la/wiki/Toki_Pona_census)
- 2024 census: **1,997 responses** in 6 weeks, about the same as 2022. 80% say they know Toki Pona (74.2% in 2022). 53% say they're at conversation level or above (44% in 2022). About 16% answered in Toki Pona (about 14% in 2022, 10.8% in 2021). About 78% were first-time respondents. — [Census 2024 results](https://tokiponacensus.github.io/results2024/)
- Usage: 49% use it at least weekly (64% among those who answered in Toki Pona). Discord dominates (88% among Toki Pona respondents). Reddit is declining; Mastodon and VRChat are growing. About 60% have created something (mostly translations, 15% original works, about 9% learning materials). 85% know *sitelen pona* (71% in 2022, 61% in 2021). The main motivation is "fun learning and using the language" by a large margin. — [Census 2024 results](https://tokiponacensus.github.io/results2024/)
- Learning path: jan Misali's "12 days of sitelen Emoji/Toki Pona" video series is the most common entry point at 52% (61% in 2022). Next come pu, ma pona, and jan Kekan San's lessons. About 10% use Anki or Memrise. — [Census 2024 results](https://tokiponacensus.github.io/results2024/)
- The 2024 results were announced around February 2025. A community newsletter mentions a demographics release in 2025. I found no evidence of a 2025 or 2026 census in reachable sources. — [sin newsletter 2025](https://sin-kulupu.pona.la/2025/k/); [sona pona](https://sona.pona.la/wiki/Toki_Pona_census)

**Drift and linguistic studies**
- Huang & Joo (Aug 2025) built a corpus of 1.23M sentences / 6.39M tokens covering 2016 to Jan 2025. They found "changes in preferences of content words for different syntactic positions over time" and variation in fluid word classes and transitivity across corpora. They conclude that "sociolinguistic factors influence Toki Pona in the same way as natural languages". — [arXiv 2508.10246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10246); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394488073_A_Computational_Approach_to_Analyzing_Language_Change_and_Variation_in_the_Constructed_Language_Toki_Pona)
- 2026 (SCiL): "Examining the Limits of Word2Vec with Toki Pona" used 1.4M sentences / 7.95M tokens of community text. This shows a sizeable, growing corpus. Findings were not extracted. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.scil-main.4/); [arXiv 2606.17299](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17299)
- A 2012 formal context-free grammar with a CKY parser was tested on 100 sentences, which shows the syntax is small enough to formalize. — [Tomaszewski 2012](https://www2.hawaii.edu/~chin/661F12/Projects/ztomaszewski.pdf)

### Inferences
- The lexicon is not frozen at 120. It grew to 137 "essential" words within about 7 years of pu, and Lang chose to ratify community usage rather than resist it. Extreme minimalism leaks: the community adds words where compounding is too costly. This is a direct precedent for a ~470-root design: expect pressure to add roots, and plan a ratification mechanism.
- The 4,027-synset mapping implies roughly 30+ senses per word on average. Precision comes from context and shared convention, not from the code. This is the opposite of "faithful transmission" by design, yet it is the one of the three with a living community.
- Adoption drivers in the census are fun, community (Discord) and a free video course. None of them is expressive power. Low learning cost plus a social venue beats precision for adoption.
- Mandatory "adjectival" treatment of names (a head noun plus a phonologically adapted name) is a workable model for proper nouns and borrowings in a small-root language.

### Gaps
- **Duolingo**: I found no source showing a Duolingo Toki Pona course. My recollection (unverified) is that there is none and that learners use jan Misali, lipu sona, ma pona and others. Verify before stating.
- **nimi sin process**: Linku (linku.la) runs word-usage surveys that categorize words (core/common/uncommon/obscure/sandbox). I didn't fetch Linku data, so no figures are given. Specific community words (e.g. *kijetesantakalu*, *tonsi*, *kokosila*) and their usage rates were not verified.
- **Technical topics**: I found no study of how speakers handle technical vocabulary (circumlocution vs. untranslated borrowing in quotes). Anecdotally, borrowed names are tokiponized behind a head noun, but I have no source for technical vocabulary specifically.
- **Ambiguity in practice**: I found no empirical comprehension study measuring how often listeners misread Toki Pona utterances, only the descriptive analysis above.
- The ROILA paper and the "therapeutic" studies were seen only through Wikipedia.

## Ithkuil: goals, versions, categories, speakability, the Russian/Ukrainian episode, commentary

### Takeaway
Ithkuil maximizes semantic explicitness per syllable, at enormous cognitive cost. In over 20 years and four versions, no one is known to speak it fluently. Its creator says he is "fluent" only in the morphology and never memorized the lexicon. Its most visible real-world "adoption" was by a Russian/Ukrainian psychonetics movement whose politics horrified Quijada. It is treated by its creator and by critics like Lakoff as a demonstration or art project, not a communication tool.

### Cited Findings
**Goals**
- Quijada's aim: to "demonstrate how human language could be used to convey much deeper levels of human cognition and semantic nuance/exactitude than are found in natural human languages". Morpho-phonological conciseness is secondary, there to keep the extra content from making utterances long. — [ithkuil.net FAQ](https://ithkuil.net/faqs.html) (undated, pre-2023)
- Designed "to express more profound levels of human cognition briefly yet overtly and clearly, particularly about human categorization". Quijada "did not create Ithkuil to be auxiliary or used in everyday conversations". — [Wikipedia: Ithkuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil)
- He calls it "a specialized language for specific purposes where exactitude and clarity of cognitive intention is called for", comparing it to Classical Latin in academia. — [ithkuil.net FAQ](https://ithkuil.net/faqs.html)
- As quoted by Foer, the language is "an idealized language whose aim is the highest possible degree of logic, efficiency, detail, and accuracy in cognitive expression". Also Quijada: "Natural languages are adequate, but that doesn't mean they're optimal." — [Foer, "Utopian for Beginners", via blog reproduction](https://theleavingyears.blogspot.com/2018/02/utopian-for-beginners.html) (secondary copy; New Yorker original unreachable)

**Versions and size**
- 2004 original: 65 consonants, 17 vowels. 2007 Ilaksh: 30 consonants, 10 vowels. 2011 Ithkuil: 45 consonants, 13 vowels. 2023 New Ithkuil (v4): 31 consonants, 9 vowels. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil) (phoneme counts not verified against Quijada's grammars)
- New Ithkuil: 96 grammatical cases, 9 configurations, over 6,000 roots, about 520 affixes. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil)
- Foer's article (on the 2011 version): 58 phonemes (consistent with 45+13) and 22 grammatical categories for verbs versus English's six. Example: "gawk" becomes a single word, *apq'uxasiu*, that encodes surprise, impropriety and involuntary response. — [Foer via blog](https://theleavingyears.blogspot.com/2018/02/utopian-for-beginners.html)
- The FAQ says the 2011 version was his "final intended design", with no structural redesign planned. This was overtaken by New Ithkuil in 2023, which shows the FAQ is stale and that the design kept changing anyway. — [ithkuil.net FAQ](https://ithkuil.net/faqs.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil)

**Can anyone speak it?**
- "No person is hitherto known to be able to speak Ithkuil fluently; Quijada, for one, does not." He is "fluent" only in the morphology and has "never tried to memorize the lexicon". — [ithkuil.net FAQ](https://ithkuil.net/faqs.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil)
- Quijada: "a single word requires a lot more thought before it can be spoken than it would in a natural language". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil)
- He rejects the "think 5× faster" claim. It presupposes that thought is entirely linguistic, and "the sheer volume of morphological information which Ithkuil requires one to express" would cancel out any phonological brevity. He claims instead "deeper, critical, and analytical" thinking. — [ithkuil.net FAQ](https://ithkuil.net/faqs.html)
- Recognition: David J. Peterson gave it the 2008 Smiley Award. — [Foer via blog](https://theleavingyears.blogspot.com/2018/02/utopian-for-beginners.html)

**Russian/Ukrainian episode**
- 2004: the Russian magazine *Computerra* featured Ithkuil. Stanislav Kozlovsky wrote that fluent speakers would think "about five or six times as fast", invoking Sapir–Whorf. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil)
- 2010–2011: Quijada was invited to a conference in Kiev by Oleg Bakhtiyarov, a professor in the "psychonetics" movement who promoted ultra-nationalist ideas and saw Ithkuil as a tool for developing "intellectual special forces". Quijada found that Igor Garkavenko, a far-right militant with anti-Semitic writings, had attended. He then distanced himself from the movement. — [Foer via blog](https://theleavingyears.blogspot.com/2018/02/utopian-for-beginners.html) (secondary summary; names and quotes should be checked against the New Yorker original)

**Commentary**
- George Lakoff, whose cognitive linguistics inspired Quijada, told Foer that Ithkuil "has an assumption of efficiency that really isn't efficient, given how the brain works". He suggested it works better "as a conceptual-art project". — [Foer via blog](https://theleavingyears.blogspot.com/2018/02/utopian-for-beginners.html)
- Foer's framing: natural language's ambiguity and metaphor, which Ithkuil tries to remove, are integral to thought and communication. — [Foer via blog](https://theleavingyears.blogspot.com/2018/02/utopian-for-beginners.html) (this is the summarizer's paraphrase of Foer's conclusion)

### Inferences
- Obligatory marking of many categories on every word makes production slow even for the designer. Explicitness that the speaker must compute every time is a cost speakers do not pay. This is the strongest argument for keeping evidential, confidence and speech-act markers **optional**, as the comparison language does.
- The pattern (designer can't speak it, repeated redesigns) suggests that when a design has no users, nothing anchors it, so it keeps getting redesigned. Toki Pona, by contrast, was anchored by its users (ku).
- The Russian episode shows that a "precision/think faster" pitch attracts Whorfian and self-improvement audiences who may project ideologies onto the language. That's a reputational risk for any "better thought" framing.

### Gaps
- **Okrent**: I didn't access *In the Land of Invented Languages* text. I recall (unverified) that it discusses Ithkuil among "logical" languages, but I have no quote. Don't cite it without checking.
- **Community and translations, 2023–2026**: I found no reliable figures on New Ithkuil learners or any Discord/server size, and no evidence of extended translations or conversation by anyone. The FAQ is silent on this. Community lore says a small Discord group helped shape New Ithkuil. Unverified.
- Exact year and details of the Kiev conference, and the name "Vyacheslav Bulaev" (from the brief), were not confirmed in reachable sources. The summary names Bakhtiyarov and Garkavenko instead.
- Foer says "34 years" of work in the summary. That number seems doubtful (around 30 years from the late 1970s to 2012 is more plausible). Unverified, so don't use it.

## Láadan: goals, hypothesis, evidentials and speech-act morphemes, outcome, community

### Takeaway
Láadan (1982) was an explicit experiment. Elgin hypothesized that women, offered a language built for their perceptions, would either nurture it or build a better one. Neither happened. By 2007 she judged the hypothesis failed: "Láadan got very little attention". She pointed to Klingon's success as the ironic contrast. Its grammar includes sentence-level speech-act particles and evidential particles. I found no evidence on how learners actually used them, because there were too few users to study.

### Cited Findings
**Goals and hypothesis**
- Created in 1982 to test Sapir–Whorf, specifically "if development of a language aimed at expressing the views of women would shape a culture". A related hypothesis was that Western natural languages "may be better suited for expressing the views of men than women". — [Wikipedia: Láadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1adan)
- Elgin's prediction: if women were offered a language that better fit them, "one of two things would happen — they would welcome and nurture it, or it would at minimum motivate them to replace it with a better women's language of their own construction". — [Lithub, via search excerpt](https://lithub.com/this-science-fiction-novelist-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/)
- Her framing: "language is our best and most powerful resource for bringing about social change" (Afterword to the 2000 Feminist Press reprint of *Native Tongue*). — [Lithub](https://lithub.com/this-science-fiction-novelist-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/)
- *Native Tongue* (novel) appeared in 1984. *A First Dictionary & Grammar of Láadan* appeared in 1988 (often cited 1985 for the first edition; Wikipedia gives 1988), "in the hopes that a community of speakers could form". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1adan)

**Structure**
- Two tones (low unmarked, high marked). Five vowels /ɑ ɛ ɪ o u/. It lacks /p t k ɡ s z f v/. Word order is VSO (active) and OVS (passive). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1adan)
- Speech-act particles: *bíi* (statement), *báa* (question), *bó* (command), *bóo* (request), *bé* (promise), *bée* (warning). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1adan)
- Evidential particles: *wa* (perceived), *wi* (self-evident), *we* (perceived in a dream), *wáa* (assumed true because of a trusted source), *waá* (assumed false because of a distrusted source), *wo* (imagined/hypothetical), *wóo* (unknown/evidence not given). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1adan)
- Speakers "must clarify their position" with these morphemes plus suffixes such as *-li*, *-ya*, *-d*. — [Conversationalist 2020](https://conversationalist.org/2020/07/23/in-her-1984-science-fiction-novel-native-tongue-linguist-suzette-haden-elgin-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/) (journalistic paraphrase of obligatoriness)

**Outcome**
- 1985: Anthony Burgess called it "ingenious" but predicted failure because "not enough women care sufficiently". March 1987: Elgin noted men found it impractical while women "were more positive about it". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1adan)
- 2007 interview: "Láadan got very little attention… Not once did any feminist magazine (or women's magazine) ask me about the language or write a story about it." And: "The Klingon language, which is as 'masculine' as you could possibly get, has had a tremendous impact on popular culture" (institute, journal, bestselling grammars, cassettes; "nothing like that happened with Láadan"). — [Lithub](https://lithub.com/this-science-fiction-novelist-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/)
- Blog post: "Meanwhile, the Klingon language thrives — from which you are free to draw your own conclusions." — [Lithub](https://lithub.com/this-science-fiction-novelist-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/) (original at ozarque.livejournal.com, not fetched)
- Her ten-year window (to about 1994) expired without adoption, and she considered the hypothesis failed within that frame. — [Lithub](https://lithub.com/this-science-fiction-novelist-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/); [Conversationalist](https://conversationalist.org/2020/07/23/in-her-1984-science-fiction-novel-native-tongue-linguist-suzette-haden-elgin-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/)
- The Conversationalist attributes the failure partly to women's reluctance to parse emotions constantly. That is the journalist's interpretation, not Elgin's statement. — [Conversationalist](https://conversationalist.org/2020/07/23/in-her-1984-science-fiction-novel-native-tongue-linguist-suzette-haden-elgin-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/)

### Inferences
- Láadan is the cleanest natural experiment of the three. A falsifiable adoption hypothesis was stated in advance with a time limit, and it was falsified. Good intended fit to a user group did not produce adoption. The missing pieces relative to Klingon and Toki Pona were a fan or social venue, entertainment value and low entry cost.
- Láadan's sentence-initial speech-act particle and evidential are close cousins of the comparison language's particles. The difference is that Elgin made them (per journalistic sources) part of every sentence. With almost no speakers there is no usage evidence on whether they would be kept or dropped.

### Gaps
- **Community size**: I found no number for Láadan learners or speakers, past or present. Community lore says a small online group (laadanlanguage.org, an expanded dictionary by later contributors) keeps it alive. Unverified, so don't cite a figure.
- **Obligatoriness in the grammar**: Elgin's *First Dictionary and Grammar* wasn't accessed. I recall (unverified) that the speech-act particle is optional in some contexts, e.g. it can be dropped when obvious from context and the evidential is likewise said to be "usually" present. Check the primary grammar before claiming "every sentence".
- **Vocabulary size**: not found. The 1988 dictionary is commonly described as about 1,000+ words. Unverified.
- Elgin's exact four-way hypothesis wording (including "or Esperanto" alternatives) was not found verbatim. The Lithub two-outcome version is the best sourced.

## Cross-cutting: expressiveness vs. precision vs. learnability vs. adoption; are obligatory markers used or dropped?

### Takeaway
Adoption tracks learning cost and social venue, not expressive precision. The lowest-precision language (Toki Pona) has thousands of users and a multi-million-token corpus. The highest-precision one (Ithkuil) has no fluent speaker, not even its author. The hypothesis-driven one (Láadan) failed its own adoption test. There's no direct empirical evidence about learners dropping obligatory markers in Ithkuil or Láadan, because neither has enough users to study. The available evidence is the designer's own testimony about Ithkuil production cost.

### Cited Findings
- Toki Pona: about 2,000 census respondents (2022 and 2024), 49% weekly users, 53% self-reported conversational. — [Census 2024](https://tokiponacensus.github.io/results2024/)
- Toki Pona corpus: 1.23M sentences / 6.39M tokens (2016 to Jan 2025), and 1.4M sentences / 7.95M tokens in a 2026 study. — [arXiv 2508.10246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10246); [ACL Anthology 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.scil-main.4/)
- Ithkuil: no fluent speakers. The author is fluent only in the morphology, and every word "requires a lot more thought before it can be spoken". — [ithkuil.net FAQ](https://ithkuil.net/faqs.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil)
- Lakoff: Ithkuil's "assumption of efficiency… really isn't efficient, given how the brain works". — [Foer via blog](https://theleavingyears.blogspot.com/2018/02/utopian-for-beginners.html)
- Láadan: "very little attention". The hypothesis was judged failed after its ten-year window. — [Lithub](https://lithub.com/this-science-fiction-novelist-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/)
- Toki Pona's community drives change: the descriptive ku dictionary (11,000+ poll entries) and the documented drift in word-position preferences. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona); [arXiv 2508.10246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10246)

### Inferences
- **Trade-off shape**: precision bought by obligatory morphology costs speaker effort on every utterance (Ithkuil). Precision left to context costs listener effort only when it matters (Toki Pona). Speakers pay the second cost; nobody has been seen paying the first. This supports the comparison language's "optional particles" and "cost speakers will actually pay" goals.
- **Lexicon size**: Toki Pona's 120 → 137+ growth suggests the floor for comfortable use sits above about 120 roots even for a community that values minimalism. A ~470-root design sits between Toki Pona and Ithkuil's 6,000+. The Toki Pona evidence says users will still add words, so give them a sanctioned path (a ku-style usage survey) instead of resisting it.
- **Adoption is social**: none of the three succeeded or failed on linguistic merit alone. Toki Pona had a free viral video course, Discord and a "fun" identity. Klingon (Elgin's foil) had a franchise. Láadan and Ithkuil had a book and a website. A universal language needs a venue and an on-ramp as much as a grammar.
- **Hypothesis-testing lesson (Láadan)**: stating an adoption criterion in advance with a time limit made the failure legible. That's worth copying if the comparison language makes adoption claims.
- **Obligatory markers**: there is no direct data. Indirect evidence is that Ithkuil's obligatory categories made even the author non-fluent, and Láadan's markers never had a speech community to test them. Optional marking, as in the comparison language, avoids an untested risk. The add-back trigger would be evidence that listeners systematically misjudge evidence or speech act without them.

### Gaps
- I found no study of learners dropping or regularizing obligatory evidentials in any conlang. Natural-language evidential acquisition literature (e.g. Turkish, Quechua) would be the nearest evidence and was out of scope here.
- I found no comparative study of the three languages by a linguist. Okrent's book likely covers Láadan and Loglan-type languages but wasn't accessed.
