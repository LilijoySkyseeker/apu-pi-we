# Modern international auxiliary languages (Elefen, Globasa, Lidepla, Pandunia, Kotava, Sambahsa, and relatives)

Scope note: research done 2026-10-09 from about 25 web lookups. The English Wikipedia articles for Globasa and Pandunia return 404, apparently deleted, so those two rest on the projects' own sites plus Kupsala's worldlang list. Most community numbers are self-reports or old snapshots, and they are flagged as such.

## Q1. Creator, date, stated goals and philosophy

### Takeaway
The six projects split into three design families. **Worldlangs** (Pandunia 2007, Lidepla 2006/2010, Globasa 2019) borrow roots from many unrelated major languages. **Zonal or family-based** languages (Elefen = Romance creole; Sambahsa = reconstructed Proto-Indo-European plus some non-IE loans) take their roots from one family. **A priori** Kotava (1975/1978) invents every root for neutrality. The newest worldlang, Globasa, began as a fork that grew out of discussions with Pandunia's creator.

### Cited Findings
- **Elefen / Lingua Franca Nova (LFN)**: created by C. George Boeree of Shippensburg University, Pennsylvania. Design began in 1965, it was first presented on the internet in 1998, and Boeree died on 5 January 2021. Stated goal: "an international auxiliary language simple, coherent and easy to learn for international communication." — [Wikipedia: Lingua Franca Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Franca_Nova)
- Elefen is inspired by the Mediterranean Lingua Franca ("Sabir") and modelled on creoles such as Papiamento and Haitian Creole. — [Wikipedia: LFN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Franca_Nova); the official site describes its grammar as "greatly reduced, similar to Romance creoles" — [elefen.org](https://elefen.org/)
- **Globasa**: created by Hector Ortega (USA). He concluded around 2000 that a world language simpler than Esperanto was possible, met Neo Patwa in 2009, and found Pandunia in July 2017, after which he discussed phonology with its creator Risto Kupsala. Within weeks he decided to build a separate project because of gaps in Pandunia's "morphology and syntax to disambiguate". It took about 2 years to develop: year 1 on function words and the grammar framework, year 2 on the core grammar and a 1,000-root lexicon. It was published on 26 July 2019. Earlier working names were *Geokreol* and *Gayabas*. — [Globasa history](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/histori-de-globasa)
- Globasa's founding thought experiment: 1,000 randomly selected volunteers from around the world are settled on an uninhabited island, and a village language develops over three generations. It was "created with the average world citizen in mind to be as easy as possible to learn and to use without sacrificing clarity or expressiveness." The site describes the creator as "a linguist, translator and expert in adult language acquisition and teaching." — [globasa.net](https://www.globasa.net/)
- Globasa's three design pillars are internationality, "optimal simplicity balanced with functionality", and balance between opposing linguistic parameters. The design mirrors creole languages. — [Globasa design principles](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/diseno-principi)
- **Pandunia**: created in 2007 by Risto Kupsala (Finland) and still active. The same author's related projects are Panlingue (2017, inactive) and Panglo (2021). — [Kupsala, Worldlangs](https://www.kupsala.net/risto/worldlangs/). Its motto is "ze hol dunia, do hol dunia" ("from the whole world, for the whole world"). Stated purposes include "getting to know multicultural international words", "learning scientific vocabulary" and "dreaming of a better world, where all people are equal." The site says "a new version of Pandunia is in the making." — [pandunia.info/pandunia](https://pandunia.info/pandunia/index.html)
- Kupsala's definition of a **worldlang**: "a constructed international auxiliary language that has mostly a priori vocabulary that is borrowed from many unrelated languages around the world". He lists Neo Patwa (2005, Jens Wilkinson, Japan, inactive), Lingwa de Planeta (2006), Sambahsa-mundialect (2007), Pandunia (2007) and Globasa (2019), and also puts Toki Pona (2001) in the table. — [Kupsala, Worldlangs](https://www.kupsala.net/risto/worldlangs/). (The definition says "a priori", which reads oddly next to "borrowed"; it is quoted as given.)
- **Lingwa de Planeta (Lidepla)**: project leader Dmitri Ivanov, a psychologist, with linguist Anastasia Lysenko as the main contributor from 2007 (also A. Vinogradova and E. Ivanova), based in Saint Petersburg. Development started in 2006 and the basic version was published on 1 June 2010. Goal: "a harmonious whole on the base of the most widespread and influential national languages." — [Wikipedia: Lingwa de planeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingwa_de_planeta)
- **Kotava**: created by Staren Fetcey, a Canadian traveller and linguist. Work began in summer 1975, it was released in 1978, and there were major revisions in 1988 and 1993. The language committee *Kotava Avaneda* (seven members) was set up in 2005. The name means "the language of one and all". Fetcey explicitly rejected Esperanto's "Western origin" as a disadvantage. — [Wikipedia: Kotava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava). The official genesis page says earlier projects functioned as "un pidgin occidental, une sorte de néo-latin" (a Western pidgin, a kind of neo-Latin), and that Kotava chose "un vocabulaire et des racines totalement inventées, sans aucune filiation" (vocabulary and roots invented entirely, with no lineage). — [kotava.org, Genèse](https://www.kotava.org/fr/fr_nasbara.php)
- Kotava's stated construction principles: a simple, limited phonetic system pronounceable by most people; a simple, totally regular grammar "that reflects the grammars of the majority of languages in the world"; a clear morphology where each morpheme has one exclusive function; an a priori lexicon that does not favour any language; and productive derivation and compounding. — [Wikipedia: Kotava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava) (via search snippet; the Wikipedia article is flagged for relying heavily on primary sources)
- **Sambahsa-mundialect**: created by French linguist Olivier Simon and released in July 2007 after what the creator says were 8 years of prior work. It is based on Proto-Indo-European and has a "complex grammar". The name comes from *sam* "same" + *bahsa* "language"; *Mundialect* blends *mundial* + *dialect*. — [Wikipedia: Sambahsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambahsa)

### Inferences
- Of the six, only Kotava shares the comparison language's a priori principle. Kotava wants a priori roots for neutrality alone. The comparison language picks its a priori roots for robustness against mishearing, a separate motive that none of these projects claims.
- Globasa and Pandunia both appeal to a "creole" or "village" story to justify an isolating, simplified grammar. Elefen makes the same appeal with Romance creoles.

### Gaps
- No reliable biographical details for Ortega or Kupsala beyond the project sites.
- I could not confirm the current state (2026) of Pandunia's announced "new version", or how Panglo relates to it.
- Neo Patwa: only its creator, date and "inactive" status (Kupsala's list). No design details were gathered.
- Novial revivals: not researched. Nothing found suggests they matter.

## Q2. Vocabulary sourcing method and resulting recognisability

### Takeaway
Globasa has the most explicit, rule-based selection procedure. Candidates are ranked by **how many language families share the word**. European languages count together as one family. Ties go to East Asian sources first, then Arabic, Hindi and so on. Lidepla uses a looser "familiar to speakers of several of 10 languages" test. Pandunia gives a list of source languages but published no weighting that I could find. None of them scores by speaker numbers in a formula. Sambahsa uses reconstructed PIE. Kotava invents every root. I found **no blind recognisability study** for any worldlang vocabulary.

### Cited Findings
- **Globasa methodology** (official). The European languages English, French, German, Russian and Spanish are treated as **one family**. Mandarin, Korean, Japanese, Vietnamese, Hindi, Telugu, Arabic, Swahili, Persian, Turkish, Indonesian and Filipino each count as a separate family.
  - Step 1: pick the source form shared by the most families.
  - Tie-breakers, in order: (1) two or more East Asian languages; (2) Arabic plus another language; (3) Hindi plus another; (4) European plus a non-European language; (5) Persian and Turkish.
  - Step 2: if there is no agreement even after checking synonyms and other parts of speech, fall back to Mandarin (two-character words only), then Vietnamese, Telugu, Swahili, Arabic, Hindi.
  — [Globasa Word Selection Methodology](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)
- The language count is inconsistent across Globasa's own pages. The methodology page reportedly says 18 languages, but the summary lists 17. The home page says roots come "primarily from 16 of the world's major languages". The design-principles page names Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese and Swahili plus nine families: Dravidian, Germanic, Indic, Iranian, Malayo-Polynesian, Romance, Semitic, Slavic, Turkic. — [methodology](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti); [home](https://www.globasa.net/); [design principles](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/diseno-principi). Their stated rationale for the language set: "less widely spoken languages have… borrowed from the languages above", so those languages share cognates with the chosen sources. — [design principles](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/diseno-principi)
- A secondary search summary says some Globasa words need sources in at least 10 families and others as few as 4–8, depending on syllable complexity. I **could not confirm this on the fetched methodology page**, which shows no explicit point thresholds. Treat it as unverified. — [search result pointing to globasa.net methodology](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)
- Globasa form rules:
  - Avoid one-syllable roots and roots longer than three syllables; "Globasa favors three-syllable words over two-syllable words."
  - English: when an English phoneme can be rendered faithfully, pronunciation wins; otherwise spelling wins. English nouns or verbs in *-ation/-ate* "always add -a".
  - Other European words generally follow spelling.
  - An "e-" is prefixed to initial clusters that Globasa phonotactics forbid.
  - Mandarin forms are simplified "when supported by other East Asian languages, particularly Japanese".
  - Arabic /au̯/ is often reduced to ⟨o⟩.
  — [Globasa methodology](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)
- **Lidepla** has ten official source languages: Arabic, Mandarin, English, French, German, Hindi, Persian, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish. Criteria: prefer short words without consonant clusters; a word must be "widespread and/or phonetically familiar for speakers of at least a few different national languages"; pronunciation matters more than original spelling. About 4,000 entries (about 10,000 words) as of 2014. — [Wikipedia: Lingwa de planeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingwa_de_planeta)
- **Pandunia** says its words come from "widely spoken languages, including English, French, Spanish, Japanese, Chinese, Russian, Arabic, Hindi and Swahili". The page has no published scoring. — [pandunia.info/pandunia](https://pandunia.info/pandunia/index.html)
- **Elefen**'s vocabulary is "based on Catalan, Spanish, French, Italian and Portuguese". — [elefen.org](https://elefen.org/)
- **Sambahsa**'s core is reconstructed Indo-European, with extra words from Arabic, Persian, Chinese, Indonesian, Swahili and Turkish. The dictionary had "more than 19,500 entries" as of April 2021. — [Wikipedia: Sambahsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambahsa)
- **Kotava**'s lexicon was invented entirely and has grown in batches: about 2,000 roots in 1975, about 5,000 added in 1978, 5,000 in 1988 and 3,000 in 1993. — [kotava.org Genèse](https://www.kotava.org/fr/fr_nasbara.php). Wikipedia gives "17,000+ basic roots". — [Wikipedia: Kotava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava). (The batch figures add up to about 15,000, which does not match 17,000. Later additions are a likely but unconfirmed explanation.)
- **Recognisability trade-off (opinion, not data).** The blogger Vecderg (11 Oct 2025) contrasts Elefen's "me labora en la pizeria" (understandable to many) with Globasa's "te suki roso pingo" (needs multilingual knowledge). The post says worldlang sentences are "less easily-recognizable and require more study for everyone". Its rough share of vocabulary a learner must memorise is about 10% for eurolangs, about 50% for worldlangs and 100% for a priori languages. **These percentages are the author's guesses and the post cites no studies.** — [Vecderg, "Auxlangs"](https://www.vecderg.com/articles/auxlangs)
- The search-result snippet for Kupsala's worldlang page says comprehensibility of worldlangs "is greatly reduced without study… since most sentences will combine many unrelated languages despite an attempt at optimizing recognizability". — [search snippet attributed to Kupsala, Worldlangs](https://www.kupsala.net/risto/worldlangs/). (Treat as unconfirmed: the fetched copy of that page did not show this text.)

### Inferences
- Globasa's "count families, European languages = one vote" rule is the nearest existing analogue to an algorithmic root selector. It **optimises for breadth of partial recognition**, not for acoustic robustness. The comparison language's mishearing-model selection is a different objective function, and no surveyed project uses one.
- Collapsing English, French, German, Russian and Spanish into one family is a deliberate de-weighting of Europe. As a result, Globasa words are often less recognisable to Europeans than eurolang words, and no more than partly recognisable to anyone else (Vecderg's example illustrates this, anecdotally).
- The comparison language takes its technical vocabulary from English. That mirrors Globasa's handling of Greco-Latin and English *-ation* words and Pandunia's stated aim of teaching "scientific vocabulary". In practice even the worldlangs end up with largely international (European) technical terms.

### Gaps
- **No blind recognisability or comprehension study of any worldlang vocabulary** (Globasa, Pandunia, Lidepla) turned up. Searches for "recognizability study worldlang" returned only blog posts.
- For zonal languages, a Leiden student thesis exists: "Mutual intelligibility and Slavic constructed interlanguages" ([Leiden thesis repository](https://studenttheses.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item:3069585/view)). The fetch returned no content, so its methods and results are unknown. The INCOMSLAV platform ([ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.cllrd-1.6.pdf)) tests natural Slavic intercomprehension, not conlangs as far as the snippet shows.
- Slovio and Interslavic: I gathered no design details (not fetched).
- Interlinguistics literature (Gobbo, Fiedler, Schreyer) on worldlangs: not reached within budget, so I can't say whether it contains empirical tests.

## Q3. Morphology and grammar

### Takeaway
Elefen, Pandunia, Lidepla and Globasa are all **isolating or near-isolating**: roots never change form, and tense comes from particles. Globasa adds a little agglutinative derivation and two broad word classes. Kotava is agglutinative and highly regular, with object–subject–verb order. Sambahsa is the outlier, with fusional PIE-style inflection (4 cases, 4 genders).

### Cited Findings
- **Elefen**: SVO, isolating. The plural is -s/-es. Verbs are invariant, with particles *ia* (past), *va* (future), *ta* (conditional). Derivational suffixes include -or (person), -ador (device), -eria (place), -i (become/cause), -eta (diminutive), -on (augmentative), and -al/-in/-osa (adjective). — [Wikipedia: LFN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Franca_Nova)
- **Globasa** "leans towards the simpler analytic side of the spectrum, but adds just enough by way of affixes", using agglutination rather than inflection, and "Root words always retain their form." It is head-last in simple phrases and word formation, and head-first in complex phrases. — [Globasa design principles](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/diseno-principi). It has two word classes, noun/verb and adjective/adverb, as a "middle ground" between classless words and Esperanto-style class vowel endings, which were rejected as un-creole-like. — [Globasa history](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/histori-de-globasa)
- **Pandunia**: "Words stay always the same… There aren't any special inflected forms for cases, tenses, moods, singular and plural etc." The "basic grammar can be described in only ten rules." — [pandunia.info/pandunia](https://pandunia.info/pandunia/index.html)
- **Lidepla** has three core rules: constant word form, word-class membership, and direct word order. Its exceptions are a plural -s and irregular forms of "to be" (*bi, es, bin*). Tense uses particles or suffixes (e.g. *ve* future, *te* past). — [Wikipedia: Lingwa de planeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingwa_de_planeta)
- **Kotava** has mainly object–subject–verb word order (flexible) and invariable nouns and pronouns with no declension. Verbs have:
  - seven persons, including inclusive and exclusive "we"
  - 3 tenses marked by interfixes (-y- past, -t- future)
  - 4 moods
  - 5 voices: active, passive, reflexive, reciprocal, complementary

  There is no grammatical gender; sex is marked by the suffixes -ya and -ye. — [Wikipedia: Kotava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava)
- **Sambahsa** has 4 cases (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive), 2 numbers and 4 genders (masculine, feminine, neuter, plus an "undetermined" gender that does not come from PIE). All verbs are regular except *ses* "be", *habe* "have" and *woide* "know". — [Wikipedia: Sambahsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambahsa)

### Inferences
- The comparison language's isolating design matches the dominant post-1980 trend (Elefen, Pandunia, Lidepla, Globasa). Globasa's history page records why it backed away from fully classless words: disambiguation. That is a known pressure point for strictly isolating designs.

### Gaps
- No Globasa affix inventory or tense particles were collected (the FrathWiki page was 403; the grammar is at [xwexi.globasa.net](https://xwexi.globasa.net/eng/), not fetched).

## Q4. Phonology and orthography

### Takeaway
All of them use the Latin alphabet with one letter (or digraph) per sound, mostly with 5 vowels. Globasa is the only one with a published rule against confusable minimal pairs (v/w, s/z, final m/n, and where possible l/r, b/p, f/p, h/r). That rule is the closest precedent to the comparison language's mishearing model. Sambahsa deliberately keeps a "complex yet regular" spelling to preserve word recognisability.

### Cited Findings
- **Globasa** has 5 vowels and 20 consonants. Further reduction was rejected because it would sacrifice "fidelity to the words in their original source languages" and generate excessive homophony. Onset clusters are limited, with "a maximum of one coda (syllable-final) consonant". — [Globasa design principles](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/diseno-principi). It uses a "strict one-letter-one-phoneme system", choosing phonemes "familiar to at least 50% of the population". — [Globasa history](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/histori-de-globasa)
- **Globasa minimal-pair rule**: never adopt root pairs that differ only by v/w, s/z, or final m/n, and avoid l/r, b/p, f/p, c/j, c/x and h/r distinctions "when possible". Roots normally end in a vowel. Nouns and verbs must not begin with le-, xa-, nun-, ger-, be- or du-, to avoid confusion with particles, and words that look affixed are generally avoided. — [Globasa methodology](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)
- **Elefen** is "spelled as it sounds, using 22 letters" and has 5 vowels. Cyrillic was co-official until 2021. — [elefen.org](https://elefen.org/); [Wikipedia: LFN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Franca_Nova)
- **Pandunia** uses "One sound = one letter or a pair of letters". Stress falls on the syllable before the root's last consonant. — [pandunia.info/pandunia](https://pandunia.info/pandunia/index.html)
- **Lidepla** has 25 Latin letters, 17 basic consonants plus 3 optional ones, and 5 vowels. Stress falls on the vowel before the final consonant, with some exceptions. — [Wikipedia: Lingwa de planeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingwa_de_planeta)
- **Kotava** uses the Latin alphabet without H or Q, 5 vowels plus 5 diphthongs, and predictable pronunciation. Stress is final if the word ends in a consonant and penultimate if it ends in a vowel. Its one diacritic, an acute accent, marks stress on first-person verbs. — [Wikipedia: Kotava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava)
- **Sambahsa** has 22 consonants and 9 vowels and uses only the basic 26-letter alphabet. Its orthography is "complex yet regular", which preserves word recognisability, and its stress rules follow German/Italian patterns. — [Wikipedia: Sambahsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambahsa)

### Inferences
- Globasa's rule avoids minimal pairs **within its own lexicon**. It does not model cross-linguistic perceptual confusion; it is a hand-made list of risky contrasts. The comparison language's mishearing model generalises the same idea, and Globasa's list can serve as a sanity check of which contrasts a human designer judged risky.
- Globasa's preference for three-syllable roots and avoidance of one-syllable ones is a different choice from the comparison language's two-syllable roots. Globasa's reason, fidelity to sources, does not apply to a priori roots.

### Gaps
- No project's documentation that I reached mentions designing for resistance to future sound change.

## Q5. Community size, activity, corpora, institutions, apps (as of 2026 where possible)

### Takeaway
All six communities are small. Speaker counts run from dozens (Lidepla, Globasa) to the low hundreds of online members (Elefen). Elefen is the most institutionalised: an ISO code, its own Wikipedia (about 4,700 articles), a dictionary of 20,000+ entries and an original novel. No current figures from 2026 could be verified. None of these languages appeared in search results as having a Duolingo course.

### Cited Findings
- **Elefen**:
  - ISO 639-3 code *lfn* (January 2008).
  - Regulated by the Asosia per Lingua Franca Nova (ALFN).
  - Dictionary of 20,000+ entries (2018).
  - Wikipedia edition launched on 18 April 2018.
  - 12+ literary translations, including Carroll, Shakespeare, Dickens and Poe.
  - Original novel *La xerca per Pahoa* (online May 2020, print May 2021).
  - Yahoo group (2002) with about 300 contributing members; Facebook group (2007) with 740+ members. These are old snapshots.

  — [Wikipedia: LFN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Franca_Nova). The Elefen Wikipedia's main page currently says "Aora nos ave 4,686 articles" (undated live counter, fetched 2026-10-09). — [lfn.wikipedia.org](https://lfn.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paje_xef). Learning resources include WikiBooks lessons in 8 languages and Memrise courses (1,500+ core words), with Discord, Telegram and Facebook groups. — [elefen.org](https://elefen.org/)
- **Globasa**: its own site calls the community "small but vibrant", on Reddit, Discord, Bluesky and Telegram. Resources: a beginner's course, a dictionary with 3,000+ roots and "over 9,000 total dictionary entries", and reading materials. — [globasa.net](https://www.globasa.net/). A search summary claimed "as of 2025, approximately 15 speakers". Its source was unclear (one of the Globasawiki, FrathWiki or EverybodyWiki results), so **treat it as unverified**. — [search results incl. Globasawiki](https://wiki.globasa.net/wiki/Globasa)
- **Lidepla**: "About 10–15 people have mastered the language, and about 50 can use it in communication" (undated in the article), and 25+ users were reported in 2012. Outputs include translations (*Alice*, Russian literature), an album by Jonny M and the magazine *Jiva*. Its ISO 639-3 request (2017) was **rejected in 2018** for "not appearing to be used in a variety of domains nor for communication within a community that includes all ages." — [Wikipedia: Lingwa de planeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingwa_de_planeta)
- **Kotava**: ISO 639-3 *avk*. It is mainly known in French-speaking regions and most of its materials are in French. Wikipedia mentions "hundreds of literary translations" but flags itself for overreliance on primary sources. — [Wikipedia: Kotava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava). kotava.org runs in ten languages; worldwide internet distribution started in 2005. — [kotava.org Genèse](https://www.kotava.org/fr/fr_nasbara.php)
- **Sambahsa**: its ISO code proposal was **rejected in 2018**. Translations include *Alice in Wonderland*, *Demian* and *The Little Prince*, and some films have Sambahsa subtitles. — [Wikipedia: Sambahsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambahsa)
- **Pandunia**: forums on Discord, Reddit, Facebook and Telegram, plus a web forum. — [pandunia.info/pandunia](https://pandunia.info/pandunia/index.html). Vecderg reports that its creator considers development still premature after about 20 years (secondhand). — [Vecderg](https://www.vecderg.com/articles/auxlangs)

### Inferences
- The ISO 639-3 outcomes are a useful hard external signal. Elefen (2008) and Kotava have codes; Lidepla and Sambahsa were rejected in 2018 for lack of a real multi-domain, all-ages community. Globasa and Pandunia have no code (Wikidata exists for Globasa: [Q66311273](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q66311273)).
- Taken together, the decades-old a priori Kotava and the young worldlangs have similar small communities. Neither "neutral a priori" nor "worldwide a posteriori" sourcing visibly produced adoption. The only language among the six with a sizeable corpus, Elefen, is the one with the most recognisable (Romance) vocabulary for Western learners, plus a long-lived single author.

### Gaps
- No verified 2026 member counts for r/globasa, Discord servers or Elefen groups.
- I found no evidence about any of these languages on Duolingo (absence in search results is not proof of absence).

## Q6. Criticisms, internal debates, reforms; learnability evidence

### Takeaway
The recurring criticism is the worldlang trade-off: "fair" multi-source vocabulary is recognisable to nobody without study, while eurolangs are recognisable mostly to Europeans. Reforms are frequent in young projects: Globasa's adjustments after publication exceeded expectations, and Pandunia has a new version and spin-offs. I found **no controlled learnability or comprehension studies** of these six.

### Cited Findings
- Globasa was published "at approximately 95% stability", but adjustments after publication exceeded initial expectations. — [Globasa history](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/histori-de-globasa)
- Globasa itself began as a critique of Pandunia's morphology and syntax for disambiguation. — [Globasa history](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/histori-de-globasa)
- Pandunia: "a new version… is in the making" — [pandunia.info](https://pandunia.info/pandunia/index.html). Kupsala also started Panlingue (2017, now inactive) and Panglo (2021) — [Kupsala](https://www.kupsala.net/risto/worldlangs/).
- Kotava had major reforms in 1988 and 1993; an academy has existed since 2005. — [Wikipedia: Kotava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava)
- Elefen dropped Cyrillic as a co-official script in 2021. — [Wikipedia: LFN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_Franca_Nova)
- The "Euroclone" pejorative reflects auxlang community fatigue with European-based designs. Vecderg also argues that a priori neutrality is "the ultimate choice of equality! It's also not very popular!" and that true neutrality is impossible even a priori (opinion, citing anecdotes and informal interviews). — [Vecderg](https://www.vecderg.com/articles/auxlangs)

### Inferences
- Kotava is the relevant precedent for the comparison language: 50 years of a priori roots with a dedicated academy, and a community still small and mostly francophone. Its a priori lexicon gave neutrality but did not visibly win learners. That does not show a priori roots fail, but it suggests neutrality alone is not a selling point.

### Gaps
- Academic critiques (Gobbo, Fiedler, Schreyer) were not reached.
- I found no published learnability experiments for any of the six.
- I found no criticism specific to Sambahsa's complex spelling or Lidepla beyond the ISO rejections.

## Q7. Cultural neutrality, taboo/offensive word collisions, sound-change resistance

### Takeaway
Neutrality is handled through source balancing (Globasa's family counting with European languages pooled, Lidepla's 10 languages) or through a priori invention (Kotava). **None of the projects documented a systematic check of roots against offensive or taboo words in other languages**, and none mentions designing for resistance to sound change. On both points the comparison language would be doing something this group of projects has not done, as far as I can tell.

### Cited Findings
- Kotava pursues neutrality by inventing roots "sans aucune filiation" to avoid favouring Western speakers. — [kotava.org Genèse](https://www.kotava.org/fr/fr_nasbara.php). The genesis page summary does not mention any criterion for avoiding resemblance to real words or offensive words.
- Globasa's methodology page has no explicit guideline on offensive or taboo words. Its only anti-collision rules concern minimal pairs and particle-like onsets. — [Globasa methodology](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)
- Globasa rationalises its neutrality by noting that smaller languages borrowed from its source languages and so share cognates. — [Globasa design principles](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/diseno-principi)

### Inferences
- An offensive-word check across 34 languages appears to be novel compared with these six. The likeliest exceptions are informal, undocumented checks in community word-proposal threads, such as [Globasa's word proposal process](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/proceso-fe-lexijeni) (not fetched).
- Sound-change resistance: these projects get stability from fixed orthography and academies or regulators (ALFN, Kotava Avaneda), not from phonological design.

### Gaps
- Globasa's word-proposal process page and the Kotava grammar were not read, so undocumented taboo screening can't be ruled out.
- Pandunia's and Lidepla's sites were not checked for taboo or neutrality pages.
