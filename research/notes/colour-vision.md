# Colour vision and the six-root colour vocabulary

Scope: does vision science justify six basic colour roots (black, white, red, yellow, green, blue), with blue and green kept separate?

Legend: **[primary]** means I read it in the paper's text. **[abstract/secondary]** means it comes from an abstract, a review or a search snippet. **[UNVERIFIED]** means it is from memory.

## 1. Opponent channels and Hering's six

- The cone-opponent channels are established physiology. Derrington, Krauskopf & Lennie 1984 (J Physiol 357:241) found that parvocellular LGN neurons in macaques fall into two chromatic classes: L–M, and S against L+M. With a luminance axis, these define the "DKL" space. [abstract/secondary] — [J Physiol](https://physoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1113/jphysiol.1984.sp015499)
- Mollon & Jordan 1997 describe the L–M channel as "phylogenetically recent" and the S channel as "phylogenetically older". [primary] — [PDF](https://vision.psychol.cam.ac.uk/jdmollon/papers/MollonJordan1997UniqueHues.pdf)
- Hering's elementary colours are a claim about how colours appear, not about wiring. Four unique hues look unmixed; black and white are the achromatic pair. Kay & McDaniel 1978 (Language 54:610) mapped basic colour terms directly onto these "six primary colors of opponent theory". [abstract/secondary] — [Blutner summary](http://www.blutner.de/color/color.app.evol.pdf), [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9926261940c1aa3f4568da10eff99854f2dfd3b)

## 2. Unique hues are not the cardinal axes

- Webster et al. 2000 (JOSA A 17:1545) state: "with the possible exception of unique red—the unique hues do not correspond to activity along a single cardinal axis." Across observers, unique green "varied over a range of 60 deg" of hue angle, while red covered 20°. Differences between observers were "uncorrelated" between hues and unrelated to sensitivity on the cone-opponent axes. The hues "appear largely unconstrained by" those axes. [primary] — [PDF](https://labs.psych.unr.edu/websterlab/files/WebsterJOSA2000b.pdf)
- Mollon & Jordan 1997: the line from unique yellow (about 572 nm) to unique blue (about 475 nm) "is oblique, not vertical" in the MacLeod–Boynton cone space. "The nature of the unique hues remains mysterious and we do not know whether they tell us anything about the neural organisation of the visual system." In 97 men, unique green had a mean of 511 nm and an SD of 13 nm. [primary]
- Witzel & Gegenfurtner 2018 (Vision Res 151:152) found only a weak link between perceptual categories and second-stage mechanisms. Unique hues were set no more consistently than binary hues such as orange. [abstract/secondary] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S004269891830049X)

**Verdict (inferred):** the mismatch is strong. It breaks the argument "six terms because the retina and LGN have six poles". Kay & McDaniel's direct mapping is outdated as physiology. The proposed explanations are a later cortical stage, or the environment: the daylight locus, and singularities in surface reflectance (Witzel, Cinotti & O'Regan 2015, [JoV](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2346729)). So the opponent-process account can motivate the six, but it cannot prove them.

## 3. Unique hues and colour naming

- Regier, Kay & Cook 2005 (PNAS 102:8386) used 110 unwritten languages from the World Color Survey (WCS). Their best-example choices "cluster near the prototypes for English white, black, red, green, yellow, and blue". [abstract/secondary] — [search record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2e2f9eab55c18bf7f22a901f45cab07b306a898). Note that the comparison is with English prototypes, not with measured unique hues.
- Kay & Cook's WCS encyclopedia entry cites Kuehni 2007. In 38 WCS languages, focal choices "agreed well with unique hue judgments of 300 speakers of several written languages". [primary for the entry; Kuehni not read] — [PDF](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~kjameson/ECST/Kay_Cook_WorldColorSurvey.pdf)
- The sequence in the brief is a simplification. The revised 2009 sequence (Kay & Cook, Fig. 3) starts from composites: Stage I is W/R/Y vs Bk/G/Bu. Stage V is the six-term "Hering" system: W, R, Y, G, Bu, Bk. Blue is the last of the six to separate, either from grue or from a black/blue term. The 1969 sequence continues past six to stages VI and VII, which add brown, purple, pink, orange and grey. [primary]
- WALS ch. 134 (Kay & Maffi), sample of 120 languages: "83%" follow the single main trajectory. Systems with fewer than five terms "tend strongly to occur in the tropics." [primary] — [WALS 134](https://wals.info/chapter/134)

## 4. Why many languages lump blue and green

- **The ~57% figure checks out, with caveats.** In WALS 134, 68 of 120 languages have a grue term (56.7%). Counting black/green/blue (15) and yellow/green/blue (2) terms too, 85 of 120 (70.8%) lump green and blue somehow. Only 30 (25%) keep them distinct. The sample is "mainly unwritten contemporary languages" from the WCS, so it is not representative of the world's speakers. [primary; percentages computed]
- **The lens-yellowing (UV) hypothesis is weak.** Lindsey & Brown 2002 (Psych Sci 13:506) tied grue to UV-B-induced lens yellowing.
  - Regier & Kay 2004 noted that technology level is confounded with the tropics. If grue came from distorted perception, its best examples should form one peak between green and blue. They form separate peaks: black (J0, 87 hits), green (F17, 79) and blue (F29, 45). So grue "tend[s] not to be perceptual distortions of green, but rather genuine abstractions over green and blue—and black." [primary] — [PDF](https://lclab.berkeley.edu/papers/sunlight.pdf)
  - Hardy et al. 2005 (Psych Sci 16:321) compared younger and older adults (mean ages 23.2 and 73.9). Lens yellowing was "thoroughly compensated at the level of color naming". [abstract/secondary]
- **Environmental correlates are real but contested.** Josserand et al. 2021 (Sci Rep), 142 populations:
  - A distinct blue term is less likely with high UV-B and near large lakes.
  - It is more likely with larger populations and greater cultural complexity. [abstract/secondary] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8476573/)
  - Hardy, Werner, Regier, Kay & Frederick 2023 replied that colour constancy compensates for lens yellowing. They cite Japanese and Nafaanra, which split grue with no fall in UV. [primary] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9892488/)
  - Josserand et al.'s reply concedes that "lens brunescence alone is unlikely to explain" the pattern. [primary] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9892588/)
- **Communicative need.** Gibson et al. 2017 (PNAS) found that warm colours are always communicated better than cool colours, in every language they studied. "Industrialization, which creates objects distinguishable solely based on color, increases color usefulness." They suggest there "simply are not that many natural blue objects." [abstract/secondary] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5635863)

**Verdict (inferred):** grue is a coarser partition of the same perceptual space, not a perceptual error. The best explanation is low need, linked to technology and society size. UV may add a small effect. Nobody serious argues that grue speakers cannot see the difference.

## 5. Bottom line

The support is moderate, and it comes from naming data, not from neurophysiology.

- **For:**
  - The six foci are near-universal.
  - Every system that grows past five terms splits green from blue.
  - Grue is explained by low need, not by a different way of seeing.
  - For speakers in industrial settings, where colour often tells objects apart, a separate blue fits the communicative need. That is a principled choice, not a legacy one.
- **Against:**
  - Don't justify the six with the cone-opponent axes. Only red lines up with a cardinal pole, and unique hues vary widely between people.
  - Vision science gives no reason to stop at six. Stages VI and VII add brown, purple, pink, orange and grey, and industrialised languages typically have about 11 basic terms (Berlin & Kay 1969 [UNVERIFIED count]). Six is a size choice, so its source should be stated: learnability, or a minimal root budget.
