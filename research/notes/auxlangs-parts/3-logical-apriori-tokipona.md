# Loglan, Lojban, Toki Pona, and a priori schemes (Wilkins, Solresol, Ro, aUI, Ithkuil): working notes

Part 3 of the auxlangs report (`../auxlangs.md`). Started 2026-10-13. Labels: claims carry an inline link or full reference; **[unverified]** = not confirmed in a source I read; **[secondary]** = reported by a source I read, original not read.

## 1. Loglan (James Cooke Brown, 1955–)

- **Aim.** Brown: "Loglan is a language which was originally devised to test the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis that the structure of language determines the boundaries of human thought"; the idea was to introduce "a reasonably culture-free second language with known formal properties into a laboratory-like setting" ([Brown, *Loglan 1*, 4th ed. 1989, ch. 1](http://www.loglan.org/Loglan1/chap1.html)). Begun 1955; goals first published as "Loglan", *Scientific American*, June 1960 ([CLL ch. 1 §1](https://lojban.github.io/cll/1/1/)). The 1960 article itself not read [secondary].
- **The test never happened.** Brown, 1989: "the crucial experiments have not yet been run"; the hypothesis "is still untested" ([Loglan 1 ch. 1](http://www.loglan.org/Loglan1/chap1.html)). So the founding scientific aim was never cashed in, ~34 years in.
- **Vocabulary algorithm (the ancestor of Lojban's).** Primitives built to be recognisable to speakers of the "eight most widely spoken human tongues" as of 1955: English .28, Chinese .25, Hindi .11, Russian .10, Spanish .09, French .06, Japanese .06, German .05 ([Loglan 1 ch. 6](https://www.loglan.org/Loglan1/chap6.html)). Brown's metric was a *recognition score*: "The average recognizability score of a Loglan composite word over this immense source population is 45%" (expected share of a word's sounds a random speaker would recognise from their own language); ~.70 for English speakers (ibid.). A trial ninth language, Arabic, "turned out to make no significant contribution to the average recognizability of our composite words; so Arabic was not retained" (ibid.).
  - Note what 45% means: it is a *computed overlap*, not a measured recall rate. Brown gives no controlled memory experiment in the chapters I read; his learnability evidence is anecdotal: "30 days of an hour a day of vocabulary drill with flashcards plus somewhat less time spent in passive listening to cassettes seems to have been sufficient" to start instruction; Loglan "does seem to be easily learned" ([Loglan 1 ch. 1, note 11 and text](http://www.loglan.org/Loglan1/chap1.html)).
- **Governance and the split.** Brown founded The Loglan Institute (TLI) in 1962 to promote the language [secondary: search-result summary of the Federal Circuit opinion, below]. Per Bob LeChevalier's account (written 1991, updated 2000), when volunteers built a vocabulary flashcard program in 1986, Brown demanded they "sign a copyright acknowledgement and agree to pay royalties, claiming copyright on the individual words"; the community instead decided to "reinvent a public domain vocabulary and grammar", i.e. relexify everything ([LeChevalier, "The Loglan-Lojban Dispute", lojban.org](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/the_Loglan-Lojban_Dispute)). This is a partisan source (LeChevalier led the LLG).
  - TLI registered LOGLAN as a trademark for "Dictionaries and Grammars" (application 17 Aug 1987); the TTAB cancelled it as generic and the Federal Circuit affirmed: "the Institute's mark, LOGLAN, is generic" ([*Loglan Institute, Inc. v. Logical Language Group, Inc.*, 962 F.2d 1038 (Fed. Cir. 1992)](https://www.leagle.com/decision/19922000962f2d103811839); full text at [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/962/1038/91957/), which returned 403 to me). Dates of LLG founding conflict: CLL says the project has been "led since 1987 by The Logical Language Group" ([CLL 1.1](https://lojban.github.io/cll/1/1/)); the court-opinion summary says the Language Group was founded November 1988 [secondary, search snippet only]. Unresolved.
- **Afterlife.** Loglan continues as a small project; a "De Facto Reference Grammar of the Loglan Language" by M. Randall Holmes is dated 10/11/2025 ([randall-holmes.github.io](https://randall-holmes.github.io/Loglan/Reports/reference_grammar_proposed.pdf), title seen in search only) [unverified beyond title].
- **Lesson flagged for Apupiwe:** the inventor's IP control is what produced the schism, at the cost of throwing away an entire vocabulary. One designer + AI has the same single-point-of-control shape; decide licence/ownership of the lexicon up front.
