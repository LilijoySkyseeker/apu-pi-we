# Loglan, Lojban, Toki Pona, and a priori schemes (Wilkins, Solresol, Ro, aUI, Ithkuil): working notes

Part 3 of the auxlangs report (`../auxlangs.md`). Started 2026-10-13. Labels: claims carry an inline link or full reference; **[unverified]** = not confirmed in a source I read; **[secondary]** = reported by a source I read, original not read.

## 1. Loglan (James Cooke Brown, 1955–)

- **Aim.** Brown: "Loglan is a language which was originally devised to test the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis that the structure of language determines the boundaries of human thought"; the idea was to introduce "a reasonably culture-free second language with known formal properties into a laboratory-like setting" ([Brown, *Loglan 1*, 4th ed. 1989, ch. 1](http://www.loglan.org/Loglan1/chap1.html)). Begun 1955; goals first published as "Loglan", *Scientific American*, June 1960 ([CLL ch. 1 §1](https://lojban.github.io/cll/1/1/)). The 1960 article itself not read [secondary].
- **The test never happened.** Brown, 1989: "the crucial experiments have not yet been run"; the hypothesis "is still untested" ([Loglan 1 ch. 1](http://www.loglan.org/Loglan1/chap1.html)). So the founding scientific aim was never cashed in, ~34 years in.
- **Vocabulary algorithm (the ancestor of Lojban's).** Primitives built to be recognisable to speakers of the "eight most widely spoken human tongues" as of 1955: English .28, Chinese .25, Hindi .11, Russian .10, Spanish .09, French .06, Japanese .06, German .05 ([Loglan 1 ch. 6](https://www.loglan.org/Loglan1/chap6.html)). Brown's metric was a *recognition score*: "The average recognizability score of a Loglan composite word over this immense source population is 45%" (expected share of a word's sounds a random speaker would recognise from their own language); ~.70 for English speakers (ibid.). A trial ninth language, Arabic, "turned out to make no significant contribution to the average recognizability of our composite words; so Arabic was not retained" (ibid.).
  - Note what 45% means: it is a *computed overlap*, not a measured recall rate. Brown gives no controlled memory experiment in the chapters I read; his learnability evidence is anecdotal: "30 days of an hour a day of vocabulary drill with flashcards plus somewhat less time spent in passive listening to cassettes seems to have been sufficient" to start instruction; Loglan "does seem to be easily learned" ([Loglan 1 ch. 1, note 11 and text](http://www.loglan.org/Loglan1/chap1.html)).
- **Governance and the split.** Brown founded The Loglan Institute (TLI) in 1962 to promote the language [secondary: search-result summary of the Federal Circuit opinion, below]. Per Bob LeChevalier's account (written 1991, updated 2000), when volunteers built a vocabulary flashcard program in 1986, Brown demanded they "sign a copyright acknowledgement and agree to pay royalties, claiming copyright on the individual words"; the community instead decided to "reinvent a public domain vocabulary and grammar", i.e. relexify everything ([LeChevalier, "The Loglan-Lojban Dispute", lojban.org](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/the_Loglan-Lojban_Dispute)). This is a partisan source (LeChevalier led the LLG).
  - TLI registered LOGLAN as a trademark for "Dictionaries and Grammars" (application 17 Aug 1987); the TTAB cancelled it as generic and the Federal Circuit affirmed: "the Institute's mark, LOGLAN, is generic" ([*Loglan Institute, Inc. v. Logical Language Group, Inc.*, 962 F.2d 1038 (Fed. Cir. 1992)](https://www.leagle.com/decision/19922000962f2d103811839); full text at [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/962/1038/91957/), which returned 403 to me). Dates of LLG founding conflict: CLL says the project has been "led since 1987 by The Logical Language Group" ([CLL 1.1](https://lojban.github.io/cll/1/1/)); the court-opinion summary says the Language Group was founded November 1988 [secondary, search snippet only]. Unresolved.
- **Afterlife.** Loglan continues as a small project; a "De Facto Reference Grammar of the Loglan Language" by M. Randall Holmes is dated 10/11/2025 ([randall-holmes.github.io](https://randall-holmes.github.io/Loglan/Reports/reference_grammar_proposed.pdf), title seen in search only) [unverified beyond title].
- **Lesson flagged for Apupiwe:** the inventor's IP control is what produced the schism, at the cost of throwing away an entire vocabulary. One designer + AI has the same single-point-of-control shape; decide licence/ownership of the lexicon up front.

## 2. Lojban (Logical Language Group, 1987–)

### Aim, designers, sources
- Relexified, public-domain successor of Loglan; LLG founded July 1987, initial gismu list completed December 1987, LLG incorporated May 1988 ([Lojban timeline, lojban.org wiki](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)). Reference grammar: John Woldemar Cowan, *The Complete Lojban Language* (CLL), LLG 1997, ISBN 0-9660283-0-9 ([online 1st-ed text](https://lojban.github.io/cll/)); CLL published 28 Nov 1997 per the [timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline). A "Version 1.1" PDF was generated 2016-08-26 ([lojban.org PDF](https://www.lojban.org/publications/cll/cll_v1.1_2016-08-26.pdf), date from search listing; not opened). A 2026 GitHub fork (int19h/cll) is rewriting CLL ch. 1's finality claims to reflect post-1997 changes (xorlo, dotside, etc.) ([issue #12, opened 2026-07-12](https://github.com/int19h/cll/issues/12)).
- Stated design features (CLL §1.1): grammar "based on the principles of predicate logic"; "an unambiguous yet flexible grammar"; "phonetic spelling, and unambiguously resolves its sounds into words"; "neutral between cultures"; "simple ... easy to learn"; "1300 root words can be easily combined to form a vocabulary of millions of words"; "regular; the rules of the language are without exceptions" ([CLL 1.1](https://lojban.github.io/cll/1/1/)). Note: "easy to learn" is asserted, not evidenced.
- Explicitly *not* invented by one person: "Lojban was not 'invented' by any one person or committee. Often, grammatical features were introduced into the language long before their usage was fully understood" ([CLL 1.2](https://lojban.github.io/cll/1/2/)).

### Grammar type
- Isolating/analytic: "Lojban words don't change form depending on how they are used"; tense, number etc. are separate words; but rich *derivational* morphology ([CLL 4.1](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/1/)). Three word classes: cmavo (structure words), brivla (predicates: gismu, lujvo, fu'ivla), cmene (names); each class is identifiable from its *shape* so the speech stream segments unambiguously (ibid.). Predicates have numbered argument places ("place structures"); a sentence = bridi (predicate + sumti). Critic's note: "For each word you must not only remember the plain definition, but also the ideas the word relate (differing in numbers from 1 to 5)" ([Arnt Richard Johansen, "Reasons not to like Lojban", undated personal page](http://arj.nvg.org/lojban/anti-lojban.html)).
- Sounds/writing: Latin letters, one sound per letter; 17 consonants b c d f g j k l m n p r s t v x z, vowels a e i o u + y; apostrophe = /h/ between vowels; 48 permissible initial consonant pairs ([CLL 4.1](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/1/)). Period marks obligatory pauses; no ordinary punctuation ([Johansen](http://arj.nvg.org/lojban/anti-lojban.html)).

### Vocabulary: the gismu algorithm (directly comparable to Apupiwe's optimiser)
- Size: "There are about 1350 gismu" plus "perhaps 200 major cmavo" to communicate ([CLL 4.4](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/4/)). Shape: always 5 letters, CVCCV or CCVCV, stress on first syllable, exactly one consonant pair (ibid.).
- **No semantic system.** "The gismu do not represent any sort of systematic partitioning of semantic space. Some gismu may be superfluous, or appear for historical reasons: the gismu list was being collected for almost 35 years and was only weeded out once" ([CLL 4.4](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/4/)). The concept list was inherited and accreted, not designed.
- **Algorithm, verbatim essentials** ([CLL 4.14](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/14/)):
  1. For each concept, find a word in each of Chinese, English, Hindi, Spanish, Russian, Arabic; render "into Lojban phonetics rather liberally" (stop+fricative → fricative, "tc"→"c"; non-Lojban vowels mapped; morphological endings dropped; same rules for all six).
  2. "All possible gismu forms were matched against the six source-language forms." Score per language: if ≥3 letters are the same and in the same order (gaps allowed), score = number of shared letters; if exactly 2, score 2 only if adjacent in both words or separated by one letter in both; reversed order scores 0; else 0.
  3. Divide by the Lojbanized source word's length; multiply by language weight (proportional to L1 + L2 speakers, "Second-language speakers were reckoned at half their actual numbers"); weights sum to 1.00; sum = total score.
  4. Remove candidates that conflict with existing gismu: identical; identical except final vowel (would share a 4-letter rafsi); or differ by one consonant from a "too similar" set: b~p,v; c~j,s; d~t; f~p,v; g~k,x; j~c,z; k~g,x; l~r; m~n; p~b,f; s~c,z; t~d; v~b,f; x~g,k; z~j,s.
  5. "The gismu form with the highest score usually became the actual gismu. Sometimes a lower-scoring form was used to provide a better rafsi." Transcription blunders survive: "the gismu 'gismu' should have been 'gicmu', but it's too late to fix it now."
  - Weights (1985 data): Chinese 0.36, English 0.21, Hindi 0.16, Spanish 0.11, Russian 0.09, Arabic 0.07. "A few gismu were made much later" with Chinese 0.347, Hindi 0.196, English 0.160, Spanish 0.123, Russian 0.089, Arabic 0.085 (ibid.). Urdu speakers counted with Hindi ([CLL 4.15](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/15/)).
  - **Vowels deliberately exempt from the confusability filter:** "the stressed vowel ... was considered sufficiently distinctive that two or more gismu may differ only in this vowel; as an extreme example, 'bradi', 'bredi', 'bridi', and 'brodi' ... are all existing gismu" ([CLL 4.14](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/14/)). The design goal was the *opposite* of Wilkins: "the set of gismu was specifically designed to reduce the likelihood that two similar sounding gismu could be confused. For example, because 'gismu' is in the set of gismu, 'kismu', 'xismu', 'gicmu', 'gizmu', and 'gisnu' cannot be" ([CLL 4.4](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/4/)).
  - Exceptions made by hand: brod- variables, cmavo/lojbo/lujvo/mekso, metric prefixes, SI units, and ~70 "cultural gismu" (nationalities, languages, religions) all ending in -o, "otherwise a rare letter in Lojban gismu" ([CLL 4.15](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/15/)). Even an algorithmic lexicon needed a hand-coined escape hatch.
- **Were the gismu memorable? Evidence is weak and mostly negative.**
  - Official claim: "a working vocabulary including the complete set of 1350 root words can take 8-12 weeks of study at 1 hour per day" with flashcards ([LLG brochure, "Questions and Answers on Lojban"](https://www.lojban.org/publications/level0/brochure/lojbanmo.html), undated). No study found behind it [no controlled study located].
  - Critics: "The vocabulary is unmemorable (all the about 1350 'basic words', gismu, have 5 letters, and most have little similarity with their English counterparts)" ([Johansen](http://arj.nvg.org/lojban/anti-lojban.html)). Robin Lee Powell (former BPFK chair, camxes author), 2014: "The language is *physically* hard to speak, to an extent that is distinctly unpleasent", citing "self-similarity of existing words, and the vast array of accidental tongue twisters" ([lojban Google Group thread "Lojban's Biggest Problem or Why Still Nobody Speaks It", 26 Oct 2014](https://groups.google.com/g/lojban/c/e7cg5vy2EfA/m/aJaC09ERWasJ)). The thread starter (selpa'i) argued instead that the main problem is too few words: "there aren't enough words, and the few words that exist do not enable us to make nuanced descriptions of the world" (ibid.).
  - Inference: a 6-way weighted blend gives each speaker only fragments of their own word (Brown's own computed figure for the 8-language Loglan blend was 45% of sounds recognisable on average, see §1). Recognisability-by-algorithm was never validated by a recall experiment that I could find. For Apupiwe, the takeaway: optimiser scores ≠ learner memory; test recall with humans.
- **Lujvo / rafsi / tanru.** tanru = juxtaposed brivla, modifier first ("seltau"), head second ("tertau") ([CLL 5.2](https://lojban.github.io/cll/5/2/)). lujvo = a tanru fused into one word from rafsi (combining forms); every gismu has 2–5 rafsi: the 5-letter (gismu itself, final only), the 4-letter (minus final vowel), and up to three 3-letter CVC/CCV/CVV short rafsi, assigned by presumed usefulness; a scoring algorithm picks the standard dictionary form of a lujvo ([CLL 4.6](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/6/)). The 3-letter rafsi are a second vocabulary to memorise ([Johansen](http://arj.nvg.org/lojban/anti-lojban.html)). Rafsi were reassigned wholesale in the "Great Rafsi Reallocation", 1 June 1993 ([timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)).

### Precision vs ambiguity
- Syntactic: CLL ch. 21 gives the official YACC grammar (EBNF secondary; "In case of discrepancies, the YACC version is official") ([CLL 21](https://lojban.github.io/cll/21/1/)). But the grammar file itself concedes the YACC part covers only a portion: YACC "is used to generate a parser for a portion of the grammar, which is LALR1 ... but most of the rest of the grammar must be parsed using some language-coded processing" (pre-processing steps for quotes, erasure words si/sa/su); the "sa" erasure rule "is hard to formalize and may receive further definition later" (ibid.). Morphology: "a detailed algorithm for breaking sounds into words has not yet been fully debugged, and so is not presented in this book" ([CLL 4.1](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/1/), 1997 text). So "unambiguous grammar" was a design property proven for the core, not end to end, in 1997.
- Later parsers: camxes, a PEG grammar, development began 2003 under Robin Lee Powell ([timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)); ilmentufa (GitHub repo created 2016, active to Sept 2026) ships five PEG grammars and JS parsers incl. `camxes.peg` (standard) and `camxes-beta.peg` ("with the addition of the most popular and backward-compatible experimental cmavo and grammar changes"), plus variants for the "Cmevla-Brivla Merger" and "Ce-Ki-Tau" experimental grammars ([ilmentufa README](https://github.com/lojban/ilmentufa)). I.e., the formal grammar became the medium in which dialects are defined.
- Semantic: deliberately vague at the tanru level. "All tanru are ambiguous semantically": *klama jubme* "goer-type-of table" could be a wheeled table, a table owned/used by goers, etc. ([CLL 5.2](https://lojban.github.io/cll/5/2/)); "Making tanru is essentially a poetic or creative act, not a science. While the syntax ... is unambiguous, tanru are still semantically ambiguous" ([CLL 4.5](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/5/)). The official FAQ: "there are still ongoing disputes about 'how to say it best in Lojban'; we expect these to continue indefinitely" ([Lojban FAQ, last modified 2005](https://lojban.org/static/resources/faq.html)).

### What speakers changed
- **xorlo** (gadri/article reform): proposed by xorxes (Jorge Llambías), written up by BPFK head Robin Lee Powell; BPFK vote 11-0, Dec 2004 ([xorlo page, lojban.org wiki](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/xorlo)); "official status" 10 Nov 2007 ([timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)). Change: *lo* went from CLL's "some things which really ('veridically') are cats" to "any or some cats"; "There are no default quantifiers. At all" (CLL's default for lo had been su'o "at least one"); "Expect to see a lot more lo compared to 'The Complete Lojban Language' book!" ([xorlo](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/xorlo)). The logic-derived defaults proved unwieldy in practice and were replaced by a vaguer, usage-driven article.
- **Experimental cmavo**: CLL itself reserved escape-hatch shapes (≥3 vowels, xVV): "'Experimental use' means that the language designers will not assign any standard meaning or usage to these words ... Experimental-use words provide an escape hatch for adding grammatical mechanisms ... the need for which was not foreseen" ([CLL 4.2](https://lojban.github.io/cll/4/2/)). Community members used it: e.g. la zipcpi proposed *i'au* ([FAQ about i'au](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/zipcpi:_zo_i'au_ki'a?_FAQ_about_the_new_cmavo%2C_i%27au=), title only) and co-authored the "gadganzu" article reform, marked non-official and incompatible enough that users are told to flag their dialect with *jo'au gadganzu* ("jo'au ... indicates the used version/dialect of Lojban") ([zipcpi: Yet another gadri article, last edited 2015](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/zipcpi:_Yet_another_gadri_article)). A language with a word for "I am now speaking dialect X" shows the formal grammar fragmenting into competing grammars.
- **Growth of the closed class**: Loglan in the 1960 article had 112 cmavo; the 1994 cmavo list had 1085 entries ([Johansen](http://arj.nvg.org/lojban/anti-lojban.html); single, hostile source, numbers not cross-checked). Moonbase pattern: "filling up the cmavo space" (ibid.).

### Freeze, baseline, governance
- 1997 baseline: "as of 0000 GMT on 10 January 1997, the Lojban language design is baselined and frozen for a minimum period in excess of 5 years"; only "typos, HTML conversion errors, and other severe errors" fixable; lujvo and fu'ivla coinage free. Rationale: other artlangs "continued to be changed by reformers and lost the few speakers" ([1997 Baseline Announcement, Robert LeChevalier](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/1997_Baseline_Announcement)). CLL promised learners the language "will not be subject to further fiddling by language-meisters" ([CLL 1.2](https://lojban.github.io/cll/1/2/)).
- **What the freeze cost (in the LLG's own words, 2002):** "Most of the language design has been under such a freeze for approximately the last 5 years, but people have been unable or unwilling to work on completing the documentation of a baseline lexicon under the freeze conditions"; the dictionary "has not yet been published; our statement, that the language design was 'complete', was therefore premature" ([Official Baseline Statement 2002/2003](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/Official_Baseline_Statement_(2002/2003))). Ratified 9 Dec 2002 ([timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)). The new BPFK (baupla fuzykamni, "language planning commission") could fix contradictions and clarify definitions but "The published gismu and cmavo lists will be presumed as valid, by default", with another freeze of "a minimum 5 years" ([Baseline Statement](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/Official_Baseline_Statement_(2002/2003))). BPFK opened 29 Mar 2003 ([timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)); "chartered in 2003, dissolved in 2019", succeeded by the LFK ([LLG page](https://mw.lojban.org/papri/Logical_Language_Group)). No printed dictionary resulted; the online jbovlaste dictionary (announced April 2001, [timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)) became the de facto lexicon [the int19h issue says future books were "effectively superseded by jbovlaste", [issue #12](https://github.com/int19h/cll/issues/12)].
- Inference: the freeze locked in the *design* while the community was still discovering how the language had to work (xorlo shows the article system needed changing); meanwhile the one artefact users most needed (a dictionary) stalled. Stability of a half-documented design bought neither growth nor completion.

### Adoption
- Jan 2002 figures: "around 375 people have purchased the Complete Lojban Language", "about 250 people on the Lojban List", "Over 100 people have posted text in Lojban", "a couple dozen have demonstrated ability to converse in Lojban in real time", several (notably Nick Nicholas) "considered to be completely fluent" ([Lojban FAQ, page last modified 2005](https://lojban.org/static/resources/faq.html)). Undated LLG brochure: mailing list "over 200 subscribers, with well over 20 regular posters"; conversational speakers "beyond what can be counted on the fingers of one hand" ([brochure](https://www.lojban.org/publications/level0/brochure/lojbanmo.html)). Critic: "Even the most daring estimates don't leave Lojban with much more than one hundred competent speakers" ([Johansen](http://arj.nvg.org/lojban/anti-lojban.html), undated). No census-style survey found; no recent count found [gap].
- Okrent's *In the Land of Invented Languages* (Spiegel & Grau, 2009) covers Loglan/Lojban; publication is noted as boosting CLL sales ([timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)). Could not read her Lojban chapter (book not accessible); her speaker estimates are [unverified]. A review summarises her Brown portrait: he "won devotees and helpers and drove them away with his need for absolute control" ([languagehat review](https://languagehat.com/the-bookshelf-in-the-land-of-invented-languages/)).
