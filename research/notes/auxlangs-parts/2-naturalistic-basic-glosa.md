# Occidental/Interlingue, Novial, Interlingua (IALA), Basic English, Glosa (+ Latino sine flexione): working notes

Part 2 of the auxlangs report (`../auxlangs.md`). Started 2026-10-13. Labels: claims carry an inline link or full reference; **[unverified]** = not confirmed in a source I read; **[secondary]** = reported by a source I read, original not read.

## 0. Precursor: Latino sine flexione (Peano, 1903; "Interlingua" of the Academia pro Interlingua)

Main source read: Giuseppe Peano, *Interlingua*, Academia pro Interlingua, Cavoretto-Torino, 1927, 28 pp., written in the language itself ([archive.org/details/PeanoInterlingua](https://archive.org/details/PeanoInterlingua), text file `PeanoInterlingua_djvu.txt`). Cited below as **Peano 1927**.

- **Aim / principle.** The whole program in one line: "Interlingua adopta: vocabulario internationale, orthographia latino, sine grammatica" (Peano 1927, p. 1). Claim: Europe's learned languages already share a large common vocabulary, "intelligibile ad primo visu aut quasi" to anyone who knows his own scientific vocabulary (Peano 1927, p. 3). This is the "readable at sight" claim that Interlingua (IALA) later inherits.
- **Vocabulary rule.** Academy rules voted 1910 (Peano 1927, p. 4): (1) adopt every word common to English, French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, German, Russian ("A. F. H. I. P. T. R."), and every Latin word with English derivatives; (2) any word that exists in Latin takes the form of the Latin stem; (3) suffix *-s* marks plural. Nouns take the "thema-ablativo" form (*rosa, anno, dente, cornu, die*); verbs the imperative (*ama, habe, scribe, audi*) (Peano 1927, p. 1). Note: this is a one-family (Latin/Romance plus Latinate English) vocabulary from the start.
- **Grammar.** "Sine grammatica": no case, no person/tense inflection; plural "logico" in *-s*, i.e. used when needed (Peano 1927, p. 1). Example text from the 1927 booklet: "Hodie quasi omne auctore scribe in proprio lingua nationale" -- Latin roots in fixed analytic order.
- **Size.** Peano says a medium dictionary of the cultured European languages, "circa 30 000 vocabulo", suffices as source (Peano 1927, p. 4). The 14,000-word 1915 vocabulary figure in Wikipedia [unverified; I did not read the 1915 *Vocabulario commune*].
- **Origin and governance.** Peano published mathematics in "latino sine flexione" after 1903; the Academia (descended from the 1887 Volapük academy; under Rosenberger 1893-1898 it compiled "4500 vocabulo commune ad linguas de Europa" = Idiom Neutral) elected him director on 26 Dec 1908 (Peano 1927, pp. 2-3). From 1910 anyone paying 10 francs a year was a member, and the Academy's voted rules were "solo consilio ... obligatorio pro nullo" -- advice binding on no one; dissenters were asked to publish their own rules (Peano 1927, p. 4). So: deliberately no standard. Peano's 1903 founding article "De Latino sine flexione, lingua auxiliare internationale" in *Revue de mathématiques* (vol. 8) [secondary: Wikipedia; not read].
- **Adoption / decline.** Used mainly by Peano's circle for mathematics, including the 5th edition of *Formulario mathematico* (1905-08) [secondary]. Academy journals *Discussiones* (1909-13), *Circulares*, *Academia pro Interlingua* (1925-27) [secondary: [Wikipedia, Academia pro Interlingua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_pro_Interlingua)]. Effectively faded after Peano's death in 1932 [unverified for exact trajectory].
- **Why it matters here.** (a) First clear statement of the "international vocabulary already exists, just extract it" thesis that Occidental and IALA Interlingua build on; IALA reused the name "Interlingua". (b) Governance by non-binding vote shows the opposite failure to Esperanto's fixed *Fundamento*: no norm at all, so no stable target for learners.

## 1. Occidental / Interlingue (Edgar de Wahl, 1922)

Primary sources read (transcribed at the *Bibliotheca Occidental*, [dicta.bplaced.net/bibliotheca_occidental](http://dicta.bplaced.net/bibliotheca_occidental/)):
- **de Wahl 1930a** = E. von Wahl, "Wege und Irrwege zur Weltsprache", in *Occidental – die Weltsprache*, Stuttgart: Franckh, 1930 ([text](http://dicta.bplaced.net/bibliotheca_occidental/odw/13_wege.html)).
- **de Wahl 1930b** = E. von Wahl, "Begründung", same book ([text](http://dicta.bplaced.net/bibliotheca_occidental/odw/65_begruendung.html)).
- **de Wahl 1925** = *Radicarium directiv del Lingue International (Occidental) in 8 lingues*, Tallinn 1925 ([text](http://dicta.bplaced.net/bibliotheca_occidental/radicarium_directiv.html)).
- **Pigal** = Engelbert Pigal, "Li essentie de Occidental" (in Occidental; date not shown in transcription, c. late 1920s [unverified]) ([text](http://dicta.bplaced.net/bibliotheca_occidental/essentie.html)).

**Aim and principles.**
- Explicitly *European* aim. de Wahl rejects English as world language because it would mean "die geistige Herrschaft des Angelsachsentums über Europa ... Europa muß seine eigene Sprache haben" (de Wahl 1930a, §Englisch). Against Novial's a-priori pronouns he writes that to *introduce* an IAL "die Gewohnheiten der Europäer doch mehr in Betracht zu ziehen sein [dürften], als die Anpassung an Orientalen, die an den Formen nicht mäkeln werden" (de Wahl 1930a, §Novial). So the one-family choice was a conscious trade: optimise for Europeans, assume others will learn whatever is offered. This is the plainest primary statement of the naturalist bargain.
- Core thesis (borrowing Prof. Lorenz, 1908): the existing international scientific/cultural vocabulary is already a de facto auxiliary lexicon; any IAL that destroys it (Volapük, Esperanto, Ido) is condemned (de Wahl 1930a, §Novial). Volapük/Esperanto/Ido are faulted for distorting international words into "Worträtsel".
- No a-priori principles except Ostwald's energy imperative: economy not in the number of letters, words or rules but in the effort of learning *and using* ("nicht Ökonomie in der Anzahl der Buchstaben, Wörter, Paragraphen und Regeln, sondern im Kraftaufwand beim Erlernen und Gebrauch") (de Wahl 1930a, §Zusammenfassung).
- Admits the trade-off openly: perfect regularity, phonetic spelling, logical word formation, full internationality, naturalness and instant intelligibility "für alle Menschen" cannot all be had; harmonising them is a matter of "künstlerischen Empfindens" (de Wahl 1930a, §Zusammenfassung). Claims Occidental gives "sofortige absolute Verständlichkeit für jeden Westeuropäer und Amerikaner, sowie für alle Gebildeten" (ibid.) -- claim only, no test cited.
- Pigal frames the novelty as reconciling *naturalness with regularity*, which others thought mutually exclusive (Pigal, §"Naturalitá e regularitá"). Pigal also argues against one-word-one-meaning: strict monosemy "es un reva ínrealisabil"; polysemy is natural and context resolves it, quoting the Esperanto weekly *Heroldo de Esperanto* no. 3, 1928, conceding the same for Esperanto (Pigal).

**Designer and how designed.** One person, long gestation: de Wahl says he spent thirty years on it and published in 1922 (prompted by the League of Nations taking up the IAL question) rather than keep polishing (de Wahl 1930a, §Occidental). First issue of *Kosmoglott* Feb 1922, then an eight-page "key" with full grammar and word-formation rules, in German, then French, English, Russian; "eight years" of trial in use before 1930 (ibid.). The 1925 dictionary credits about 15 named helpers for advice, proofreading and money (de Wahl 1925, Anteparole). Earlier history: Volapük user, early Esperantist, then reformer; proto-version "Auli" 1906-21 [secondary: [Wikipedia, History of Interlingue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Interlingue)].

**Sounds and writing.** Etymological, not phonetic, spelling. *c* = "ts" before e/i/y, else "k"; de Wahl argues every literate European already reads *c* this way, so making it phonetic (Esperanto *c*=ts always, Peano *c*=k, Novial dropping *c*) is "scholastische Prinzipienreiterei" (de Wahl 1930b, §Alphabet). He tried a Castilian *th* value for *c*, abandoned after "längere praktische Versuche" (ibid.) -- an instance of design changed by use. Pigal: phonetic spelling breaks visible derivational links (*critic : criticism*, *electric : electricitá*), and the learner meets the language mostly in writing (Pigal, §ortografie). Accents mark irregular stress [secondary: [Wikipedia, Interlingue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlingue)].

**Grammar type.** Analytic Western-European: article *li*/*un*, plural *-s/-es*, invariable adjectives, infinitives in *-ar/-er/-ir*, auxiliaries *va* (future), *vell* (conditional), *ha* (perfect) [secondary: Wikipedia, Interlingue]. Present participle = present stem + *-nt* (Pigal, footnote).

**De Wahl's rule (the key engineering idea).** Problem: most international words derive from ~100 irregular Latin verbs (de Wahl 1930a, §Occidental). Solution (as stated by Pigal): keep the stem vowel (*crea-r* → *creation, creator, creatur*); for derivatives, form the "perfect stem" from the infinitive: drop *-r/-er*; if the remainder ends in a vowel or *n'*, add *-t* (*indica-t*); if it ends in *d* or *r*, change to *s* (*vid-er → vis-ion*, *respond-er → respons*, *curr-er → curs*); otherwise the remainder is the perfect stem (*construct-er, act-er*). Nouns of action add *-ion* to the perfect stem; state nouns add *-ie* to the present participle (*distantie, existentie*), split by a "static vs dynamic verb" distinction (Pigal). Pigal claims this regularises all Latin irregular verbs "exceptet ad maxim un dozen" and yields "decmilles" of natural derivatives (Pigal). Cost: the learner must memorise verbs in their perfect-stem-friendly form (*restricter*, not *restringer*), and still distinguish static/dynamic; Pigal admits the boundary "ne es absolut strict" (*tolerantie / toleration*) (Pigal).

**Suffixes.** de Wahl "discovered" rather than invented affixes: e.g. *-in* is not feminine (as in Esperanto) because international *-in* words mean origin (*marin, alpin*); *-aci* "inclined to" from *mordace/tenace/vivace*; *-bil* not *-ebl* (Pigal, §suffixes). Esperantists criticised the large number of suffixes; Pigal's answer: unfit ones will die in use ("Quo ne es vivicapabil in un lingue, to mori per se self") (ibid.). So: many affixes with partly overlapping uses, accepted as natural.

**Vocabulary.** Romance/Latinate core plus Germanic common words; dictionary glosses in 8 languages (de Wahl 1925 lists A, D, F, Gr, H, I, L, P, R, S, Sv, T abbreviations). Title "directiv": deliberately *not* complete or definitive; it shows the "direction" in which users should extend it "quam in lingues vivent" (de Wahl 1925, Anteparole). Root count at launch not found [unverified].

**Reforms, governance, schism.** Occidental-Union founded 1928 [secondary: Wikipedia, Interlingue-Union]; Kosmoglott renamed *Cosmoglotta* in 1927 (issue 38 continued Kosmoglott's numbering) ([Archiv de Cosmoglotta](http://dicta.bplaced.net/bibliotheca_occidental/cmg/)). Two Ido societies (Vienna, Brno) went over in 1924 [secondary]; de Wahl himself boasts of a "beachtenswerten Zustrom von Idisten" (de Wahl 1930a). Banned/suppressed in Nazi Germany and annexed lands; only Swiss and Swedish groups fully active in WWII; de Wahl isolated in Tallinn, died 1948 in a sanatorium [all secondary: Wikipedia, History of Interlingue]. 1949: renamed *Interlingue* by vote of the Union (reported 91% in favour), partly to look neutral and to ease fusion with IALA's coming language [secondary]. 1951 Interlingua appeared; key leader Ric Berger (Cosmoglotta co-editor 1934-50) switched to Interlingua and urged others to follow [secondary: [Wikipedia, Ric Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ric_Berger)].

**Adoption and decline.** Peak in the 1930s around Vienna and Switzerland; *Cosmoglotta* went bimonthly 1952, quarterly from 1963, ceased 1985; revived online from 1999; Interlingue Wikipedia approved 2004; *Cosmoglotta* no. 333 for Jan-Jun 2024 [all secondary: Wikipedia, Interlingue / History of Interlingue]. No reliable speaker count found; de Wahl corresponded with ~30 people "in good Occidental" in 1924 [secondary]. Why it stalled (my reading): it lost its single natural niche (naturalistic IAL) to a better-funded, better-documented competitor built on the same thesis (IALA Interlingua), lost its founder and its central-European base to the war, and Esperanto held the movement's network effects.

**Precision/ambiguity.** Accepted polysemy and "natural" irregularity as the price of recognisability (Pigal). No test of comprehension found; "instant intelligibility" is asserted, not measured.

**Takeaways.** (1) de Wahl's rule is a real engineering contribution: a small algorithm recovering naturally-shaped derivatives -- regularity of *derivation* rather than of *surface*. (2) The naturalist bargain was stated openly as Eurocentric. (3) Governance was loose ("directiv") and the community defected to a near-twin when one appeared.

Addendum to Occidental from a near-contemporary history: Henry Jacob, *A Planned Auxiliary Language*, London: Dobson, 1947, ch. III.1 (transcribed at [archive.org/download/interlanguages/PALoch.html](https://archive.org/download/interlanguages/PALoch.html)) -- **Jacob 1947**. De Wahl submitted a memorandum (not a language) to the Delegation in 1907 with five principles: no existing system is satisfactory; build on international material; own rules of word formation that output really international words; a grammar producing "no unnatural forms"; an international orthography. The Delegation did not accept them. In 1935 (*Novialiste* no. 6, conversation with Jespersen) he added the introduction requirements: comprehensible "at first sight and without previous instruction to all civilized Europeans", not shocking the public, easy for practical use as well as reading. IALA later classed Occidental among the systems "of demonstrated usefulness" (Jacob 1947, III.1).

## 2. Novial (Otto Jespersen, 1928)

Primary source read: Otto Jespersen, *An International Language*, London: Allen & Unwin, 1928 (transcription by J. Chandler et al., 1997, chapter files at [archive.org/details/AILjespersen](https://archive.org/details/AILjespersen); the transcriber notes it "has not been thoroughly checked") -- **AIL**, cited by chapter. Also **Jacob 1947** ch. IV (Novial), sections "History" ([NovHist](https://archive.org/download/interlanguages/NovHist.html)) and "Subsequent changes" ([NovChanges](https://archive.org/download/interlanguages/NovChanges.html)).

**Designer and how designed.** One person, a top professional linguist; Jespersen calls Novial "the first interlanguage ever framed by a professional philologist", built on "a lifelong study of various national languages and a study begun in 1903" of earlier schemes; he refuses to issue "ukases" and gives arguments for each choice (AIL, "Novial"). Background: member of the Delegation's committee (1907), president of the Ido Academy 1907-1910 (Jacob 1947, IV.1; Delegation membership also in Jacob 1947, II.1 [PALih](https://archive.org/download/interlanguages/PALih.html)). He presided over IALA's linguistic conference in Geneva, 20 Mar-2 Apr 1930 [secondary: Jacob 1947 ch. on IALA, via search snippet of [interlanguages.net/PALiala.html](http://interlanguages.net/PALiala.html); page not loadable when checked].

**Aim and principles.**
- "Auxiliary": a second language for everybody, not a replacement; he rejects Schleyer's "one humanity, one language" motto in favour of Bollack's "The second language to everybody" (AIL, Introduction).
- Maxim (first written in his 1918 preface to the Feder & Schneeberger Ido-German dictionary): "that international language is best which in every point offers the greatest facility to the greatest number" (Jacob 1947, IV.1). Crucially, facility for writer and speaker as well as reader: an irregular word easy to recognise for those who know it "may be very difficult to anybody else" (ibid.). This is the explicit counter-position to Occidental's reader-first naturalism.
- Internationality measured by *speakers*, not languages: count "the number of people who know it from their mother-tongue"; Idiom Neutral's counting of languages (including Latin) is wrong (AIL, "Vocabulary"). He gives the weights (English 150-160 M, German 120 M, Russian 90 M, Spanish/French 60 M, Italian 40 M) and concludes the result "will necessarily be very much more Romanic in appearance than might be anticipated" because English's Romance layer joins F/I/S, while German-English cognates are often too divergent for a middle form (AIL, "Vocabulary"). So even a speaker-weighted method produced a Romance-looking language -- but its population base was explicitly European-civilisation speakers ("that part of the world with which we are principally concerned", ibid.).
- Admits Germanic words where they win the count: *sam, blind, yes, bora* (AIL, "Vocabulary"). Defends the resulting mixed look against "emotional or aesthetic" objections (ibid.).
- Precision vs economy: rejects both extremes. Against over-precision: those who want every nuance usually want the nuances of their own language and ignore "the burden thus laid on all the rest of mankind"; his 1911 *Progreso* note against three Ido verbs for "to tame" (AIL, "Vocabulary"). Against Zamenhof's root economy: compounds like *malsato* "hunger", *vagonaro* "train" must each be learnt anyway (ibid.).
- On Occidental: "perfect regularity and perfect naturalness cannot possibly be combined"; Occidental's derivation rules still produce non-existent words (*scrition, analysation, interprension, descovrition*), and "when Occidental is praised as very easy, it is chiefly by people who are already familiar with two or three of the great European languages" (AIL, "Occidental"). This is the clearest contemporary expert statement of the naturalism-favours-the-already-multilingual critique -- an argument, not a measurement.
- On Latino sine flexione: gives his own failures to parse LsF sentences (*more* = "custom" or "died"?), notes that "take the ablative" is useless advice to someone without Latin, and concludes an IAL "must necessarily be autonomous and have its own grammar and dictionary"; no language can do without grammar, and "Chinese grammar" uses tones, word order and particles (AIL, "Latino sine flexione").

**Sounds and writing (1928).** Phonetic spelling; *c* "kicked ignominiously out" and replaced by *s/k*; *z* dropped; *ch/sh* both kept "provisionally" with free pronunciation [ʃ] or [tʃ] (AIL, "CH, SH"). De Wahl's attack: the resulting "Rätselwörter" *sent* (100), *sink* (5), *sene* (scene), *sone* (zone) (de Wahl 1930a, §Novial).

**Grammar.** Analytic. No person/number on verbs; invariable stem plus auxiliaries: *me protekte, me protekted / did protekte, me ha protekte, me sal protekte, me vud protekte*, passive with *bli* (AIL, "Verbal System"). Pronouns *me, vu, le (lo, la), nus, vus, les*, with common-gender *le* beside *lo*/*la*; a single polite/familiar *vu*, because an IAL "should be democratic or polite enough to treat everybody alike" (AIL, "Pronouns"). Sex: nouns in *-e* common, *-o* male, *-a* female (the "e/a/o-words") (AIL, "Novial"; "Sex"). Optional genitive in *-n* (*sen*) (AIL, "Pronouns").
- Ambiguity case: critics pointed out that *me ha lekte e studia* is ambiguous (is *studia* under *ha* or present?). Jespersen's answer: like English *put*, context and the option of adding *nun* or repeating the subject resolve it; "language is only meant for such [natural sentences]" (AIL, "Verbal System"). He also chose *bli* over German-like *vird* for the passive because *vird* would be misread by Germans as future (ibid.) -- an example of choosing against false friends.

**Vocabulary size.** *Novial Lexike: International Dictionary* (1930) [secondary: [Wikipedia, Novial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novial); entry count not found, unverified].

**What long-term use changed (1934-37).** Jespersen admitted alternatives "saying that experience and experiment should show which are the better": *ve* beside *sal* (future); *-tore* beside *-ere*; suffixes *-on* and *-ilo* dropped; *sempre*, *mem* restored from Ido; and most strikingly an "Orthographic Novial" re-introducing *c, ç, sc, z* and vocalic *y* (*civil, scientie, zone, symbole*) beside the original "Phonetic Novial"; e/a/o-words changed to use the bare infinitive as noun (Jacob 1947, IV.6, citing *Novialiste* 18, 1937). I.e. under use the phonetic purist moved toward Occidental's historical spelling -- and solved the disagreement by allowing both, so there were now two spelling standards.

**Community and governance.** Ido journal *Mondo* (Stockholm) became *Novialiste* from 1934, the organ of discussion (Jacob 1947, IV.1). No academy; Jespersen was the authority. 

**After Jespersen's death (1943).** Widely reported as dormant from 1943 [secondary: Wikipedia, Novial; Wikipedia itself flags this as needing a better source]. 1990s internet revival (Don Blaheta's "Novial 98"; a Novial Wikipedia) [secondary]. I found no careful history of the 1943-1990 gap [unverified].

**What happens when a single designer dies (assessment).** Novial is the clean case: (1) all authority was personal -- reforms were his, alternatives were "admitted" by him; (2) he had left two spelling systems and several doublets unresolved, so there was no frozen standard to inherit; (3) the small community had already come from Ido and had the newly announced IALA project (1945 report, 1951 Interlingua) to move to; (4) WWII cut the European network. Compare Esperanto, where Zamenhof deliberately gave away authority (the *Fundamento* and an Academy) before he died (see part 1). Lesson: a living designer is a single point of failure unless a frozen core and an independent body exist before the designer goes.
