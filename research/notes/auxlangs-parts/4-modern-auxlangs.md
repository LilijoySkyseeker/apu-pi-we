## Kotava (Staren Fetcey, 1978)

**Aim/principles.** Official grammar (Fetcey / Kotava Linguistic Committee, *Kotava Official Complete Grammar*, v4.03 May 2016, English trans. A. Huchelmann, April 2018, [PDF](https://www.kotava.org/en/Kotava_Grammar_v4.03_englava.pdf), pp. 4-5): "absolute cultural and philosophical neutrality ... conceived under an 'a priori' logic, notably by not drawing its lexicon from any existing language, past or present, but inventing it in its entirety". Stated postulates: Neutrality, Originality, Universality, Regularity and clarity, Simplicity, Richness, Upgradability ("the language needs to be living and evolve with its speakers"). The site's own presentation (dated 15 Aug 2005) frames neutrality explicitly against Esperanto's Western bias ([kotava.org/en/en_kotava.php](https://www.kotava.org/en/en_kotava.php)). Note the grammar claims "Simplicity is absolute rather than relative since no known language is borrowed from" (p. 4) — an untested assertion: a priori means *equally* unfamiliar to everyone, not *easy*.

**Designer/process.** One person (Staren Fetcey; Wikipedia says project begun summer 1975 — [en.wikipedia Kotava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava), used only as a pointer). Since 2005 a seven-member committee (Wikipedia, citing no primary I read) [secondary]; the grammar says it "is regulated by a Linguistic Committee (Kotava Avaneda) which meets annually and validates or rejects certain proposed evolutions" (grammar p. 5). French Wikipedia cites the "13e comité linguistique, 6 mai 2018" ([fr.wikipedia Kotava](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotava)) [secondary]. The grammar explicitly refuses a frozen "Fundamento": the grammar is "simply a description of the language at some point in time" (p. 5).

**Reforms.** Grammar's own timeline (p. 5): 1978 birth; 1983 1st general reform; 1993 2nd general reform; 2008 plural reform; 2011 removal of letter H. (English and French Wikipedia say 1988 and 1993 — conflicts with the grammar's 1983; trust the grammar.) The 2011 reform replaced palatalising *h* (after l, m, n) with *y*; the grammar admits "acquired habits and the corpus of previously written texts mean that its use will only gradually disappear" (p. 5) — a small, concrete cost of churn: legacy corpus is now non-standard.

**Sounds/writing.** 24 letters: 5 vowels a e i o u, 17 consonants, 2 semivowels w y; Latin alphabet "without diacritical marks", c = /ʃ/, j = /ʒ/, x = /x/ (p. 5). Stress fully predictable: final if word ends in consonant/semivowel, else penultimate; one exception (1sg verb, marked with an accent, e.g. *estú*) (p. 5-6) — so it does in fact use one diacritic.

**Grammar type.** "Mostly agglutinating ... with flexive (personal conjugation, euphonic agreement ...) and isolating streaks"; no noun cases, no morphological gender; every nominal complement governed by a preposition; "rather free syntactic order, even if it is mainly SOV ... or OSV" (p. 4). Verb is the hub: speech acts, moods, tenses, polarity, aspects, modalities, voices, 7 persons (incl. inclusive/exclusive "we") (contents, pp. 1-2; Wikipedia). Rich system — the table of contents lists 17 verb-related subchapters.

**Vocabulary.** Entirely invented. Size claims conflict: the 2005 site text gives "approximately 17,000 Kotava entries" in the dictionary ([kotava.org](https://www.kotava.org/en/en_kotava.php)); English Wikipedia "more than 17,000 basic roots"; French Wikipedia "over 30,000 base radicals", citing Kotapedia at 31,015 terms [secondary]. How roots were generated (random, phonotactic template, sound symbolism?) is **not documented** in any primary source I found [unverified]. Numerals: 21 basic numeral roots, everything else by compounding (grammar, numerals chapter). Large affix inventory (general, verbalising, verbal, nominal, determinative affixes; grammar Chap. X).

**Precision/ambiguity.** Grammar lists "contextuality, minimality, univocity, persistence, non-ambiguity" as governing principles (p. 4) — design claims, no evaluation.

**Learnability evidence.** None found. No study of Kotava acquisition located.

**Adoption.** ISO 639-3 `avk`, change request 2007-145 adopted 2008-01-14 ([SIL avk](https://iso639-3.sil.org/code/avk)). Speakers: French Wikipedia "estim. 40" flagged [réf. nécessaire]; Wikidata gives 50 [unverified source chain] — treat as folklore, but order of magnitude (tens) is consistent with the Wikipedia data. Kotava Wikipedia: approved after >2 years in Incubator with ~10,000 articles; self-description: "10-15 regular contributors each month", "women are the majority in this movement", users mostly French, Spanish, Russian, "very few native English speakers" ([Meta: Tell us about Kotava Wikipedia](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Tell_us_about_Kotava_Wikipedia)). Today 29,905 articles but only ~92 words/article and 17 active accounts (table above). Materials mostly in French (grammar is a translation from French, p. 5).

**Tooling.** Official grammar (PDF, v4.03), online dictionary (~17k/31k entries, see above), Kotapedia, forum, library/literature section ([kotava.org](https://www.kotava.org/en/en_kotava.php)). No parser/corpus tools found.

**What Kotava shows about a priori vocabulary today.** (1) A priori roots buy *equal* unfamiliarity, and the design documents claim this makes it "absolutely" simple — but there is no data. (2) After ~48 years it has a dictionary of tens of thousands of entries, a living committee, a Wikipedia edition — i.e. a priori vocabulary is *sustainable* for a small committed group — but the user base is still tens, concentrated in one language area (French), which suggests the "neutrality" benefit did not translate into global uptake: its users came through French materials, as with any project. (3) The large invented lexicon is a memorisation cost every learner pays in full; there is no cognate head start for anyone.

## Globasa (Hector Ortega, 2019)

Sources: globasa.net "More" pages, read from the site and from the site's source repo [Globasa-Team/main-pages-grav](https://github.com/Globasa-Team/main-pages-grav) (2026-10-13): [Design Principles](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/diseno-principi), [History](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/histori-de-globasa), [Stability Q&A](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/andinya-de-globasa), [Changes and Adjustments](https://github.com/Globasa-Team/main-pages-grav/blob/HEAD/10.max/04.alogi-ji-lilalogi/default.eng.md), [Methodology for Lexical Development](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti), [Word Proposal Process](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/proceso-fe-lexijeni), [home](https://www.globasa.net/eng/).

**Aim.** A thought experiment: 1,000 random people from around the world settle an island — "What would the village language look like within three generations?" ([home](https://www.globasa.net/eng/)). Principles: Internationality; "Optimal simplicity with the goal of maximum learnability and ease of use for both speaker and listener"; "Optimal balance between opposing linguistic parameters"; design "reminiscent of the world's creole languages" ([Design Principles](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/diseno-principi)). Notable for explicitly weighing *listener* ease, not just speaker ease, and for rejecting extreme minimalism ("Taken too far ... simplicity can ... result in reduced functionality, impracticality or even greater difficulty").

**Designer/process; the Pandunia split.** Ortega, a linguistics graduate with a creole interest, had the core ideas by ~2000; met Neo Patwa ~2009 and Pandunia in July 2017. After Facebook discussions with Pandunia's creator Risto Kupsala, Pandunia's phoneme inventory was changed "back to a simpler, earlier inventory"; Ortega then judged that "Pandunia lacked the necessary morphology and syntax to disambiguate simple sentences" and left "only a few weeks after encountering Pandunia" to start his own project ([History](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/histori-de-globasa)). So Globasa is a 2017 *departure* from Pandunia, published 2019, not a formal fork of its code/lexicon (the task brief's "forked from Pandunia in 2019" overstates it). Earlier names: *Geokreol*, *Gayabas*. Published 26 July 2019 (Esperanto's *Unua Libro* anniversary) with core grammar and 1,000 roots; Ortega wanted ≥95% stability before publishing but concedes it was perhaps "under 95% stable when first published, but certainly not under 90%" (History). Function words and affixes were designed first ("the most difficult part"). Since Phase 5 (Jan 2022) a "language development committee" votes on grammar/word-form changes ([Stability](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/andinya-de-globasa)). Any speaker may propose roots on the subreddit, discussed and checked with admin tools for homonym/minimal-pair conflicts ([Word Proposal Process](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/proceso-fe-lexijeni)).

**Sounds/writing.** Latin alphabet without q, 5 vowels + 20 consonants, one letter one phoneme; stress on last vowel if word ends in consonant, else penultimate ([reference grammar, Wikiversity](https://en.wikiversity.org/wiki/User:Sir_Beluga/Globasa)). Inventory chosen from "phonemes familiar to at least 50% of the population" within Roman-alphabet limits (History). Phonotactics: limited onset clusters, max one coda consonant; since Sept 2019 only nine word-final consonants (f l m n r s w x y) (Changes, Phase 1). Explicit trade-off stated: fewer consonants → more similar-sounding words or longer words (Design Principles).

**Grammar.** Creole-like analytic: invariant roots, word order and function words mark relations; no articles, no plural marking, gender by adjective; verb particles not suffixes; affixes "primarily for word formation" (Design Principles; History). Two major word classes (noun/verb words, adj/adv words) — a deliberate middle path between Pandunia-style classless roots and Esperanto vowel endings (History). Started fully head-first, moved to head-last for simple phrases and word formation, head-first for complex phrases (Design Principles). Flexible SVO/SOV/OSV (Wikiversity grammar).

**Vocabulary selection (the interesting part).** Algorithm ([Methodology](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)): first try to express the concept by derivation/compounding; else check 17 languages (English, French, German, Russian, Spanish, Mandarin, Japanese, Korean, Vietnamese, Hindi, Telugu, Arabic, Swahili, Persian, Turkish, Indonesian, Filipino) on Wiktionary/dictionaries; "Select the source with the most language families represented"; tie-breakers prefer East Asian clusters, then Arabic+support, Hindi+support, European+support, Persian+Turkish; final fallback order Mandarin (two-character words), Vietnamese, Telugu, Swahili, Arabic, Hindi. Form constraints trump source: never certain word-final minimal pairs, avoid any minimal pair where possible, prefer 2-3 syllables (favouring three), avoid look-alike affixed forms, avoid initial syllables that clash with verb particles; a blend may differ from some natural language by only one phoneme (two if close). The home page says roots are "sourced primarily from 16 of the world's major languages" vs. 17 in the algorithm — minor inconsistency.

Outcome by source (Menalari [Etymological Stats](https://menalari.globasa.net/eng/estatisti-fe-lexiasel), 2026-10-13; % of roots whose etymology lists that language, multi-counted): English 60.63, Spanish 56.96, French 55.62, Turkish 53.74, German 52.97, Persian 51.37, Indonesian 50.8, Russian 49.2, Japanese 46.46, Hindi 42.21, Korean 39.94, Arabic 39.3, Swahili 30.49, Vietnamese 22.7, Telugu 22.64, Mandarin 22.29. **So even with an algorithm that favours East Asian sources on ties, the top four contributors include the three big Western European languages, and Mandarin — the largest L1 — is last.** That is the predictable effect of counting families: European words have spread via colonial and scientific borrowing, so they "win" on breadth. (A 2021 snapshot reported on the IAL fandom wiki had English 44%, Spanish 39%, Mandarin 16% — [ial.fandom.com/wiki/Globasa](https://ial.fandom.com/wiki/Globasa) [secondary]; the method of counting may differ.)

**Size.** Launch: 1,000 roots (History). Now: the official word-list export ([cdn.globasa.net/api2/word-list.csv](https://cdn.globasa.net/api2/word-list.csv), documented in [globasa-docs api.md](https://github.com/Globasa-Team/globasa-docs)) has ~9,520 lines; by its Category column ≈ 3,220 roots, 4,811 derived words, 913 proper words, 447 phrases, 91 affixes (my naive CSV count, ±a few rows). Home page: "over 9,000 dictionary entries"; claim that the 500 most common roots give "basic fluency and comprehend over 90% of the words in typical Globasa texts" ([home](https://www.globasa.net/eng/)) — a coverage claim about Globasa texts, not a learning result.

**Churn.** The [Changes and Adjustments](https://github.com/Globasa-Team/main-pages-grav/blob/HEAD/10.max/04.alogi-ji-lilalogi/default.eng.md) page records six phases. Counting "old → new" lines (my count): Phase 1 (2019, incl. Sept 2019 phonology change) 175; Phase 2 (Dec 2019) 15; Phase 3 (Jul 2020) 21; Phase 4 (Jan 2021) 36; Phase 5 (Jan 2022) 39; Phase 6 (Jul 2023) 29. Phase 1 examples: obligatory infinitive particle *na* because optional *na* made *Mi suki oko* ambiguous ("I like the eye" vs "I like to see"); new *-li* adverb marker to stop *dayo bala uma* meaning both "big strong horse" and "very strong horse"; dozens of root respellings (*lexi* → *leksiko* → *leksi*; *seleci* → *seleksi* → *seleti*), "Different Root Word" swaps (*rangi* → *kolor*, *xunjaw* → *xerca*) and "Greater Mandarin representation" adjustments. Project's self-assessment: "started off around 90% stable and is now about 99% stable"; reforms are "fewer and fewer" ([Stability](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/andinya-de-globasa)). Cost: several words changed twice within months; early learners relearned ~hundreds of items in the first year. Notable that *most* Phase-1 grammar changes were fixes for ambiguity discovered in use — exactly what "no ambiguity" designs on paper miss.

**Learnability evidence.** None beyond design claims; no learner study found [searched; none].

**Adoption.** Globasa's own Discord server: approximate 494 members (Discord invite API, 2026-10-13, invite `JCaqAvapGR` linked from [home](https://www.globasa.net/eng/)). Subreddit r/Globasa exists (linked from home) — subscriber count not retrievable (Reddit blocked automated access). No Wikipedia edition, no ISO 639-3 code found [unverified: none listed in SIL search]. Home page claims "a small but vibrant community". Active fluent users: unknown; no census. Translations/readings collected at [Doxo](https://doxo.globasa.net/eng).

**Tooling.** Public-domain (CC0) everything; machine-readable dictionary with API (CSV/JSON/YAML, translations to eng/epo/spa) ([globasa-docs](https://github.com/Globasa-Team/globasa-docs)); minimal-pair/homonym checker in "admin tools" used in word proposals; course (Xwexi, 20 lessons), reader (Doxo), wiki; third-party Minecraft pack and browser extension on GitHub. This is the best-engineered *tooling and process* of the group.
