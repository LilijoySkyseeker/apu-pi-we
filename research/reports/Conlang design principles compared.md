# Planned languages die of governance, not grammar

Across 350 years of constructed languages, the design principle a language was built on almost never decided its fate. Who controlled the norm, and whether a speech community formed around it, did. Volapük had the largest early following of any planned language and collapsed when its author claimed ownership. Esperanto survived because Zamenhof gave his language away and froze a small core. Every later "improved" design (Ido, Novial, Interlingua, the modern worldlangs, the a priori Kotava) stayed in the hundreds or low thousands. The one modern success, Toki Pona, has about 2,000 census respondents. It won through a free video course, Discord and fun, not precision, while the most precise design, Ithkuil, has no fluent speaker, not even its author. Learnability evidence is thin. The only well-controlled result says English-speaking children learn Esperanto faster than French. Nobody has ever tested a worldlang, an a priori lexicon or a spoken taxonomic vocabulary with real learners. Apu Pi We sidesteps the questions that killed the others, because as a native-language thought experiment it needs no adopters, no governance and no adult recognisability. So the part of the record that applies to it is what speakers *do* to designs in use. On that evidence it is well placed: isolating, with optional particles and arbitrary, maximally distinct roots (the lesson of Wilkins and Ro). It is also doing five things no predecessor tried, chief among them choosing roots with a mishearing model and drift checks. Its main repeated habit is an old one: like Hooke praising Wilkins and Brown promising Loglan's single parse, it has validated itself only in layers its designers control (a confusion model scored against itself, and AI readers), and almost entirely through English.

## Every tradition optimised one property and paid for it elsewhere

Each family of constructed languages picked one property to maximise and paid for it elsewhere. Classical auxiliary languages optimised ease for European adults and paid in neutrality. Worldlangs optimised fairness of sources and paid in universal unfamiliarity. Philosophical languages optimised meaning-in-form and paid in confusability. The experimental extremes isolated a single variable (minimalism, density, a hypothesis) and showed which costs speakers will actually bear. The table below summarises the designs and what became of them. The sections that follow explain the patterns.

### Classical auxlangs traded neutrality for European recognisability

All seven classical designs came from one person or a small committee between 1879 and 1951. All aimed at a neutral second language, not a replacement for native ones. They split into **schematic** designs (Volapük, Esperanto, Ido), built for regularity and easy production, and **naturalistic** ones (Occidental, Interlingua), built for instant reading by Western Europeans. Every one of them drew its vocabulary from Europe. They differed only in how far they bent their source roots. Schleyer clipped English and German words into monosyllables and avoided /r/ "for the sake of children and old people, also for some Asiatic nations" ([Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/truth-beauty-and-volapuk/)). The result was *vol* + *pük* for "world speech", recognisable to almost nobody. Volapük is the nearest historical precedent for deliberately reshaped roots, and its 1931 reform restored /r/ to win recognisability back ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volap%C3%BCk)). Esperanto kept roots recognisable to Europeans. Ido chose roots for "maximum internationality" across six European languages, so 91% of them are found in French ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ido_language)). Interlingua went furthest, adopting each word's "nearest etymological prototype" across its control languages. It claims written comprehension by "over 900 million" Romance speakers, but it has a few hundred actual speakers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interlingua)).

The grammatical history runs in one direction: **each successor deleted mandatory marking its predecessor kept**. Volapük's four cases and 1,584 verb forms gave way to Esperanto's moderate agglutination, with an obligatory accusative and adjective agreement ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanto_grammar)). Ido then made the accusative optional and dropped agreement. Novial formed tenses with particles and fixed SVO order. Interlingua has no agreement and no conjugation at all ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novial)). The criticisms the set attracted cluster in the same places: Eurocentric vocabulary and sounds (Esperanto's /x/, /ʒ/ and /ts/ are rare outside Europe), redundant mandatory morphology, and Esperanto's male-default roots, where "mother" is derived from "father" with *-ino* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_reform_in_Esperanto)). Basic English stands apart as a subset of one national language. Rudolf Flesch's verdict was "it's not basic, and it's not English". Roosevelt mocked it to Churchill for reducing "blood, toil, tears and sweat" to "blood, work, eye water and face water" ([International Churchill Society](https://winstonchurchill.org/publications/finest-hour/finest-hour-135/riddles-mysteries-enigmas-basic-english/)). What outlived Ogden's operators was the looser principle of a small controlled core plus whatever domain terms a text needs. VOA Special English, for example, used about 1,500 words "plus any terms needed to explain a story" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learning_English_(version_of_English))).

### Worldlangs bought fairness with universal unfamiliarity

The post-1990 worldlangs answered the Eurocentrism charge by drawing roots from many unrelated languages. Globasa's procedure is the most explicit. It ranks candidate forms by how many language families share them, counts English, French, German, Russian and Spanish together as *one* family, and breaks ties in favour of East Asian, then Arabic, then Hindi sources ([Globasa](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)). Lidepla requires a word to be familiar to speakers of "at least a few" of its ten source languages ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingwa_de_planeta)). Kotava rejected borrowing altogether as "a Western pidgin, a kind of neo-Latin" and invented every root "with no lineage" ([kotava.org](https://www.kotava.org/fr/fr_nasbara.php)). Grammatically, the group converged on isolating designs: Elefen, Pandunia, Lidepla and Globasa all keep roots invariant and mark tense with particles. Globasa's history records that it backed away from fully classless words because they were too hard to disambiguate ([Globasa history](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/histori-de-globasa)).

The cost is the one critics predicted: a sentence that mixes many sources is fully recognisable to nobody. One blogger contrasts Elefen's "me labora en la pizeria" with Globasa's "te suki roso pingo". He guesses that learners must memorise about 10% of the vocabulary in a eurolang, 50% in a worldlang and 100% in an a priori language, but **those figures are his estimates, not data** ([Vecderg](https://www.vecderg.com/articles/auxlangs)). No blind recognisability or comprehension study exists for any worldlang. The hard external signal is ISO 639-3, which requires use across domains by a community that includes all ages. Elefen and Kotava got codes. Lidepla and Sambahsa were refused in 2018 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingwa_de_planeta)). Kotava's half-century matters most for Apu Pi We's a priori roots. Fifty years of invented vocabulary and a seven-member academy produced a small community that is still mostly French-speaking, so neutrality on its own did not attract learners. Nor did it visibly repel them more than worldlang sourcing did, because every member of the group stayed small.

### Philosophical languages put meaning in sound and made errors invisible

Wilkins's 1668 *Essay* reduced all notions to 40 genera and spelled each concept as genus syllable + difference consonant + species vowel. His own showcase examples expose the flaw. *Tida* is redness and *Tide* greenness, *Detα* rainbow and *Deta* halo ([Wilkins 1668 scan](https://archive.org/details/AnEssayTowardsARealCharacterAndAPhilosophicalLanguage)). One misheard vowel produces a plausible neighbouring meaning that context cannot catch. A contemporary critic complained that "closely related ideas have almost the same form… it would be exceedingly difficult to remember all these minute distinctions" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Essay_Towards_a_Real_Character,_and_a_Philosophical_Language)). Ro repeated the problem in 1904, colour words all beginning *bofo-*, and drew the same criticism: one consonant separates meanings that "often cannot be guessed from context" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_(language))). Solresol made opposites mirror images (*fala* "good", *lafa* "bad"), which is the worst possible case for a noisy channel. It also needed pauses between words so that its seven syllables would not run together ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solresol)). Wilkins's fixed nine slots per level forced an L/R insertion hack when a category had more species, and Wilkins admitted the hack blocked adjective derivation. No one is recorded speaking any of these languages conversationally. Wilkins's "enthusiasts… were numerous… practitioners less so", and one of the few practitioners used the script "merely… as a way of writing English in code" ([Notes Rec. R. Soc.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5906426/)).

Blissymbolics is the instructive exception. As a world script it failed. From 1971 it found a lasting niche as a pointing-board system for non-speaking children with cerebral palsy, where there is no acoustic channel to garble and a few hundred composable symbols spare users costly selections ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blissymbols)). Even there, the controlled studies favour plain pictures. Adolescents with CP learned iconic pictures faster than Blissymbols, generalised them better and used them more in daily life ([Hurlbut et al. 1982](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ267186)), and CyberGlyphs beat Bliss on learning and retention ([Alant et al. 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1080/13682820400009980)). Loglan and Lojban moved the logic out of the roots and into the syntax. Lojban's root algorithm scored candidate five-letter forms against Chinese, English, Hindi, Spanish, Russian and Arabic words, weighted by speaker numbers (Chinese 0.36, English 0.21 and so on). It then removed any candidate that differed from an existing root only in its final vowel or by one similar consonant ([CLL 4.14](https://lojban.org/publications/cll/cll_v1.1_xhtml-section-chunks/section-gismu-making.html)). The unambiguity held where it was mechanical (morphology and parsing) and was given up where speakers needed brevity. Lojban's own grammar concedes that all tanru compounds are semantically ambiguous and that full disambiguation "can require too much effort" ([Lojban brochure](https://lojban.org/publications/level0/brochure/lingissues.html)).

### The experimental extremes show which costs speakers pay

Toki Pona, Ithkuil and Láadan form a natural experiment on precision. Toki Pona's roughly 120 words must cover what one study mapped to 4,027 WordNet senses, about 30 per word ([Fabbri et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.09359)). It leaves precision to context, and it has a living community. The 2024 census drew 1,997 responses: 53% reported conversational level or better, 49% used the language weekly, 88% of those answering in Toki Pona used Discord, and the most common way in (52%) was jan Misali's free video series ([census](https://tokiponacensus.github.io/results2024/)). Its corpus passed 6 million tokens, and a 2025 study found that "sociolinguistic factors influence Toki Pona in the same way as natural languages" ([Huang & Joo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10246)). Ithkuil sits at the other pole: 96 cases in its 2023 version and more than 6,000 roots. Its creator is fluent only in the morphology, has never memorised the lexicon, and says a word "requires a lot more thought before it can be spoken than it would in a natural language" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithkuil)). Lakoff called its "assumption of efficiency" inefficient "given how the brain works" ([Foer, via reproduction](https://theleavingyears.blogspot.com/2018/02/utopian-for-beginners.html)). Láadan stated an adoption hypothesis in advance with a ten-year window. Women would nurture it or build a better one. Elgin later judged that it had failed: "Láadan got very little attention", while Klingon thrived ([Lithub](https://lithub.com/this-science-fiction-novelist-created-a-feminist-language-from-scratch/)). The conclusion this set supports is that speakers will pay a listener-side cost when ambiguity actually bites, but **no one has been seen paying an obligatory speaker-side cost on every utterance**.

## Governance and community decided every outcome that grammar was blamed for

Volapük did not die of umlauts. In 1889 the Paris congress gave its Academy authority over the grammar. Schleyer "insisted strongly on retaining his proprietary rights", dissolved the Academy and set up a rival one, and "by 1890 the movement was in disarray" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Volap%C3%BCk_Academy)). Its splinter projects "immediately became obscure", and the recruits went to Esperanto ([Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/truth-beauty-and-volapuk/)). Ido's 1907 split was tainted by its author's concealed identity. It then lost learners to "constant modifications seeking to perfect it" until it stabilised in 1922 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ido_language)). Novial died with Jespersen, and his 1934 respelling had already alienated "old and faithful friends" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novial)). The pattern recurs outside the auxlangs. James Cooke Brown claimed copyright over Loglan's components and registered a trademark, which produced the Lojban fork and a lawsuit that ended in 1992 ([Lojban timeline](https://mw.lojban.org/index.php?title=Lojban_timeline)). Charles Bliss fought the clinicians who adapted his symbols until a 1982 settlement ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blissymbols)). Three governance models appear in the record. **A single owner** means the language dies or splits with that person. **An open reform process** churns away learners. **A frozen core with additive growth** survives, at the price of locking in early mistakes: Esperanto's accusative and male-default roots persist because of what Piron called a "social contract on the untouchability of the basic core" ([HandWiki](https://handwiki.org/wiki/Social:Esperanto)). The Akademio has rejected every gender reform, even as younger speakers use the unofficial pronoun *ri* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_reform_in_Esperanto)).

Toki Pona found a fourth model. Lang compiled her 2021 dictionary from more than 11,000 poll entries on a Discord server, and so ratified usage rather than resisting it. The lexicon grew from 120 to 137 "essential" words in seven years ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)). Lojban shows what happens otherwise. Its 1997 baseline promised a frozen design. Usage moved anyway, and in 2026 an unofficial revision of the reference grammar is still converting a book that uses *le* 6.4 times as often as *lo*, when speakers now prefer *lo* ([int19h/cll #9](https://github.com/int19h/cll/issues/9)).

Esperanto's success came from community, not design. Zamenhof's "crucial insight", in Mark Fettes's words, was that the language "must develop through collective use". He published only a grammar sketch, about 900 words, some samples and an essay, and spoken use "grew rapidly following the first international congress in 1905" ([Fettes](https://www.esperantic.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Fettes_Esperanto-and-Language-Awareness.pdf)). Esperanto's ceiling was then set from outside: French opposition in the League of Nations, persecution under Hitler and Stalin, and the post-1945 rise of English ([Lins](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1057/978-1-137-54917-4)). The planned and revived languages that truly took hold (Modern Hebrew, Indonesian, Swahili, Tok Pisin) all had a territory, a state or proto-state, schools, and an existing base such as liturgical Hebrew or trade Malay. Indonesian was the mother tongue of about 5% of the population when it was chosen, and its neutrality worked because it served a national project ([ScienceDirect topic summary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/social-sciences/bahasa-indonesia)). For a world auxlang, François Grin's 2005 report put the barrier precisely. An Esperanto scenario would save the EU about €25 billion a year, but it depends entirely on coordinated political will, so it is a coordination failure that no grammar can fix ([Rapport Grin](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapport_Grin)).

## Learnability evidence is thin, European, and points at children

The famous learnability claims rest on weak studies. IALA's 1933 Thorndike study estimated that Esperanto takes about a fifth of the time needed for French or German, but even a sympathetic reviewer says "the experimental conditions were not adequate to allow strong conclusions" ([Fettes](https://www.esperantic.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Fettes_Esperanto-and-Language-Awareness.pdf)). The Paderborn studies reported English learned 20–35% faster after Esperanto. They were run by a leading Esperantist and published outside mainstream journals ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paderborn_method)). The best evidence comes from the Essex studies. In a randomised study of 28 English nine-year-olds, those taught Esperanto gained **45% against 14% for French** over 22.5 hours. A 2019 quasi-experiment with 178 children, however, found **no** later advantage in French from having learned Esperanto first. The authors call the results "sobering": Esperanto "may be easier to learn than another European L2" but is "not a superior starter language" ([Roehr-Brackin & Tellier](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/24243/1/Roehr-Brackin_Tellier_LPLP_Repository.pdf)). Every propaedeutic study used a Western European target language related to Esperanto. None tested adults, and none tested speakers of non-European languages. No learnability study exists for any worldlang, for Kotava, or for a spoken taxonomic vocabulary. That last gap, the actual confusion rate of minimal-pair roots like Wilkins's, is the missing evidence most relevant to Apu Pi We.

The artificial-language laboratory says more, and it speaks to native acquisition. Children regularise unpredictable variation where adults probability-match it ([Hudson Kam & Newport, review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7500460/)). In chains of learners passing a language down, transmission errors fell and structure rose, and when nothing pushed against ambiguity the languages collapsed into underspecified forms that were easy to learn but useless ([Kirby, Cornish & Smith 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2504810/)). Learners given optional case marking moved it onto the arguments where it carried the most information, recreating a pattern natural languages have ([Fedzechkina, Jaeger & Newport 2012](https://www.academia.edu/6644965/Balancing_effort_and_information_transmission_during_language_acquisition_Evidence_from_word_order_and_case_marking)). Learners also favour harmonic word orders and avoid the orders that are typologically rare ([Culbertson et al. 2012](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Learning-biases-predict-a-word-order-universal-Culbertson-Smolensky/26431edcecf7d6e37bda6368a5235d5579164d76)). Larger groups build more systematic languages ([Raviv et al. 2019](https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item3138714/larger-communities-create-more-systematic-languages)).

Real nativisation shows the same forces. Bergen's eight native Esperanto speakers used no compound tenses or aspect affixes, reduced unstressed grammatical syllables, and used the accusative in only about half its contexts ([Bergen 2001](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~bkbergen/papers/NEJCL.pdf)). Lindstedt's rebuttal is the more useful finding. Native speakers changed little because a century of non-native use had "already adjusted" Esperanto to natural-language constraints ([Lindstedt 2006](https://journal.fi/finjol/article/view/153130/98645)). Modern Hebrew kept its Semitic verb templates but took its phonology and syntax from its Yiddish-speaking founders ([Zuckermann](http://www.zuckermann.org/pdf/new-vision.pdf)). Tok Pisin's native speakers ground *baimbai* down into the future marker *bai* ([Tung](https://ejournals.bc.edu/index.php/lingua/article/download/5419/5049/0)). The lessons agree. Redundant mandatory marking erodes. Unstressed grammar words reduce. Rare periphrastic tense and aspect forms disappear. Phonology drifts toward the founders' first languages. Usage, not the designer, sets the lexicon.

## Apu Pi We sidesteps the old killers but repeats three old habits

The native-language framing removes almost every reason the languages above failed. A thought experiment needs no adopters, so the coordination problem Grin described does not arise. It needs no academy, so the Schleyer and Brown failures cannot happen. Children acquire whatever they hear, so the complaint that a priori roots are recognisable to no adult largely stops mattering. That complaint is the main knock against Kotava and Volapük. What remains relevant is the evidence about what speakers do to a design in use, and on most of it Apu Pi We's choices match the attractors. It is isolating, with fixed SVO and no case, agreement or gender, which is where Ido, Novial, Interlingua and the worldlangs were all heading and where native Esperanto drifted. Evidence, confidence and speech acts are optional particles, the cost profile Toki Pona's users accept and Ithkuil's never did. Gender is a modifier stated once, so it avoids Esperanto's *-ino* problem. Roots are arbitrary on purpose, citing Wilkins and Solresol ([language.md](../../docs/language.md)), so a misheard sound produces nonsense rather than a plausible neighbouring meaning. It also converged independently on Toki Pona's syllable template: (C)V(n), with a vowel-only syllable allowed only at the start of a word and *n* as the only coda ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)). Its core-plus-borrowed-terms split is the part of Basic English that survived. Three habits from the record nonetheless recur.

### It validates in the layer its designers control

Hooke praised Wilkins's character as "perfectly free from all manner of ambiguity". Brown promised a single parse. Each claim was true in the formal layer the designer controlled and false in use. Apu Pi We's two main instruments share that structure. Its mishearing model takes its consonant data from English listeners in noise (Miller and Nicely, 1955). Its vowel, glide and drift parameters are estimates, and the reference says plainly that "its scores measure the model against itself and have not been checked against real listeners" ([language.md](../../docs/language.md)). Its fidelity measure is blind back-translation, with results of 86% content in test 01 and 80% content and 97% tone in test 02. The readers, though, are AI agents, which "guess well from world knowledge", and the second first-principles review flagged this as the project's biggest weakness ([design-log.md](../../docs/design-log.md)). The only human data so far is one native English speaker reading ten sentences aloud. That reader found it "remarkably easy to pronounce and keep from blending together", but no second person listened ([human test 01](../../tests/human-01-reading/report.md)). Lojban is the warning here. After decades of use, its own reference grammar says the ramifications of its optional attitudinal particles in actual usage "are unknown" ([Lojban Reference Grammar ch. 13](https://www.lojban.org/publications/reference_grammar/chapter13.html)).

### It is English-centred in data, tests and loans

The European auxlangs were criticised for optimising recognisability for Europeans without ever testing it on anyone else. Apu Pi We's mishearing data come from English listeners, its test texts and readers are English, its one human tester is an English speaker, and its technical vocabulary is borrowed from English by the author's ruling. Its own open questions concede that "universal is a goal, not yet a result" ([language.md](../../docs/language.md)). The English loans repeat Basic English's political exposure more than Esperanto's: the more a lexicon comes from one living national language, the more it reads as that nation's project. Even so, the worldlangs ended up with European technical terms (Globasa adds *-a* to English *-ation* words), so the choice is closer to what every rival does in practice than it looks ([Globasa](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)). The design log already records the cost once: the loans favour English speakers "in sound, not in meaning". The comparison adds one point. For a native language this bias matters little to learners and a lot to symbolism, which is exactly the kind of cost that sank Basic English politically.

### It churned fast, and its one-syllable space is full

In its first two days the language rebuilt every word under drift rules, moved from base 6 to base 10, moved confidence from sixths to tenths, and moved six words that sounded offensive. That would be Ido's mistake if anyone were learning the language, and it costs nothing while no one is. The real exposure is the one Esperanto shows: a freeze locks in whatever the core holds at that moment. The offensive-word filter kept **lon** "I" despite its match to a Vietnamese vulgarity, because moving it would mean rebuilding all 64 grammar words ([design-log.md](../../docs/design-log.md)). That is precisely the kind of early mistake a frozen core preserves for a century. The full one-syllable grammar space (digits 6–9 had to take two syllables, and future grammar words must too) echoes, mildly, Wilkins's nine fixed slots and Solresol's seven syllables. A small fixed inventory of short forms runs out, and later additions pay a length penalty. Toki Pona's growth from 120 to 137 words, and the Linku survey's tail of obscure words, suggest that pressure for new short forms will come ([sona pona](https://sona.pona.la/wiki/number_of_words)).

### Five things none of them did

First, **choosing root shapes with a perceptual model**. The nearest precedents are Globasa's hand-made ban on root pairs differing only by v/w, s/z or final m/n ([Globasa](https://www.globasa.net/eng/max/metode-fe-lexiseleti)) and Lojban's filter rejecting roots that differ by one final vowel or one similar consonant ([CLL 4.14](https://lojban.org/publications/cll/cll_v1.1_xhtml-section-chunks/section-gismu-making.html)). Both screened for collisions inside a lexicon chosen for other reasons. Apu Pi We makes distinctness the objective, and it pushes opposites and same-slot alternatives apart, the exact inverse of Solresol's *fala/lafa*. The only external hint that sparse phonotactics help recognition is the ROILA robot-language project, which reported a modified Toki Pona vocabulary beating English in machine speech recognition (secondary, via [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toki_Pona)).

Second, **designing against sound change**. None of the surveyed projects mentions resisting future drift. They rely on fixed spelling and an academy instead. Apu Pi We requires content words to stay distinct after the likely mergers (e/i, o/u, medial voicing, loss of final -n) and gives grammar words that share a slot different initial consonants. That is the precise answer to Bergen's finding that unstressed grammatical syllables reduce.

Third, **an offensive-word filter**. It checks 32,910 vulgar or taboo pronunciations from 34 languages against every word ([design-log.md](../../docs/design-log.md)). Neither Globasa's nor Kotava's documentation mentions any such check.

Fourth, **number-anchored confidence alongside optional evidentials, protected by a politeness outlet**. Lojban's attitudinal intensity scale and its ten evidentials are the closest precedent, and Láadan's evidential and speech-act particles come next. Neither scaled belief numerically, and neither had any mechanism to stop social pressure eroding the markers. Apu Pi We's sixth principle, that every social pressure gets its own outlet, has no counterpart in the conlang record.

Fifth, **the native framing itself, tested for fidelity rather than ease**. Every auxlang was judged by how fast adults learn it. No predecessor ran a blind comprehension test of any kind. Apu Pi We's back-translation series, flawed as its readers are, is the first systematic fidelity measurement in this lineage. By adopting what native Esperanto and creoles converged on (no inflection, no tone, preverbal aspect, optional tense), it applies Lindstedt's lesson deliberately: a language shipped already nativised gives native speakers little to change.

## What Apu Pi We should take from the record

The comparison sorts the lessons into three groups. Some apply directly, some apply only if the language ever has users, and some apply not at all. Adoption is in the third group. Every adoption lesson in the record (venues, free courses, fun, Discord, a franchise) serves speakers the project has not asked for. In the author's terms, building toward them would turn a thought experiment into a moonbase. The rest are summarised below, ranked by how much each serves the north star.

| Lesson | Precedent | What it implies here | Trigger to act |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test in the layer you don't control | Hooke, Loglan, Lojban's "unknown" attitudinals | A listening test with human listeners, ideally including non-English L1s, on root pairs the model rates closest | Now: it is the only check on the model that chose every word |
| Fix before freezing | Esperanto's accusative and *-ino* survive a century | Decide on **lon** and any other known flaw before declaring the core stable | When a second human starts learning the language |
| Freeze core, ratify usage | Esperanto's Fundamento; Toki Pona's *ku* | Keep "existing words don't move", plus a descriptive path for new words from real use | When anyone besides the designers produces text |
| Expect precision layers to bend | Lojban's *le*→*lo*; Kirby's underspecification; Bergen's lost aspect | Predict **kon** collapsing to a few values, **wan/yi** yielding to topic, **le/win** rarely used, and track them in tests | When back-translation shows systematic misreads in these layers |
| Phonology follows founders | Hebrew's uvular r; Esperanto stress following each child's other language | Drift-proofing protects distinctness, not a standard accent; expect regional accents | No action; note in the reference |
| Avoid the "better thinking" pitch | Ithkuil's Russian episode, Loglan's untested Whorf claim | Keep the north star about transmission, not cognition | If anyone frames the language as improving thought |

The first row matters most and is cheapest. No constructed language in this record ever measured how often listeners confuse its roots. The research flagged that missing experiment as the most relevant gap in the whole record. A two-person listening test of the model's closest pairs would, as far as this research found, be the first such data in 350 years of the field, and it would check the instrument that chose every word in Apu Pi We. Running it first is consistent with the author's step 4, shortening the feedback cycle. The fourth row is the clearest prediction the record makes. Wherever a rule is mechanical and speakers want brevity, speakers have won: tanru, *lo*, the accusative. Apu Pi We's recency rule for **wan/yi** is mechanical by design, and Claude itself slipped toward the topic reading in vocabulary test 03 ([design-log.md](../../docs/design-log.md)). That is the first place to look for the language's equivalent of *le* and *lo*.

## Conclusion

The record changes which history matters for this project. Most of what is written about planned languages concerns adoption and governance, and that history explains Volapük, Ido, Interlingua and the worldlangs well, but none of it binds a language imagined as native. What binds Apu Pi We is the smaller, harder literature on what learners and native speakers do to a design. On that literature the language is unusually well aligned, because it was built from the same evidence: drift, creoles, iterated learning. The open risk sits elsewhere. Its instruments, a confusion model fitted to 1955 English listeners and AI readers who share the designer's world knowledge, are exactly the kind of self-referential validation that let Wilkins and Brown believe their designs worked.

The most distinctive thing Apu Pi We does is invert the philosophical-language bet. Wilkins put meaning into sound and logic into the lexicon. Apu Pi We puts noise-robustness into sound and logic into composition. Its predecessors offer no evidence for or against that inversion, because none of them ever measured a listener. Apu Pi We can therefore be the first constructed language whose central design claim is tested in the one place every predecessor skipped, and that test needs two people and a list of word pairs, not a community.
