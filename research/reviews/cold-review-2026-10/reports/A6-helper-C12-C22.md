# A6 helper batch: claims C12–C22 (from run 1's verification helper, finished after run 1 stopped)

Summarised by Claude (main session), 2026-10-11, for the judge and for merging into A6-factcheck.md. URLs are in the helper's message in the main session log.

- **C12 partly:** Monaghan et al. 2011/2014 find a division of labour (arbitrariness helps learn meanings, systematicity helps categories; early words more systematic). Nothing says similar or opposite words should be pushed apart in sound; that's the notes' extrapolation. Dautriche et al. 2017 (100 languages): semantically similar words are more phonologically similar than chance. "Maximally different" unsupported.
- **C13 verified:** Bentz & Ferrer-i-Cancho 2016: law of abbreviation in 1262 texts, 986 languages, every case.
- **C14 partly, misleading:** Dediu & Cysouw 2013 find word-order *type* features (which order) stable; nothing about fixed vs free order. Greenhill et al. 2017: order features fall in the fast-rate category for Austronesian/Papuan.
- **C15 wrong as stated:** Modern Hebrew is SVO but heavily inflected (binyanim, agreement, obligatory tense); Saramaccan (creole) has lexical and tonal morphology; Esperanto was already mostly SVO and native speakers kept most inflection.
- **C16 partly:** end weight holds for head-initial languages (Behaghel, Wasow 2002); head-final languages put long before short (Hawkins 1994; Yamashita & Chang 2001). Not universal.
- **C17 partly:** Bergen 2001 (8 children): accusative use tracked the other native language (transfer); vowel reduction ~5%; children used neither compound tenses nor aspect affixes ("keep aspect" wrong); Lindstedt: no convincing nativization, effects are transfer/register/incomplete acquisition.
- **C18 wrong:** Esperanto is agglutinative (Parkvall 2010: typologically average, not isolating); creoles often analytic but not all isolating (Tok Pisin -im, Berbice Dutch suffixes).
- **C19 partly:** mitigated speech and hierarchy are real safety problems (Fischer & Orasanu 1999; Avianca 52 was lexical: never said "emergency"); evidence supports explicit lexical marking, but "melody alone fails in noise" is extrapolated (no source).
- **C20 verified:** Budescu et al. 2014 and Wintle et al. 2019: verbal + numerical probability formats improve agreement ("strongest fix" only among tested formats).
- **C21 wrong, source says the opposite:** Friedman et al. 2018 (888,328 forecasts): coarsening to categories like the 7-term scale "consistently sacrifices predictive accuracy" for forecasters at all skill levels. Not evidence that ~7 bands is what people can use.
- **C22 partly:** WALS 77A correct (418 languages; 237 with some grammatical evidential). Láadan's failure is attributed by Elgin to lack of uptake, not to evidentials.
