# A6 fact-check: empirical claims behind the apu pi we design

Merged from the main run (claims C1–C22, verified in-session) and five helper
batches that independently re-checked every claim C1–C52 (`A6-helper-C01-C11.md`
through `A6-helper-C44-C52.md`, same folder). Where both judged a claim, the
better-sourced verdict is kept below and any disagreement is noted in that
row. Decisions each non-verified claim supports are named from
`packets/evidence/rationale.md`.

## Claims rated wrong

- **C15** — "Native Esperanto, Modern Hebrew and creoles all moved to SVO, no inflection..." Hebrew kept rich verb inflection; Esperanto natives didn't "move" to SVO (they and L2 speakers were both already mostly SVO). Low-medium impact: the SVO/particle decisions stand on other grounds; cut the sentence.
- **C18** — "Isolating languages are where all successful auxlangs and creoles converged." Esperanto, the only real auxlang success, is agglutinative, not isolating; the isolating auxlangs are the unsuccessful ones. Low-medium impact: the particles-not-inflection decision survives on creoles and Bergen 2001 alone.
- **C21** — "Sixths match the ~7 bands forecasters can use (Friedman et al. 2018)." The cited paper finds the opposite: coarsening forecasts to a 7-bin scale consistently *reduces* accuracy at every skill level. **High impact** — this was one factor in switching the confidence scale back from tenths to sixths on 2026-10-10; the cited research actually favours tenths or finer. The syllable-economy argument for sixths stands independently.

No claim was found to be likely fabricated — every citation checked traced to a real source; the failures are overstatement or wrong-direction readings, except C32 below.

## Five most consequential partly/unsupported claims

1. **C36** (partly) — The language's own name, **apu pi we**, and the **apu**/**dunan** root-sharing decisions rest on specific CLICS4 family-balanced lump rates (apu/language 50.2%, moon/month 69.6%) that could not be independently recomputed, and one internal inconsistency in the counting method surfaced (FINGER-TOE has more raw links than WORD-LANGUAGE but a stated much lower rate). 50.2% is barely over the lump/split threshold — worth hand-checking before treating the name as settled.
2. **C19** (partly) — The obligatory sentence-initial speech-act particle rests on "melody alone fails in noise and across cultures," which has real supporting incidents (Tenerife, Avianca 52) but no study showing intonation-only failure or that an explicit marker prevents it; sentence-initial position is also a WALS minority form (129 vs 314 final). The particle is defensible on a different (untested) argument.
3. **C31** (partly) — The omission rule ("anything context supplies may be left out, including verbs") overstates what Mandarin/Japanese/Korean actually license (arguments drop, verbs usually don't); this is one of the four fixes behind a 12%-syllable economy gain tested only on AI readers, so both its natural-language grounding and its test method are each separately weaker than stated.
4. **C32** (unsupported) — "Polite pronouns cycle every 150-300 years" has no traceable source, and real counter-examples (*usted*, German *Sie*, English "you") have been stable 300-400 years. Doesn't change the no-polite-pronouns decision (independently backed by WALS 45), but it's a specific invented-looking number stated as fact and should be removed or sourced.
5. **C44** (partly) — "A two-syllable digit survives a noise burst (the ICAO lesson)" attributes to ICAO a rationale ICAO doesn't give (its two-syllable digits exist for phonetic distinctness, not burst survival); this is the live, unresolved half of the digits 6-9 decision, already flagged independently in the Internal tests table.
