# A6 helper batch: claims C23–C33 (from run 1's verification helper, finished after run 1 stopped)

Saved verbatim in summary by Claude (main session) on 2026-10-11, for the judge and for merging into A6-factcheck.md.

- **C23 verified** (figure second-hand): answers to negative questions split between agreement-based (Japanese hai/un) and polarity-based (English); "62 of 130 languages use verb-echo" cited as Holmberg (2016) via the 2025 Linguistic Typology paper "Echo answers"; Holmberg's book not opened.
- **C24 verified, minor overstatement:** Hudson Kam & Newport 2005 (LLD 1:151–195): adults matched input probabilities, many (not all) children regularized. Hudson Kam & Newport 2009: adults do regularize with complex, scattered variation, so "children, not adults" holds for this paper only.
- **C25 partly:** obviation is partly discourse/topic-driven (Goddard, Dahlstrom), but also obligatory with two animate third persons in a clause, one proximate per span, and the possessor rule is purely grammatical. "Follows the topic, natively" overstates it.
- **C26 partly:** Diessel (1999, 85 languages) treats discourse deixis as a basic use of demonstratives; secondary literature calls it "common, if not universal"; some languages use a dedicated form, so "demonstratives alone" may be too strong. Diessel's own wording not found.
- **C27 verified:** Greenhill et al. 2017 (PNAS 114(42):E8822, 81 Austronesian languages) list inclusive/exclusive among highly stable features (snippet; full text 403).
- **C28 verified with nuance:** median rates grammar 7.93e-05 vs basic vocabulary 1.48e-05 (ratio ~5.4) "on average"; some grammar very stable; grammar's speed partly due to contact and homoplasy.
- **C29 partly, misleading as stated:** Pagel, Atkinson & Meade 2007: slowest numbers, pronouns, special adverbs; fastest prepositions and conjunctions. "Function words are replaced fastest" holds only for prepositions/conjunctions; pronouns are among the slowest.
- **C30 verified:** Bybee 2002 (LVC 14:261–290): reductive sound change reaches high-frequency words first (frequency in triggering contexts).
- **C31 partly, overstated:** argument drop is real (Mandarin; Japanese/Korean argument ellipsis), but verbs stay overt in those constructions; Mandarin gapping is disputed; fragment answers exist in English too. "Anything context supplies may be left out, including verbs" goes beyond the sources.
- **C32 unsupported, figure looks invented:** no source for a 150–300-year politeness cycle; inflation is real but irregular, and several systems have been stable 300–400 years (usted, German Sie, English you).
- **C33 verified, narrower:** Bonnefon & Villejoubert 2006 (Psych Science 17:747–751): "possible" with a severe outcome read as tact; specific to face-threatening contexts.
