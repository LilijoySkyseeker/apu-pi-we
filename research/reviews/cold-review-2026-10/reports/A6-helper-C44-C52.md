# A6 helper batch: claims C44–C52 (from run 1's verification helper, finished after run 1 stopped)

Summarised by Claude (main session), 2026-10-11, for the judge and for merging into A6-factcheck.md. URLs are in the helper's message in the main session log.

- **C44a verified:** ICAO Annex 10 Vol II 5.2.1.4: digits sent one by one (except whole hundreds/thousands).
- **C44b wrong as ICAO's rationale:** most ICAO digits are one syllable (wun, too, tree, fife, six, ait); only fow-er, sev-en, nin-er have two, for distinctness (niner vs German nein, fife vs fire, fower vs for), not for surviving a lost syllable. No ICAO text says two-syllable digits survive a noise burst. "The ICAO lesson" is not ICAO's stated rationale.
- **C45 partly:** Miller, Smith, Zhu & Zhang 1995 (Psych Sci 6:56–60) supports regular numerals helping children learn to count; it is not about mishearing fifteen/fifty (documented in ESL and a BSRLM study, not in aviation or phone-number perception research).
- **C46 partly:** ~20% of Esperanto's leaders but 3–4% of ordinary Esperantists went to Ido; Ido dwindled; churn and reform schisms hurt it. "Most Ido supporters later returned" not found.
- **C47 verified, caveat:** Iceland's 1940–60 school campaign eradicated flámæli among children (42% of Reykjavík children in 1929); it was a vowel shift or near-merger, "merger" is loose. The new impersonal and dative sickness persist despite prescription.
- **C48 verified, nuances:** the 2021 Toki Pona Dictionary (ku) reinstated community words via polls (kin, oko, namako, kipisi, leko, monsuta among nimi ku suli); pu listed kin/namako/oko as synonyms rather than dropping them; apeja and pake came back only as minor words.
- **C49 verified (abstract level):** Logan & Klapp 1991 (JEP:LMC 17(2):179–195): automaticity depends on presentations per item, not total practice.
- **C50 verified:** Vietnamese lồn is very vulgar (tone matters; toneless lon also means "can"); ISO 8601 weekdays run 1–7 from Monday.
- **C51a partly:** Chinese and French coin native computer terms; German and Japanese borrow heavily (Japanese "search" is native kensaku). "Major non-English languages" as a group is overstated.
- **C51b partly:** most Western languages keep foreign names' spelling, but Latvian respells by law, Lithuanian has a phonetic form, and many add case endings.
- **C52a verified:** NSM has 65 primes (current count since 2014).
- **C52b partly:** IDS and WOLD descend from Buck 1949; CLICS draws on them among many datasets; Swadesh and Leipzig–Jakarta lists don't descend from Buck.
