# A6 helper batch: claims C1–C11 (from run 1's verification helper, finished after run 1 stopped)

Summarised by Claude (main session), 2026-10-11, for the judge and for merging into A6-factcheck.md. Several sources were graded on abstracts or snippets (PDFs unreadable, some publishers 403). URLs in the helper's message in the main session log.

- **C1 verified:** five vowels is the most common inventory size (WALS 2: 188 languages); /i e a o u/ in ~97% of 5-vowel UPSID languages (Schwartz et al. 1997, via snippets); Liljencrants & Lindblom 1972 is the standard dispersion source.
- **C2 verified (original unread):** Miller & Nicely 1955: voicing and nasality survive noise, place is most damaged; "survive to −12 dB" consistent but unconfirmed.
- **C3 partly:** PHOIBLE v 0.27, z 0.30 look right, but "rare" overstates sounds in a quarter to a third of inventories ("uncommon" is fair).
- **C4 partly, one half wrong:** v/b confusion supported (Miller & Nicely; Phatak & Allen 2007; Spanish betacism). "Drifting toward /w/" is wrong: w > v is the common change, and the design's own count (24 w→v vs 9 v→w) contradicts it. s/z merging unsourced but plausible.
- **C5 partly:** Zuckermann's Founder Principle: the founders' sounds win (Israeli Hebrew keeps Yiddish phonology); "rare sounds are at risk" is the notes' extrapolation (at risk are sounds the founders lacked).
- **C6 verified:** initial stress cues word starts for Finnish/Dutch listeners (Vroomen et al. 1998; language-specific: French listeners didn't use it); final lengthening heard at word ends universally (Tyler & Cutler 2009).
- **C7 partly:** initial stress followed by loss of later vowels is standard history (Germanic, Old Irish, Latvian, Estonian); "Finnish and Czech protected by length contrasts" unsourced, and Estonian, Latvian, Old Irish had length and still lost vowels: a hypothesis.
- **C8 verified, wording nuance:** Gurevich 2004: 212 of 230 mostly-leniting processes (92%) non-neutralizing, in languages chosen from lenition databases.
- **C9 verified with caveat:** CV is universal or near-universal (Arrernte, Gokana contested).
- **C10 partly:** declination is near-universal in declaratives, but suspended in questions (e.g. Hausa) and limited in multi-level-tone languages (Yoruba).
- **C11 Wilkins verified, Solresol partly:** Wilkins' Tida "redness" / Tide "greenness" confirmed; Solresol reversal gives some opposites but isn't a rule.
