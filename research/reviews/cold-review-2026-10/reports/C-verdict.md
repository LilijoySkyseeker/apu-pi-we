# C — Judge's verdict on Apupiwe (apu pi we)


## Overall verdict: keep with repairs

The architecture holds. Every structural choice that the goal's own method (B5, C1) can be checked against — five vowels, (C)V(n), no tone, invariant words, SVO with roles by position, head-first noun phrase, `man` relatives, four derivation markers, transparent decimal numerals, Latin one-letter-per-sound writing, `en` for names, echo answers, clusivity, a topic-tracked third person — survives all seven reviews and the drift review's three-generation projection ("meaning rarely breaks"). What fails is concentrated in one thin layer and one process:

1. **The short-word layer** (digits, one-syllable grammar words, two-syllable operators): `fa`/`sa`, `wun`/`un`, `wo`/`o`, `yen`/`en`, `la`/`le`, `manni`→`man`, `konsin`→`kon`. Every one of these is a word move, not a redesign, but B2 is a guarantee and the model that chose every form has never been checked against a human listener.
2. **Things that have lost their source**: 30 senary/unit rows in the core lexicon (N6 removed their reason), 7 loans that fail L4 outright and 8 adapted from English rather than the world's form, and a handful of prescriptive "never" rules (never hint, never reduce, never generic `me`) that B9 and N4 forbid.
3. **The evidence base**: 0 fabricated citations, 24 of 52 verified, but 3 wrong (one of them the citation given for the confidence scale), the B2 confusion model unvalidated and inconsistent with its own published digit figure, every reading test done by AI readers, and the vocabulary stop condition triggered by AI translators who paraphrase better than people.

None of the repairs touches the sound system, the sentence template, the noun phrase, the relative clause, the derivation markers, the numeral system, the writing or the pronoun set. That is why this is "keep with repairs" and not "substantial repair". The one thing that could escalate it: a human listening test showing digits 0–5 or same-slot grammar words confusing at rates far above content words. Then the short-word layer (about 70 forms) is rebuilt, which is still not the language.

Severity inflation ran as expected for adversarial reviewers: of the 14 findings rated high, five stand as high (fa/sa, loans, senary rows, pauses, grammar-word phonology), five stand or partly stand at medium, and four partly fall. The defence conceded 15 of 51, and most of its concessions are the right ones; its three full defences (`gun` copula, `sin`, unattested machinery) are all convincing. Where it over-defended: the pause rule ("B4 is met formally"), the Friedman citation, and `pi` overload.

## The three most important changes

1. **Run one human listening test** (digits in strings, same-slot grammar-word pairs, carrier phrases, listeners from three L1 families, noise and phone band), then fix the short-word layer by its results: move `fa`, re-solve `wun`/`un` (the strict-group check passed it by a bug), rename `manni` and `konsin`, and only then decide whether digits 0–5 need rebuilding.
2. **Add one optional two-syllable closer** for relative clauses and phrasal derived words, and make the comma its written mirror rather than the only device. This is the single finding that three reviews reached independently from different evidence (production prosody, texting, Tok Pisin *ia*), and the rationale's own add-back trigger already names it.
3. **Delete what lost its source and fix the loans**: the 30 senary/unit lexicon rows, the 7 loans with no world-shared form (`awuwa` first), re-adapt the other loans from a cross-family median, and strike the prescriptive norms ("never hint", "every syllable keeps its full vowel", "grammar words keep their consonants", the `yi` newcomer rule) in favour of stated defaults.

## 1. Rulings on every high and medium finding

Scale: **stands** / **partly stands** / **falls**. "Verified" means I re-checked it in the packet myself. Where reviews make the same point they are ruled together. The defence is "B".

### The short-word layer (B2)

**A3 2.1 (H) digits and short grammar words too close — stands.** B's own model puts `sa`→`fa` at 0.120, three times the worst content pair, and both words follow a noun and stand alone (`to ibe totu fa / sa`). B concedes, correctly. B's correction of A3's side-claim ("140 monosyllables, 67 used, so 'ran out of short sounds' is false") is half right: the design's same-slot, -n and offensive constraints do shrink the space, but those constraints are the design's own, so the learner-guide sentence is still misleading. B's restraint on the fix is right: move `fa` now; do not rebuild all ten digits on the say-so of the model under question (`sa`/`ka`/`da` are 2–3% under that model, which may or may not be real).

**A7 F3 (H) grammar-word phonology fights reduction — stands.** B concedes and adds the decisive detail: `wun` "that" (freq 5) and `un` "but" are in the same strict group and passed the drift check only because "no onset" is counted as a consonant. Glide pairs score ~0.04 in the model, 30× the 90th-percentile content pair. The "grammar words keep their consonants" norm is one speakers cannot obey (Bybee 2001; Jurafsky et al. 2001); A7's replacement constraint (no grammar-word contrast may rest on a declared harmless variant) is the correct form of the rule.

**A5 1 (H) minimal-pair grammar words (19/61) — partly stands, severity medium.** Verified: the six vowel-only sets exist. But B's slot argument is right and A3 said the same (Japanese *wa ga no ni de to*): `ba`/`bi`/`bo`/`bu` never compete for one position. What remains is the same-slot residue — `la`/`le` (0.029, adjacent slots) and the glide pairs above — which are already covered by the two rulings before this one. "A vowel slip swaps categories" overstates it.

**A3 2.6 (M) vowel-only packing, ban on reduction — partly stands.** The same-slot pairs are real but few; the ban ("every syllable keeps its full vowel") is a norm against B9, and B concedes the wording. Not urgent; repaired with the above.

**A7 F5 (M) `manni`→`man`, `konsin`→`kon` — stands.** B concedes. `konsin` is literally `kon`+`sin`, both take a number; `manni` and `man` both sit between noun and verb. Clipping of grammaticalising two-syllable words (*baimbai > bai*) is ordinary. Rename both and add the juncture rule B proposes.

### Pauses carrying grammar (B3, B4)

**A3 2.3 (H), A7 F2 (H), A5 5 (M) — stand (A5's partly).** This is the best-supported finding in the set: three reviews reached it from three evidence lines (speakers produce disambiguating prosody unreliably, Allbritton et al. 1996 and Kraljic & Brennan 2005; texters drop commas, Tagliamonte & Denis 2008; natives invent a closer, Sankoff & Brown 1976). B's "B4 is met formally because the comma is the written pause" is not convincing: B4 is about meaning carried, and a guarantee that neither channel reliably produces fails. B's own tests were AI readers with the rules in hand, and comma-01 contained no topic opener. B does concede the substance and names the right fix: one optional two-syllable closer, pause kept as the casual form, pulled on the rationale's own add-back trigger. A5's "a second grammar bolted on" partly stands: six of the eight pause positions are ordinary (address, openers, tags, dates); the two that carry truth conditions (closing a relative clause or a derived subject) are the problem.

### The senary system (L3, C1, N6)

**A3 3.1 (H), A4 F3 (H), A5 2 (H) — stand on the deletion; A4's framing partly falls.** Verified: 20 unit rows, 9 senary number rows and `senali`, all tier 1 except `boyo` "36-day block" at tier 3, sit in the core lexicon while 9.3 makes SI loan names the practice. B concedes: N6 removed their only source (worldbuilding). B's L3 point is fair — vocabulary for a topic no more breaches L3 than English "dozen" — so A4's "a senary calendar sits inside the core" falls as stated (8.3 uses today's calendar), though `boyo` at tier 3 shows the leak was real. A3's "it distorts the digits" partly stands: B's sources confirm 0–5 kept forms optimised for a six-digit set and 6–9 got what was left; but the digit repair should be driven by the listening test, not by the deletion.

### Loans (L4)

**A4 F1 (H), A3 5.12 (M) — stand.** B concedes. Verified in the lexicon: `awuwa`, `ilekutulisiti`, `osupitalu`, `bayisikalu`, `diguli`, `kontakutu`, `senali` have no world-shared form; `tuleyin`, `layisansu`, `kilamita`, `pasentu`, `intalunetu` follow English vowels. L4 is a hard limit and the rationale admitted the practice ("usually English"). B's mitigation (the policy predates L4; the UDHR pass already applied a stricter test) is honest context but changes nothing. A4's 20-language panel and median-form adaptation are the right repair.

### Confidence in sixths

**A4 F2 (H), A6 C21 (high impact), with A3 3.2 and A7 F13 (low) — partly stands, severity medium.** I read Friedman et al. 2018. Table 1: at seven equal bins untrained individuals lose 0.5% mean (p<.001) and 0.2% median (n.s.); superforecasters lose 40%. The per-question threshold averages 6.1 bins (SD 4.4) and exceeds seven bins for 42% of questions; the paper's conclusion is that coarsening "consistently sacrifices predictive accuracy". So A6 is right that the design's sentence ("sixths match the ~7 bands forecasters can use") cites the paper against its conclusion, and B is right that a narrower claim ("ordinary forecasters lose little at seven bins") is supported. Neither reading favours sixths over tenths; the honest reason for sixths is that 0–5 are one syllable, a legitimate C1 argument that should be stated as such. A4's B5 point stands (no language grammaticalises numeric probability) but its "high" severity does not: `kon` is optional and unmarked by default, and A5's count (0 uses in 2,558 UDHR tokens) shows the everyday cost is nil. The real gaps, which B concedes: no vague "maybe" (`kon ka` claims 50%), and nothing stops `kon ka` lexicalising as exactly that (A7 F13), so B's `kon fu ka` or leaving it to use are both fine. The counterfactual `don kon po`: B's defence (transparent irrealis; no tense means no "fake past" to borrow) is reasonable; keep.

### Optional markers, future, commands, existence (what natives will do)

**A7 F1 (H) optional markers go categorical — partly stands, severity medium.** B's premise defence is right: Hudson Kam & Newport's children regularised *unconditioned* variation, whereas here absence of a marker has a stated default reading, so most of the "optionality" is really default-vs-marked. What stands: the three breaks A7 names. `mu sin` "not yet" merging with "didn't" once `sin` becomes past is a real, uncovered consequence (B concedes); bare `obu.` as a statement is a reading natives will not have; and principles 1 and 7 should be reworded as defaults. The cure is wording plus one form for "not yet".

**A3 2.2 (M) no future marker — partly stands.** The frame mechanism covers most future sentences and B's defence of it is sound (Mandarin/Yoruba). But there is no "later" root and "she'll come some time" needs bare `wo` (B concedes; the design's own consistency pass asked the same question). A3's evidence (future/irrealis is the most convergent TAM marker after aspect in creoles and tenseless languages) is good. B's fix (one time root "later", let a particle grammaticalise from `momu`/`isi` as A7 predicts) is better than A3's new particle, because it adds no grammar.

**A7 F6 (M) `tin` required, bare-verb commands universal — partly stands.** Aikhenvald's bare-stem universal and A4 F20 make the prediction near-certain; B concedes a subjectless bare verb as the casual command with `tin` as the precise form, which also keeps A3 3.3's point (B4 is why `tin` exists). Severity low after the wording change.

**A7 F8 (M) positional existential `gun` — stands.** B concedes: a rule that makes "there is" depend on a subjectless `gun` being clause-initial will not survive pro-drop (Spanish *es dinero*), and A5 6 flagged the same rule as a patch. B's fix (bless `ibe` as the existential, the Mandarin 有 / Haitian *gen* path) is right.

### Pragmatics and social words (C2, L2, N4)

**A3 2.4 (M), A4 F9 (M) — partly stand.** What stands: "Being polite never means hinting" is a usage ban with no goal source (B concedes and deletes it), and principle 6 ("every social pressure has its own word") is the builder's own requirement, which the user's rules flag as the most dangerous kind. What falls: A4's "7 obligatory illocution particles" misreads 7.1 (a statement has no particle; only commands and requests need one), and A3's "prescribes which stance particles exist" misreads 7.2 (open class). `nun` and `lanno` earn their place on B1 grounds (politeness separated from the act), as A3 itself says. `fon`/`de`/`yo` are already optional; keep them as cheap, flag for deletion if a multi-register corpus shows no use.

### Lexicon size and vocabulary method

**A3 4.1 (M) lexicon too small — partly stands.** Verified: 483 roots + 23 loans, with real UDHR losses ("barbarous acts" → `musu bi yomo`; religion → `lite pi nosen`, a theistic framing that fails C2's fairness). A5 reads the same numbers as right-sized, so the ~1,000 figure is a designers' prior, not a finding. What stands is the method defect: the stop condition fired on AI translators (B concedes, A6 agrees), so the size is unproven rather than wrong. A3's measurement (paraphrase cost at 500/1,000/1,500 roots on chat, news, instructions, with human readers) is the arbiter.

**A6 vocabulary tests 01–05 (medium) — stands.** Conceded.

### Typology and B5 leakage (A4)

**A4 F4 (M) oblique relatives strand the preposition — partly stands.** It is a minority strategy (WALS 123A) and the retention form should be the named precise form; B's point that this is object-drop under a general rule rather than movement-based stranding is fair and makes the cost small. Fix: one example.

**A4 F5 (M) `fa` without verbal negation — partly stands.** Verified: the UDHR uses `fa` standalone twice (`musu fa kubo tan`, `fa pi kemin tan …`), the SAE pattern (WALS 115A: 11/206). The B3 gap (`mu … fa` has no stated reading) is the part that matters, and B concedes it with a one-sentence fix (concord).

**A4 F6 (M) copula required for adjectives; `gun`+verb is an SAE passive — falls.** B is right on the text: 4.2 allows bare `guyi kudu` as a statement and 4.5 gives `bi upe.`; `gun` is the clear form, as Mandarin 很 is. A7 F1/F18 treat copula > passive as the path natives would take anyway, which contradicts the leakage charge. What survives at low severity: the experiencer asymmetry (`to gun nuta` "I am feared") is a learnability trap worth a learner-guide warning.

**A4 F7 (M) three rare reference systems stacked — partly stands.** Each piece is rare, and the stack is real. But the UDHR shows `yi` (12) and `sun` (3) doing disambiguating work (verified), clusivity is among the most stable features where it exists (Greenhill 2017, verified by A6), and A7 predicts children may *strengthen* `yi`. A4's replacement (`ben`) conflicts with the packet: `ben` is already the non-topic *thing*. What stands: the newcomer rule for `yi` reads as a prescription (A3 3.4 agrees); B concedes rewording. Keep the words, demote the rules.

**A4 F8 (M) one areal isolating type, undocumented — partly stands.** The coherent-type choice is legitimate and A4 says so; what is wrong is its stated support, which rests on A6's C15 and C18 (conceded). A4's "favours adults over natives (N2)" is contradicted by A7's evidence that natives push designed languages toward exactly this profile (Bergen 2001, Lindstedt 2006, creoles); I side with A7. Documentation fix.

**A4 F10 (M) C1 method not shown; `apu` claim overstated — partly stands.** Partly a scoping artefact (A4 had no rationale; A6 audited 52 claims, 0 fabricated). But the reference's one checkable claim ("senses most language families lump together: `apu`") is overstated — B concedes "about half" — and the 50.2% figure that the language's own name rests on could not be reproduced (C36). Stands for the reference text.

### Complexity (A5)

**A5 3 (H) doubling is six memorised meanings — partly stands, severity low.** B's reading of principle 5 ("widens *or* strengthens") covers `mu mu`, `nun nun`, `yo yo`, `feko feko` and `genso genso`; `nen nen` "each other" is the one that cannot be predicted and B concedes it as lexicalised. The "never not-not" line is a patch, but a one-line one.

**A5 4 (M) confidence/evidential/stance/politeness unattested — falls as a finding, stands as a gap.** Verified: all zero in 2,558 tokens, and A5's one `min` is an indirect-question `wun min`, not a stance tag. But a legal declaration contains no hedging or politeness in English either, the systems are optional and unmarked by default, and A5 itself flags the register caveat. B cites casual-register tests I cannot see, all AI-read. The gap — no human multi-register evidence for any optional system — is real and belongs to the evidence-base verdict.

**A5 6 (M) patch rules — partly stands.** The positional existential is a patch (conceded, see A7 F8). `tun` for a recipient with a bare determiner is ordinary dative marking and A5's alternative would be its own attachment rule. The bare noun+word ambiguity is the deliberate economy default, the Indonesian/Mandarin pattern; falls.

**A5 7 (M) `sin` has three readings — falls.** "Done relative to the frame" is one meaning (the creole anterior; Bickerton 1981); a learner who knows the frame rule predicts all three readings. What survives: the stipulation "`sin` and `le` don't combine" is arbitrary, natives will combine them (Guyanese *bin a*), and the reference should allow it; and learn.md should teach frame-relativity early.

### Phonology prior and SVO (A3)

**A3 5.3 (M) Toki Pona-like phonotactic prior — partly stands.** The shared prior is visible: five vowels, (C)V(n), and grammar words `pi` (with nearest-noun attachment), `la`, `en`, `a`, `o`, `mu`, `sin`, `wan`, `li`, `kin` are all Toki Pona forms, and the pronoun the design replaced for being a Vietnamese vulgarity, `lon` (A6 C50), is one too. B says the optimiser, not a hand, assigned the forms; I cannot check that, and it does not explain the inventory choice. But a prior is not an error: the inventory is independently defensible (PHOIBLE frequencies, B2, B10), nothing in the evidence says it is wrong, and B8 is not yet hurt (roots average ~2.06 syllables). Keep; run A1's 13-vs-20 modelling and a perception test.

**A3 5.4 (M) SVO without case untested — partly stands, as a gap in evidence only.** Gibson et al. 2013, Greenberg U41 and creole convergence are real if indirect; A4 passes it. No change to the language.

### `pi`, manner slot (A7)

**A7 F4 (M) `pi` overloaded; ordinals and dates rest on it — partly stands.** The fragility is real: `dili oma` "seven days" vs `dili pi oma` "Sunday", `dunan yawu` vs `dunan pi yawu` "October", resting on an unstressed freq-8 linker that erodes in every language that has one. B's defence (alternatives rejected for collisions; A7's fix breaks N-Num) is fair, but a watch-list entry is not a repair for a daily subsystem. This also underlies A4 F19 (low): "day two" is Monday for Portuguese, Arabic, Hebrew, Vietnamese and Greek speakers. A stressed label word for weekdays and dates would answer both. Medium.

**A7 F7 (M) manner between verb and object — partly stands.** Verified contradiction: language.md 4.1 puts manner (slot 9) before objects (slot 10); learn.md lesson 14 puts "who gets it, then what" (9) before "how" (10). V-Adv-O is a minority order and verb–object bonding is strong in acquisition. Fix the contradiction first; which default to adopt should follow a count of which post-object reading is more frequent, as B says.

### Fact-check (A6)

**C15, C18 — stand** (Hebrew stayed inflected; Esperanto is agglutinative; conceded, strike both sentences). **C17 — stands** (Bergen's eight children show transfer, not nativisation; conceded). **C19 — partly stands**: the particle is defensible on B4 and the early-signal argument, not on "melody alone fails in noise", which no study shows; initial position is a WALS minority with a stated reason. **C31 — partly stands**: verb drop is not what Mandarin/Japanese/Korean license; the rule's own examples are fragments, which every language has; recite. **C32 — stands**: a 150–300-year politeness cycle with no source, contradicted by *usted*/*Sie*; delete (conceded). **C36 — partly stands**: raw CLICS counts confirmed, family-balanced rates unreproducible, one anomaly unexplained; the name of the language sits on 50.2%; recompute. **C44 — stands**: ICAO's two-syllable digits exist for distinctness, not burst survival (conceded). **Internal tests:** confusion model unvalidated — **stands** as the deepest gap, and B's own run adds that the tool now gives digits 0–5 a mean cross-confusion of 0.0185 against the published 0.0002, so the base-transmission test — **stands** — cannot be cited at all until re-run. Economy test with AI readers — **partly stands** (the claim "no loss detected" is what was made; humans needed). Multilanguage test cited as typology — **stands** (B agrees it is a functional test only). Vocabulary stop condition — **stands** (conceded).

### Where the defence was and wasn't convincing

Convincing: `gun` (A4 F6), `sin` (A5 7), unattested machinery (A5 4), the misreadings list (A4 F9's "obligatory particles", A3 2.4's "which stance words exist", A4 F3's "calendar in the core"), the slot argument on vowel-only sets, the "default vs optional" reply to A7 F1, and every concession. Not convincing: "B4 is met formally" on pauses; the claim that the Friedman note supports sixths (it supports "seven bins cost ordinary people little", which is not the same); the watch-list-only response to `pi`; and "forms were never hand-picked" as an answer to the shared-prior charge, which concerns the inventory, not the assignment. The defence also produced two findings of its own that the reviews missed: the glide-pair bug in the strict-group check, and the base-test discrepancy.

## 2. Area verdicts in full

### Sounds — keep

**Reason.** Five vowels /a e i o u/ (WALS 2A modal; dispersion theory), (C)V(n) with one nasal coda, no tone, fixed initial stress, 13 consonants drawn from PHOIBLE's commonest segments, l/r merged, no /h/. All three blind designs and the built language chose the same vowels and no tone, and A3 is right that these two would stand without any model agreeing. The inventory size is the least-sure shared choice (A1 #1, A8 #2, A3 5.3): a shared prior, untested, but not shown wrong, and the content lexicon is uncrowded (5 single-feature pairs in 547, verified). A4's note that /f/ is the weakest-supported consonant (absent from much of South and East Asia, Africa, Australia; carries digit `fi`) is the one inventory fact worth recording.

**Changes if any.** (1) Restate "every syllable keeps its full vowel", "stress is pitch only, never longer", "grammar words keep their consonants" and "harmless variants inside content words only" as the reference pronunciation, not rules (A3 2.6, A4 F22, A7 F3; B concedes). (2) Replace them with A7's constraint: no grammar-word contrast may rest on a declared harmless variant. (3) Run A1's modelling of word length and confusability at 13 vs ~20 consonants; revisit /f/ only if a listening test implicates it.

### Word forms — repair

**Reason.** Two populations with opposite results. Content roots: two syllables, initial stress, well spread, the digit-onset rule honoured (0 violations, verified), the empty-CV rule for every CVn grammar word honoured (0 counterparts exist, verified; A7 calls it nearly unique among designed languages). Short words: `sa`/`fa` at 0.120 in the design's own model, glide pairs `wun`/`un`, `wo`/`o`, `yen`/`en` at ~0.04 (one of them passing the strict check by a bug), `la`/`le` in adjacent slots, `manni`/`konsin` clipping onto `man`/`kon`, digits 6–9 at two syllables as a left-over of base 6. And the model that chose every form (Miller & Nicely at −6 dB with /l w y/, vowels and coda loss assumed) has never been checked against a listener, and no longer reproduces its own published digit figure.

**Most important changes.** (1) A human listening test on the pairs in B's table (digits in three-digit strings; same-slot grammar pairs in carrier phrases; noise and phone band; listeners from ≥3 L1 families). (2) Move `fa` by the existing promotion rule. (3) Fix the strict-group check to treat no-onset and glide as the same onset and re-solve the `link` group. (4) Rename `manni` and `konsin` and add the juncture rule (a two-syllable operator's first syllable may not be a same-slot grammar word). (5) Add a digit-vs-same-slot-grammar-word confusion check. (6) Decide on digits 0–5 only after (1). Add-back trigger for leaving 6–9 at two syllables: none needed; it is the status quo. Trigger for rebuilding all ten: the listening test shows any digit pair above the content-word 90th percentile.

### Vocabulary and loans — repair

**Reason.** 483 roots + 23 loans + 41 number/unit/senary rows (verified). The loan policy breaches L4, a hard limit, for about a third of loans and adapts the rest from English (conceded). The 30 senary/unit rows have lost their source (conceded). The stop condition for roots fired on AI translators (conceded), and the UDHR shows the losses: "barbarous", "religion" (as `lite pi nosen`, which fails C2's fairness for non-theistic traditions), nationality, justice, competent. Three concept boundaries copy English (`tinli` key/button, `yebin` call/phone, religion as "way of God"; A4 F11, unrebutted). The CLICS family-balanced rates behind lumping decisions, including the language's own name, could not be reproduced (C36). On the other side: derivation by `no`/`ge`/`du`/`pu` plus `pi` and `man` is sound (Thai/Vietnamese preposed nominalisers; A3 4.9), the 52 compounds are transparent, and A5 finds the root count lean rather than bloated.

**Most important changes.** (1) Re-run all 23 loans through a 20-language, 6-macro-area panel (A4 §3); replace the seven with no shared form (`awuwa`, `diguli`, `osupitalu`, `bayisikalu`, `kontakutu`, `ilekutulisiti`, `senali`) with native or built words; re-adapt the survivors from the median form (`kilometa`, `minutu`, `sekundu`, `internetu`, `tuleni`, `lisensu`). (2) Delete the 30 senary/unit rows from the core lexicon into an appendix; add-back trigger: a speaker community using base-6 units. (3) Drop the AI-triggered stop condition; add roots by a human-read paraphrase test on chat, news and instructions at 500/1,000/1,500 roots (A3 4.1). (4) Split `tinli` and `yebin`; gloss religion as "way of belief" or "the sacred". (5) Recompute the CLICS rates and change "most families" to "about half" in §14. (6) Allow bare N+N as a loose compound with `pi`/`man` as the precise form (A3 2.5, A7 §5): the closed compound list will not survive natives anyway.

### Core grammar — keep with repairs

**Reason.** SVO without case (Gibson 2013, Greenberg U41, creoles; A4 "pass, justified"), prepositions, head-first N-Adj-Num-`la`-Det-`pi`-Rel (Universal 20's common postnominal order), `man` relatives with subject/object gaps, invariant words, preverbal `mu` with scope by position, `min` + wh-in-situ `yu` built on full roots (avoiding Esperanto's one-sound correlative grid), echo answers, `ke … se` comparison, serial verbs. A7 rates all of these robust over three generations and says the language "is already in the state three generations of natives would push a designed language toward"; A4's scorecard passes 28 of 46 rows outright and most of the rest as harmonic minorities. The failures are all local: pauses as the only closer, a manner slot that contradicts the learner guide, an existential by position, `mu … fa` undefined, stranding named as the clear oblique form, no "later", `tin` obligatory.

**Most important changes.** (1) One optional two-syllable closer for relative clauses and phrasal derived words; comma mirrors it. (2) Fix the manner-slot contradiction (language.md 4.1 vs learn.md lesson 14), then test which post-object reading is more frequent before fixing the default. (3) Existence by `ibe` (or `go ibe`), subjectless `gun` as plain "is". (4) State `mu … fa` as single negation. (5) List pronoun retention (`… mon bu`) as the precise oblique relative beside stranding. (6) Accept a subjectless bare verb as the casual command, `tin` as the precise form and the form of prohibitions. (7) One time root "later"; let a future particle grammaticalise. (8) Allow `sin le`. (9) Make `tun` the normal recipient marker when the thing is a pronoun or determiner (A7 F9). (10) Document the type choice honestly: one coherent head-initial isolating type, because majority-per-feature is incoherent (A4 F8), and strike the C15/C18 sentences.

### Reference and pronouns — keep

**Reason.** `to`, `me`, `pen`/`din`, `wan`/`yi`, `bu`/`ben`, `sun`, `nen`, `go`, `tan`/`ben` for events. Clusivity is stable where it exists (Greenhill 2017, verified) and cheap; `yi` and `sun` are the one "exotic" system with usage evidence (12 and 3 in the UDHR, verified; A5 F10), and A7 expects children to strengthen rather than drop `yi`. `go` removes the generic-"you" ambiguity; `nen`/`nen nen` sit on well-attested paths. A4's "additions begetting additions" charge is real in form but the usage evidence answers it, and A4's replacement (`ben`) collides with `ben`'s existing job. Two low items: `me` is number-indifferent while first person is not (A4 F14) — `me la` already exists; and the Principle-B rule ("a pronoun never means the subject of its own clause") is acquired late everywhere and will be violated by children for a while, harmlessly.

**Changes.** Reword "a newcomer is `yi` until the speaker makes them the topic" as a default. Replace "`me` is never generic", "`lanno` never means you", "`de` is only for the speaker" with "in the reference variety" and list the expected extensions (A7 F11). Add-back trigger for a neutral "we": corpus evidence of `pen`/`din` misuse.

### Precision and optional systems — repair (trim)

**Reason.** Speech acts: `min` (question), `so`/`tin` (directives) and `nun` (politeness kept separate from the act) are attested, cheap, and serve B4 and B1; `genso` is a sensible written irony marker; `fon`, `de`, `yo` and `ba` are optional and cost nothing unused, though no language grammaticalises a five-way directive split and their only source is Searle's taxonomy (A4 F9). Confidence: optional, zero everyday cost, a verified case for numbers over words (Budescu 2014; Wintle 2019) — but sixths are a base-6 residue defended with a citation that reads the paper against its conclusion, and there is no vague "maybe". Evidentials: three optional words, WALS 77A majority; fine. Stance: three seeds plus an open class; fine. Politeness: `nun`, `lanno` fine; "never hint" is a usage ban with no source and principle 6 is the builder's own requirement. Doubling: one rule plus one idiom. The 14-slot template is a description and costs little (A3 3.6).

**Most important changes.** (1) Delete "being polite never means hinting" (learn.md lesson 8) and restate principle 6 as a design heuristic, not a norm. (2) Reword the Friedman citation to what it shows (ordinary forecasters lose ~0.5% at seven bins; skilled ones lose more; the paper concludes coarsening costs accuracy) and give sixths their real reason: syllable cost. (3) Add a vague "maybe" (`kon fu ka`, or let `kon ka` lexicalise). (4) List `nen nen` as lexicalised. (5) Mark `fon`/`de`/`yo` as candidates for deletion; add-back trigger: a multi-register human-read corpus showing warnings or commitments misread without them. (6) Teach `sin`'s frame-relativity and the `to gun nuta` trap early in learn.md.

### Numbers, time and measurement — repair

**Reason.** Decimal, fully regular numerals with order-based tens (`yawu te` 15 / `te yawu` 50), a priori `tonen`/`siwin` sidestepping the long/short-scale conflict, digit reading for codes and years, ISO weekday numbering, both clock styles, big-unit-first dates, pass-through of digits and unit symbols: all L2/L3 applied directly and A3 3.7 is right to keep them. Defects: `fa`/`sa` (above); 0–5 one syllable and 6–9 two as a base-6 left-over, with a false reason in the learner guide and the "ICAO lesson" conceded; `awuwa` "hour" (tier 4) with no world-shared form; the count/label contrast (`dili oma` vs `dili pi oma`) resting on unstressed `pi` alone, and "day 2" being Monday for several large cultures (A4 F19); senary rows in the core; "age as lived time" fine.

**Most important changes.** (1) `fa` moved; digits reconsidered after the listening test. (2) `awuwa` replaced (a native unit word, or `nane` if the time-unit root is repurposed, with the SI symbol `h` read by culture). (3) A stressed label word for weekdays and dates so the daily subsystem does not rest on `pi`; this also answers F19. (4) Correct learn.md's "ran out of short sounds". (5) Senary rows out.

### Writing — keep

**Reason.** Latin alphabet, 18 ASCII letters, one letter per sound, `y` for /j/ (shared across unrelated orthographies), lowercase standard, `en` marking names in both channels (A3 4.12: capitals don't exist in speech), digit grouping by ISO 80000, pass-through of clock, dates, unit symbols, handles and code. All required by L3 or directly serving B4. The one B4 defect is the comma carrying grammar on its own (ruled above). A7 F14's predictions (names capitalised, `!` and `?` becoming meaningful, spacing following segmentation) are all harmless and all forbidden on paper.

**Changes.** The comma mirrors the optional closer. Restate "`?` and `!` add nothing", "capitals carry no meaning", and the spacing rule as the reference variety rather than rules. Add-back trigger per A7: a community that actually keeps them.

### Drift robustness — keep, with wording repair

**Reason.** A7's headline is the verdict: meaning rarely breaks. Robust: five vowels, (C)V(n), invariant words, SVO, N-A-Num-Dem, `man`, operators before scope, preverbal negation, clusivity, wh-in-situ, final particles, transparent numerals, reduplication, serial verbs, the empty-CV rule. Fragile: whatever rests on a pause, on a glide-only or voicing-only grammar contrast, on `pi` alone, or on a "never". The reference's drift strategy over-claims that regularity gives drift "nothing to do" (A3 5.14; all four Claude authors made the same over-claim, a shared prior), and the two bans on reduction are the tell. The three real breaks (`mu sin`, no closer, glide pairs) are repaired in the areas above.

**Changes.** Replace every "never" with "in the reference variety" and list expected extensions; cut C15/C18/C32; keep the empty-CV rule as an explicit constraint for new grammar words; give "not yet" a form that does not depend on `sin` meaning "done".

### Evidence base and method — substantial repair

**Reason.** The good: 52 claims, 0 fabricated, 24 verified, 24 partly; a disclosed, scripted method for forms; tests actually run. The bad: three wrong citations, one of them (C21) a factor in a design decision; the B2 confusion model unvalidated, with listening test 01 abandoned, and now giving 0.0185 where the published base test said 0.0002 (B's own finding); every reading test (economy, comma, nesting, regression, multilanguage, vocabulary 01–05, names) read or translated by AI, which recovers omitted material and circumlocutes better than people; the multilanguage test cited as typology; the vocabulary stop condition set by that evidence; CLICS rates unreproducible; the reference's only lumping claim overstated. And the structural fact the task asks me to weigh: builder, designers, reviewers and defence are one model family, so the places where all four designs agree without external evidence (13 consonants, SVO, clusivity, "regularity resists drift") are shared priors, not findings.

**Most important changes.** (1) The human listening test (B2). (2) Human readers for the deletion/comma test and the paraphrase test (B1, B4, B6). (3) Re-run or withdraw the base-transmission numbers. (4) Strike C15, C18, C32, C44's "ICAO lesson", and reword C21, C17, C19, C31. (5) Recompute the CLICS family-balanced rates. (6) A one-line frequency source per optional subsystem (A5 12). (7) Where possible, one reviewer or rater who is not a Claude model.

## 3. Overall verdict in full: keep with repairs

**Why not "rebuild from the goal".** The three blind designs made from the goal alone converge with the built language on nearly every architectural decision (A3 §1), and where they differ the built choice usually has the better argument on the packet (initial `min` with a final tag, `en` for names, preposed derivation markers, a priori large-number words, no fronting plus a `pi` topic, separate antonym roots). A rebuild would reproduce this architecture and lose the parts that are demonstrably well made: the spread content lexicon, the empty-CV rule, the clock/date/notation rules, the UDHR translation as a test bed. The lessons a rebuild would carry are all available as repairs.

**Why not "substantial repair".** Every high-severity finding that stands is a word move, a deletion or a wording change: `fa`, `wun`/`un`, `manni`, `konsin`, one added closer, 30 rows out, 7 loans replaced and 8 respelled, five "never" sentences reworded. The only candidate for rework of a whole subsystem — the ten digits — waits on a test that hasn't been run. The counting of repairs is long (about 25 items) because the reviews were thorough, not because the language is broken.

**What would most change my mind.**
- Toward *substantial repair*: a human listening test showing digit pairs or same-slot grammar pairs confusing at rates well above content pairs (then the short-word layer is rebuilt, ~70 forms); or a human-read deletion test (A7 M2) showing meaning loss above 10% without commas (then the closer becomes the default and the pause rules are redesigned); or a human paraphrase test showing the lexicon loses meaning on everyday registers at a rate C1 cannot excuse (then roots roughly double).
- Toward *rebuild*: evidence that the 13-consonant (C)V(n) profile forces average root length above ~2.5 syllables once the lexicon reaches 1,000–1,500 roots (B8), or that caseless SVO fails role recovery in noise against SOV plus one marker (A8's experiment). Either would change forms throughout.
- Toward *keep as is*: a casual-register human corpus in which the optional systems are used and read back correctly, and a listening test in which the model's rankings hold.
- On the shared-prior worry: a reviewer who is not a Claude model, or the blind leakage test with five native-speaker linguists (A4 §3), flagging constructions none of the seven reviews saw.

## 5. What is solid

Every line of evidence (three blind designs, typology, complexity, fact-check, drift, defence, and my own checks) supports these. A repair or a rebuild should keep them.

- **Sounds:** /a e i o u/; (C)V(n), onsetless syllables only word-initially; no tone; fixed initial stress realised as pitch; l/r merged; no /h/; declination free. (A1, A2, A8, A4 scorecard, A7 §4, A3 5.1–5.2.)
- **Word-form discipline for content roots:** two or more syllables, stressed first; no single-feature consonant pairs among them (5 in 547, one involving a loan); no content root beginning with a digit syllable; every CVn grammar word's CV partner left empty. (A3 2.1, A7 F3d, verified.)
- **Invariant words, no case, no agreement, no gender, no articles, optional plural `la`.** (All designs; WALS 30A/33A/37A; A7 robust list.)
- **SVO with roles by position, prepositions, operators before their scope.** (Gibson 2013; Greenberg U41; creoles; A4 pass; A7 robust.)
- **Head-first noun phrase N-Adj-Num-`la`-Det-`pi`-Rel; `pi` for possession; `man` relatives with subject/object gaps; "whose" by `pi wan`.** (Universal 20; WALS 87A/90A/122A; A7 robust.)
- **Four preposed derivation markers `no`/`ge`/`du`/`pu`, with `pi` and `man` for other relations; separate antonym roots with `mu` + word available.** (Thai/Vietnamese nominalisers; A3 4.9–4.10; A8's antonym argument.)
- **Preverbal `mu` with scope by position; `na`/`fe`/`wi`/`o` operators; `ke … se` comparison; `fu` for "about/as … as".** (WALS 112A/143A/121A; A3 4.8.)
- **`min` question particle plus wh-in-situ built from full roots with `yu`; echo answers; a final `min` tag; sentence-final stance slot as an open class.** (WALS 116A/93A; Holmberg 2016; A7 robust; A3 3.5, 5.7.)
- **Pronouns `to`, `me`, `pen`/`din`, `wan`/`yi`, `bu`/`ben`, `sun`, `nen`, `go`; `tan`/`ben` for events; `en` for names.** (UDHR usage; Greenhill 2017; WALS 45A; A5 F9–F10.)
- **Aspect by `sin`/`le`/`buntu`/`manni` relative to a frame set by time words; no obligatory tense; modality by verbs `lenu`/`keya`/`wupi`/`efon`; optional evidentials `wanme`/`inwun`/`wemen`; optional `kon`.** (Bybee 1994; WALS 65A–67A/77A; A4 pass; A5 7 falls.)
- **Speech acts in words, not melody: `min`, `so`, `tin`, `nun` separate from the act.** (B4; A3 3.3; A4 F9 partial.)
- **Decimal numerals, fully regular, order-based tens, a priori `tonen`/`siwin`, digit reading for codes and years, `mimi`/`guti`/`unun`; labels by `pi`; ISO weekday and date order; both clock styles; borrowed SI unit names; pass-through of digits, units, handles, code.** (L3; Miller et al. 1995; A3 3.7, 4.11, 5.9.)
- **Writing:** 18 ASCII letters, one per sound, `y` for /j/, lowercase, no diacritics, comma as the written pause, `.` and `" "`; names in the owner's spelling after `en`. (L3; A3 5.10, 4.12.)
- **Economy by default, precision on demand, with stated default readings when a marker is absent; omission of what context supplies; "heavy goes last".** (C1; Coupé 2019; A3 5.13; A7 §7, with the wording change in F1.)
- **The loan adaptation rule itself** (nearest native sound, glide between vowels, added `u`, two syllables minimum), as distinct from the English sourcing it was applied to. (A1, A2, A8 all specify the same rule.)
- **The method's good parts:** a scripted, reproducible form-assignment process; a disclosed confusion model; drift-proofing by slot; an add-back trigger already written for the clause closer; the UDHR as a standing test text; 0 fabricated citations in 52.
