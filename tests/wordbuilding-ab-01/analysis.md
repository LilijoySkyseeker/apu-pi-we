# Word-building A/B test 01

**Question:** do dedicated role markers (doer, done-to/result, tool, place) decode better than generic heads ("person"/"thing"/"place" + verb)? The literature has no direct comparison (`research/notes/word-formation.md`).

**Method:** 38 coined words for everyday modern concepts, built two ways. Two blind readers each saw one version and the reference without the other version's rules. A judge scored best guesses from 0 to 2 against the intended meanings.

**Result:**
- **Markers (A): 71/76. Generic heads (B): 69/76.** Both got the role right on 37 of 38 items.
- With one reader per version, the gap is noise: **markers don't decode better**.

**What the differences showed:**
- **ge** covered both the person acted on and the thing made, so "ge TOfo" was read as "lesson" rather than "student". The reader had the same doubt on three more items.
- A relation word mattered more than the head: "person **from** sky" gave angel/alien; bare "person sky" gave pilot.
- Two shared misses had nothing to do with either scheme: factory read as construction site, message as statement.

**Decision:**
- **Keep the markers** on length and drift. They're shorter ("ge TUsi" is 3 syllables, "MUsu gun TUsi" is 5). Generic heads in frequent words grammaticalize into such markers anyway; Mandarin 者 and 器 grew that way.
- **ge** is now things only. A person acted on uses **Ewo gun** + verb.
- **The relation-word rule for noun bases is confirmed.**
- **Add-back trigger for dropping the markers:** a larger test showing they decode worse than generic heads.

**Side finding: the one-syllable grammar space is full.** With digits and the rule against grammar words differing only by a final n, 69 of 70 (onset, vowel) slots are used. New grammar functions will need two-syllable grammar words, or composition from existing ones.
