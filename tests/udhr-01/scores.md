# UDHR round trip: meaning scores

Scored `back-translation.md` against `source.md` and `source-fr.md`, paragraph by paragraph. 2 = the legal meaning survives, 1 = the core survives but something that matters is lost, weakened, added or made ambiguous, 0 = wrong or missing. Causes: L = a missing word in the language, G = a gap or ambiguity in the grammar, T = a translator's choice that the existing language could have done better, B = a back-translator misreading. Each 1 has one cause, the main one.

## Totals

- **89 / 116 points (77%)** over 58 paragraphs: 31 scored 2, 27 scored 1, 0 scored 0.
- Causes of the 27 losses: **L 8, G 6, T 13, B 0.**
- By part: Preamble and Proclamation 12 / 16, Articles 77 / 100.

These run through the whole text but aren't deducted per paragraph:
- "Rights and freedoms" comes back as "rights" (the G6 workaround).
- "Dignity" comes back as "respect".
- "The United Nations" comes back as "the group of countries".

All three keep the legal meaning in context.

## Five most serious losses

1. **Art 11.2: international crimes.**
   - The original allows punishment if the act was an offence under national *or* international law.
   - What came back bars conviction if national *or* international law "allowed" it. That turns a ¬(N ∨ I) condition into ¬N ∨ ¬I.
   - An atrocity that was legal at home could not be tried. This is the Nuremberg principle, reversed.
   - Cause T: the translator flipped "not an offence" into "allowed" and kept **lu**.
2. **Art 29.3 (and 14.2): "breaks the law".**
   - "Principles of the United Nations" became **wabun** "law".
   - "Cannot use these rights if that breaks the law and the goals of the group of countries" can be read as subordinating every right to ordinary law. That defeats the Declaration's purpose.
   - Cause T, made worse by **yen** scope after a **pi** phrase (G).
3. **"Arbitrary" as "without any reason" (Art 9, 12, 15.2, 17.2).**
   - Arbitrary arrest, interference, and loss of nationality or property are judged by lawfulness and justice. "Without any reason" is met by any stated reason.
   - Four protections are weakened by one term choice.
   - Cause T.
4. **Art 21.3: periodic elections.**
   - The duty to hold elections periodically comes back as "this choosing usually happens". It describes elections; it doesn't require them.
   - "People must show this will" can also be read as compulsory voting.
   - Cause G: **keya** can't reach **manni muwo**, and the language has no "regularly" separate from "usually".
5. **Art 12: honour and reputation.**
   - "Attacks upon honour and reputation" (unlawful attacks) became "other people may not speak badly about his/her honour and name". That reads as a general ban on criticism, the kind of insult law that Art 19 protects against.
   - The same article also loses "arbitrary".
   - Cause T.
