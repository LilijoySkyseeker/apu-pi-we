# Document of the rights of all people

Conventions: "anyone" renders **go** (anyone, people in general); "he/she" renders **wan**, "they" renders **wan la**, "the other one" renders **yi**. "Keep" (**kuwi**) could also be read as "protect" or "uphold" throughout. "Courts" renders **pu pi wabun**, literally "place(s) of law".

## Preamble

Each person of the family of all people has the importance of a person, because he/she is a person. All people also have the same rights. No one can take them from him/her. On the whole earth [**fowo** "ground, earth, soil": the world?], the root of freedom is that all people agree to this. That is also the root of correctness [rightness, justice?] and the root of peace.

People do not respect [tense unmarked: did not respect] the rights of people [of the person]. They also feel disgust toward them [**bebu bu la**, lit. "disgust them"; could also be read "they also disgust them"]. So, they have done very bad things. Because all people know good and bad [or: "know well and badly", since a word right after a verb can describe the action], these things make them angry. People hope that the whole earth becomes good. On this earth [i.e. in that hoped-for world], anyone speaks freely and has beliefs freely [lit. "has what-is-thought-true free"; or: "has free beliefs"]. In it, anyone is also not afraid, and has enough. This hope is higher than all [the highest of all], and someone has told it to all people.

The power of law must keep the rights of people, so that people do not have to fight the bad power [bad control] of leaders [**mu keya** "not must"; read strictly as "need not", not "must not"]. This fight is the last way [last resort; **lite fugi** "way end" is unclear].

All people must help so that countries become friends of each other.

In the document of the group of countries, the people of this group have said again that they trust [believe in] the important rights of the person, and that they trust the importance of the person, and that they trust that men and women have the same rights. They have also chosen to help so that all people grow. They have also chosen to help so that all people live better. They have also chosen to help so that all people have more freedom.

The countries of the group of countries have said: "We commit: we will work with the group of countries so that all people respect important freedom [the important freedoms] and the rights of the person. We also commit to work with them so that all people keep them." [The first "them" (**bu la**, things) is unclear: the freedoms and rights, or the group/countries.]

A same [shared] understanding of these rights is more important than everything [the most important thing] for the countries to be able to make that which was said whole [fully carry out what was said].

So, the gathering [assembly] of the group of countries tells the document of the rights of all people to all people. All people and all countries have this document as a same thing-wanted [a common goal]. May each person and each group of all people [society? "of all people" may attach only to "group"] remember this document at all times. May they try, through teaching, to make people respect these rights more. With work that grows in its own country [unclear: **nen** "self" here formally points to "work"; probably "in their own countries"], may they also try so that all people agree to [accept] these rights. May countries also work with each other for this. May all people truly keep these rights in the countries of the group of countries. May all people also keep them in the places that these countries lead [govern].

## Article 1

All people start living [begin life] with freedom, and have the same importance and the same rights. They can think, and can know good and bad [or: "know well and badly"]. They must treat each other like siblings [**muge nen nen fu faba la**, lit. "do each other like siblings"].

## Article 2

Anyone has all the rights of this document. Nothing changes this. The kind of person, or the kind of skin, or man or woman, or language, or the way of god [religion; god(s), number unmarked], or the country or group that he/she comes from [the relative clause formally attaches only to "group"], does not change this. His/her things [property], or his/her start of life [birth], or other things, or thoughts about leading [political opinions; lit. "what is thought of leading"], or other thoughts, also do not change this.

As for his/her country or place: if it is free, or another country leads it, or it does not lead itself, or it has less power in some other way [lit. "like another way"], its power also does not change this.

## Article 3

Life and freedom and safety are the rights of anyone [everyone].

## Article 4

No one is allowed to hold a person for unfree work [lit. "in order to work not free"]. No one is allowed either to own a person, or buy a person, or sell a person. This is true for every kind [of it].

## Article 5

No one is allowed to cause anyone great pain on purpose [lit. "make anyone very pain"]. No one is allowed either to treat anyone very badly, or treat anyone not like a person, or treat anyone like a thing. This is true even for the pain of the law [punishment].

## Article 6

In all places, anyone has the right that the laws think of him/her as a person [regard him/her as a person].

## Article 7

All people are the same for the law, and have the right that the laws keep them the same way [equally]. All people also have the right that the laws keep them equally from people who do not treat them the same, in a way this document does not allow [lit. "like this document not-allow"; the scope of this phrase is unclear]. They also have the right that the laws keep them from people who try to get others to do that.

## Article 8

If someone breaks an important right that other laws, or the important law of the country [constitution?], give to the other one [the victim, **yi**], that other one has the right that the courts of the country really [**o**, insistent focus] make that good [put that right].

## Article 9

If correct [just] law does not allow it, no one is allowed to take anyone, or hold anyone, or make anyone leave his/her country [whose country is formally open; most naturally the person made to leave].

## Article 10

If the courts choose about [decide on] his/her rights and duties [lit. "what must (be done)"], or choose whether he/she has broken the law, he/she has the right that the courts listen to him/her correctly [fairly]. The courts must listen to him/her while all people can see [in public]. These courts must be the same for all [impartial], and must be free [independent]. This is the same for all people.

## Article 11

1. If someone says that another person [**yi**] has broken the law, the laws must treat that person like a person who has not broken it. This is true before [until] the courts show that he/she has broken it. The courts must show that according to [lit. "like"] the law, at a hearing [lit. "a listening"] that all people can see. At this hearing, they [**bu la**, "they" as things: the courts or the laws] must give that person all the things that he/she needs in order to keep [defend] himself/herself.

2. If someone has done something or not done something, and the law of the country allowed that at that time, and the law between countries also allowed that at that time, the courts must not say that he/she has broken the law. The courts also must not give him/her pain heavier than the pain that the law allowed at that time [heavier punishment].

## Article 12

As for each person: if correct law does not allow it, other people are not allowed to enter his/her private life, or enter his/her family, or enter his/her house, or enter his/her sent things [letters, messages]. Other people are also not allowed to break his/her honour [respect], or break his/her good name. Anyone has the right that the laws keep him/her from that.

## Article 13

1. Anyone has the right to move freely inside the country where he/she stays. He/she also has the right to live freely inside it.

2. Anyone has the right to leave every country, and even his/her own country. He/she also has the right to return to his/her own country.

## Article 14

1. Anyone has the right to seek a safe place in another country [other countries], if people hunt him/her in order to hurt him/her. He/she also has the right to live in this safe place.

2. If someone truthfully says that he/she has broken the law, and this breaking is not about leading [not political; lit. "is not like leading"], he/she cannot use this right. If someone truthfully says that he/she has broken the aims of the group of countries, or broken its important ideas, he/she also cannot use this right.

## Article 15

1. Anyone has the right to have a country.

2. If correct law does not allow it, no one is allowed to take his/her country from him/her, or to take away the right that he/she has to change his/her own country.

## Article 16

1. If a man or a woman has grown [up], he/she has the right to marry. He/she also has the right to start a family. The kind of person cannot stop this. The country [nationality?] also cannot stop this. The way of god [religion] also cannot stop this. Men and women [a man and a woman] have the same rights in marrying. They also have the same rights during marriage. They also have the same rights at the end of a marriage.

2. Two people can marry only if they themselves agree freely and wholly.

3. The family is a small group that is the root of all people [of society], and has the right that all people and the country keep it. [The second half can also be read as part of the relative clause: "a small group that is the root of all people and has the right ...". The meaning is the same.]

## Article 17

1. Anyone has the right to have things with no one [alone]. He/she also has the right to have things with other people.

2. If correct law does not allow it, no one is allowed to take his/her things from him/her.

## Article 18

Anyone has the right to think freely. As for good and bad, he/she also has the right to think freely. He/she also has the right to freely have a way of god [religion] or belief [lit. "what is thought true"]. This right is also the right to change religion or belief. He/she also has the right to freely show religion or belief. He/she can do that with no one [alone]. He/she can also do that with other people. He/she can do that in a private place, or do that in a place that is not private. Teaching and doing and keeping [practising, observing] are ways of showing them.

## Article 19

Anyone has the right to freely have thoughts [opinions], and has the right to speak freely. This right is also the right that other people do not treat him/her badly because he/she thinks something. As for ideas and news [information], this right is also that he/she can seek them, and can get them, and can share them. He/she can do this with all things for telling [all media]. The between of the countries [borders?] does not stop that.

## Article 20

1. Anyone has the right to gather freely and in peace. He/she also has the right to build groups in peace. He/she also has the right to freely enter [join] a group.

2. No one has to enter a group [**go mu keya**, strictly "anyone not must": "does not have to", not "must not"]. Other people are not allowed to say that he/she must enter some group.

## Article 21

1. Anyone has the right to lead his/her own country with other people. He/she can do this himself/herself, or leaders whom people freely choose can do this for him/her.

2. Anyone has the same right to enter the work of his/her own country [public service].

3. The power of leaders must come from the will [lit. "what is wanted"] of the people of the country. People must be able to show this will through true choosing of leaders [elections]. These choosings must happen again and again. During these choosings, all people have the same right to choose. Anyone must be able to choose privately [in secret], or must be able to choose in another way that is as free as that.

## Article 22

Anyone is one of all people [a member of society], and has the right to help from all people [lit. "right to help from all people"; could also be read as a right to give help]. He/she also has rights that are about money. He/she also has rights that are about all people [social rights]. He/she also has rights that are about the way of living of groups [cultural rights]. He/she needs them in order to keep his/her own importance [dignity]. He/she also needs them in order to grow freely. His/her country works for this, and countries work with each other for this. Each country uses ways and things and its own money for this ["its own" formally attaches only to "money"].

## Article 23

1. Anyone has the right to work. He/she also has the right to freely choose his/her own work. He/she also has the right to work that is correct and good [fair and good]. He/she also has the right that the country keeps him/her from having no work.

2. If people work the same [do the same work; or "work equally"], they have the right to the same money. This is the same for all.

3. A person who works has the right to money that is correct and good [fair]. He/she and his/her family must be able to live with the importance of a person [human dignity] with this money. If he/she needs it [what "it" is is unclear: more money? help?], all people and the country must also help him/her in other ways.

4. Anyone has the right to build groups of workers with other people. He/she also has the right to enter groups of workers. He/she can do this to keep his/her own good [interests].

## Article 24

Anyone has the right to stop work [rest; or "quit work"]. He/she also has the right to have free time. This also means that he/she does not have to work very many hours. This also means that he/she has free days with money [paid days off] again and again.

## Article 25

1. Anyone and his/her family have the right to have enough for a good life. This also means that they have enough for a good body [health]. This also means that they have food and clothes and a house. This also means that all people and the country help them while they are sick. This also means that all people and the country help them when they need it. If he/she does not have work, or is sick, or cannot work, or is old, or his/her spouse has died, or he/she does not have enough in another way that he/she cannot change, he/she also has the right to help from all people and the country.

2. Mothers and children have the right to more keeping [protection], and have the right to more help [lit. "right to keep more" and "right to help more"; strictly the mothers and children would be the doers, though "be kept / be helped" is the evident sense]. As for all children: even if their parents are not spouses [married], they have the right that all people keep them equally.

## Article 26

1. Anyone has the right to learn. No one has to pay for at least the beginning teaching [lit. "teaching of the start": early/basic education]. All children must get the beginning teaching. All people must be able to get the teaching of work [training for work]. All people must also be able to enter high teaching equally, according to their own ability [lit. "like their own can"].

2. Teaching must help so that a person grows wholly. It must also help so that people respect important freedom and the rights of the person more. It must help so that all countries understand each other, and are friends, and allow that others think otherwise [lit. "allow that other thinks other"]. This is also true for all groups of the kinds of people. This is also true for all groups of religion or belief. It must also help the work of the group of countries to keep peace.

3. Parents have the right to choose the kind of teaching of their own children. Their choice is more important than the choice of other people.

## Article 27

1. Anyone has the right to freely enter [take part in] the way of living of the group where he/she lives. He/she also has the right to enjoy things made for beauty [art]. He/she also has the right to enter [take part in] the growth of knowing [the advance of knowledge]. He/she also has the right to get the good of this growth [its benefits].

2. If someone has made a thing of knowledge, or a writing, or a thing made for beauty, he/she has the right that the laws keep his/her name on it. He/she also has the right that the laws keep the money that he/she gets from it.

## Article 28

Anyone has the right to a system in which all the rights of this document can become wholly true. This system is a system of all people [society], and is a system between countries.

## Article 29

1. Anyone can grow freely and wholly only in a group. So, anyone has duties [lit. "what must (be done)"] toward his/her own group.

2. When anyone uses his/her own rights, only the laws are allowed to make his/her rights fewer [limit them]. The laws are allowed to do that only for two reasons. One reason is that people agree to [accept] the rights of other people, and respect them. Among all people who choose their own leaders, the other reason is that all people correctly keep good ways [or: "keep the correct, good way"], and keep peace, and keep the good of all people.

3. If anyone uses these rights, and that breaks the aims of the group of countries, or breaks its important ideas, no one at all is allowed to do that [**mu mu**, emphatic "not"].

## Article 30

Nothing in this document [lit. "none of this document"] means that some country or some group or some person has a right to do something in order to break one of the rights of this document [**fi pi sogan** "one of the rights": most likely "any of"].
