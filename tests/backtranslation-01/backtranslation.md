# Back-translation of /tmp/bt/source.txt

Sources used: language.md, grammar.tsv, lexicon.tsv only. Capitals ignored for meaning.

---

## Text A (one speaker's turn)

### A1. san san. e GAka BUdu: e Ife yo la MEni ban Itu yo pen TEli PIbi on NIte TAlan YUyu zo FEbu NOgo,

**Gloss:** NONLITERAL NONLITERAL. I say true: I think THAT.comp BE idea RATHER good THAT.comp GENERIC hit head OF person sky enter WITH wood long,

**Reading:** (This whole turn is tongue-in-cheek.) I'm telling you the truth: I think there's a pretty good idea, namely that you hit the heads of the alien invaders with long sticks,

**Notes:**
- **san san** marks the whole turn as non-literal, so "e GAka BUdu" ("I speak truly") is part of the joke.
- **NIte TAlan YUyu** = "intruding alien", taken from the example in language.md. Without that example I would only have got "sky person entering".
- **la MEni ban Itu** has no subject, so I read it as "there is a rather good idea". The second **yo** clause then spells out what the idea is ("heavy goes last").
- **zo FEbu NOgo**: rule 8 attaches this to TEli ("hit with long sticks"), not to YUyu ("entering with sticks"). Plural is unmarked, so it could be "a long stick".

### A2. gu pen DUda Ozi ban Owu on FEbu, men Ozi fi yo NIte TAlan la na TAde DUli on pen.

**Gloss:** AND GENERIC need know RATHER small OF wood, FOR know BEFORE THAT.comp person sky BE NOT child woman OF GENERIC.

**Reading:** and you need a little knowledge about sticks, so that you know beforehand that the alien isn't your own daughter.

**Notes:**
- **Ozi ban Owu on FEbu** = "rather little knowledge of sticks", taken from the example in language.md.
- **men Ozi** = "for knowing": a verb after a preposition names the act.
- **fi** ("before") has no object of its own here. I read **Ozi fi** as "know beforehand". It could also be **fi** + yo-clause ("before the fact that…"), which makes no sense, so I rejected it. Nothing in the reference says a preposition can stand alone as an adverb.
- **TAde DUli** ("child woman") = girl or daughter. With **on pen**, "one's daughter" fits best.
- **NIte TAlan** here lacks YUyu, so it means just "the alien".
- **la na TAde DUli** means "is not a girl", but rule 7 only covers **la** with adjectives and verbs, not nouns.

### A3. u FOve men MEni on e: dun FEzi vun, MOpu on NIte TAlan in!

**Gloss:** MORE important FOR idea OF I: EXCLAIM CURSE you, friend OF person sky PLURAL!

**Reading:** Even more important to my idea: damn you, you alien-lover!

**Notes:**
- **u FOve** has no subject and no **la**, so it is a fragment working as a heading. Under rule 7 an adjective without **la** only modifies a noun, and there's no noun here.
- **FEzi** (CURSE) is used like a verb with **vun** as its object ("curse you"). Its grammar isn't documented.
- **MOpu on NIte TAlan in** literally means "friend of the aliens". I read it as an insult ("alien-lover", "traitor").

### A4. PEpa on DONma men BIve tun NIte TAlan in WIye pen, la yo pen wun NOnu LANpu PIbi YEta FEzi on lu in, zan lu in DAba zo SUsa ke LInu DANyu on pen.

**Gloss:** way OF zero FOR live AFTER person sky PLURAL take GENERIC, BE THAT.comp GENERIC AT.LEAST try break head moon CURSE OF HE/SHE/IT PLURAL, WHILE HE/SHE/IT PLURAL move WITH light TO place bed OF GENERIC.

**Reading:** Step zero for surviving after the aliens abduct you: at the very least, try to smash their damned moon-heads while they come gliding with their light toward your bedroom.

**Notes:**
- **PEpa on DONma** = "step zero", from the example in language.md. The lexicon gloss is "way", so I wouldn't have got "step" without the example.
- **tun** + a full clause ("after the aliens take one") isn't covered anywhere. **tun** is listed as a relation (preposition), not a linker.
- Rule 8 says prepositional phrases attach to the clause's main verb. Here that would be **la**, which gives "for surviving / after they take you, step zero is…". I attached **men BIve** to PEpa instead ("step zero for survival"). The meaning is about the same either way.
- **la yo …**: a subject + **la** + yo-clause ("X is that …") isn't one of the documented uses of **la**.
- **wun NOnu**: AT LEAST before a single word gives "at least try".
- **NOnu LANpu**: by principle 2 this is verb + modifying verb ("try breaking"). I read it as "try to break".
- **PIbi YEta FEzi** is noun + noun + expressive. I read it as "damned moon-heads", i.e. a head like a moon, by analogy with BOzen NOwi = fire-snake. It could also be "the head of the moon".
- **DAba zo SUsa** ("move with light") could mean carrying a lamp or travelling in a beam. I picked the abduction-beam reading from context.
- **LInu DANyu** = "bed place" = bedroom.
- **lu in** = the aliens. **pen** is generic, not a second third person, so **tan** isn't triggered.

## Text B (first-person story; LIlifi = Lilith)

Because the story is narrated, I render the unmarked tense as past. From B6, B14 and B16, the narrator ("e") is Lilith, and the leader speaks to her.

### B1. e SUwon BIga on e.
**Gloss:** I turn eye OF I.
**Reading:** I rolled my eyes.
**Notes:** It could also be "I turned my eyes away" or "I looked away". **SUwon** is just "turn". The sarcasm in B2 led me to "rolled".

### B2. san e YEba men yo e YEba.
**Gloss:** NONLITERAL I apologize FOR THAT.comp I apologize.
**Reading:** (sarcastically) Sorry for apologizing.
**Notes:**
- **san** marks this one sentence as not literal, which is the sarcasm.
- **men** + yo-clause ("for the fact that…") isn't documented, but it's clear.

### B3. gu Ibo vun FOta gen FOta.
**Gloss:** AND now you leave FROM leave.
**Reading:** And now you're backing out of leaving.
**Notes:**
- Unclear. Literally it's "now you leave from leaving". Other readings I considered: "now you're running away from running away", or "now you quit quitting", i.e. you're changing your mind again.
- I also couldn't tell who says it: the narrator, or another character quoted without a frame.
- **Ibo** comes before the subject. Nothing in the reference says where time words go.

### B4. e— vun ZIzi yo e GAka mi?
**Gloss:** I— you want THAT.comp I say WH?
**Reading:** I— you want me to say *what*?
**Notes:** This is almost word for word the rule-5 example. **e—** is a broken-off "I".

### B5. BOwa on NIte GOni OLe GEli.
**Gloss:** face OF person lead become dark.
**Reading:** The leader's face darkened.
**Notes:** **NIte GOni** ("leading person") = leader. It is used this way throughout the text.

### B6. dun LIlifi, vun NIte BOsi!
**Gloss:** EXCLAIM Lilith, you person fear!
**Reading:** "Lilith, you coward!"
**Notes:**
- **NIte BOsi** ("fearing person") = coward. With no **la**, I read it as an epithet ("you coward!") rather than a predicate ("you are a coward").
- No speaker is named. From context it's the leader.

### B7. e NEyu ke LOve, BIga VONte on e.
**Gloss:** I walk TO back, eye wide OF I.
**Reading:** I stepped backward, my eyes wide.
**Notes:**
- **LOve** is the body part "back". The lexicon has no directional "backward", so **ke LOve** ("to the back") is my guess.
- The second part is a noun phrase with no verb ("my wide eyes"). I took it as an absolute phrase.

### B8. yi DOye bon zan e Ozi lu, NIte GOni POlo ENmo on lu.
**Gloss:** AT time NONE WHILE I know HE/SHE/IT, person lead lose calm OF HE/SHE/IT.
**Reading:** Never, in all the time I had known him, had the leader lost his composure.
**Notes:**
- **yi DOye bon** ("at no time") = never. The negation comes from the determiner **bon**, not **na**.
- A prepositional phrase placed before the subject seems to clash with "nothing moves". The reference doesn't say whether this is allowed.
- **ENmo** ("calm") is used as a noun here.

### B9. YEba SUyu Ime PAsa ke TAma on e, fen e SIdo e.
**Gloss:** apologize other come fast TO mouth OF I, BUT I hold I.
**Reading:** Another apology rushed to my lips, but I held myself back.
**Notes:**
- The verb **YEba** is used as a noun ("an apology").
- **e SIdo e** = "I held myself back", from the rule-6 example.

### B10. la BOsi ZUvo yi ZIsa on e.
**Gloss:** BE fear big AT heart OF I.
**Reading:** There was a great fear in my heart.

### B11. lu gi Avu wo va win mi?
**Gloss:** HE/SHE/IT ONGOING do LIKE THIS BECAUSE WH?
**Reading:** Why was he acting like this?
**Notes:**
- **win mi** = why, left at the end where the answer would go.
- This is the narrator's inner thought. There's no **yun** at the start, so I treated **mi** as enough to make it a question.

### B12. e Avu WAsu tan wo mi?
**Gloss:** I do stop OBVIATIVE LIKE WH?
**Reading:** How could I make it stop?
**Notes:**
- **Avu WAsu tan** is a causative ("make it stop"), with the result before the object.
- **tan**: **lu** is the leader in B11, so **tan** must be someone or something else. I read it as "it" (the situation), since rule 6 says the lu/tan split also separates "she" from "it".
- Alternative: "How do I make *him* stop?" That would need **lu**, unless the main character has changed. The reference doesn't say which of the two gets **tan** when "it" is involved.

### B13. dun VOnnu NOwi! NIte GOni GAka wo BOzen.
**Gloss:** EXCLAIM god fire! person lead say LIKE snake.
**Reading:** "Fire of the gods!" the leader hissed.
**Notes:**
- **VOnnu NOwi** is literally "god fire", i.e. a fire-god, by analogy with BOzen NOwi. I treat it as an oath. It could also be "God of fire!".
- **GAka wo BOzen** ("said like a snake") = hissed.
- Rule 12 says quotes are framed by a say-verb. Here the frame comes after the quote, which the reference doesn't mention, but it works.

### B14. vun KApo Ife zan ONo AYon, LIlifi.
**Gloss:** you sit think DURING day seven, Lilith.
**Reading:** "You meditated for seven days, Lilith."
**Notes:** **KApo Ife** = meditate, and **zan ONo AYon** = "for seven days". Both come from examples in language.md.

### B15. yun vun gi SEfo zan DOye YUfin?
**Gloss:** Q you ONGOING dream DURING time whole?
**Reading:** "Were you dreaming the whole time?"

### B16. na SEfo, e—
**Gloss:** NOT dream, I—
**Reading:** "I wasn't dreaming, I—"
**Notes:** The answer echoes the verb, as rule 4 says.

### B17. NIte GOni VOko OPun yi e, gu va Avu WAsu PEpu e yi Ibo.
**Gloss:** person lead look angry AT I, AND THIS do stop talk I AT now.
**Reading:** The leader glared at me, and that made me stop talking at once.
**Notes:**
- **VOko OPun** ("look angry") = glare.
- **Avu [WAsu PEpu] e** = "make me stop talking", with the result before the object.
- **va** stands alone as a pronoun ("this/that"). Determiners are documented only as following a noun.
- **yi Ibo** ("at now") = right then. In past narration, "now" means "at that moment".

### B18. la wo ti Osa vi SUwon: NUvu in len FOta, gu SIdo Osa Owu in on tan in yi Ate.
**Gloss:** BE LIKE IF thing SOME turn: feel PLURAL ALL leave, AND hold thing small PLURAL OF OBVIATIVE PLURAL AT far.
**Reading:** It was as if something had switched over: all my feelings drained away and held other people's little things at a distance.
**Notes:**
- **la wo ti** ("there is like if") = "it was as if".
- **NUvu in** turns the verb "feel" into a noun ("feelings").
- After **gu** the subject is dropped. Rule 11 says it must repeat, so the feelings are the ones holding things at a distance.
- If the intended subject is "something (that turned)" or "I", the dropped subject breaks rule 11. In a looser reading: "…and kept other people's small concerns far away from me".
- **tan in** = other people.

### B19. BOsi ZUvo la FEson yi MUnpi on e, e YAnu Avu Itu va, fen e la SAmun TEye.
**Gloss:** fear big BE close AT belly OF I, I must do good THIS, BUT I BE free again.
**Reading:** The great fear was shut away in my gut. I had to make this right, but I was free again.
**Notes:**
- **la FEson** = "is closed" (the resulting state, rule 7).
- **Avu Itu va** could be a causative ("make this good", i.e. fix it) or plain "do this well". **Avu** works both as plain "do" (B11, B21) and as the causative "make".
- Two separate clauses are joined by a comma with no linker.

### B20. VOko OPun on NIte GOni OLe NUvu MOfe, gu va Avu ZUvo u BOsi ZUvo yi LInu SUyu on FOpu DImin vi mu POkun tan gen Ozi on e.
**Gloss:** look angry OF person lead become feel bad, AND THIS do big MORE fear big AT place other OF wall glass SOME WHO.rel keep OBVIATIVE FROM know OF I.
**Reading:** The leader's glare turned into hurt, and that made the great fear grow even bigger, on the far side of a glass wall that kept it out of my awareness.
**Notes:**
- **VOko OPun** ("glare") is used as a noun phrase with **on**.
- **OLe NUvu MOfe** ("became feel-bad") = turned to hurt or distress. I can't tell whose bad feeling it is: the leader's or the narrator's.
- **Avu ZUvo u BOsi ZUvo** is the hardest bit. Degree words go *before* the word they modify, so **u** should modify **BOsi** ("made bigger the more-fear big"). That makes little sense, so I read it as "made the great fear bigger", taking **ZUvo u** as "bigger". The reference doesn't allow **u** after the word, so the two rules conflict here.
- **yi LInu SUyu on FOpu DImin vi** ("at the other place of some glass wall") = on the other side of a glass wall.
- **mu POkun tan gen Ozi on e** ("which kept it from my knowing"): **tan** = the fear, as "it" again. The relative clause ends the sentence, as principle 4 requires.

### B21. NIte GOni, e GAka ENmo, e na Ozi yo e Avu mi, fen e YEba men yo e Avu OPun vun.
**Gloss:** person lead, I say calm, I NOT know THAT.comp I do WH, BUT I apologize FOR THAT.comp I do angry you.
**Reading:** "Leader," I said calmly, "I don't know what I did, but I'm sorry I made you angry."
**Notes:**
- **e Avu mi** inside a **yo** clause is an indirect question (rule 5).
- **Avu OPun vun** = "make you angry" (the principle-4 example).
- **ENmo** ("calm") is used as an adverb.

### B22. vun na Avu OPun e, lu GAka u ENmo.
**Gloss:** you NOT do angry I, HE/SHE/IT say MORE calm.
**Reading:** "You didn't make me angry," he said, more calmly.

### B23. vun Avu OPun NIte mu BUfo vun BOsi ZUvo nun.
**Gloss:** you do angry person WHO.rel teach you fear big THAT.dem.
**Reading:** "You made angry the one who taught you that great fear."
**Notes:**
- Grammatically clear. The reference doesn't document the order of a verb with two objects (teach *you* *the fear*), so I assumed recipient, then thing.
- Alternative: "the one who taught you to be so afraid".
- I can't tell who "the one" is. From B27–28 it may be her parents, but **NIte** has no plural.

### B24. e OLe TINfo.
**Gloss:** I become ice.
**Reading:** I froze.

### B25. BOsi ZUvo MAwa MOfe, zan LEvu on e NOnu FOgo mi FEzi gi DIfo.
**Gloss:** fear big change bad, WHILE body OF I try understand WH CURSE ONGOING happen.
**Reading:** The great fear turned ugly, while my body tried to understand what the hell was happening.
**Notes:**
- **mi FEzi** = "what the hell", my guess at how FEzi works after a question word.
- Rule 5 says an embedded question uses a **yo** clause, and there's no **yo** here. Strictly, this reads as a direct question ("my body tried to understand what?"). I assumed **yo** was dropped.
- **MAwa MOfe** ("change bad") could be "got worse" or "turned sour".

### B26. vun na ZIzi la TAde gen Ibo, NIte GOni GAka.
**Gloss:** you NOT want BE child FROM now, person lead say.
**Reading:** "From now on you don't want to be a child anymore," the leader said.
**Notes:**
- **la TAde**: there's no subject, and rule 7 says **la** without a subject means "there is". Strictly, that gives "you don't want there to be a child from now on". I chose "be a child" as the obvious intent.
- Want + verb with no **yo** isn't documented. B4 uses **ZIzi yo**.
- **na** comes right before **ZIzi**, so it negates only "want". The line might be stern advice ("you mustn't want to be a child any more"), but it has no **to** or **gon**.

### B27. win va, gon GInne KOnu gu VUge on vun.
**Gloss:** BECAUSE THIS, INSTRUCT throw mother AND father OF you.
**Reading:** "So cast off your mother and father."
**Notes:**
- **win va, gon …** = "so, do …", from the rule-1 example.
- **GInne** = "throw". I read it figuratively ("throw away", abandon). Literally it would be "throw your mother and father".
- I assumed **on vun** covers both nouns.

### B28. tan in BUfo vun, yun?
**Gloss:** OBVIATIVE PLURAL teach you, Q?
**Reading:** "They're the ones who taught you, aren't they?"
**Notes:**
- A clause-final **yun** is a tag question.
- **tan in** = others, not the leader. I took it to mean the parents, linking back to "the one who taught you that great fear" in B23.

## Problems with the reference

1. **Parts of speech are undocumented.** The lexicon has no word-class column, yet the texts constantly use one word as another:
   - verbs as nouns: YEba "an apology", VOko OPun "a glare", NUvu in "feelings", Ozi "knowledge";
   - adjectives or mind words as nouns: ENmo "his calm", BOsi "fear";
   - adjectives as adverbs: GAka ENmo "said calmly", Ime PAsa "came fast".

   Principle 2 implies a word's role comes from its position, but the reference never says so directly.
2. **Verb + verb sequences have two meanings.** Principle 2 says the second verb modifies the first (KApo Ife "sit thinking"). The texts also need:
   - complement readings: NOnu LANpu "try to break", YAnu Avu "must do", DUda Ozi "need to know", ZIzi la TAde "want to be";
   - a causative reading: Avu X Y.

   **Avu** is also both plain "do" and causative "make", so "Avu Itu va" can be "make this good" or "do this well". Nothing says how to tell these apart.
3. **The copula is under-specified.** Rule 7 covers **la** + adjective, **la** + verb and subjectless **la**. It doesn't cover:
   - **la** + noun: "la na TAde DUli", "ZIzi la TAde";
   - **la** + yo-clause: "PEpa … la yo …";
   - nouns used as predicates without **la**: "vun NIte BOsi".

   "No subject = there is" also clashes with embedded clauses whose subject is left out (B26).
4. **FEzi (CURSE, field "expressive") has no usage rules.** The texts use it as a verb with an object (A3), as a modifier after a noun (A4), and after **mi** ("what the hell", B25). That's three different syntaxes, all guessed.
5. **Prepositions are unclear in several ways:**
   - **tun**, **men** and **fi** take whole clauses (A4, B2, A2), and **fi** appears with no object at all (A2 "Ozi fi yo"). Only **zan** and **yo** are documented as clause-takers.
   - Rule 8 says PPs attach "to their clause's main verb", but A4 seems to need **men BIve** to attach to the noun PEpa.
   - PPs and time words come before the subject (B8 "yi DOye bon…", B3 "gu Ibo vun…"). That seems to clash with "nothing moves", and no rule says where time adverbs go.
6. **Position of degree words.** The rule puts **u** before the word it modifies. B20 "Avu ZUvo u BOsi ZUvo" only makes sense if **u** follows ZUvo ("bigger").
7. **Embedded questions without yo.** B25 "FOgo mi …" has no **yo**, but rule 5 only mentions **mi** inside **yo** clauses.
8. **lu/tan for "it".** Rule 6 says lu/tan "separates she from it", but not which gets which. In B12 and B20 I had to guess whether **tan** meant the leader or "it".
9. **Dropped subjects.** Rule 11 says the subject repeats after **gu**. In B18 that gives an odd reading ("feelings … held others' things far away"). It's unclear whether the subject of the colon's head clause or the narrator can be the one dropped.
10. **No order for two objects.** The order with verbs like teach or give ("BUfo vun BOsi ZUvo") isn't given.
11. **Compounds aren't listed and their direction is ambiguous.** Examples: NIte TAlan "alien", TAde DUli "girl/daughter", NIte GOni "leader", PIbi YEta "moon-head?", VOnnu NOwi "fire-god?", LInu DANyu "bedroom". Several key readings ("alien", "step zero", "meditate", "little knowledge") were only recoverable because the exact phrase appears as an example in language.md. A reader without those examples would have read "sky person entering" and "way zero".
12. **Missing lexicon senses.** There's no direction word "backward" (LOve is only the body part). "right" (direction) could be confused with "correct". "light" and "light.weight" are split, but "back" isn't.
13. **Broken links.** language.md links to design-log.md, ../research/ and ../data/lexicon.tsv, none of which the reader has. The lexicon's own count is consistent: 279 roots + 13 numbers.
14. **Minor.** The self-interruption "e—" looks the same as the pronoun "I". Narrative tense is unmarked, so past versus present is a guess throughout Text B. Who says each line of dialogue is often unmarked (B3, B6), since direct quotes need a say-verb frame, and several lines have none.
