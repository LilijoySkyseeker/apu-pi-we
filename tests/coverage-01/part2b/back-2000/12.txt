People in Finland pay by phone to travel on underground trains and street trains

Now, three thousand people who live in Helsinki pay by phone to travel on underground trains and street trains.
According to the news from RIA Novosti, this innovation was introduced in December 2001, and passengers quickly came to like it.

In the opinion of Kirsi Niemelä, who is a representative of public transport in Helsinki, this innovation succeeded not only because paying by credit is better than paying with money, but also because it happened while the euro was being introduced in Finland.
This lets people who buy a ticket by phone not have to look for coins, because the ticket's 1.4 euros is added automatically to the phone bill.

In the inspectors' opinion, this innovation is still not easy.
They explain that most passengers pay, that is, send the travel message, after getting on the train or the street train, but in the inspectors' opinion, you need to do this before getting on.
