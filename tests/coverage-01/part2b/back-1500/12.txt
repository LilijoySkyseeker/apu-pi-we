People in Finland pay by phone to ride underground trains and street trains

Now, 3,000 people who live in Helsinki pay by phone to ride underground trains and street trains.
According to a RIA Novosti report, this new method was introduced in December 2001, and passengers quickly came to like it.

In the opinion of Kirsi Niemelä, who is a representative of public transport in Helsinki, this new method is a success not only because paying on credit is better than paying with cash, but also because it happened while the euro was being introduced in Finland.
It means that people who buy a ticket by phone don't have to look for coins, because the ticket's 1.4 euros are added to the phone bill automatically.

In the opinion of the ticket inspectors, this new method still isn't easy.
They explain that most passengers pay, that is, send the travel message, after getting on the underground train or street train, but in the inspectors' opinion, you need to do it before getting on.
