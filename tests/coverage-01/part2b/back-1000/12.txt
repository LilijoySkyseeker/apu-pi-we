People in Finland pay by phone to ride the underground and trams

Now 3,000 people who live in Helsinki pay by phone to ride the underground and trams.
According to RIA Novosti news, this new method started in December 2001, and travellers quickly came to like it.

In the opinion of Kirsi Niemelä, who speaks for public transport in Helsinki, this new method works well not only because paying afterwards is better than paying with cash, but also because it came about while the euro was starting in Finland.
It allows people who buy tickets by phone not to have to look for small change, because the 1.4 euros for the ticket is added to the phone account by itself.

In the opinion of the ticket inspectors, this new method is not yet easy.
They explain that many travellers pay, that is, send the ticket message, after getting on the train or tram, but in the inspectors' opinion, people should do this before getting on.
