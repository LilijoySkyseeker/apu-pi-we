People in Finland pay for rides on the underground train and the tram with their phones

Now 3 000 people who live in Helsinki pay for rides on the underground train and the tram with their phones.
According to RIA Novosti, this new thing started in December 2001, and quickly became popular with passengers.

According to Kirsi Niemelä, who represents Helsinki's public transport department, the success of the new thing can be explained not only because credit is better than paper money for paying, but also because the new thing came at the time when Finland started using the euro.
So people who buy a ticket with their phone don't need to look for small coins, because the price of the ticket, which is 1.4 euros, is added to the phone bill by itself.

For the ticket inspectors, the new thing is still not easy.
They explain that most passengers pay (meaning they send a ride message) after getting on the train or tram, but for the ticket inspectors, people need to do that before getting on.
