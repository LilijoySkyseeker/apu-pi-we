People in Finland pay for metro and tram trips by phone

Now 3,000 people who live in Helsinki regularly pay for metro and tram trips with their phones.
According to RIA Novosti, this new method was started in December 2001, and travellers quickly took to it.

According to Kirsi Niemelä, who speaks for the group that runs public transport in Helsinki, the new method succeeded not only because paying from an account is better than paying with paper money, but also because it started at the same time as Finland was starting to use the euro.
So a person who buys a ticket by phone doesn't need to look for small change, because a ticket is 1.4 euros, and the system itself adds it to the phone bill.

According to the inspectors, the new method is still not easy.
They say that more than half of travellers pay (send a text message for the trip) after they get on the metro or tram, but according to the inspectors, you should do that before getting on.
