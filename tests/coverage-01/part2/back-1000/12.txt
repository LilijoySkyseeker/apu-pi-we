People in Finland pay for travel on the metro and trams with their phones

Now 3,000 people who live in Helsinki pay for travel on the metro and trams with their phones.
According to RIA Novosti news, this new thing was introduced in December 2001, and suddenly became popular among passengers.

In the opinion of Kirsi Niemelä, who is a representative of the Helsinki public transport office, the new thing's success can be explained not only because paying afterwards is better than paying with paper money, but also because the new thing came at the same time as the introduction of the euro in Finland.
This makes it possible for people who buy tickets with a phone not to have to look for small coins, because the computer adds the price of the ticket (1.4 euros) to the phone bill.

In the opinion of the ticket inspectors, the new thing isn't easy yet.
They explain that most passengers pay (meaning that they send a message about the trip) after getting on the metro or tram, but in the inspectors' opinion, one should do this before getting on.
