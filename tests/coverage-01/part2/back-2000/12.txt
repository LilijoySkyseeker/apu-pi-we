People in Finland pay for metro and tram rides by phone

Now 3,000 residents of Helsinki pay for metro and tram rides by phone.
According to RIA Novosti, this innovation started in December 2001, and quickly became popular among passengers.

According to representative Kirsi Niemelä, who works for Helsinki's public transport department, the innovation's success can be explained not only by paying on credit being better than paying with paper money, but also by the innovation having started while Finland was just starting to use the euro.
So a person who buys a ticket by phone doesn't need to hunt for small coins, because a ticket costs 1.4 euros, and it is added to the phone bill automatically.

For ticket inspectors, the innovation is still not convenient.
They explain that most passengers pay (that is, send a message for the ride) after getting on the metro or the tram, but in the inspectors' view, people should do that before getting on.
