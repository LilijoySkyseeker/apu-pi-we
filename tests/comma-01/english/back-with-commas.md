# Back-translation

A: You won't believe this. Somebody towed my car. From our own parking lot!
B: Wait. The building's lot? Why would they tow you from there?
A: That's what I said! This morning at 7:15 I went downstairs. I was already running late. There was just an empty spot. An oil stain and nothing else.
C: Did you have a permit on the car?
A: I do, I have one. But it's the old permit. The new permits probably came out in September. But mine is still good until the end of this month. I checked.
B: Wait. So you didn't put the new permit on your car.
A: No, I didn't. But that's not the point. The point is the old permit is still valid. It says October! In big numbers.
C: Who towed your car? What company?
A: Some place way out by the highway. Rex Recovery or something like that. I had to call the number on the screen. And the guy on the phone said, "The parking lot manager requested the tow." That's all he said. Like he was reading off a menu.
B: Did you call the manager, Dennis?
A: Of course I called Dennis! He didn't answer. I sent him two messages. Then I took an Uber out to that place. Paid thirty-one dollars for it. And the tow people wanted two hundred eighty dollars to give me my car back.
C: Two hundred eighty? That's awful.
A: Two eighty, cash or card, and it's more if you pay by card. And that guy kept telling me it was my own fault, because there'd been an email. What email? Nobody sent me an email.
B: But management did send something. I got one. It said you had to switch permits before the start of the month.
A: Mm. But did it say they'd tow people? Because if all it says is "Please update your permit," that's not the same as "We will tow your car."
B: I think it said something like "Vehicles without a new permit may be towed." Something along those lines.
A: "May be." See? "May be" isn't a warning. That's lawyer talk. You read that and go, "Mm, I'll do it at the end of the week." And the old permit still says October! How was I supposed to know they meant the start of the month?
C: Mm, I get it. That is confusing. But I don't know if you'll win. They'll just show the email.
A: I'm not trying to win in court. I'm trying to get my money back. I want Dennis to admit it was a problem. And I want him to pay for the tow, too. Half the lot probably hasn't switched yet. Did they tow anyone else?
B: They did. I think the Hendersons' big car is gone too. I saw Mr. Henderson outside. He was yelling into his phone.
A: See? So it's not just me being dumb. If two of us got towed in one night, the email didn't work. That's on them, not us.
C: Mm, that's a pretty good point. Maybe take the Hendersons with you. If one person reports a problem, Dennis doesn't listen. If it's three or four, he has to do something.
A: I'll do that. And I'll keep the receipt for the tow, and the Uber one too. Thirty-one dollars. He's paying for that Uber!

## Unsure

- **B: "min me yebin no wungu, en danisu."** By the grammar, a name at the end of a sentence after a comma is someone being addressed, so strictly this reads "Did you call the manager, Dennis?" with B calling A "Dennis". But A answers "I did call Dennis... he didn't answer", so Dennis must be the manager, as an afterthought or appositive (the grammar would say "no wungu en danisu", title + name). I took the context reading. The English keeps the same ambiguity.
- **A: "mon oma fi te"** is three bare digits. 24-hour time should be four digits (07 15), and 12-hour time would be counted ("oma yawu te"). I read it as 7:15.
- **A: "to sin gun wo muyi"**, literally "I was after time". I took it as "I was late / running late".
- **A: "gan wifu kudu"**, literally "with a big number". I read it as the month printed large on the permit ("in big numbers"). It could also mean "with a big number on it".
- **A: "to keya yebin wifu man gun mon du wuye"**: "du wuye" is "screen", so it's the number on a screen. I'd have expected a sign at the lot, so the setting is a guess.
- **"en likusu likabali"** is an adapted name. I guessed "Rex/Rick's Recovery" (r→l, v→b). **"ubalu"** = Uber, **"kolutu"** = court, **"en danisu"** = Dennis, **"en endalusan"** = Henderson (h dropped) or possibly Anderson. **"[oil]"** was left in English in the source.
- **A: "to keya lugo fu yu wun wan la yofu tuko pi dunan."** There's no "min", so it's a statement, literally "I'm supposed to know somehow that they meant the start of the month". I rendered it as the natural sarcastic rhetorical question.
- **A: "un min bu oyi wun sun la nunya ewo la."** "sun la" (logophoric) = the email's authors, the management. "nunya ewo la" (pull people) is read as towing people's cars.
- **C: "ben gun gofa li lafi"**: "that's hard to understand" could mean the email wording or A's situation. I took it as the email ("That is confusing").
- **"lowu en endalusan"**: "man" before a name, read as "Mr. Henderson". It isn't the respect title "lanno".
- **A (last line): "ba wan unfu li ubalu ben"** has no tense marker. I read it as "He's paying (will pay) for that Uber!", meaning Dennis.
