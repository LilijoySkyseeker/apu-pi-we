# Translation

A: You're not going to believe this. Someone towed my car. From our own parking lot!
B: Wait. The lot at your building? Why did they tow you from there?
A: That's exactly what I said! I went down at 7:15 this morning, already running late, and there was just an empty space. An oil stain and nothing else.
C: Did you have a permit on the car?
A: Yes, I have one. But it's the old permit. The new permits probably came out in September. But mine is still valid until the end of this month. I checked.
B: Wait, so you didn't put the new permit in the car?
A: No, I didn't. But that's not what matters. What matters is that the old permit is still valid. It says October! In big numbers!
C: Who towed your car? What company?
A: Some place way out by the highway. Rick's Recovery or something like that. I had to call the number on the sign, and the guy who answered the phone said, "The lot manager requested the tow." That's all he said. Like he was reading off a menu.
B: Did you call Dennis, the manager?
A: Of course I called Dennis! He didn't answer. I sent him two messages. Then I took an Uber out there, which cost me $31. And the towing people wanted $280 to give me my car back.
C: $280? That's awful!
A: $280 cash or card, and card costs extra. And that guy kept telling me it was my own fault because there had been an email. What email? Nobody sent me any email.
B: But the management did send something out. I got one. It said everyone had to switch permits before the start of the month.
A: Mm. But did it say they'd tow people? Because if all it said was "please renew your permit," that's not the same as "we will take your car."
B: I think it said something like "vehicles without a new permit may be towed."
A: "May be." See? "May be" isn't a warning. That's lawyer language. You read that and think, "Mm, I'll do it at the end of the week." And the old permit still says October! How was I supposed to know they meant the start of the month?
C: Mm, I get it. That is confusing. But I don't know if you'd win. They'll just point to the email.
A: I'm not trying to win in court. I'm trying to get my money back. I want Dennis to admit that this was a problem. I also want him to pay the towing fee. Half the lot probably hasn't switched yet. Did they tow anyone else?
B: They did. I think the Hendersons' big car is gone too. I saw Mr. Henderson outside, yelling into his phone.
A: See? So it's not just me being stupid. If two of us got towed in one night, the email didn't work. That's their fault, not ours.
C: Mm, that's a pretty good idea. Maybe get the Hendersons to go with you. If one person reports a problem, Dennis doesn't listen. If it's three or four, he has to do something.
A: I'll do that. And I'll keep the receipt for the tow, and the Uber one too. $31. He's paying for that Uber!

# Lines I was unsure about

- A, line 3, "ba to sin oyi tan" ("I said this!"): read as "That's exactly what I said!", agreeing with B's indignant "why?". It could also be "That's what I asked them!"
- A, line 3, "mon oma fi te": three digits, so it isn't the four-digit 24-hour style (that would be "po oma fi te"), and it isn't counted 12-hour style either. Read as 7:15.
- A, line 3, "to sin gun wo muyi" (literally "I was after time"): read as "I was running late". The lexicon's word for "late" is yodun, so this might mean something else, such as "I got there a while after".
- A, line 9, "du wuye": compounds.tsv gives "screen", but a phone number on a screen in a parking lot is odd. Translated as "sign" (literally "thing for showing").
- A, line 9, "en likusu likabali": an adapted name; my guess is "Rick's Recovery". "fu wan le lilu mabo pi pasu" is literally "like he was reading a food paper (menu)". Read as reading from a script.
- A, line 5, "kon da du efon duwa la obu mon dunan pi mape": "came in month 9" is read as "came out in September", with "probably".
- B, line 16, "kon bo" inside the quote (literally "unknown how sure"): rendered as "may be", which is what A then picks apart.
- B, line 20, "en endalusan": read as Henderson (h dropped, r becomes l). "tale kudu" (big car) might be an SUV or truck. "gun kusa" (is left) is read as "is gone".
- C, line 22, "don ewo fi iko batu en danisu mu doma": there's no comma, so it could also read "tell Dennis's problem" or "tell Dennis a problem". I read it as "if one person reports a problem, Dennis doesn't listen". The two-object rule (recipient first) would want "iko en danisu batu".
- C, line 22, "ben gun gason tason upe" ("that's a rather good idea"): it follows A's point rather than a proposal, so it's slightly odd. It seems to look ahead to C's own suggestion, or to A's plan to push back.
- A, line 17, "to keya lugo fu yu …" (literally "I must know how …"): read as the rhetorical "How was I supposed to know …?".
- "ubalu" (Uber), "kolutu" (court) and "[oil]" are not in the lexicon. They are read as an adapted brand, an adapted loan and an English placeholder (the lexicon has yunen for oil).
