# Back-translation

A: Hi! Are you all still coming on Saturday?
B: Probably, yeah. What time was it again?
A: Around 7 pm. Maybe 7:30. I have to clean first.
C: Wait, is it at your place or at Malukusu's?
A: My place. Malukusu can't do it. His landlord is doing something with the water pipes.
C: Ugh, that's bad! Again? Didn't that happen a month ago too?
A: Same thing happened. Water flooded his kitchen. It's a mess.
B: He really needs to move out at this point.
A: I know. I told him that. He says he can't find a cheaper rental.
C: True. Rents are crazy high right now!
B: So do I need to bring anything?
A: Maybe chips or something? Or drinks. Anything's fine, it doesn't matter.
C: I can bring the dip I made for Desu's birthday.
B: The spinach one? Oh please bring it! It's so good!
C: Will do. But I have to remember to buy the stuff to make it.
A: Is Desu coming? I messaged her but she hasn't answered yet.
B: She's probably working Saturday. She said maybe afterwards, around 10 pm.
A: That's fine. We'll probably still be up.
C: Wait, do I need to park on the street, or can I park in your parking spot?
A: The street's fine. Just don't block the neighbour's stuff, he gets really angry.
B: He yelled at me once.
C: I won't block anything. See you all Saturday!

## Lines I was unsure about

- **Lines 4–5, 13, 16 ("en malukusu", "en desu"):** names in adapted form. Malukusu is probably "Marcus" or "Malcolm"; Desu could be Des, Dez or Tess. I kept the forms as written. "wan" for Desu is gender-neutral; I chose "her" arbitrarily.
- **A, line 3 ("fu fi mape po po / kon ka fi mape ka po"):** 24-hour digit reading, so 19:00 and 19:30; rendered as 7 / 7:30 pm.
- **B, line 8 ("eda wan wupi kusa"):** literally "now he needs to leave". Could be "he needs to move out at this point" (my reading) or "he needs to get out of there right now".
- **A, line 9 ("guyi pi sanwa ke meli"):** literally "house of rent smaller". Read as a cheaper rental (smaller rent); could also be read as "a smaller rental place".
- **C, line 10 ("eda ba sanwa gun bi kudu"):** **ba** after the time word; read as an exclamation about rent being very big (high).
- **A, line 12:** "musu bo gun upe" (literally "something is good") read as "anything's fine"; "tan mu gun bawe" as "it doesn't matter".
- **B, line 14 ("min gan [spinach]"):** literally "with spinach?" Read as "the spinach one?" "bu gun bi upe" is unmarked for tense: "it's so good" or "it was so good".
- **C, line 15 ("musu li siten bu"):** "things for cooking it" = the ingredients.
- **A, line 20 ("tin mu tapi musu pi no nume gelu"):** literally "don't close/shut the neighbour's thing(s)". "tapi" is "close, shut"; I read it as "block" (e.g. their driveway or car), given the parking context. Uncertain what exactly is meant.
- **B, line 21 ("ya muyi fi"):** **muyi** is time as duration, not an occasion (that's **gimu**), so this is literally "before one time-span" = "a while ago". I rendered it as "once", which is probably the intent, but by the grammar "He yelled at me a while back" is the stricter reading. "wan" read as the neighbour, the current topic.
- **C, line 22 ("de mu tapi"):** "I'll not close/block (it)"; object left out, filled as "anything".
