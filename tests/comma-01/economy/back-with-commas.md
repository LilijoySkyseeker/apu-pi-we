# Back-translation

A: Hi! Are you all still coming on Saturday?
B: Probably, yeah. What time was it again?
A: Around seven. Maybe seven thirty. I have to clean up first.
C: Wait, is this at your place or at Marcus's?
A: My place. Marcus can't do it. His landlord is doing something with the water pipes.
C: Ugh, that's bad. Again? That happened a month ago too, didn't it?
A: Yeah, same thing. Water flooded his kitchen. It's bad.
B: At this point he needs to move out.
A: I know. I told him that. He says he can't find a place with cheaper rent.
C: True. Rent is so high right now!
B: So, do I need to bring anything?
A: Maybe chips or something. Or drinks. Anything's fine, it's not a big deal.
C: I can bring the dip I made for Des's birthday.
B: The one with spinach? Oh, please bring it. That was so good!
C: Will do. But I have to remember to buy the stuff to make it.
A: Is Des coming? I messaged them, but they haven't answered yet.
B: They're probably working on Saturday. They said maybe afterwards, around 10 pm.
A: That's fine. We'll probably still be up.
C: Wait, do I need to park on the street, or can I park in your parking spot?
A: The street's fine. Just don't block the neighbour's driveway. He gets really angry.
B: He yelled at me once.
C: I won't block it. See you all Saturday.

## Unsure

- **Names (lines 4, 13).** **en malukusu** is an adapted form, so I took it back to Marcus/Markus (r → l plus an added u). **en desu** could be Des, Dez or Desi, or the person's own spelling "Desu". Des's gender isn't given, so I used "they".
- **Line 9, "guyi pi sanwa ke meli".** By the heavy-last rule, **ke meli** "smaller/less" modifies **sanwa** "rent", which gives "lower rent". It could also be read as "a smaller rental". Line 10, about rent being high, supports the first reading.
- **Line 20, "tapi musu pi no nume gelu".** Literally "shut the neighbour's thing". I filled in "block his driveway" from the parking context. The repeat in line 22 ("won't shut [it]") fits that reading.
- **Line 21, "ya muyi fi".** **muyi** is a duration now (occasions use **gimu**), so the strict reading is "one time-span ago", meaning "a while back". I went with the natural "once", which reads like the older use of **muyi** for occasions.
- **Line 5, "du dinyo osa".** Literally "thing for carrying water". I read it as pipes or plumbing.
- **Line 7, "osa muge kete pu siten".** "Water made the kitchen full": I read that as flooding.
- **Line 13, "siten mon [birthday]".** "Cooked at the birthday": I rendered it as made for Des's birthday.
