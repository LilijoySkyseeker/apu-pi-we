# Back-translation of /tmp/bt2/source.txt

Sources used: only language.md, grammar.tsv, lexicon.tsv and compounds.tsv in /tmp/bt2/.

Names given in the source header: MAla, TOmasa, BEne. None of them is marked for gender, so where English needs a pronoun I use "he" for Tomasa (he/she is equally possible) and otherwise use names.

**Who is speaking.** Most quoted lines are not attributed. My working assumption is that this is a two-person conversation between Mala and Bene: Mala asks "you" in S1, and the person who is then asked to phone (S9) and does phone (S10) is Bene. My turn assignment:

- Mala: S1, S3, S6, S8, S9, S13
- Bene: S2, S4, S5, S7, S12, S14
- S15 is Mala again, because the line is introduced with "MAla BUbo".

The line-by-line meaning doesn't depend much on who says S3 and S4.

---

**S1.** "pin me YIdu YUfa NImo pi lon?" MAla MIdo.

1. **Gloss:** Q you take bag money OF I? Mala ask.
2. **Reading:** "Did you take my money bag?" Mala asked.
3. **Notes:** I read YUfa NImo ("bag money", noun modifying noun) as "money bag". It isn't in compounds.tsv. "pi lon" goes with the whole phrase ("my money bag"), following "heavy goes last". No narrative tense is ever set (no **afe** and no **sin** on a first verb), so by the rules the time is "present or unspecified". I translate the narration in the past because that's the normal storytelling reading.

**S2.** "lon mu YIdu yi. TOmasa YIdu yi."

1. **Gloss:** I NOT take OBV. Tomasa take OBV.
2. **Reading:** (Bene:) "I didn't take it. Tomasa took it."
3. **Notes:** **yi** = the bag. It is a thing, and **yi** covers "any other third person or thing". It is unclear whether the rule's condition, "once a second third person is in play", is met at this point (only Mala and a thing have appeared so far). It is also unclear whether a thing could have been **wan** here. The meaning is clear anyway.

**S3.** "TOmasa gun mon GUyi YEsa nin DIli MINwe."

1. **Gloss:** Tomasa BE AT house learn DURING day whole.
2. **Reading:** (Mala:) "Tomasa was at school all day."
3. **Notes:** I read GUyi YEsa ("house learn", with the verb modifying the noun) as "learning-house" = school. It isn't in compounds.tsv. An alternative is "at home, studying, all day". I rejected it because in S4 Tomasa *comes* home at three. **nin** + noun phrase = "during", so **nin DIli MINwe** = "the whole day". This could also be Bene continuing to talk; the meaning is the same.

**S4.** "wan Obu tun GUyi mon KOse, yen Oyi wun sun WUpi NImo li SIwa KUdu."

1. **Gloss:** HE/SHE come TO house AT three, AND say THAT LOGOPHOR need money FOR cart big.
2. **Reading:** (Bene:) "He came home at three and said he needed money for a car."
3. **Notes:** **wan** = Tomasa, the most recent subject (S3). The subject of **Oyi** is dropped after **yen**, so it is Tomasa again. **sun** = Tomasa, the person whose words are reported. **SIwa KUdu** literally means "big cart". I take it as "car" because the lexicon has no word for car, and the reference says modern concepts are missing. **mon KOse** = "at three", which matches the reference's own example. I read it as three o'clock.

**S5.** "gun NImo WIfu yu mon yi?"

1. **Gloss:** there.is money number WH AT OBV?
2. **Reading:** (Bene:) "How much money was in it?"
3. **Notes:** **gun** at the start of a sentence with no subject = "there is". I read **NImo WIfu yu** ("money, which number") as "how much money". The reference never says how to ask "how much", so I built that from **yu** "which?". **yi** = the bag again. It can't be Tomasa: he is the most recent subject, so he would be **wan**. There is no **pin**, but the reference's own wh-question example has none either. Bene is asking because Mala, the owner, is the one who answers in S6.

**S6.** "lon PUme: DOla FI LUbu TI, yen ga NO MAbo SIwa KUdu pi lon."

1. **Gloss:** I think: DOla 4 10 2, AND CONF 8 paper cart big OF I.
2. **Reading:** (Mala:) "I think: forty-two dollars, and probably (80%) my car papers too."
3. **Notes:** **DOla** isn't in any file and isn't listed as a name. I take it as the loanword "dollar(s)". After a counted noun, FI and TI are short number forms; LUbu (10) has only a long form. So the number is 4·10+2 = 42. **ga NO** = 8 in 10. It sits before the noun phrase, so it covers only "my car papers": she is sure about the money and 80% sure the papers were in there. **MAbo SIwa KUdu** = "big-cart paper", which I read as car papers (registration or title).

**S7.** "pin mu me Udin BEya pi me?"

1. **Gloss:** Q NOT you lock door OF you?
2. **Reading:** (Bene:) "Didn't you lock your door?"
3. **Notes:** **mu** starts the clause, so it negates the whole clause.

**S8.** "mu Udin. lon YAkin."

1. **Gloss:** NOT lock. I forget.
2. **Reading:** (Mala:) "No, I didn't. I forgot."
3. **Notes:** This is an echo answer to S7, as rule 4 describes.

**S9.** "so ku YEbin wan. WIfu pi wan gun: Ome PUmu FIdi TIwe, DAsa NObi SAge, KOse Ome YAlu."

1. **Gloss:** REQUEST POLITE call HE/SHE. number OF HE/SHE BE: 0 1 4 2, 7 8 5, 3 0 9.
2. **Reading:** (Mala, to Bene:) "Please call him. His number is 0142 785 309."
3. **Notes:** **wan** = Tomasa. The last third-person subject was Tomasa in S4; S5 to S8 have only *I*, *you*, or no subject. The digit string correctly uses long forms. The request has a dropped subject, which is allowed for the addressee of a request. It's possible Bene says this to Mala instead, but then it would be odd that Bene is the one who calls in S10.

**S10.** BEne YEbin. TOmasa FEbo mon YEbin pi TIwe.

1. **Gloss:** Bene call. Tomasa answer AT call OF 2.
2. **Reading:** Bene called. Tomasa picked up on the second try.
3. **Notes:** **pi** + long number = order, so "call number two" = the second call (or ring). This is the first time narration and quoted speech mix in the story, which matters for the pronouns from here on.

**S11.** TOmasa Iko BEne wun sun SIgo NImo mon WUma wo PUmu.

1. **Gloss:** Tomasa tell Bene THAT LOGOPHOR return money AT tomorrow AFTER 1.
2. **Reading:** Tomasa told Bene that he would bring the money back tomorrow after one o'clock.
3. **Notes:** **sun** = Tomasa (his words are reported). **WUma wo PUmu** could also be read as "anchor + offset", meaning "one after tomorrow" = the day after tomorrow, since rule 9 says time words name "an anchor plus an offset". I chose the clock-time reading because S4 uses **mon KOse** for a clock time. The reference doesn't explain clock times or how **wo** + number works.

**S12.** "wan mu YIdu NImo," BEne Oyi. "yi YIdu MAbo."

1. **Gloss:** HE/SHE NOT take money, Bene say. OBV take paper.
2. **Reading:** "He [Tomasa] didn't take the money," Bene said. "He [Tomasa] took the papers."
3. **Notes:** This was the hardest line.
   - **First wan:** the most recent subject before it is Tomasa (S11, both **TOmasa Iko** and **sun SIgo**), so **wan** = Tomasa.
   - **The yi after "BEne Oyi":** the narration clause makes Bene the most recent subject. Applied mechanically, **wan** would now mean Bene, so every other third person, Tomasa included, has to be **yi**. That is how I explain the switch from **wan** to **yi** for the same person. Bene can't be **yi** because he is the speaker (**lon**).
   - **Alternative:** if narration frames don't count for pronouns inside quotes, **yi** = "someone other than Tomasa" (an unknown third party). The text gives no candidate for that person.
   - **Contradiction:** with **wan** = Tomasa, "he didn't take the money" clashes with S11, where Tomasa promises to bring the money back. I couldn't make the two consistent. Maybe "return money" here means "bring/pay money" (for example, from selling something using the car papers), or maybe **wan** was meant for someone else. Following the rules, nobody else qualifies.

**S13.** "don wan Agi MAbo ben, de lon Iko WEde."

1. **Gloss:** IF HE/SHE lose paper THAT, INTEND I tell father.
2. **Reading:** (Mala:) "If he loses those papers, I'm telling Dad."
3. **Notes:** **wan** = the most recent subject, which is the **yi** of S12b. On my reading that is Tomasa. **de** (intend) comes in the middle of the sentence, after the if-clause. Rule 1 says only a linker to the previous sentence may come before the speech-act particle, so this order isn't covered by the rules. I treat **de** as marking the main clause as a stated intention. **WEde** has no possessive; I read it as "Dad" in the family sense, presumably Mala's and Tomasa's.

**S14.** "WEde sin LUgo. TOmasa YEbin wan do."

1. **Gloss:** father DONE know. Tomasa call HE/SHE BEFORE.
2. **Reading:** (Bene:) "Dad already knows. Tomasa called him first."
3. **Notes:** **wan** = Dad. A pronoun never means the subject of its own clause (Tomasa), so it means the subject before that, which is WEde. **do** on its own = "beforehand". **sin LUgo** is "done + know", i.e. "already knows". The reference doesn't say how aspect works with state verbs.

**S15.** MAla BUbo. "ba yi sin MUge. yi gun ke GOle se pen."

1. **Gloss:** Mala laugh. EXCLAIM OBV DONE do. OBV BE MORE smart FROM WE.incl.
2. **Reading:** Mala laughed. "Ha! He's already taken care of it. He's smarter than us."
3. **Notes:**
   - **Who yi is:** I chose Tomasa. "MAla BUbo" makes Mala the most recent subject, so inside her speech every other third person is **yi**, and Tomasa is the most salient one. The punchline also works: he'd already called Dad before anyone could tell on him. If narration frames don't count, the most recent subject is Tomasa (S14), so **yi** cannot be Tomasa. It would then most likely be Dad: "Dad has already handled it; he's smarter than us."
   - **"than":** **ke GOle se pen** = "more smart from us". I read **se** as "than", but comparison isn't documented anywhere.
   - **sin MUge** has no object, so I read it as "has already done it / dealt with it".
   - **ba** is lowercase, so it's the exclaim particle and not the number 6 (**BA**).

## Problems with the reference

1. **Pronoun tracking across narration and quotes is undefined.** The reference says nothing about whether a narration clause ("Bene said", "Mala laughed") counts as "the most recent subject" for pronouns inside a quote. It also doesn't say whether the speaker's own name in the frame, or speech-act participants (**lon**/**me**), reset the tracking. This one gap decides the referent in S12b, S13 and S15, and it flips the meaning of S15 between Tomasa and Dad.
2. **yi is underdetermined.** "Any other third person or thing" gives no rule for choosing between several candidates (S12b: Tomasa or an unknown third party; S15: Tomasa or Dad). The rule is called "mechanical" and "obligatory", but it only fixes **wan**. Also undefined: whether things count toward "a second third person in play", and what **wan** means before that condition is met (S2).
3. **The S11/S12 contradiction.** Applying the rules mechanically gives "Tomasa will return the money" followed by "Tomasa didn't take the money". Either the rules gave a wrong referent, or SIgo + object has some meaning other than "give back". The reference doesn't say whether SIgo ("return", motion) can take an object.
4. **Missing words and constructions.**
   - **Words:** **DOla** is in no file and isn't declared as a name. There are no words for car, school, phone, papers/documents or "how much". I had to coin readings for GUyi YEsa, SIwa KUdu, MAbo SIwa KUdu and YUfa NImo, none of which are in compounds.tsv.
   - **Comparatives:** "than" is undocumented (**se** "from" in S15).
   - **Quantity questions:** "how much/many" is undocumented (**WIfu yu**).
   - **Clock times:** only **mon KOse** is shown. **wo PUmu** is ambiguous between "after one o'clock" and "one day after" (rule 9's anchor+offset).
5. **Speech-act particle placement.** Rule 1 allows only a linker to the previous sentence before the particle. S13 puts a whole **don** clause before **de**, and nothing says whether that's legal or what it means.
6. **Tense in narrative.** Rule 9 says a narrative sets its time once, but this one never does. It's unclear whether unmarked narration then means "present" or whether past may be assumed. The reference also doesn't explain how **sin** works with state verbs (**sin LUgo**).
7. **Homographs in plain writing.** The language is written in plain lowercase, but short numbers collide with grammar words: **o** (seen) / **O** (0), **ba** (exclaim) / **BA** (6), **no**, **sa** and others would only be told apart by stress, which plain writing doesn't show. The name **MAla** is also identical to **mala** "heart".
8. **Unattributed dialogue.** The language has no quote particle and no way to mark turn-taking, so with quotes in a row (S2–S9, S13–S14) the speaker must be guessed. This affects who **me** and **lon** are.
9. **Small inconsistencies.**
   - **Root count:** language.md says the lexicon has 284 roots, but lexicon.tsv has 289 non-number entries.
   - **Broken links:** language.md links to design-log.md, drift.md, ../research/ and data/rename-2026-10-08.tsv, none of which are in the test set.
   - **Locatives:** **mon** is only glossed "at", so "in (the bag)" in S5 is an inference.
