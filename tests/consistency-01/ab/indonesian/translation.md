## Notes on hard decisions

- **Register.** Indonesian marks formal vs casual (saya/Bu vs gue/lo). The language has no speech levels (10.2), so A and C get **nun** and **lanno** when writing to B, and nothing when writing to each other. "Bu", "Pak" and "Ibu"-as-"you" all lose their gender; "Ibu cek" / "ruangan Ibu" become **me**, because **lanno** never means "you".
- **Oke / Baik / Bagus** are kept apart: **mm** (heard, acknowledged), **upe** (fine, OK), **ben gun upe** "that's good".
- **Particles:** "ya" (softening) → **buni**; "dong ya" → **a, min**; "kok" (I *did* …) → focus **o** before the verb; "deh" → **buni**; "berarti" → source **wemen**; "aja" → **na**; the cleft "Dimas yang …" → **o en dimas**. "sih" and "nih" have no counterpart and are left out.
- **[X1]**: the lexicon has no word for it. Section 11 would allow a loan, since the world shares the form, but the lexicon doesn't list one and the adaptation tool was off-limits. So I used the marker instead of coining a word.
- **Interjections** "oh" and "wkwk" are kept as interjections (2: outside the sound system). For "Cieee", the teasing goes into **genso ba**.
- **Built phrases** for concepts the lexicon lacks: report **kemin pi koda** "document of selling"; data **wifu la** "numbers"; recap **fanmu** "gather"; presentation **ge wuye** "what is shown"; board of directors **no wungu la**; slides **lunyu la** "pages"; draft **kemin mu minwe** "document not whole"; chart **musu man wuye wifu la** "thing that shows the numbers"; target **wifu man pen keya wonku** "the number we must reach"; overtime **doku tason ke netu** "work a bit longer"; pray **mido nosen** "ask God"; scold **oyi bonko** "say angrily"; bestie **sonu sonu** (doubling strengthens); "semangat" as a wish **so me gun konke** "may you be strong".
- **"paling lambat Jumat jam 3"** → **mon … , lu ya** "at …, or before"; **mu ke se** "at most" is defined only for amounts (9.3).
- **"dibagi sama Dimas"** is read as "divided with Dimas" (**gan**); colloquial "sama" can also mark the agent ("by Dimas"). The subject is dropped to match the agentless passive.
- **"gudang lelet"** is translated by meaning as the warehouse team (**funti pi pu kuwi**), as in message 2.
- **Next month** → **wo dunan fi**, on the model of **wo nelon fi** "next week" (8.2).

## Rules that were ambiguous or that I had to guess

1. **Aspect in verb + verb** (4.2, 6.2): the order "operators, manni, aspect, verb" does not say where **sin** goes when it applies only to the second verb. I wrote **keya sin liwon** "must have arrived".
2. **Deadlines**: "by / no later than" a time has no rule. **mu ke se** "at most" (9.3) covers amounts only, so I used **mon X, lu ya**.
3. **Group address**: calling someone (10.1) lists names, titles, roles and kin words, not pronouns. It is unclear whether **me la we** "you all" can be called, so I used **upe leso tun me la we**. It is also unclear whether a greeting formula can take **tun**.
4. **Determiner inside a pi-compound**: "which folder" with the compound **nansa pi kemin**. The NP template puts the determiner before the pi phrase, so I wrote **nansa yu pi kemin**. That splits the listed compound, and **nansa pi kemin yu** would read as "folder of which documents".
5. **Naming a thing (a folder)**: there is no apposition (10.1), and titles are defined for people. I wrote **nansa pi kemin en laporan q3** by analogy with "title + name". It could also attach as **kemin en laporan q3** "the document Laporan Q3". It is also unclear whether file names keep their capitals and quotes (3: "pass through as written") or get **en** and lowercase (11).
6. **Pauses after derived-word subjects**: **pu kuwi** "warehouse" has a one-verb base, so it is unclear whether it "runs to the end of its phrase" (5.4) and needs a pause before the verb. I added one (**funti pi pu kuwi, gun gake**). In that position, **, gun …** could also be misread as clause-initial **gun** "there is" (4.5).
7. **Nested derived words**: in **ge wuye tun no wungu la mon dili pi fi**, it is unclear whether **mon dili pi fi** attaches to **wungu** (the inner base) or to **wuye**.
8. **genso before ba**: 7.1 says **genso** or **ba** may come before a particle, but **ba** is itself a particle. Whether **genso ba** is well formed is a guess.
9. **so with "you" as subject**: 7.1 says **so** with "another subject" is a wish, but not whether **me** counts as "another subject" (**so me gun konke**, **so me la doku konke**).
10. **Acknowledgments**: the language has no "OK". It is unclear whether **upe** (also the greeting) or **mm** (outside the grammar) is the right word for "Baik/Oke".
11. **Interjections**: 2 allows "any other interjection … speakers use", but not whether culture-specific written forms (wkwk, cieee) belong in the text or should be translated.
12. **Unlisted loans**: 11 says a loan enters the lexicon "once it is common". It is unclear whether a translator may introduce one, and in which adapted form, or must write a marker instead. I chose the marker for [X1].
13. **Relation phrase with a clause object, li + gun**: whether **li gun muyi …** "so that there is time …" is allowed. 4.4 allows a clause after a relation word, but **gun** with no subject is defined only at the start of a clause (4.5/4.7).
14. **Labels with letters**: whether **Q3** passes through as a letter label (3, 11), even though digits are never allowed inside a word.
