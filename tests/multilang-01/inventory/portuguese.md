# Portuguese: register inventory checked against Apu Pi We

Source language: Portuguese, Brazilian (BR) and European (PT), marked where they differ.
Apu Pi We (APW) checked against `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv` and `data/compounds.tsv` only.

**Status column:** obligatory = the grammar forces a choice; habitual = speakers in that register use it routinely; occasional = used, but not routinely.
**APW column:** yes = the meaning can be said with existing words and rules; partly = the core survives, something named is lost; no = no channel for it.

Gloss abbreviations: 1/2/3 person, SG/PL, M/F, PRS present, PST perfective past, IPFV imperfect, PLUP pluperfect, FUT future, COND conditional (*futuro do pretérito*), SBJV subjunctive, FUT.SBJV future subjunctive, INF infinitive, INF.3PL personal infinitive, GER gerund, PTCP participle, IMP imperative, DIM diminutive, AUG augmentative, REFL reflexive/*se*, DEF/INDF article, MED medial demonstrative.

Words not in the lexicon are flagged in the APW column. **kofi** (coffee) is used as an English loan under the borrowing rule.

## 1. Close and informal (friends, family)

| # | Distinction | Status | Example (original + gloss) | APW | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | T-form address: *tu* (PT; south and north BR) or *você* (most of BR) signals closeness/solidarity | obligatory (some form must be chosen) | *Tu vens amanhã?* "you come.PRS.2SG tomorrow" | partly | **min me obu wuma.** **me** is "you" to anyone. Closeness shows only as the absence of **nun** plus a first name; there is no positive marker of intimacy. |
| 1.2 | Subject drop, person recovered from the verb ending | habitual | *Cheguei.* "arrive.PST.1SG" | yes | **sin liwon.** Rule 11 drops whatever context supplies. Portuguese recovers the person from the ending even out of context; APW from context only. |
| 1.3 | *a gente* (informal "we", 3SG verb) vs *nós* | habitual (BR), common (PT) | *A gente vai à praia.* "the people go.3SG to.DEF beach" | partly | **pen isi tun puya.** ("sea"; no root for beach). The informal flavour of *a gente* is lost; APW adds an inclusive/exclusive split (**pen/din**) Portuguese lacks. |
| 1.4 | Plural "you": *vocês* (BR, PT), *vós* (northern PT, liturgy) | obligatory | *Vocês vêm?* "you.PL come.3PL" | yes | **min me la obu.** |
| 1.5 | Solidarity vocatives and endearments: *cara, mano, véi, mana* (BR); *pá, ó pá, meu* (PT); *amor, querida, meu bem, filho* | habitual | *Ó pá, anda cá!* "oh man, walk.IMP here" (PT) | partly | **en joan, tin obu.** A name with **en** (or a kin/role word) can address someone; there is no vocative particle and no slang address. Endearments must be literal; there is no root for "love" (**tana** is "like, enjoy"). |
| 1.6 | Three-way deixis: *este/esse/aquele*, *aqui/aí/ali/lá* (*esse/aí* = near you) | obligatory (a degree must be chosen) | *Passa-me esse copo aí.* "pass.IMP-me that.MED cup there.MED" | partly | **so neke lon ladon ben.** **tan/ben** are two-way, and there is no "here/there" root (**mon kemu tan** "at this place"). The "near you" degree is lost. |
| 1.7 | Affective diminutive *-inho/-zinho* (tenderness, cuteness; also on names and words with no size: *Joãozinho, obrigadinho, beijinho*) | habitual (very frequent in BR) | *Olha o cachorrinho!* "look.IMP DEF dog-DIM" | partly | **tin miya totu meli.** **meli** gives size only; the affection, and any diminutive on things without size, is lost. |
| 1.8 | Intensifying diminutive on adverbs/adjectives: *pertinho, cedinho, agorinha, rapidinho* | habitual | *Fica pertinho.* "is.located.3SG near-DIM" | yes | **gun bi gelu.** "is very near". **bi** carries the intensity; the warmth goes. |
| 1.9 | Minimizing diminutive in requests and offers: *um minutinho, um cafezinho, só uma perguntinha* (shrinks the imposition) | habitual | *Só um minutinho!* "only a minute-DIM" | partly | **so nun sefu foke.** "(please) wait a moment". **nun** does the politeness, **na** can make it "only"; the shrinking device itself is gone. |
| 1.10 | Augmentative *-ão/-aço/-ona*: size, admiration (*golaço, mulherão*) or contempt (*narigão*), many lexicalized (*palavrão*) | habitual | *Que casarão!* "what house-AUG" | partly | **ba guyi bi kudu.** Size via **kudu**, praise via **upe**; the admiring/derogatory evaluation has no channel. |
| 1.11 | *ser* vs *estar* (inherent trait vs current state) | obligatory | *Ela é triste. / Ela está triste.* "she is.SER sad / she is.ESTAR sad" | partly | **wan gun bayi** covers both. The current-state reading needs a time word (**eda wan gun bayi** "now she is sad"); the inherent-trait reading has no marker. |
| 1.12 | Progressive: *estar + GER* (BR), *estar a + INF* (PT) | habitual | *Estou comendo. / Estou a comer.* "am eat.GER / am to eat.INF" | yes | **lon le nawi.** |
| 1.13 | Periphrastic near future *ir + INF* (replaces the synthetic future in speech) | habitual | *Vou sair.* "go.1SG leave.INF" | yes | **de kusa.** (intention) or **lon win kusa.** (about to). |
| 1.14 | *já / ainda / ainda não* (already / still / not yet) | habitual | *Já comeste?* "already eat.PST.2SG" (PT) | yes | **min me sin nawi.** still = **le**, not yet = **mu sin**. |
| 1.15 | Tag questions *né?, não é?, não?* | habitual (BR *né* constant) | *Ela é legal, né?* "she is nice, not-is" | yes | **wan gun upe, min.** Clause-final **min** is a tag. *né* as a pure filler with no question has no equivalent. |
| 1.16 | *tipo* "like": approximator, hedge, example-giver, filler | habitual (BR, younger speakers) | *Eram tipo vinte pessoas.* "were.IPFV.3PL like twenty people" | partly | **gun ewo fu sa yawu.** "there are about 20 people". Approximation via **fu**; filler and hedge-on-anything uses have no word. |
| 1.17 | Hedges *meio, meio que, um pouco* | habitual | *Tô meio triste.* "am half sad" | yes | **lon gun to bayi.** |
| 1.18 | Turn openers and attention-getters: *olha, escuta, ó, vê lá* (PT warning), *sabe?, pô* | habitual | *Olha, não dá.* "look.IMP, not gives" ("look, it's not possible") | partly | **mu lenu.** The content survives; **tin miya** "look" would be taken literally. A word that flags or softens a point has no equivalent. |
| 1.19 | Backchannels *ahã, sei, é, tá, pois* (PT) | habitual | *— ... — Ahã.* | yes | **mm** "heard you"; an echo = "understood". |
| 1.20 | Alignment *pois é, é isso aí, exato, pois pois* (PT) | habitual | *— Ela é esperta. — Pois é.* "she is smart / so is" | yes | Echo **gun gole.** or **feko.** "true". |
| 1.21 | Yes/no answered by echoing the verb | habitual | *— Vens? — Venho.* "come.2SG / come.1SG" | yes | **min me obu. — obu.** The same strategy. |
| 1.22 | Emotion-specific interjections: *nossa!, eita!, caramba!, ai!, ui!, opa!, xi!, credo!, oxe!* (NE BR), *ena!* (PT) | habitual | *Nossa, que frio!* "(Our Lady), what cold" | partly | **ba bi weki.** **ba** marks exclamation; which emotion (surprise, pain, oops, disgust, alarm) needs a content word (**ba wekun**, **ba bebu**). Only **mm** and **huh?** exist as interjections. |
| 1.23 | Profanity and taboo intensifiers: *porra, caralho, merda, foda-se* | habitual | *Porra, perdi a chave!* "(semen), lose.PST.1SG DEF key" | no | **ba, lon sin agi du udin.** gives the content and the outburst, but there is no taboo vocabulary, so the transgressive force (and its bonding use among friends) is lost. |
| 1.24 | Affectionate teasing insults: *seu bobo, sua chata, sua tonta* | habitual | *Seu bobo!* "your.M fool" | partly | **a no mu gole.** "(not literal) one who isn't smart". The teasing is recoverable only from **a** and context. |
| 1.25 | Hypocoristics/nicknames: *Zé, Juju, Nando, Bia* | habitual | *Ó Zé!* "oh Zé" | yes | **en se.** Any name takes **en**. |
| 1.26 | Sympathy word *coitado/coitadinha* "poor thing" | habitual | *Coitada da tua mãe!* "poor.F of.DEF your mother" | partly | **lon feme bayi gan mole pi me.** A statement of feeling, not a set exclamation of pity. |
| 1.27 | Hortative *vamos, bora* (BR), *vá* (PT) | habitual | *Bora comer!* "let's eat.INF" | yes | **so pen nawi.** |
| 1.28 | Exclamatives *que + adj/noun*, *como + clause* | habitual | *Que lindo!* "what beautiful" | yes | **ba sesa.** |
| 1.29 | Disbelief checks *sério?, a sério?, jura?* | habitual | *Sério?* "serious" | yes | **min feko.** |
| 1.30 | Dismissal and indifference: *sei lá, tanto faz, deixa pra lá* | habitual | *Sei lá.* "know.1SG there" | partly | **lon mu lugo.** states ignorance; *tanto faz* -> **ben gun mu bawe**. The shrug (not caring) is lost. |
| 1.31 | Emphatic double negation (BR *não ... não*) | habitual (BR) | *Não vou não.* "not go.1SG not" | yes | **mu mu isi.** |
| 1.32 | Speaker/referent gender agreement, including *obrigado/obrigada* | obligatory | *Obrigada, estou cansada.* "obliged.F, am tired.F" | partly | **lota.** No agreement exists; the speaker's gender is not signalled unless stated (**futa**). Rarely needed for meaning. |

## 2. Hierarchical (to a boss, an elder, an official)

| # | Distinction | Status | Example (original + gloss) | APW | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | V-address *o senhor / a senhora* + 3SG verb; in PT *você* can be cold or rude upward | obligatory | *O senhor quer sentar-se?* "DEF sir want.3SG sit-REFL" | yes | **min nun me momu goyu.** Deference by **nun**; the addressee's gender and the "elder" sense of *senhor* drop unless stated. |
| 2.2 | Graded honorifics fixed by office: *Vossa Senhoria, Vossa Excelência, Vossa Majestade, Meritíssimo* (judge) | obligatory in official address | *Vossa Excelência permite?* "your excellency allow.3SG" | partly | **min nun nun nun me efon.** Degree survives by repeating **nun**; which form goes with which office is lost. |
| 2.3 | Titles before names: *Seu João, Dona Maria* (BR), *Senhor Engenheiro, Senhora Doutora* (PT), *Doutor* for any graduate (BR) | habitual | *Bom dia, Dona Maria.* "good day, lady Maria" | partly | **nun upe leso, en malia.** A role word may precede a name (**no tofo en mala**), but there is no generic honorific like *Dona/Seu*, and no roots for doctor or engineer. |
| 2.4 | Kin terms as respectful address to elders and teachers: *tia, tio, vó* (BR) | habitual | *Tia, posso ir?* "aunt, can.1SG go" | partly | No roots for aunt, uncle or grandparent (only phrases like **mole pi mole**), and such a phrase would be read literally. **nun** + name instead. |
| 2.5 | Third-person address by name or title instead of a pronoun (PT) | habitual (PT) | *O senhor doutor já almoçou?* "DEF sir doctor already lunch.PST.3SG" | partly | **en ..., min nun me sin nawi.** **me** is only ever the listener, and a name as subject reads as a third person, so the name becomes a vocative. The pronoun-avoiding courtesy is lost. |
| 2.6 | Subject omission to avoid choosing an address form (PT) | habitual | *Quer um café?* "want.3SG INDF coffee" | yes | **min momu kofi.** Nothing to avoid, since **me** is neutral. |
| 2.7 | Conditional of courtesy | habitual | *Gostaria de falar com o diretor.* "like.COND.1SG of speak.INF with DEF director" | yes | **so nun lon momu kegu gan no wungu.** Politeness carried by **nun**, not a hypothetical form. |
| 2.8 | Imperfect of courtesy | habitual | *Queria um café, se faz favor.* "want.IPFV.1SG INDF coffee, if do.3SG favour" (PT) | yes | **so nun lon momu kofi.** |
| 2.9 | Request framed as an ability question: *Poderia...? Será que podia...?* | habitual | *Será que o senhor podia me ajudar?* "be.FUT.3SG that DEF sir could.IPFV me help.INF" | yes | **min nun me lenu yema lon.** or directly **so nun yema lon.** |
| 2.10 | Formal imperative in the subjunctive; *queira, tenha a bondade de* | habitual | *Queira aguardar.* "want.SBJV.3SG wait.INF" | yes | **tin nun sefu.** |
| 2.11 | Optative wishes *que + SBJV*, *Deus lhe pague*, *passe bem* | habitual | *Que tenha um bom dia.* "that have.SBJV.3SG INDF good day" | partly | **upe isi** (farewell) or **lon ganin wun dili pi me gun upe** "I hope your day is good". No optative; wishes become hope statements. |
| 2.12 | *por favor / se faz favor / faça favor* | habitual | *Um café, por favor.* "INDF coffee, for favour" | yes | **so nun kofi.** |
| 2.13 | *com licença* (pre-emptive permission to pass, interrupt or leave) | habitual | *Com licença, posso passar?* "with licence, can.1SG pass.INF" | partly | **sepi, min lon gun efon kobi.** Built from "sorry" and a permission question; no single formula. |
| 2.14 | Apology formulas *desculpe, perdão, desculpe incomodar* | habitual | *Desculpe incomodar.* "excuse.SBJV.3SG disturb.INF" | yes | **sepi.** + content. |
| 2.15 | Graded thanks *muito obrigado, agradeço desde já, fico-lhe grato* | habitual | *Agradeço desde já.* "thank.1SG from now" | yes | **lota ya.** "thanks beforehand"; **bi lota** "many thanks". |
| 2.16 | Reply to thanks *de nada, não há de quê, imagina* (BR), *ora essa* (PT) | habitual | *— Obrigado. — De nada.* "of nothing" | partly | No routine; must be composed (**ben gun bunle** "that was easy"). |
| 2.17 | Service and deference replies *sim senhor, pois não, às ordens* | habitual | *Pois não, senhor.* "well not, sir" ("certainly; how can I help?") | yes | **nun, de muge.** "(polite) will do". The service-opening *pois não?* = **min nun lon lenu yema me.** |
| 2.18 | Respectful disagreement *com todo o respeito, se me permite* | habitual | *Com todo o respeito, discordo.* "with all DEF respect, disagree.1SG" | yes | **nun nun, lon mu tayu.** |
| 2.19 | Permission requests *posso?, dá licença?* | habitual | *Posso entrar?* "can.1SG enter.INF" | yes | **min lon gun efon inwa.** |
| 2.20 | Hedged opinions offered upward (*parece-me, talvez, eu diria*) | habitual | *Parece-me que talvez haja um erro.* "seem.3SG-me that maybe exist.SBJV INDF error" | yes | **nun, kon te gun batu.** Note: low **kon** used for politeness is on APW's watch list. |
| 2.21 | Register-set greetings and farewells (*oi, e aí, tchau* vs *bom dia, boa tarde, até logo*) | habitual | *Boa tarde, senhor.* "good afternoon, sir" | yes | **nun upe kegin.** ("evening"; no root for afternoon). Formality via **nun**; there are no separate informal formulas. |
| 2.22 | Introductions *muito prazer, encantado/a* | habitual | *Muito prazer.* "much pleasure" | partly | No routine; **lon sogi nagu me** "I'm happy to see you". |
| 2.23 | Elevated vocabulary and euphemism: *falecer/morrer, residir/morar, aguardar/esperar* | habitual | *O meu pai faleceu.* "DEF my father pass.away.PST.3SG" | no | **wede pi lon sin kono.** One root per meaning, and **nun** marks deference, not formality. There is no formal vocabulary layer. |
| 2.24 | Formal syntax: clitic placement (PT mesoclisis *dir-lhe-ei*; BR proclisis *me dá* is informal), avoiding *a gente, tá, pra* | habitual in formal speech | *Dir-lhe-ei amanhã.* "say-to.you-FUT.1SG tomorrow" | no | **lon iko me wuma.** The grammar has one style, so the signal "I am speaking carefully" disappears. |
| 2.25 | Asymmetric address (superior uses first name and T; subordinate uses *o senhor* + title) | habitual | *— João, vem cá. — Sim, senhor.* "João, come.IMP here / yes, sir" | yes | Non-reciprocal **nun** and role words do the same job. |
| 2.26 | Humble self-reference *a minha modesta opinião* | occasional | *Na minha modesta opinião...* "in.DEF my modest opinion" | partly | **nun, lon pume wun ...** **nun** honours the listener; there is no self-lowering form. |

## Top gaps

Ordered by how often Portuguese speakers need them (judgment, not counts).

1. **Discourse markers that steer talk** (*olha, tipo, sei lá, afinal, aliás, enfim, ou seja*): register 1 and 4 (1.16, 1.18, 1.30, 4.13, 4.15). Used many times a minute in casual speech and constantly in argument; APW has only literal paraphrases.
2. **Affective diminutive and augmentative** (*-inho, -ão*): register 1, and 4 for contempt (1.7, 1.9, 1.10, 4.20). Size survives through **meli/kudu**; tenderness, minimizing and contempt don't.
3. **"Even" and concession** (*até, mesmo, nem, mesmo que, embora*): register 4 (4.8, 4.9). No scalar word; "even if" collapses into "if".
4. **Definite vs indefinite** (new vs given referent): register 5, but present in all (5.19).
5. **Contrastive focus and clefts** (*foi eu que*): register 4 (4.11). Only parallel sentences; nothing marks it in writing.
6. **Emotion-specific interjections** (*nossa, ai, opa, xi, credo*): register 1 (1.22). **ba** says "exclamation" but not which feeling.
7. **Habitual past and *ser/estar*** (inherent vs current vs habitual state): registers 5 and 1 (5.3, 1.11). Only adverbs carry it.
8. **Formality as distinct from deference** (elevated vocabulary, formal syntax, official formulas): registers 2 and 3 (2.23, 2.24, 3.20, 3.21). **nun** shows respect to a person; nothing marks a text or utterance as formal.
