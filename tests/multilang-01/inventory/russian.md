# Russian: register inventory vs Apu Pi We

Sources read for the Apu Pi We side: `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv`, `data/compounds.tsv` (nothing else).

**Columns.** *Status*: obligatory (the grammar forces a choice), habitual (speakers normally use it in this register), sometimes. *Apu Pi We*: **yes** = a standard word or construction carries the meaning with little loss; **partly** = a paraphrase or nearby device gets the core but drops something (named in the last column); **no** = no grammatical or lexical means, only describing the attitude in so many words.

Russian examples give: Russian — word-by-word gloss — 'translation'. Apu Pi We examples are in bold. Name adaptations are approximate.

## 1. Close and casual (friends, family)

| # | Distinction | Status | Example (Russian + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ты as the pronoun of closeness; switching to ты is itself a social event | obligatory | Ты придёшь? — you come.FUT.2SG — 'Will you come?'; Давай на ты — let's on ты — 'Let's drop the formal you' | partly | **min me obu.** **me** is the same for everyone; closeness shows only as no **nun** plus first names. There is no pronoun to switch, so "let's be on ты" can only be described (**so pen mu oyi nun** "let's not say nun"), and an unwanted ты has no form that could offend. |
| 2 | Affectionate diminutive nouns (водичка, чаёк, домик) | habitual | Дай водички — give.IMP water.DIM.GEN — 'Give me a drop of water' | no | **so neke lon osa in.** "(request) give me some water". **meli** means physically small; the warmth the suffix adds has no carrier. |
| 3 | Minimizing diminutives that soften a request (секундочку, минуточку) | habitual | Подожди секундочку — wait.PFV.IMP second.DIM.ACC — 'Hang on a sec' | partly | **so sefu foke fi.** "(request) wait one moment". The request keeps its shape; the softening by smallness is lost. **nun** softens too, but it is deference, not intimacy. |
| 4 | Hypocoristic name gradient (Мария → Маша → Машенька / Машка) | habitual | Машенька, иди сюда — Masha.DIM.AFF, go.IMP here — 'Come here, Mashenka (fondly)'; Машка! — Masha.PEJ/FAM | no | **en masa** is one fixed adapted name. Each Russian form could be adapted as a separate name (**en masenka**), but nothing grades fondness vs. rudeness, so a non-Russian listener just hears a different name. |
| 5 | Short vocative (Мам!, Саш!, Петь!) | habitual | Мам, ты где? — mum.VOC, you where — 'Mum, where are you?' | partly | **mole, me gun mon kemu yu.** Address is a bare kin word or name at the start. No separate calling form, so the intimacy of the clipped vocative is lost. |
| 6 | Kin terms for non-kin (тётя, дядя, бабушка, братан) | habitual | Тётя, а где автобус? — aunt, and where bus — 'Excuse me (lady), where's the bus?' (child to stranger) | partly | Kin words could be used to address someone, but there's no such convention; **faba** "sibling" for 'bro' would be taken literally. The designed address is a role word, which carries no warmth. |
| 7 | Endearments (солнышко, зайка, милый/милая) | habitual | Спокойной ночи, солнышко — calm.GEN night.GEN, sun.DIM — 'Good night, sunshine' | partly | **upe yami, giwe** is sayable but reads literally ("sun"), with no diminutive and no endearment convention. There's no root for "dear"; **tana** is "like, enjoy". |
| 8 | Particle ну: urging, hesitation, resignation, starting a turn | habitual | Ну давай уже! — well give.IMP already — 'Come on, already!'; Ну, не знаю — well, not know.1SG — 'Well, I dunno' | partly | Urging takes words (**so me obu eda** "(request) you come now"); hesitation gets its epistemic part from **kon bo** "don't know how sure". Lost: one cheap, unstressed marker of impatience, reluctance or hesitation. |
| 9 | Particle же: obviousness, reproach, insistence | habitual | Я же говорил! — I PTCL said.M — 'I *told* you!' | partly | **ba lon sin oyi ben.** "(exclaim) I said that". The emphasis survives; the presupposition "you knew / should have known", which is where the reproach lives, is lost. |
| 10 | Contrastive topic -то | habitual | Ключи-то я взял, а телефон забыл — keys-PTCL I took, but phone forgot — 'The keys I did take, but I forgot the phone' | partly | **lon sin yidu du udin, un lon yakin liga.** Parallel clauses carry the contrast, as the reference says. On its own (Я-то знаю 'well, *I* know', implying someone else doesn't), there's no way to evoke the unstated alternative. |
| 11 | вот: presenting, pointing, summing up | habitual | Вот твои ключи — here your keys — 'Here are your keys'; Вот это да! — here this yes — 'Wow!' | partly | **tan gun du udin pi me.** "this is your key"; the exclamation is **ba** + content. Lost: the handing-over word and summing-up uses (вот и всё 'and that's that'). |
| 12 | Softening/egging-on imperative -ка | habitual | Покажи-ка — show.IMP-PTCL — 'Go on, show me' | partly | **so wuye lon.** is a plain request; the friendly, slightly teasing push is lost. |
| 13 | Ethical dative (speaker's emotional stake) | sometimes | Ты мне не болей! — you me.DAT not be-ill.IMP — 'Don't you go getting sick on me!' | no | **tin mu simi.** "don't be sick". **li lon** "for me" would mean a beneficiary, not the speaker's emotional stake. |
| 14 | Dative experiencer states (мне холодно, мне скучно, мне лень) | habitual | Мне лень — me.DAT laziness — 'I can't be bothered' | partly | **lon feme weki** "I feel cold" works fine. лень becomes **lon mu momu muge bu** "I don't want to do it", which turns a state that comes over you into a choice; the excuse-like tone is lost. |
| 15 | Non-volitional reflexive (не спится, хочется) | habitual | Мне не спится — me.DAT not sleep.REFL — 'I just can't get to sleep'; Мне хочется есть — me.DAT want.REFL eat — 'I feel like eating' | partly | **lon mu lenu wopo** frames it as lack of ability. хочется (a want that comes over you) and хочу (I want) are both **momu**. |
| 16 | Subject/object drop and fragment answers | habitual | — Пришёл? — Пришёл. — came.M? — came.M — 'Did he come? — He did.' | yes | **min sin obu. — sin obu.** Rule 11 (leave out what context supplies) plus echo answers. |
| 17 | Answering negative questions (да нет, приду) | habitual | Ты не придёшь? — Да нет, приду — you not come? — yes no, come.1SG — 'Aren't you coming? — No, I am' | yes | Echo answers: **obu** / **mu obu** (rule 4). Russian's да/нет ambiguity disappears. |
| 18 | Tag questions (да?, правда?) | habitual | Классно, да? — cool, yes — 'Cool, right?' | yes | **ben bi upe, min.** Clause-final **min** is a tag. |
| 19 | Backchannels (ага, угу) and sarcastic agreement (ну да, конечно) | habitual | Ну да, конечно — well yes, of-course — 'Yeah, right' | yes | **mm** = heard, an echo = understood; sarcasm takes **a**: **a kon yawu.** "(not literal) certain". |
| 20 | Emotional interjections (ой, ай, ух ты, фу, эх, ура) | habitual | Ой, забыла! — oops, forgot.F — 'Oops, I forgot!' | partly | **ba lon yakin.** **ba** marks an exclamation but not which feeling (surprise, pain, disgust, regret, joy); that has to be named (**ba bebu** "disgust!"). Only **mm** and **huh** sit outside the system. |
| 21 | Mild swearing / мат as intensifier and in-group marker | habitual | Блин, ключи забыл! — pancake(euph.), keys forgot.M — 'Damn, forgot my keys!' | partly | **ba lon yakin du udin.** **ba** and **bi** carry the intensity. There's deliberately no taboo layer (word forms are screened against offensive words), so the transgressive, in-group flavour is lost. |
| 22 | Affectionate teasing insults (дурачок, глупышка) | sometimes | Ну ты и дурачок — well you and fool.DIM — 'You silly thing' | partly | **a me gun mu gole.** "(not literal) you aren't smart" shows it isn't meant; the fondness the diminutive adds is lost. |
| 23 | Speaker's gender in past-tense verbs and short adjectives | obligatory | Я пришла — I came.F — 'I came' (speaker is a woman) | no | **lon sin obu.** Gender isn't part of the grammar; it can only be stated outright (**lon, futa, …**). |
| 24 | мы с тобой / мы с мамой (we = I + one other) | habitual | Мы с мамой ходили в магазин — we with mum.INS went to shop — 'Mum and I went to the shop' | yes | **lon yen mole sin isi tun pu bika.**, or **din** (we, not you) / **pen** (we incl. you). Clearer than the Russian. |
| 25 | Past or perfective form as immediate future (Я пошёл; щас приду) | habitual | Ну, я пошёл — well, I went.M — 'Right, I'm off' | yes | **lon win isi.** ("about to") or **de isi.** (intend). The meaning is kept; the "as good as done" vividness isn't. |
| 26 | Approximation by inverted order (минут пять) | habitual | Буду минут через пять — be.FUT.1SG minutes in five — 'I'll be there in five minutes or so' | yes | **lon obu wo minitu fu te.** with **fu** + number "about". |
| 27 | Expressive reduplication (быстро-быстро, да-да-да) | habitual | Беги быстро-быстро — run.IMP fast-fast — 'Run really fast' | yes | **gabe bi yobe**; doubling strengthens (principle 5), though the reference only illustrates it with grammar words. |
| 28 | давай: let's / OK / bye | habitual | Давай завтра — give.IMP tomorrow — 'Let's do it tomorrow' | yes | **so pen muge bu wuma.** ("let's" = **so pen**); as a farewell, **upe isi**. |
| 29 | Presumptive guesses (небось, наверно) | habitual | Небось есть хочешь? — PTCL eat want.2SG — 'Bet you're hungry?' | yes | **kon oma me momu nawi, min.** confidence plus a tag. |

## 2. Hierarchical (boss, elder, official)

| # | Distinction | Status | Example (Russian + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | вы to one superior, elder or stranger (V form) | obligatory | Вы придёте? — you.PL come.FUT.2PL — 'Will you come?' | partly | **min nun me obu.** **nun** carries deference, but per speech act, not as a standing choice tied to the relationship. The default distance вы marks with strangers, whatever their status, has no form. |
| 2 | Asymmetric ты/вы (ты downward, вы upward) as a display of rank | habitual | Иванов, зайди ко мне. — Да, Пётр Сергеевич, иду. — Ivanov, drop-in.IMP.2SG to me — yes, Pyotr Sergeyevich, go.1SG | partly | Both sides say **me**; only one side using **nun** can mirror the asymmetry. Nothing in the grammar records rank. |
| 3 | Name + patronymic as respectful-but-personal address | obligatory | Иван Петрович, можно вопрос? — Ivan Petrovich, possible question — 'Ivan Petrovich, may I ask something?' | partly | **no wungu en iban, min nun lon gun efon mido.** Role word + name + **nun**. The patronymic could be adapted into the name (**en iban petulobitu**), but its meaning (respect without the coldness of a title) is lost. |
| 4 | Titles and rank address (товарищ майор, господин министр) | habitual | Товарищ майор, разрешите доложить — comrade major, permit.IMP.PL report.INF | yes | Role word before the name plus **nun**: **no wungu, min nun lon gun efon iko me …** |
| 5 | Permission-seeking formulas (разрешите, позвольте) | habitual | Разрешите войти? — permit.IMP.PL enter.INF — 'May I come in?' | yes | **min nun lon gun efon inwa.** |
| 6 | Requests softened with the conditional (не могли бы вы, я бы хотел) | habitual | Не могли бы Вы подписать? — not could.PL would you sign.INF — 'Could you possibly sign?' | yes | **so nun nun tusi suso pi me.** "(request, very politely) write your name". **nun** does the job the hedge does, by design. |
| 7 | Formulaic polite imperatives (будьте добры, прошу Вас) | habitual | Будьте добры, закройте дверь — be.IMP.PL kind, close.IMP.PL door | yes | **so nun tapi beya.** |
| 8 | Paired formal/informal greetings and farewells (Здравствуйте/Привет, До свидания/Пока) | obligatory | Здравствуйте — be-healthy.IMP.PL — 'Hello (formal)' | partly | **upe**, **upe isi**. **nun** goes after a speech-act particle or first in a statement; whether it can go on a routine word like **upe** isn't specified, so the obligatory formal/informal choice has no fixed form. |
| 9 | Imperfective vs perfective imperative as politeness (Садитесь vs Сядьте) | habitual | Садитесь, пожалуйста — sit.IPFV.IMP.PL, please — 'Do sit down' | partly | **so nun goyu.** / **me gun efon goyu.** "you may sit" / **tin goyu.** The speech-act words give a similar invitation/request/order scale; lost is aspect's social meaning (an invitation already expected vs. a fresh demand). |
| 10 | Humble performative requests in writing (Прошу предоставить…) | habitual | Прошу предоставить мне отпуск — ask.1SG grant.INF me.DAT leave — 'I request that I be granted leave' | yes | **so nun nun efon lon mu doku nin dili yawu.** "(request, very politely) allow me not to work for ten days". The genre formula is gone; the meaning is kept. |
| 11 | Apologizing for an imposition (извините за беспокойство) | habitual | Извините за беспокойство — excuse.IMP.PL for disturbance — 'Sorry to bother you' | yes | **nun lon sepi gi lon yebin me.** "I apologize for calling you" (polite). |
| 12 | Graded thanks (спасибо / благодарю Вас / премного благодарен) | habitual | Благодарю Вас — thank.1SG you.PL — 'I thank you (formal)' | yes | **nun lon bi lota me.** As a statement it can take **nun** and **bi**. |
| 13 | Service responses (Есть! Так точно! Слушаюсь) | habitual | Есть! — is — 'Yes, sir!' | yes | **de muge.** "will do"; an echo = understood. |
| 14 | Cushioned refusal or bad news (к сожалению, вынужден) | habitual | К сожалению, вынужден отказать — to regret.DAT, forced.M refuse.INF — 'Unfortunately I have to decline' | yes | **nun, lon bayi wun lon keya mu tayu.** "(politely) I'm sad that I must not agree". |
| 15 | Hedged suggestion to a superior | habitual | Может быть, стоит проверить ещё раз? — may be, worth check.INF again — 'Perhaps it's worth checking again?' | yes | **fon nun, pen moti miya bu.** **fon** leaves the decision with the listener, which is what this item is for. |
| 16 | Agentless framing so no one is blamed (было утеряно, произошла ошибка) | habitual | Документы были утеряны — documents were lost.PTCP — 'The documents were lost' | partly | **batu sin muwo.** "a problem happened"; **ge tusi la gun agi.** "the documents are lost" (state). There's no dynamic agentless passive; **go** "anyone" names a doer after all. |
| 17 | Honorific plural for an absent superior (директор просили) | sometimes | Директор просили зайти — director asked.PL drop-in.INF — 'The director (hon.) asked you to come by' | no | **no wungu sin oyi wun me obu.** **nun** applies to the speech act; nothing marks deference to a third person who is being talked about. |
| 18 | Capitalized Вы in letters | obligatory (formal letters) | Благодарим Вас — thank.1PL You.PL | partly | Writing is all lowercase, by rule; deference is only **nun**. There's no written-only mark. |
| 19 | Letter formulas (Уважаемый Иван Петрович! … С уважением) | obligatory (formal letters) | Уважаемый Иван Петрович! — respected Ivan Petrovich | partly | **upe, en iban** + **nun** in the body. There are no fixed opening or closing formulas, and no "respected" adjective (only **nun**). |
| 20 | Clinical or authorial "мы" (как мы себя чувствуем?; мы полагаем) | sometimes | Ну, как мы себя чувствуем? — well, how we self feel.1PL — 'And how are we feeling today?' | partly | **min me feme fu yu.** For the author's "we", **din** works; **pen** used for "you" would be taken literally. The caring/condescending tone is lost. |
| 21 | Orders and performatives from above (приказываю, поручаю) | habitual | Приказываю выдвигаться — order.1SG move-out.INF — 'I order you to move out' | yes | **tin isi.** or **tin nun isi.** |
| 22 | Self-lowering formulas (осмелюсь, если позволите) | sometimes | Осмелюсь доложить — dare.1SG report.INF — 'Permit me to report' | yes | **nun nun, …** |
| 23 | Addressing the public by role (граждане пассажиры) | habitual | Граждане пассажиры! — citizens passengers | yes | Role word + **la** + **nun**: **nun, no isi la, …** |
| 24 | Formal vs colloquial word pairs (проживать/жить, супруга/жена, приобрести/купить) | habitual | Где вы проживаете? — where you.PL reside.2PL — 'Where do you reside?' | partly | **min nun me nume mon kemu yu.** One root per meaning, so the formal synonym's social signal survives only as **nun**. |

## 3. Written for a stranger (instructions, technical, legal)

| # | Distinction | Status | Example (Russian + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reflexive or participial passive for procedure (подписывается сторонами) | habitual | Договор подписывается сторонами — contract sign.IPFV.REFL parties.INS — 'The contract is signed by the parties' | partly | **ge tayu gun tusi.** "the agreement is written" is a resulting state. A dynamic event needs an active clause with a named doer (or **go**). The text loses the object-as-topic with the doer pushed to the end. |
| 2 | Impersonal deontic predicates (следует, необходимо, рекомендуется, запрещается, допускается) | habitual | Следует закрыть дверь — follows close.INF door — 'The door should be closed' | yes | **fon tapi beya.** (advise) / **go keya tapi beya.** (must) / **tin …** / **go gun efon …** (permitted): each force has its own word. |
| 3 | Infinitive commands on signs (Не входить!) | habitual | Не входить! — not enter.INF — 'No entry' | yes | **tin mu inwa.** |
| 4 | Polite 2pl imperative to the reader | habitual | Нажмите кнопку — press.PFV.IMP.2PL button — 'Press the button' | yes | **tin dandi du tuko.** "push the start thing". |
| 5 | Aspect in instructions: once vs repeatedly or continuously (нажмите / нажимайте) | obligatory | Нажимайте, пока не загорится — press.IPFV.IMP until not lights-up — 'Keep pressing until it lights' | yes | **tin dandi gimu fi.** "once" / **tin le dandi …** "keep pushing". Optional, where Russian forces the choice; an unmarked instruction is ambiguous. |
| 6 | Indefinite-personal 3pl (здесь не курят; вам звонили) | habitual | Вам звонили — you.DAT called.PL — 'Someone called for you' | yes | **go sin yebin me.**; generic **go** "anyone". |
| 7 | Generic 2sg in maxims | sometimes | Тише едешь — дальше будешь — quieter ride.2SG further be.2SG — 'Slow and steady wins' | yes | **don go isi gake, go isi lini.** |
| 8 | Nominal style / канцелярит (произвести оплату, осуществление контроля) | habitual | Произвести оплату — perform.INF payment — 'Make payment' | yes | Any root is a noun in noun position (**unfu** "pay / payment"). The institutional colour of officialese has no counterpart; the meaning is kept. |
| 9 | Compound denominal prepositions (в соответствии с, в связи с, по истечении) | habitual | В соответствии с правилами — in accordance with rules — 'In accordance with the rules' | partly | **gi** because, **li** in order to, **nin** during, **wo fugi pi …** "after the end of"; but "in accordance with" is only **fu wabun la** "like the rules", which loses "as required by". |
| 10 | Exception (кроме, за исключением) | habitual | Все пункты, кроме пятого — all items, except fifth — 'All clauses except the fifth' | partly | **wabun la we, un mu wabun pi te.** "all the rules, but not rule 5". There's no "except"; **kin** "some, not all" can't name what's left out. |
| 11 | Distributive "each" vs collective "all" (каждая из сторон) | habitual | Каждая из сторон вправе … — each of parties entitled — 'Each party may …' | partly | **ewo la we gun efon …** "all the people are allowed". **we** doesn't separate each one individually from all together. |
| 12 | Conditions (если, при условии что, если иное не указано) | habitual | При условии, что оплата получена — on condition that payment received | yes | **don …**; "only if" **na don**; "unless" **don mu …**. |
| 13 | Deontic split (обязан / вправе / может / не вправе) | obligatory (legal) | Арендатор обязан … / вправе … — tenant obliged / entitled | yes | **no sanwa keya … / gun efon … / lenu … / mu gun efon …**. Ability and permission stay apart, where Russian может blurs them. |
| 14 | Definitions (под X понимается Y) | habitual | Под арендатором понимается … — under tenant.INS understood.REFL — '"Tenant" means …' | yes | **no sanwa yofu ewo man …** ("mean, signify"), or **X gun Y**. |
| 15 | Quantity limits (не менее, не более, от … до) | habitual | не менее пяти дней — not less five days — 'at least five days' | yes | **pa dili te** / **mu ke se dili yawu** / **se te tun yawu**. |
| 16 | Document-internal reference (настоящий договор, вышеуказанный, нижеследующий) | habitual | вышеуказанный адрес — above-indicated address — 'the above address' | partly | **ge tayu tan** "this agreement" is fine; "above-mentioned" needs a relative clause (**man go sin oyi ya**, "that was said before"). It's wordy, and there's no fixed term. |
| 17 | Gender/case agreement on который to pick the antecedent | obligatory | дочь соседа, которая приехала — daughter.NOM neighbour.GEN who.F arrived.F — 'the neighbour's daughter, who arrived' | partly | **man** attaches to the nearest noun (rule 15), so **tiwu futa pi ewo gelu man obu** reads "the neighbour who came". To attach to the far noun you must recast the sentence. |
| 18 | Participial phrases (лицо, подписавшее договор) | habitual | лицо, подписавшее договор — person signed.PTCP contract — 'the person who signed the contract' | yes | **ewo man sin tusi suso mon ge tayu.** |
| 19 | Adverbial participles for sequence (нажав, дождитесь) | habitual | Нажав кнопку, дождитесь сигнала — having-pressed button, wait.IMP signal.GEN | yes | **wo dandi du tuko, tin sefu.** A time clause may come before the particle. |
| 20 | Causal and additive connectives (следовательно, таким образом, кроме того) | habitual | Следовательно, … — consequently | yes | **gi tan, …** "so"; **wame** "also". |
| 21 | Concessive and contrastive connectives (однако, тем не менее, хотя, тогда как) | habitual | Тем не менее, срок не продлевается — nevertheless, term not extended.REFL | partly | Only **un** "but". There's no "although / nevertheless / whereas", so the concession's direction is left for the reader to infer. |
| 22 | Scientific hedging (по-видимому, вероятно, представляется) | habitual | По-видимому, … — apparently | yes | **san** inferred; **kon oma** 7 in 10. More precise than the Russian. |
| 23 | Source attribution (согласно данным, по словам) | habitual | Согласно данным исследования — according data study.GEN | yes | **fen** "told", or the source as subject: **ge tusi tan oyi wun …** |
| 24 | Object-first order to put the new item last (ответственность несёт Покупатель) | habitual | Ответственность за ущерб несёт Покупатель — responsibility.ACC for damage bears Buyer.NOM — 'Liability for damage lies with the Buyer' | partly | SVO is fixed: **no bika keya unfu …** puts the Buyer first. A cleft (**ewo man keya unfu gun no bika**) puts it back last, at a cost in words. |
| 25 | Formal politeness to the reader (Уважаемый покупатель!, Благодарим за покупку) | habitual | Благодарим за покупку — thank.1PL for purchase | yes | **nun din lota me gi me bika.** |
| 26 | Warnings (Внимание! Осторожно! Опасно для жизни!) | habitual | Осторожно! — careful | yes | **yo …**, **yo nun …**, **yo yo** (emergency). |
| 27 | Numbered labels (статья 5, пункт 3) | habitual | статья 5 — article 5 | yes | **wabun pi te** "rule 5" (noun + **pi** + number = label). |
| 28 | Inclusive and exclusive or (и/или; либо … либо) | habitual | либо наличными, либо картой — either cash, or card | yes | **lu X lu Y** exclusive; plain **lu** allows both. |
| 29 | Open list (и т. д., и т. п.) | habitual | и т. д. — and so on | yes | **yen musu eni** "and other things". |
| 30 | Enumeration markers (во-первых, во-вторых) | habitual | Во-первых, … во-вторых … — firstly … secondly | yes | **ukun pi fi …, ukun pi sa …** "reason 1, reason 2"; **ya** alone = "first". |

## 4. Persuasive (argument, opinion, complaint)

| # | Distinction | Status | Example (Russian + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rhetorical question expecting "no" (разве) | habitual | Разве это справедливо? — PTCL this fair — 'How is that fair?' | partly | **a min ben gun upe.** **a** marks the question as rhetorical. The expected answer ("no") and the indignation aren't encoded. |
| 2 | Incredulous question (неужели) | habitual | Неужели ты не понимаешь? — PTCL you not understand — 'Do you really not get it?' | partly | **min me mu lafi.** The disbelief/surprise is lost; **min** and **ba** are both speech-act particles, and nothing says they can combine. |
| 3 | же as reproach in argument | habitual | Ты же обещал! — you PTCL promised.M — 'But you *promised*!' | partly | **ba me sin oyi wun me muge bu.** "(exclaim) you said you'd do it". The emphasis survives; "you can't deny it" doesn't. |
| 4 | ведь to ground a claim in shared knowledge | habitual | Ведь это очевидно — PTCL this obvious — 'It's obvious, after all' | partly | **pen lugo ben.** "we (incl. you) know that" or **gi …**. Spelling it out makes an explicit claim; one light word presupposed it. |
| 5 | Contrastive focus by fronting and stress | habitual | Этого я не говорил — this.GEN I not said.M — '*That* I never said' | partly | **lon mu oyi ben. lon oyi tan.** Parallel sentences carry the contrast. Word order is fixed and there's no focus particle; **ba**/**bi** emphasize without saying what is in focus. |
| 6 | Three-way а / и / но (contrast without opposition) | habitual | Я работаю, а он отдыхает — I work, and(CONTR) he rests | partly | **lon doku, un wan luse.** **un** overstates the opposition and **yen** understates it; а has no equivalent. |
| 7 | Concessive хотя, несмотря на | habitual | Хотя он прав, я против — although he right, I against | partly | **wan gun denku, un lon mu tayu.** There's no concessive conjunction; "but" leaves the concession to inference. |
| 8 | Scalar даже ('even') | habitual | Даже ребёнок это понимает — even child this understands — 'Even a child understands that' | no | **tiwu wame lafi ben** "a child also understands that" loses the scale (the least likely person). **na**, **pa** and **wame** don't express it. |
| 9 | всё-таки / всё же ('still, after all') | habitual | И всё-таки он прав — and still he right — 'And yet he's right' | partly | **un wan gun denku.** **le** "still" is aspect (ongoing), not concession. |
| 10 | Opinion framing (по-моему, я считаю, мне кажется) | habitual | По-моему, это ошибка — by-my, this mistake — 'I think it's a mistake' | yes | **lon pume wun ben gun gagan.** or **kon …**. |
| 11 | Certainty and concession boosters (безусловно, конечно …, но) | habitual | Конечно, он опытный, но … — of-course, he experienced, but | yes | **kon yawu …, un …** |
| 12 | Intensifiers (совершенно, абсолютно, крайне) | habitual | Это совершенно неприемлемо — this completely unacceptable | yes | **ba ben bi yomo.** **bi**, **ba** and doubling (principle 6). |
| 13 | Irony and sarcasm (ну спасибо, очень умно) | habitual | Очень умно — very clever — 'Oh, very clever' | yes | **a ben bi gole.** **a** = not literal; **a a** = the whole turn. Explicit where Russian relies on intonation. |
| 14 | Hearsay with distance (якобы, мол, дескать) | habitual | Он якобы болел — he allegedly was-ill — 'He was supposedly ill' | yes | **fen kon sa wan sin simi.** "told; 2 in 10". The reference doesn't fix the order of stacked operators. |
| 15 | так называемый ('so-called') | habitual | так называемые эксперты — so-called experts | partly | **ewo la man go oyi wun gun no lugo** "people someone says are knowers". Wordy, and the scorn is lost. |
| 16 | Counterfactual (если бы … бы) | habitual | Если бы ты слушал, этого бы не случилось — if would you listened, this would not happened | yes | **don kon po me sin doma, ben mu muwo.** Note: **mu sin** means "not yet", so the negative result goes unmarked for tense. |
| 17 | Reproach for an unfulfilled duty (надо было, должен был) | habitual | Надо было раньше думать! — needed was earlier think.INF — 'You should have thought of that earlier!' | partly | **ba me sin keya pume ya.** "you had to think beforehand". "It didn't happen" and the reproach are left to inference; **fon** looks forward only. |
| 18 | Emphatic and multiple negation (ни за что, никто никогда) | habitual | Никто никогда не помогает — nobody never not helps | yes | **mu mu.**; **ewo fa yema mon muyi fa**. |
| 19 | Litotes (не могу не согласиться) | sometimes | Не могу не согласиться — not can.1SG not agree.INF | yes | **lon mu lenu mu tayu.** |
| 20 | Softened disagreement (позволю себе не согласиться) | habitual | Позволю себе не согласиться — allow.FUT.1SG self not agree.INF | yes | **nun, lon mu tayu.** |
| 21 | Exclamatory complaint (какое безобразие!, сколько можно!) | habitual | Сколько можно! — how-much possible — 'Enough already!' | yes | **ba ben bi yomo.** / **ba moti.** "(exclaim) again!" |
| 22 | Indignant ethical dative (он мне ещё будет указывать!) | sometimes | Он мне ещё будет указывать! — he me.DAT still will instruct — 'Who is he to tell me what to do!' | no | Only by naming the feeling (**ba lon bonko …**); the speaker as affronted party has no grammatical slot. |
| 23 | Pejorative and augmentative suffixes (людишки, домишко, -ище) | sometimes | людишки — people.PEJ — 'petty little people' | no | **meli** / **kudu** are literal size; the contempt is lost. |
| 24 | Attention-getters (послушайте, знаешь что) | habitual | Послушайте, … — listen.IMP.PL | yes | **so nun doma, …** |
| 25 | Threat or dare imperatives (попробуй только!) | sometimes | Попробуй только! — try.IMP only — 'Don't you dare!' | partly | **tin mu walo bu.** or **a so walo** (not-literal request). The menace of the ironic imperative is lost. |
| 26 | Emphatic agreement (вот именно!, ещё бы!) | habitual | Ещё бы! — still would — 'You bet!' | yes | **ba feko.** "(exclaim) true" / **ba kon yawu.** |
| 27 | Generalizing complaint (вечно, опять, всегда) | habitual | Ты опять забыл! — you again forgot.M | yes | **ba me moti yakin.**; "always" = **mon muyi we**. |

## Top gaps

Ranked by how often speakers need them (judgment, not measured).

1. **Stance and common-ground particles** (же, ну, ведь, -то, вот, -ка, разве, неужели): registers 1 and 4, partly. Russian speakers use these several times a minute. Apu Pi We gets the emphasis (**ba**) or the epistemic part (**kon**) but not the presupposition ("you know this", "unlike others", "come on").
2. **ты/вы and the address gradient** (V as default distance, asymmetric ты/вы, name + patronymic, short vocative): registers 1 and 2, partly. Every addressed sentence makes this choice. **nun** carries deference per utterance, but nothing encodes a standing relationship.
3. **Information structure through word order** (theme–rheme, object fronting, contrastive focus, new referents last): registers 3, 4 and 5, partly. Fixed SVO and no focus particle leave only parallel sentences, **gun** "there is" and clefts.
4. **Diminutive and pejorative morphology** (водичка, секундочку, Машенька/Машка, людишки): registers 1 and 4, no. Warmth, minimizing politeness and contempt have no carrier.
5. **Grammatical gender** (speaker's gender in every past-tense verb; он/она tracking): registers 1 and 5, no / partly. Gender is never encoded, only stated, and referents are tracked by topic, not sex.
6. **Aspect nuances outside done/ongoing/about-to** (negated-past "didn't" vs "failed", where **mu sin** means "not yet"; habitual past; historical present; saturative): register 5, partly.
7. **Non-volitional and experiencer framing** (мне лень, не спится, хочется, пришлось, удалось, оказалось, его убило): registers 1 and 5, partly. Events that happen *to* someone get recast as their choices or abilities, or given a doer.
8. **Scalar and concessive words** (даже, хотя, всё-таки, чуть не, вдруг): registers 4 and 5. даже is a no; the others are partly, carried only by **un** "but" or **win** "about to".
