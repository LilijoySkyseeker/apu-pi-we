# English: register inventory checked against Apu Pi We

Source language: English (native command). Apu Pi We sources read: `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv`, `data/compounds.tsv` only.

Column "English status": **obligatory** = the grammar forces a choice; **habitual** = speakers almost always mark it in that register though they could leave it out; **occasional** = used regularly but not in most texts.
Apu Pi We verdict: **yes** = carried, **partly** = carried with a stated loss, **no** = not carried. The Apu Pi We examples are my own compositions from the reference; none has been tested on a listener. Missing roots are noted only where the missing word *is* the distinction.

## 1. Close and casual (friends, family)

| # | distinction | English status | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | how, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | First name as address | habitual | *Sam, dinner's ready.* (calling a familiar by name) | yes | **pasu gun wonwe, en samu.** Name with **en**. Where a vocative goes in the sentence is not stated. |
| 1.2 | Endearments and buddy terms (honey, sweetie, mate, dude, bro) | habitual | *Night, sweetie.* (goodbye + affection) | partly | **upe isi, sonu** "go well, friend". A role word can address someone, but no address word carries affection or matey solidarity. |
| 1.3 | Informal kin terms and pet names (Mom/Mother, Grandpa, Lizzie/Elizabeth) | habitual | *Mom, where's Lizzie?* | partly | **mole** is the only "mother", so *Mom* vs *Mother* collapses. A nickname can be adapted as its own name (**en lisi**), but there is no diminutive. |
| 1.4 | Plural "you" (you guys, y'all) | habitual | *Are you guys coming?* | yes | **min me la obu.** |
| 1.5 | Fragments and ellipsis | habitual | *Coming? Want some?* | yes | Rule 11: **min obu.** |
| 1.6 | Reduced forms as an informality signal (gonna, wanna, y'know, ain't) | habitual | *I'm gonna crash.* (casual tone) | partly | Fast-speech variants exist (nasal vowels, softened consonants), but they carry no social meaning and there is no agreed casual vs careful contrast. |
| 1.7 | Casual and slang vocabulary (kids, guy, stuff, cool, sus, broke) | habitual | *The kids are being weird, it's kinda sus.* | no | One root per meaning (**tiwu la**). No informal synonym layer, so a word can't sound casual, in-group or fresh. |
| 1.8 | "oh": news receipt or realisation | habitual (very frequent) | *Oh, you're back.* (I've just noticed) | partly | **ba me sigo.** is an exclamation, which is stronger. **mm** only means "heard". The quiet "now I know" is lost. |
| 1.9 | "well": hesitation, softening a dispreferred answer | habitual (very frequent) | *Well, I'm not sure.* | no | No marker. Doubt can go into **kon**, but the signal "this isn't the answer you hoped for" is lost. |
| 1.10 | "I mean": self-repair and clarification | habitual | *Tuesday, I mean Wednesday.* | yes | **dili pi sa, lon yofu dili pi ka.** (**yofu** "mean, signify") |
| 1.11 | "you know": appeal to shared knowledge, holding the floor | habitual | *It was, you know, awkward.* | partly | **me lugo, min** "you know, right?" is a real question. The filler use (holding the turn, inviting empathy) is lost. |
| 1.12 | "like": quotative and loose approximation | habitual (younger speakers) | *She was like, "no way".* (gist of what she said or conveyed) | partly | A direct quote needs a say-verb (**wan oyi "…"**), which presents the words as hers. "Roughly this, maybe just a look" is lost. **fu** approximates numbers only. |
| 1.13 | "actually": correction against the listener's assumption | habitual | *Actually, I've already eaten.* | partly | **un, lon sin nawi.** "but, I've eaten" carries the contrast but not "contrary to what you assume". |
| 1.14 | Topic shift and return (anyway, by the way, so) | habitual | *Anyway, how's your mum?* | no | No marker. **gi tan** "so" is only causal. The topic change goes unsignalled. |
| 1.15 | Tag questions | habitual | *You're coming, right?* | yes | Clause-final **min**: **me obu, min.** |
| 1.16 | Biased questions (negative questions, reversed tags) | habitual | *Aren't you hungry?* (I expect you are); *You're not leaving, are you?* | partly | **min me mu …** exists, but no rule says it implies an expectation. Bias needs **kon**: **kon kefi me momu nawi, min.** The conventional bias of negative questions and reversed tags isn't defined. |
| 1.17 | Question by rising intonation on a statement | habitual | *You're leaving?* | yes | **min me kusa.** Rising pitch is allowed too. |
| 1.18 | Backchannels (uh-huh, yeah, right, mm-hm) | habitual | *Uh-huh.* | yes | **mm** = heard; an echo = understood. |
| 1.19 | Asking for a repeat (huh? what? sorry?) | habitual | *Sorry, what?* | yes | **huh?**; targeted with an in-situ question: **me oyi yu.** |
| 1.20 | Yes/no answers (yeah, nope, sure) | obligatory in answers | *Coming? Yeah.* | yes | Echo the verb: **obu** / **mu obu**. |
| 1.21 | Interjections (ouch, wow, ugh, yuck, oops, phew, yay, aww) | habitual | *Ouch! Oops. Phew.* | partly | **ba** + a content word: **ba wekun** "pain!", **ba bebu** "disgust!". No short cries, and nothing for *oops* (my small mistake), *phew* (relief) or *aww* (tenderness). |
| 1.22 | Swearing and expletives | habitual (for many speakers) | *Shit, I missed the bus.* (frustration + taboo force) | partly | Emphasis via **ba** and doubling: **ba yomo, ensu sin kusa.** No taboo layer, so the transgressive and bonding force is lost. |
| 1.23 | Teasing, irony, hyperbole | habitual | *I've told you a million times.* | yes | **a lon sin iko me gimu tonen.** **a** marks it non-literal. Note: English irony is deniable; marking it removes that. |
| 1.24 | Vague language (or something, and stuff, sort of) | habitual | *Get bread or something.* | yes | **so dunu sowe lu musu in.**; "sort of" = **to**. |
| 1.25 | Attitude carried by intonation (incredulous, sympathetic, annoyed) | habitual | *Really?* (incredulous) | yes | No tones, so pitch is free: **min bu gun feko.** said incredulously. |
| 1.26 | Contrastive stress and focus | habitual (very frequent) | *I didn't take it, TOMAS did.* / *I said TUESDAY.* | partly | No focus device yet. Parallel sentences work (**lon mu yidu bu. en tomasu yidu bu.**), but a bare correction (*I said TUESDAY*) has no means. With fixed first-syllable stress and declination, it's unclear whether pitch may single out one word. |
| 1.27 | Minimisers (just, real quick, a sec, a bit) | habitual | *Can I just ask one thing?* (this is small) | partly | **nun** softens and **foke fi** "one moment" is literal. The "I'm barely imposing" framing of *just* is lost. |
| 1.28 | Casual requests | habitual | *Can you get the door?* | yes | **so benbi beya.** |
| 1.29 | Let's | habitual | *Let's eat.* | yes | **so pen nawi.** |
| 1.30 | Graded greetings and farewells (hey/hi/hello; bye/see ya/take care) | habitual | *Hey!* vs *Good morning.* | partly | **upe**, **upe leso**, **upe isi**, **nagu me la mon dili pi ufo**. One level only (plus **nun**), so the casual-to-formal gradient is lost. |
| 1.31 | Thanks and apology (thanks, cheers, sorry, my bad) | habitual | *Thanks!* *My bad.* | yes | **lota**, **sepi**. |
| 1.32 | Sympathy (sorry to hear that, oh no, poor you) | habitual | *Oh no, I'm so sorry.* (sympathy, not apology) | partly | **sepi** is only an apology, so sympathy becomes **lon gun bayi gi ben** "I'm sad because of that". No sympathy formula. |
| 1.33 | Affection and longing (love you, miss you, proud of you) | habitual | *Love you, miss you.* | partly | No roots for love, miss or proud: **lon bi tana me** "I like you very much", **lon momu nagu me** "I want to see you". Weaker and literal. |
| 1.34 | Attention-getters (hey, listen, look) | habitual | *Hey, look!* | partly | **tin miya**, **tin doma** work as commands. There's no bare "hey". |
| 1.35 | Exclamatives (so cool, what a…) | habitual | *That's so cool!* | yes | **ba tan gun bi upe.** |
| 1.36 | Plans and near future (gonna, -ing for arrangements) | habitual | *I'm calling her tomorrow.* | yes | **wuma, de yebin wan.**; **win** about to. |
| 1.37 | Deixis (this/that, here/there, now) | obligatory | *Put it here.* | yes | **so yaga bu mon kemu tan.** |

## 2. Hierarchical (up to a boss, elder or official)

| # | distinction | English status | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | how, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Title or role + name (Dr Patel, Professor Lee, Officer) | habitual | *Professor Mara, …* | yes | **no tofo en mala** "teacher Mara": a role word replaces the honorific. |
| 2.2 | Honorifics without a name (sir, ma'am, Your Honour) | habitual | *Excuse me, sir.* | partly | No general respectful address word. You must know a role word (**no wungu**); to a stranger of unknown role there's only **nun**. |
| 2.3 | Name choice signals distance (title + surname vs first name; "call me Jane") | habitual | *Mr Jones* → *David* | yes | **no tofo en mala** vs **en mala**. |
| 2.4 | Gendered and marital honorifics (Mr, Mrs, Ms, Miss) | habitual | *Mrs Smith* (a married woman) | partly | Gender is a modifier stated only when it matters. Marital status isn't in address (it could be stated with **ofin** "spouse"). |
| 2.5 | Graded politeness (please; would you kindly; I'd be most grateful) | habitual | *Would you kindly…* | yes | **so nun**, **so nun nun**, **nun nun nun**. |
| 2.6 | Indirect, modally distanced requests (could you, would you mind, I was wondering if) | habitual | *I was wondering if you could send it.* (polite, refusable) | yes | Function: **so nun nun sege bu tun lon.** **so** already leaves the choice with the listener. The indirect form is allowed but not needed. |
| 2.7 | Asking permission | habitual | *May I leave now?* | yes | **min nun lon gun efon kusa eda.** |
| 2.8 | Deferential disagreement | habitual | *With respect, I don't think that's right.* | yes | **nun lon mu pume wun ben gun denku.** |
| 2.9 | Apologising for the imposition | habitual | *Sorry to bother you, but…* | yes | **sepi nun. so nun …** (no root for "bother", but apology + request carry it) |
| 2.10 | Formal thanks | habitual | *Thank you for your time.* | yes | **nun nun lon lota me li muyi pi me.** |
| 2.11 | Ritual humility (I might be wrong, just, I'm afraid, in my humble opinion) | habitual | *I might be wrong, but…* | partly | **kon te lon gun gagan, un …** states real doubt. Using **kon** for ritual modesty is what the watch list warns against. *I'm afraid* and *humble* have no equivalent. |
| 2.12 | Warning or raising a concern upward | occasional (critical) | *Captain, I'm concerned we're low on fuel.* | yes | **yo nun, …** then **fon nun, pen wupi …** (the reference's crew pattern). |
| 2.13 | Formal acknowledgement (yes sir, understood, right away) | habitual | *Right away, sir.* | yes | Echo, **mm**, **de nun muge eda.** |
| 2.14 | Hedged suggestion upward | habitual | *Perhaps we might wait.* | yes | **fon nun pen sefu.** |
| 2.15 | Choosing the strength of a speech act (order vs request vs suggestion) | habitual | *Do it / Could you do it / You might do it* | yes | **tin** / **so** / **fon**, each with **nun** as needed. |
| 2.16 | Interrupting and asking for the floor | habitual | *Excuse me, if I may…* | yes | **sepi nun. min lon gun efon oyi musu fi.** |
| 2.17 | Third-person address to a superior | occasional | *Would the Minister agree that…* | partly | **me** is the only listener pronoun. No rule says a role word can stand for the listener (**min no wungu tayu …**), so it would read as someone else. |
| 2.18 | Honorifics for an absent superior (His Majesty, the Honourable Member, the late Dr X) | occasional | *His Majesty has arrived.* | partly | **nun** is politeness toward the listener only. Respect for a third party comes only from the role word; "the late" needs a relative clause (**… man sin kono**). |
| 2.19 | Formal (Latinate) vocabulary (request, assist, commence vs ask, help, start) | habitual | *We will commence shortly.* | no | One root per meaning (**tuko**), so word choice can't raise the register. |
| 2.20 | Formal openings and closings (Dear Sir or Madam, Yours sincerely, Good afternoon Dr X) | habitual in letters | *Yours sincerely,* | partly | **upe**, **upe kegin**, **upe isi** + **nun**. No conventional letter frame. |
| 2.21 | Respect vocabulary (it's an honour, I appreciate, a privilege) | occasional | *It's an honour to meet you.* | partly | **lon gun bi sogi gi nagu me** "I'm very happy because I see you". No roots for honour or esteem. |
| 2.22 | Official notification formulas (please be advised, I am writing to inform you) | occasional | *Please be advised the office closes at 17:00.* | yes | A statement with **nun**: **nun, pu pi mabo tapi mon fi oma po po.** |

## 3. Written for a stranger (instructions, technical, legal)

| # | distinction | English status | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | how, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Imperative instruction | obligatory in instructions | *Close the door.* | yes | **tin tapi beya.** |
| 3.2 | Polite notice (please, kindly) | habitual | *Please close the door.* | yes | **tin nun tapi beya.** |
| 3.3 | Step order (first, then, finally; step 1, 2) | habitual | *First open the bag, then put the phone inside.* | yes | **ya, tin benbi yufa. wo, tin yaga liga mon bese pi yufa.**; labels **lite pi fi**. |
| 3.4 | Conditions (if, when, once) | habitual | *If the water is hot, wait.* | yes | **don osa gun tikun, tin sefu.** |
| 3.5 | Graded hazard words (DANGER > WARNING > CAUTION > NOTICE) | habitual on labels | *WARNING: risk of death.* | partly | **yo** vs **yo yo** plus **kon** for likelihood: **yo, kon sa go kono.** Four conventional severity levels collapse into two, so severity has to be spelled out. |
| 3.6 | Prohibition | habitual | *Do not touch.* | yes | **tin mu sanne.** |
| 3.7 | Obligation (shall, must) | obligatory in legal text | *The tenant shall pay rent monthly.* | yes | **no sanwa keya unfu nimo pi sanwa mon dunan we.** The legal ambiguity of *shall* (duty or future) can't arise. |
| 3.8 | Permission or entitlement (may, is entitled to) | habitual | *The tenant may keep a dog.* | yes | **no sanwa gun efon ibe totu.** |
| 3.9 | Scope of negation with modals and quantifiers (must not vs need not; not all vs none) | habitual | *must not* vs *need not* | yes | **keya mu ibe** vs **mu keya ibe**; **kin** "some, not all", **fa** none. |
| 3.10 | Possibility (may cause, can result in) | habitual | *This may break the glass.* | yes | **kon da bu alun degun.** It can't be confused with permission. |
| 3.11 | Quantity bounds (at least, no more than, approximately, between) | habitual | *at least 3; no more than 5; about 30* | yes | **pa ka**, **mu ke se te**, **fu ka yawu**, **se ka tun te**. It isn't stated whether **se … tun** includes its endpoints. |
| 3.12 | Numbers, units, dates, times | obligatory | *on 9 October at 15:30, 5 km* | yes | **dunan pi yawu, dili pi mape, mon fi te ka po**; `5 km`. |
| 3.13 | Definite vs indefinite (a / the) | obligatory | *Take a card.* vs *Take the card.* | partly | No articles. **mise** is open; **mise fi** "one card" and **mise tan** "this card" are stronger than *a*/*the*. The plain "the one you can identify" goes unmarked. |
| 3.14 | Each / every / any / either | habitual | *Each tenant pays $100.* vs *The tenants pay $100 (in total).* | partly | **we** "all" doesn't tell each-separately from all-together (**no sanwa la we unfu dola loki** is ambiguous). *Any* via **go**/**bo**; *either* = **lu X lu Y**. |
| 3.15 | Number (party / parties) | obligatory | *the party / the parties* | yes | **la** marks plural; counts after the noun. The writer has to add **la** where it matters. |
| 3.16 | Agentless passive, dynamic and stative | habitual (very frequent) | *Payment shall be made within 30 days. The door is locked.* | partly | Stative: **beya gun udin**. Dynamic: generic or dropped subject: **go keya unfu nin dili ka yawu.** The patient can't be the subject of an event, so the topic can't come first. |
| 3.17 | Nominalisation (termination, upon receipt) | habitual | *Upon termination of this agreement* | yes | **wo fugi pi tayu tan, …** Position turns verbs into nouns; **no / ge / du / pu**. |
| 3.18 | Defined terms (the "Tenant"; hereinafter "the Company"; X means Y) | habitual in legal text | *"Tenant" means the person renting the flat.* | partly | Definitions work with **yofu**: **apu "no sanwa" yofu ewo man sanwa guyi tan.** No capitals and no *hereinafter* device to show the word is being used as a defined term later. |
| 3.19 | Cross-reference (Section 3 above, the said, the aforementioned) | habitual | *as stated in Section 3* | yes | **fu wabun pi ka oyi**; **no sanwa tan**. |
| 3.20 | Exceptions and precedence (unless, except, provided that, subject to, notwithstanding) | habitual in legal text | *Subject to Section 5, …* | partly | *Unless* ≈ **don … mu …**; *except* via **un mu**. No device for which clause overrides which (*subject to*, *notwithstanding*). |
| 3.21 | Purpose (in order to, so that) | habitual | *to stop the fire* | yes | **li bamo lumi.** |
| 3.22 | Reason and result (because, due to, therefore) | habitual | *therefore* | yes | **gi**, **gi tan**. |
| 3.23 | What a clause attaches to (relative and time clauses) | habitual | *I watched the person who came back in the morning.* | yes | Rule 15 settles it by position: **nin leso, lon sin miya ewo man sigo** vs the clause at the end. Clearer than English. |
| 3.24 | Inclusive vs exclusive "or" (and/or) | habitual | *cash and/or card* vs *either cash or card* | yes | **nimo lu mise** vs **lu nimo lu mise**. Clearer than English. |
| 3.25 | Deadlines and periods (within, by, no later than, until) | habitual | *no later than Friday* | yes | **ya fugi pi dili pi te**; **nin dili ka yawu**; **se … tun …**. |
| 3.26 | Technical terms of art | habitual | *internet, kilometre* | yes | Borrowed from English: **intalunetu**, **kilamita**. |
| 3.27 | Legal and official vocabulary (hereby, whereas, pursuant to, terminate vs end) | habitual | *The agreement shall terminate…* | no | One root (**fugi**). A text can't signal "this is legal/official" through word choice. |
| 3.28 | Performatives (hereby) | occasional | *I hereby resign.* | partly | A plain statement or **de** (intend). Nothing marks "saying this *is* the act". |
| 3.29 | Impersonal addressee (the user, one, users should) | habitual | *Users must log in.* | yes | **no bone** "user", **go**: **no bone la keya …** |
| 3.30 | Scientific hedging (suggests, appears, is likely) | habitual | *The data suggest X is likely.* | yes | **san kon oma …**: inference + a number, which is more precise than English. |
| 3.31 | Citation and attribution (according to X) | habitual | *According to Smith, …* | yes | **en simitu oyi wun …**; **fen** when the source is unnamed. |
| 3.32 | Labels and headings | habitual | *Hot water. Wet floor.* | yes | A noun phrase alone: **osa tikun.** |
| 3.33 | Timeless present | obligatory | *Water boils at 100 °C.* | yes | Unmarked by default: **osa binla mon 100 °C.** |
| 3.34 | Layout and punctuation (colon, semicolon, parentheses, (a)/(b) lists, CAPITALS, italics) | habitual | *Note: (a) …; (b) …* | partly | Only `,` `.` `" "`, all lowercase. Labels by words (**pi** + number). Capitals for warnings and defined terms, and visual structure, are lost. |

## 4. Persuasive (argument, opinion, complaint)

| # | distinction | English status | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | how, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Framing an opinion (I think, in my view) | habitual | *I think we should go.* | yes | **lon pume wun pen keya isi.** |
| 4.2 | Boosters that assume agreement (clearly, obviously, of course) | habitual | *Obviously we need more money.* | partly | **kon yawu pen wupi ke nimo.** carries certainty, but not "anyone can see this", the pressure to agree. |
| 4.3 | Hedges (perhaps, arguably, it seems) | habitual | *Arguably, it's too late.* | yes | **kon te**, **san**. |
| 4.4 | Concession (although, even though, despite, granted) | habitual | *Although it's hard, we must try.* | partly | Coordination only: **bu gun gofa, un pen keya walo.** No subordinate concessive, no "despite X", no *even though* strength. |
| 4.5 | Contrast (however, whereas, on the other hand) | habitual | *However, …* | yes | **un**; parallel clauses. |
| 4.6 | Addition (moreover, not only… but also) | habitual | *Not only is it big, it's also fast.* | yes | **mu na bu gun kudu, bu wame gun yobe.** |
| 4.7 | Cause and result in argument (because, since, therefore) | habitual | *So we must act.* | yes | **gi tan, pen keya muge.** |
| 4.8 | Summing-up markers (in conclusion, to sum up, all in all) | habitual in essays | *In conclusion, …* | partly | **gi tan** or **mon fugi** "at the end". No conventional marker. |
| 4.9 | Examples (for example, such as) | habitual | *Some animals, such as dogs, …* | yes | **noso in, fu totu, …** |
| 4.10 | Scalar "even" | habitual (frequent) | *Even children know that.* (the least likely case) | no | No word. **tiwu la wame lugo ben** "children also know that" loses "the least expected case". **pa**, **na** and **wame** don't cover it. |
| 4.11 | Verum focus (it IS, I DID) | habitual | *I DID pay.* (against your doubt) | partly | **ba lon sin unfu.** exclaims the whole sentence; **kon yawu** asserts certainty (watch-listed as emphasis). "Yes, contrary to what you think" is lost. |
| 4.12 | Rhetorical questions | habitual | *Who wants to pay more?* (nobody) | partly | Whether **a** (non-literal) can combine with **min** isn't defined. A statement (**ewo fa momu unfu ke**) gives the content without the rhetorical force. |
| 4.13 | Speaking-manner adverbs (frankly, honestly, seriously, to be fair) | habitual | *Frankly, this plan is bad.* | partly | Literal: **lon oyi feko, lite tan gun yomo.** No conventional markers. |
| 4.14 | Loaded words (regime/government, slaughter/kill, thrifty/stingy) | habitual | *They slaughtered civilians.* | partly | One neutral root (**omen**) + modifiers and degree words. No colouring through near-synonyms. |
| 4.15 | Strength of quantifiers (most, hardly any, the vast majority) | habitual | *Most people agree.* | partly | **feli** many, **uyo** few, **in**, **kin**, **fa**, percentages. *Most/majority* needs a paraphrase (more than **timon**); *hardly any* has no form. |
| 4.16 | Deontic should / ought / must / need | habitual | *We should leave.* | yes | **fon**, **keya**, **wupi**. |
| 4.17 | Counterfactual reasoning | habitual | *If we had acted, this wouldn't have happened.* | yes | **don kon po pen sin muge, ben mu sin muwo.** |
| 4.18 | Hypotheticals (suppose, imagine) | habitual | *Suppose everyone left.* | yes | **don kon fi ewo we kusa, …** |
| 4.19 | Inclusive "we" | habitual | *We all want this.* | yes | **pen** vs **din**. More explicit than English. |
| 4.20 | Generic "you" / "one" | habitual | *You never know.* | yes | **go mu lugo.** |
| 4.21 | Sarcasm | habitual | *Oh great, another delay.* | yes | **a ben gun bi upe.** |
| 4.22 | Distancing from a word (so-called, scare quotes) | occasional | *so-called experts* | partly | **a** covers a sentence, not a word. Paraphrase: **ewo man oyi wun sun gun no lugo** "people who say they are knowers". |
| 4.23 | Understatement (litotes) | habitual | *Not bad.* | yes | **mu yomo.** |
| 4.24 | Appeals to authority and reports of others' views (studies show, critics claim) | habitual | *Experts say…; critics claim…* | yes | **fen**; **ewo in oyi wun …**; "claim" (doubted) via **fen kon …**. |
| 4.25 | Complaint emotion words (disappointed, frustrated, fed up, outraged) | habitual in complaints | *I'm really disappointed.* | partly | **bonko** angry, **bayi** sad, **bi**. No roots for disappointed, frustrated or annoyed. |
| 4.26 | Demand vs request | habitual | *Give me my money back.* vs *Could I get a refund?* | yes | **tin neke lon nimo pi lon.** vs **so nun neke lon nimo pi lon.** |
| 4.27 | Assigning vs avoiding blame (agent named or left out) | habitual | *You broke it.* / *It got broken.* | yes | **me alun bu.** / **bu sin alun.**; the agent can be dropped. |
| 4.28 | Conditional threats and promises | occasional | *If you don't pay, I'll leave.* | yes | **don me mu unfu, de lon kusa.** |
| 4.29 | Intensifiers (absolutely, totally, so) | habitual | *absolutely terrible* | yes | **bi yomo**, doubling, **ba**. |
| 4.30 | Agreement and disagreement tokens (exactly, fair point, no way) | habitual | *Exactly. No way.* | yes | **lon tayu**, an echo, **feko**, **mu mu tayu**. |

## 5. Narrative (story or news report)

| # | distinction | English status | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | how, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Past-time frame | obligatory | *Yesterday I went to the city.* | yes | **afe, lon isi tun oton.** The time is set once. |
| 5.2 | Background vs foreground (was walking when) | habitual | *I was walking home when it started to rain.* | yes | **nin lon le niba tun guyi, gegi tuko.** |
| 5.3 | Earlier past (had left) | habitual | *When I arrived, she had left.* | yes | **nin lon liwon, wan sin kusa.** Note: **sin** also sets the narrative time on a story's first verb. |
| 5.4 | Future-in-past (was about to, was going to) | habitual | *She was about to leave.* | yes | **wan win kusa.**; plans via **momu**. |
| 5.5 | Habitual past (used to, would) | habitual | *We used to swim there every summer.* | partly | **mon muyi tikun we, din lonfe mon kemu ben.** No habitual aspect; the "no longer" of *used to* is lost unless stated. |
| 5.6 | Historical present (switching tense for vividness) | occasional (oral) | *So I walk in and he says…* | no | Tense is unmarked after the frame, so there's nothing to switch to. |
| 5.7 | Present perfect for hot news | habitual | *The minister has resigned.* | partly | **no wungu sin kusa doku.** looks the same as a plain past. "Just now and it matters" needs **eda**. |
| 5.8 | Headline style | habitual | *Storm kills 3.* | yes | **lapo omen ewo ka.** |
| 5.9 | Genre openings and closings (once upon a time, so there I was, the end) | habitual | *Once upon a time there was a king.* | partly | **ya fawu feli, gun no wungu fi.** No fixed formula that signals fiction. |
| 5.10 | Existential introduction (there was a …) | habitual | *There was a woman who had a dog.* | yes | **gun futa man ibe totu.** |
| 5.11 | New vs known referent (a man → the man) | obligatory | *A man came in. The man sat down.* | partly | Bare **ewo**; **ewo fi** / **ewo tan** are optional and stronger; then **wan**. Same gap as 3.13. |
| 5.12 | Tracking characters (he/she, him/her) | obligatory | *Mara saw Ana. She looked at her.* | yes | **en mala nagu en ana. wan miya yi.** Obviation by topic instead of by sex. |
| 5.13 | Sex of characters | obligatory (through pronouns) | *the commander … she* | yes | **no wungu futa** once, then **wan**. |
| 5.14 | Reporting verbs with stance or manner (admitted, insisted, claimed, whispered, shouted) | habitual | *He admitted he'd lied.* | partly | **oyi**, **iko**, **mido**, **febo**; manner via a modifier (**oyi mange**, **oyi dibon**). No roots for admit, insist, claim or deny. |
| 5.15 | Indirect speech and who "she" is | habitual | *She said she would come.* | yes | **wan oyi wun sun obu.** **sun** is clearer than English. |
| 5.16 | Free indirect discourse | occasional (literary) | *Was he really leaving? She'd show him.* | partly | No tense or person shift to blend narrator and character. **sun** works only inside a framed report. |
| 5.17 | News attribution (police said, reportedly) | habitual | *Police said…* | yes | **fen**; named sources with **oyi wun**. |
| 5.18 | Hedged news (is believed to, allegedly) | habitual | *The man is believed to have left.* | yes | **fen kon oma, ewo tan sin kusa.** |
| 5.19 | Passive with the victim as topic (were killed, has been arrested) | habitual | *Two people were killed.* | partly | **ewo sa sin kono** "died", or **go sin omen ewo sa**. Same gap as 3.16. |
| 5.20 | Sequence (then, after that, meanwhile, later) | habitual | *Meanwhile, …* | yes | **wo**, **nin tan**. |
| 5.21 | Suddenness and surprise (suddenly, to her surprise, it turned out) | habitual | *Suddenly the door opened.* | partly | **beya benbi yobe** "the door opened fast". No *suddenly*, no surprise root, no mirative marker. *It turned out* via **wo, wan lugo wun …**. |
| 5.22 | Time relative to the story (the next day, three years later, that night) | habitual | *Three years later, …* | yes | **wo fawu ka**; **dili ben wo dili fi** "the next day". An offset with no anchor counts from *now*, so a past story has to name its anchor. |
| 5.23 | Setting the scene | habitual | *In a small village in the mountains, …* | yes | **mon oton meli man gun mon genne, …** |
| 5.24 | Direct dialogue | habitual | *She said, "I want to go."* | yes | **wan oyi "lon momu isi".** |
| 5.25 | Narrator's evaluation (luckily, sadly, unfortunately) | habitual | *Luckily, nobody was hurt.* | partly | A separate clause: **ewo fa sin wekun. ben gun upe.** No sentence adverbs, no "luck". |
| 5.26 | Evidence in personal stories (I saw, apparently, I heard) | habitual | *Apparently he quit.* | yes | **o**, **fen**, **san**. |
| 5.27 | Characters' thoughts and indirect questions | habitual | *She wondered whether he'd come.* | yes | **wan mu lugo wun min yi obu.** |
| 5.28 | Round quantities (dozens, hundreds, more than 100) | habitual | *Hundreds gathered.* | partly | **fu te yawu**, **ke se loki** work. No form for vague magnitudes like *dozens* or *hundreds*. |
| 5.29 | Almost / nearly (something that didn't happen) | habitual | *I almost fell.*; *nearly 50* | partly | **lon win dosi, un mu dosi** "I was about to fall but didn't". No *almost*; *nearly 50* ≠ **fu te yawu** "about 50". |
| 5.30 | Repetition and continuation (again, twice, kept knocking) | habitual | *He kept knocking.* | yes | **moti**, **gimu sa**, **le**. |
| 5.31 | Referring back to a situation (it was awful; that's when) | habitual | *It was awful.* | yes | **ben gun bi yomo.** |

## Top gaps

Ranked by how often speakers need them.

1. **Conversational discourse markers**: *well*, *oh*, *anyway / by the way*, *actually*, *you know* (register 1, also 2 and 4). They occur in almost every casual turn, and Apu Pi We has no marker for hesitation, news receipt, topic shift or counter-expectation (1.8, 1.9, 1.11, 1.13, 1.14).
2. **Focus by stress: contrastive and verum focus**: *TOMAS did*, *I said TUESDAY*, *I DID pay* (registers 1 and 4). Only parallel sentences, **ba** or **kon yawu** are available, and a bare correction has no means (1.26, 4.11).
3. **Interjections and emotion vocabulary**: *ouch, oops, phew*; *love, miss, disappointed, sorry (sympathy)* (registers 1 and 4). Only **ba** + a root, and several core feeling words are missing (1.21, 1.32, 1.33, 4.25).
4. **Definiteness (a/the)** (registers 3 and 5). Obligatory on nearly every English noun. Bare nouns leave identifiable vs new open; **fi** and **tan** overshoot (3.13, 5.11).
5. **Register-marked vocabulary**: casual/slang vs formal, Latinate and legal (registers 1, 2 and 3). With one root per meaning, word choice can't signal casualness, formality or officialdom (1.7, 2.19, 3.27).
6. **Address without a name or role**: *sweetie, mate*; *sir, ma'am* (registers 1 and 2). Address needs a name or role word; neither affection nor generic respect has an address word (1.2, 2.2).
7. **Sentence-level stance adverbs**: *obviously, honestly, luckily, unfortunately, suddenly* (registers 4 and 5). Only a separate clause or **kon** is available; conventional comment words are missing (4.2, 4.13, 5.21, 5.25).
8. **Scalar "even" and subordinate concession**: *even children…*, *although / even though / despite* (register 4). No "even"; concession is reduced to **un** "but" (4.10, 4.4).
