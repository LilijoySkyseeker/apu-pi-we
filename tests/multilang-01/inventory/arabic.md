# Arabic register inventory vs Apu Pi We

Two layers of Arabic are treated as one language, and each row says which one it means:

- **ECA**: Egyptian Colloquial Arabic, the spoken layer (registers 1 and 4, and register 2 when spoken).
- **MSA**: Modern Standard Arabic, the written and formal layer (registers 2 in writing, 3 and 5).

Transliteration: ʿ = ʿayn, ʾ = hamza (in ECA it also stands for the glottal stop from qāf), ḥ ṣ ḍ ṭ ẓ = emphatics, kh gh š = single sounds, ā ī ū = long vowels. ECA uses g for jīm. Glosses are word by word: M/F = masculine/feminine, DU = dual, PL = plural, SG = singular, NEG = negation, HAB = habitual, FUT = future, PRT = particle.

Frequency column: **Oblig.** = speakers must mark it; **Habit.** = normal in the register; **Some.** = sometimes.

Apu Pi We verdicts were judged only against `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv` and `data/compounds.tsv`.

- **yes** = a defined device carries the meaning.
- **partly** = the content can be paraphrased, but the social or grammatical function is lost.
- **no** = there's no device, and a paraphrase changes what is done.

## Top gaps

Ranked by how often speakers need them.

1. **Religious-social formulas** (in šāʾ aḷḷāh, il-ḥamdu li-llāh, waḷḷāhi, māšāʾ aḷḷāh, aḷḷāh yikhallīk/yirḥamu, the salām pair). Registers 1, 2 and 5; said dozens of times a day; partly or no. There's no routine slot beyond **upe/lota/sepi**. **kon** and **de** carry the content but not the social act, and the soft refusal in in šāʾ aḷḷāh runs against **kon**.
2. **Gender of "you" and "he/she".** Registers 1 and 5; obligatory in every clause; partly. **me** and **wan** are genderless. The addressee's gender, and two characters told apart by gender, are lost, so tracking falls to **wan/yi** topic choice.
3. **Honorific person reference and rank titles** (ḥaḍritak, siyadtak/maʿālīk, bāša/ḥagg, ya fandim). Register 2; partly. **nun** grades how much deference, but not which rank, and there's no polite address word or status title.
4. **Spoken discourse particles** (ma "as you know", baʾa "already", ṭab "OK then", yaʿni "I mean"). Register 1; no or partly. There are no stance or discourse-management particles; only **ba**, **a** and **mm**.
5. **Definiteness** (al-). Register 3 (and every noun phrase in every register); partly. There are no articles, so the specific-vs-any distinction must be added with **tan/ben/fi** or left to context.
6. **Topic and focus** (topic fronting, ana illi cleft, VSO vs SVO). Registers 4 and 5; partly. Word order is fixed and there's no focus particle, so emphasis on who did it falls to **na** or parallel sentences.
7. **Kin and endearment address terms** (ya ḥabībi, ya ʾalbi, ya ʿammu, ya ibni, reverse-role "ya māma"). Registers 1 and 2; partly or no. Address by name or role works, but there's no affectionate or kin-for-non-kin convention.
8. **Optative wishes, blessings and curses** (ya rēt, yikhrib bētak, aḷḷāh yikhallīk). Registers 2 and 4; partly. There's no optative mood; **lon momu wun …** / **lon ganin wun …** paraphrase them, and their use as greetings, thanks and teasing is lost.
