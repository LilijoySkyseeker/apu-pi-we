# Indonesian: what speakers mark, and whether Apu Pi We can say it

Sources read: `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv`, `data/compounds.tsv` only.

Conventions. Indonesian examples are in italics with a word-by-word English gloss. Apu Pi We is in bold, written as the reference writes it (lowercase, no `?` or `!`). Words in [brackets] are loans or roots not in the lexicon. "obligatory" = a speaker cannot avoid choosing; "common" = normal in the register; "sometimes" = available, used by some speakers or in some texts.

Verdicts: **yes** = the meaning can be said with existing grammar and roots; **partly** = the core is sayable but a named part of the meaning is lost; **no** = no way to carry it.

## 1. Casual and intimate (friends, family)

| # | Distinction | Obl./common/sometimes | Example (Indonesian + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | 1sg pronoun by intimacy/in-group: *aku* (intimate), *gue* (Jakarta casual), vs neutral *saya* | obligatory (every "I") | *Gue laper.* I.CASUAL hungry | no | **lon momu nawi.** "I want to eat". **lon** is the only "I"; nothing marks closeness or casualness (**nun** only adds deference, the opposite direction). |
| 1.2 | 2sg pronoun by intimacy: *kamu*, *kau*, *lo/lu* vs *Anda* | obligatory (every "you" that isn't avoided) | *Lo mau ke mana?* you.CASUAL want to where | no | **me momu isi tun kemu yu.** **me** is "you" to anyone; the casual/intimate stance of *lo* is lost. |
| 1.3 | Kin term used as a pronoun for oneself (parent to child, older to younger sibling) | common | *Mama pulang dulu, ya.* Mama go.home first YA ("I'm heading home first, OK?", said by mother) | partly | **mole sigo ya.** is grammatical, but nothing says the noun is the speaker; a listener reads a third-person mother. Clear only with **lon**, which drops the kin role. |
| 1.4 | Age-relative address as pronoun: *Kak* (older), *Dik* (younger), *Mas/Mbak* (young man/woman, older-ish), also to non-kin | common | *Kak, minta air dong.* older.sibling request water DONG | partly | **faba ke pani** "older sibling" and **faba loyen** compose, but there is no conventional age-relative address word for non-kin, and no vocative slot is defined (only linkers, scene-setters and if/when clauses may precede the speech-act particle). |
| 1.5 | Inclusive vs exclusive "we": *kita* / *kami* | obligatory | *Kami udah makan, kalian?* we.EXCL already eat, you.PL | yes | **din sin nawi.** vs **pen …**. Same split as Indonesian. |
| 1.6 | Hortative *yuk*, *ayo* "let's" | common | *Makan yuk!* eat YUK | yes | **so pen nawi.** (request: we) |
| 1.7 | *kok*: surprise / counter-expectation, often "how come" | common | *Kok kamu di sini?* KOK you at here | partly | **gi yu me gun mon kemu tan.** asks why; the "this contradicts what I expected" stance is lost. **ba** (exclaim) is itself a speech act, and the reference gives no way to combine it with a question. |
| 1.8 | *sih*: softens a question, or marks mild contrast/exasperation | common | *Kamu kenapa sih?* you why SIH ("what's up with you?") | partly | **muwo yu tun me.** "what happened to you". The question survives; the softening/exasperated tone does not. |
| 1.9 | *dong*: insistence, "obviously / come on" | common | *Bantuin dong!* help.APPL DONG | partly | **tin yema lon.** The instruction is there; the "you should already know this" insistence is lost. **bi** and **ba** add emphasis, not obviousness. |
| 1.10 | *kan*: appeal to shared knowledge, "as you know / remember" | common | *Aku kan udah bilang.* I KAN already say | partly | **lon sin iko me, min.** The clause-final **min** tag ("…, right?") asks for confirmation; *kan* asserts the listener already knows, and that reproach/reminder is lost. |
| 1.11 | *deh*: coaxing, "go on, just try" | common | *Coba deh.* try DEH | partly | **fon walo bu.** **fon** (advise, decision stays with you) is close; the friendly coaxing ("you'll see") is not marked. |
| 1.12 | *ya udah deh*: resigned concession, "fine, have it your way" | common | *Ya udah deh, terserah.* yes already DEH, up.to.you | partly | **ben gun upe.** "that's good/fine". Acceptance is sayable; reluctance must be stated in words. |
| 1.13 | *-lah*: softened or familiar imperative | common | *Makanlah.* eat-LAH | partly | **tin nun nawi.** **nun** adds deference, which is the wrong flavour for intimates; warm softening has no outlet. |
| 1.14 | *ya* tag/softener on requests and statements | common | *Tunggu sebentar ya.* wait moment YA | partly | **so sefu foke.** (request). The **min** tag means "right?"; *ya*'s "OK? / please" softening is lost. |
| 1.15 | Presentatives *nih / tuh* ("here it is / there, see") | common | *Ini nih bukunya.* this NIH book-the | partly | **ingo tan.** "this book". Pointing survives; the handing-over/look-here act has no marker. |
| 1.16 | *loh/lho*: alert to new or overlooked information | common | *Aku udah bayar loh.* I already pay LOH ("I did pay, you know") | partly | **lon sin unfu.** States it; "you may not have realised" is lost. **ba** makes it an exclamation, not a correction. |
| 1.17 | *aja*: minimiser "just, simply" (softener) | common | *Duduk aja.* sit just | partly | **tin goyu.** **na** is exclusive "only" (**tin na goyu** = sit and do nothing else), not the casual "just". |
| 1.18 | Subject/object drop | common | *Udah makan?* already eat | yes | **min sin nawi.** (rule 11) |
| 1.19 | Answering *sudah/belum* by echo | common | *Udah makan belum?* — *Belum.* already eat not.yet — not.yet | yes | **min sin nawi.** — **mu sin nawi.** Answers echo the verb; **mu sin** = not yet. |
| 1.20 | Progressive *lagi* | common | *Aku lagi makan.* I PROG eat | yes | **lon le nawi.** |
| 1.21 | *mau* as imminent future | common | *Mau hujan.* want rain ("it's about to rain") | yes | **win gegi.** (about to; subject dropped) |
| 1.22 | Colloquial vs standard word forms signal register: *nggak/tidak*, *udah/sudah*, *gimana/bagaimana*, *-in/-kan* | obligatory (every such word is one or the other) | *Nggak tahu.* not.CASUAL know | no | **mu lugo.** Each word has one form; there is no casual or formal variant. |
| 1.23 | Intensifier *banget* | common | *Enak banget!* tasty very | yes | **ba bi upe.** |
| 1.24 | Verb reduplication for leisurely/aimless activity: *jalan-jalan* | common | *Jalan-jalan yuk.* walk-walk YUK ("let's go out for a stroll") | partly | **so pen niba li luse.** "walk for fun" paraphrases it; doubling of content words has no defined meaning. |
| 1.25 | Noun reduplication for plural | common | *anak-anak* child-child | yes | **tiwu la.** |
| 1.26 | Typed interjections: *aduh* (pain/dismay), *wah* (amazement), *ih* (disgust) | common | *Aduh, sakit!* ouch, hurts | partly | **ba wekun.** **ba** + a feeling word names the emotion, but there is no set of interjections apart from **mm** and **huh**. |
| 1.27 | Reassurance routine *nggak apa-apa* | common | *Nggak apa-apa.* not what-what ("it's fine") | yes | **batu fa.** "no problem" |
| 1.28 | Endearment address: *sayang*, *say*, *bro*, *cuy* | common | *Sayang, udah makan?* dear, already eat | partly | **min sin nawi, en lina.** A name works; no endearment term exists and no vocative position is defined. |
| 1.29 | Gender-neutral 3rd person *dia* | obligatory | *Dia datang.* 3SG come | yes | **wan obu.** Also gender-neutral. |

## 2. Hierarchical (to superiors, elders, officials)

| # | Distinction | Obl./common/sometimes | Example (Indonesian + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Neutral-polite *saya* chosen instead of *aku* | obligatory | *Saya mohon izin pulang, Pak.* I beg permission go.home, sir | partly | **so nun efon lon sigo.** **nun** carries the deference, but on the utterance; "I" itself has no polite form. |
| 2.2 | *Bapak/Ibu* (or *Anda*) as "you"; encodes the addressee's gender, seniority and status | obligatory (to superiors *kamu* is rude) | *Bapak mau minum apa?* father want drink what | partly | **nun me momu koye yu.** Deference yes; gender, age and social rank of the addressee are not expressed by **me**. |
| 2.3 | Honorific title before a name: *Pak Budi*, *Bu Sri*, *Dok*, *Prof* | common | *Selamat pagi, Pak Budi.* safe morning, Mr Budi | partly | **upe leso, en budi.** A role word can precede the name (**no tofo en mala** "Teacher Mara"), but there is no generic, role-neutral honorific like *Pak/Bu*. |
| 2.4 | Senior refers to self by title (teacher, boss, elder) | common | *Ibu pulang dulu, ya.* mother(teacher) go.home first YA | partly | **no tofo sigo ya.** grammatical, but read as a third person; no rule makes a title self-referring. |
| 2.5 | Honorific 3rd person *beliau* | common (about respected people) | *Beliau sedang rapat.* HON.3SG PROG meeting | partly | **wan le kegu gan funti.** **nun** marks deference toward the listener for the whole utterance; it cannot single out the person talked about. |
| 2.6 | Honorific vocabulary: *wafat/meninggal/mati* (die), *santap* (dine), *berkenan* (deign) | common | *Ayah beliau sudah wafat.* father HON.3SG already pass.away.HON | no | **wede pi wan sin kono.** One word per meaning; no honorific lexicon. |
| 2.7 | Humble request *mohon / minta tolong* | common | *Mohon tanda tangan di sini.* beg sign at here | yes | **so nun tusi suso pi me mon kemu tan.** |
| 2.8 | *silakan*: offering or inviting, for the listener's benefit | common | *Silakan duduk, Pak.* please.go.ahead sit, sir | partly | **fon nun goyu.** or **me gun efon goyu.** Advice or permission; there is no "offer" act distinct from request (speaker's benefit) and advice. |
| 2.9 | *permisi*: asking leave to pass, enter or leave | common | *Permisi, Bu.* excuse.me, madam | partly | **nun sepi.** **sepi** is "apologise"; the asking-leave function is lost. |
| 2.10 | *maaf* as an opener before a request or interruption | common | *Maaf, Pak, boleh saya bertanya?* sorry, sir, may I ask | yes | **sepi. min nun lon gun efon mido.** |
| 2.11 | Conditional softener *kalau boleh / kalau Bapak tidak keberatan* | common | *Kalau boleh, saya mau bertanya.* if allowed, I want ask | yes | **don me efon, de mido.** |
| 2.12 | Hedged suggestion upward | common | *Mungkin sebaiknya kita tunda dulu, Pak.* maybe better we postpone first, sir | yes | **fon nun, pen sefu.** **fon** + **nun** do the work Indonesian gives to *mungkin sebaiknya*; **kon** is available if real doubt is meant. |
| 2.13 | Understated disagreement or refusal with *kurang* "less" | common | *Saya kurang setuju.* I less agree | yes | **nun lon mu bi tayu.** "I don't very much agree" |
| 2.14 | Deferential acknowledgement *Baik, Pak* / *Siap!* | common | *Baik, Pak, segera saya kerjakan.* good, sir, soon I do | yes | **de nun muge.** (intend + polite + do) |
| 2.15 | Formal thanks | common | *Terima kasih banyak, Bu.* receive love much, madam | yes | **nun bi lota.** |
| 2.16 | Group address *Bapak-Ibu sekalian*, *hadirin* | common (speeches) | *Bapak-Ibu sekalian yang saya hormati* fathers-mothers all who I respect | partly | **nun nun**, **me la**. No address formula; no plural honorific. |
| 2.17 | Letter formulas *Dengan hormat*, *Hormat saya*, *Yth.* (honoured) | obligatory (formal letters) | *Yth. Bapak Kepala Dinas* honoured Mr head office | partly | **nun nun** opens politely; there is no fixed opening/closing or honorific before a title. |
| 2.18 | Avoiding "you" altogether to a superior | common | *Sudah makan, Pak?* already eat, sir | yes | **min nun sin nawi.** Subject drop (rule 11). |
| 2.19 | *kami* when speaking for one's team to a superior | common | *Kami sudah selesai, Pak.* we.EXCL already finish, sir | yes | **din sin fugi.** |
| 2.20 | Superior/doctor uses inclusive *kita* for "you" (empathetic) | sometimes | *Kita minum obatnya dulu, ya.* we.INCL drink medicine-the first YA | partly | **so pen koye [medicine].** says literally "let's"; the empathetic you-meaning is not a convention. |
| 2.21 | Kin terms to strangers by age/gender: *Bu*, *Pak*, *Bang*, *Om*, *Tante*, *Dik* | common | *Bu, ini berapa?* madam, this how.much | partly | **nun, musu tan gun nimo wifu yu.** Role words (**no koda** "seller") are possible; age/gender-based respect is lost. |

## 3. Writing for strangers (instructions, technical, legal)

| # | Distinction | Obl./common/sometimes | Example (Indonesian + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Agentless prohibition *dilarang* | common | *Dilarang masuk.* PASS-forbid enter | yes | **tin mu inwa.** |
| 3.2 | Polite public notice *harap / mohon* | common | *Harap tenang.* hope calm | yes | **tin nun gun mange.** |
| 3.3 | *di-* passive with the patient as topic, agent omitted | common (default in procedures) | *Air dipanaskan sampai mendidih.* water PASS-heat until boil; *Data disimpan di server.* data PASS-store at server | partly | **tin muge tikun osa, tun bu binla.** / **go kuwi [data] mon [server].** Content is sayable via imperative or **go**; the patient can't be subject or topic, since there is no passive and nothing moves. |
| 3.4 | Resultative state *ter-* / *di-* | common | *Pintu tertutup.* door STATE-close | yes | **beya gun tapi.** (**gun** + verb = resulting state) |
| 3.5 | Abilitative *ter-* ("-able") | common | *Tulisannya tidak terbaca.* writing-the not ABIL-read | yes | **go mu lenu lilu bu.** |
| 3.6 | Modal distinctions *wajib/harus*, *boleh*, *dapat* (ability or permission), *dilarang* | obligatory (legal) | *Peserta wajib membawa KTP. Pemohon dapat mengajukan banding.* participant must bring ID. applicant can submit appeal | yes | **no inwa keya dinyo mise pi suso.** Ability **lenu** and permission **gun efon** are separate, so *dapat*'s ambiguity must be resolved. |
| 3.7 | *berhak* "is entitled to" (a right/claim) | common (legal) | *Pembeli berhak atas pengembalian dana.* buyer entitled to return-NMLZ fund | partly | **no bika gun efon dunu nimo.** Permission is not a claim on someone else; there is no word for a right. |
| 3.8 | Anaphoric definite *tersebut*, *-nya*, *yang bersangkutan* | common | *Barang tersebut harus dikembalikan.* goods aforementioned must PASS-return | partly | **go keya muge sigo musu ben.** **bu** (topic) and **ben** (non-topic) point; there is no neutral "the (already mentioned)". |
| 3.9 | Classifiers with numbers: *orang*, *buah*, *lembar*, *ekor* | common (formal counting) | *tiga orang saksi* three CLF.person witness | yes | **no nagu ka.** Noun + number; the classifier's category (human, flat, animal) is already in the noun. |
| 3.10 | Distributive *setiap / masing-masing* | common | *Setiap peserta membayar lima dolar.* each participant pay five dollar | partly | **ewo we unfu dola te.** **we** "all" doesn't separate "each pays 5" from "all together pay 5". |
| 3.11 | *dan/atau* "and/or" | common (legal) | *denda dan/atau kurungan* fine and/or detention | yes | **lu** allows both; **lu X lu Y** excludes both. |
| 3.12 | Conditions *apabila/jika*, exception *kecuali* | common | *kecuali Anda tidak setuju* except you not agree | yes | **don me mu tayu, …** |
| 3.13 | Deadline *paling lambat* "at the latest" | common | *paling lambat 30 hari* most late 30 day | yes | **mu wo dili ka yawu** "not after 30 days" |
| 3.14 | *sekurang-kurangnya* "at least" | common | *sekurang-kurangnya tiga orang* at.least three CLF.person | yes | **pa ewo ka.** |
| 3.15 | Derivational family *pe-*, *peN-an*, *-an* | common | *pembayar, pembayaran, bayaran* payer, payment, wage | yes | **no unfu**, **unfu** (position), **ge unfu**. |
| 3.16 | Generic agent *barang siapa* "whoever" | common (legal) | *Barang siapa melanggar, dikenai denda.* goods who violate, PASS-impose fine | yes | **go man alun wabun, keya unfu nimo.** |
| 3.17 | Generic *Anda* in instructions | common | *Pastikan Anda masuk.* ensure you log.in | yes | **me** (listener) or **go** (anyone). |
| 3.18 | Institutional *kami* | common | *Kami akan menghubungi Anda.* we.EXCL will contact you | yes | **din de yebin me.** |
| 3.19 | Warnings *Awas*, *Perhatian*, *Bahaya* | common | *Awas, air panas!* beware, water hot | yes | **yo, osa tikun.** (**yo yo** for emergency) |
| 3.20 | Labels: *Pasal 3*, *Langkah 1* | obligatory (legal/manuals) | *Pasal 3 ayat 2* article 3 clause 2 | yes | **wabun pi ka**, **lite pi fi** (noun + **pi** + number = label). |
| 3.21 | Technical loanwords | common | *Unduh aplikasinya lewat internet.* download app-the via internet | yes | Borrowed from English (**intalunetu**). |
| 3.22 | Formal connectives *oleh karena itu*, *namun*, *selain itu* | common | *Oleh karena itu, …* by reason that | yes | **gi tan**, **un**, **wame**. |
| 3.23 | Clock time with zone *pukul 15.30 WIB* | common | *pukul 15.30 WIB* hour 15.30 western.Indonesia.time | yes | **mon fi te ka po**, plus **muyi pi kemu** (local time). |
| 3.24 | Estimative passive *diperkirakan* "it is estimated" | common | *Diperkirakan selesai besok.* PASS-estimate finish tomorrow | yes | **kon oma, fugi wuma.** (confidence + inferred **san** if needed) |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Opinion frame *menurut saya* | common | *Menurut saya, ini salah.* according.to I, this wrong | yes | **lon pume wun tan gun gagan.** |

| 4.9 | *memang*: concession before a counter-argument | common | *Memang mahal, tapi awet.* indeed expensive, but durable | partly | **kon yawu …, un …** gives certainty, not "granted". |

| 4.13 | Strength of recommendation *harus / sebaiknya / boleh* | common | *Kamu sebaiknya istirahat.* you better rest | yes | **keya** / **fon** / **gun efon**. |
| 4.14 | *seharusnya*: "ought to / should have" (unfulfilled obligation) | common | *Pemerintah seharusnya bertindak.* government should act | partly | **wan la sin keya muge, un mu sin muge.** must be spelled out; no "ought". |

| 4.18 | Source of claim: *katanya* vs *saya lihat sendiri* | common | *Katanya dia pindah.* his.saying 3SG move | yes | **fen** (told) vs **o** (seen). |
| 4.19 | *makanya*: "that's why (and I told you so)" | common | *Makanya, dengerin orang tua!* that's.why, listen.to person old | partly | **gi tan, tin doma …** gives cause; the blame/I-told-you-so is lost. |
| 4.20 | Concessive *walaupun / meskipun* | common | *Walaupun hujan, dia datang.* although rain, 3SG come | yes | **gegi, un wan obu.** |
| 4.21 | Exasperation interjections *ya ampun*, *astaga* | common | *Ya ampun, telat lagi!* oh mercy, late again | partly | **ba …** marks an exclamation; exasperation as such is not marked. |

## Top gaps

Ordered by how often a speaker needs them.

1. **Pronoun register: *aku/gue/saya*, *kamu/lo/Anda*** (registers 1, 2): no. Every "I" and "you" in Indonesian picks an intimacy level; **lon** and **me** are fixed, and **nun** only signals deference.
2. **Stance particles *kan, sih, dong, ya, deh, loh*** (registers 1, 4): partly. They are present in most casual sentences. Apu Pi We has speech acts and a "right?" tag, but no words for shared knowledge, softening, insistence or "you may not know".
3. **Definiteness *-nya / itu / tersebut*** (registers 3, 5): partly. Possession is sayable; a neutral "the (already known)" is not, only **bu**/**ben**/**tan** or a bare noun.
4. **Titles and kin terms as pronouns (*Bapak/Ibu/Kak/Mas/Mbak*, and self-reference by title)** (registers 1, 2): partly. Deference is sayable with **nun**; the addressee's age, gender and status, and the title-as-"you"/"I" convention, are not.
5. **Colloquial vs standard word forms (*nggak/tidak, udah/sudah*)** (registers 1, 2): no. There is one form per word.
6. ***di-* passive with the patient as topic** (registers 3, 5): partly. Agentless meaning works with **go** or an imperative; patient-as-subject does not.
7. **Counter-expectation and mirativity (*kok, ternyata, malah, padahal*)** (registers 1, 4, 5): partly. The facts and **un** survive; "contrary to what I/you expected" does not.
8. **Scalar "even" and focus (*bahkan/pun, -lah, justru*)** (register 4): partly. **wame** is only "also", and there is no focus device.
