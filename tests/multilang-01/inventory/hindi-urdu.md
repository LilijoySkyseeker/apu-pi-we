# Hindi–Urdu: register inventory and Apu Pi We coverage

Hindi–Urdu is treated as one spoken language. Where the formal registers split into Sanskrit-based (Hindi) and Perso-Arabic-based (Urdu) vocabulary and the split carries meaning, both are given (Urdu script added where the Urdu form matters). Examples: Devanagari, romanization, then a word-by-word English gloss in quotes, then the free sense after "=".

Apu Pi We was checked against `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv` and `data/compounds.tsv` only. Loanwords not in the lexicon (e.g. **palisu** "police", **batan** "button", **lupi** "rupee") are adapted by the stated loan rules and marked "(loan)".

Frequency column: **obligatory** = the grammar forces a choice in every relevant clause; **habitual** = speakers mark it in most relevant utterances; **sometimes** = used when the speaker wants the nuance.

Verdicts: **yes** = the distinction is expressed at reasonable cost; **partly** = the core meaning gets across but something listed is lost or the cost is high; **no** = no device carries it.

## Register 1: intimate and informal (friends, family)

| # | Distinction | Obligatory/habitual/sometimes | Example (original + meaning) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Intimate second person: **tū** (closest intimacy: mother, small child, God, best friend; or contempt to a stranger) and **tum** (familiar), each with its own verb agreement and imperative (**baiṭh** / **baiṭho**) | obligatory | तू कहाँ था? *tū kahā̃ thā?* "you-INTIMATE where was-M" = where were you? (to a brother); बैठ! *baiṭh!* "sit-INTIMATE" | no | **me** is "you" to everyone. Leaving out **nun** is neutral, not intimate, so the warmth of *tū* (and its insult when aimed at a stranger) has no channel. Imperatives: **tin goyu** is the same for a child and a stranger. |
| 1.2 | Gender of speaker and addressee in verb and adjective agreement | obligatory | मैं थक गई हूँ *maĩ thak gaī hū̃* "I tire go-PFV.F am" = I'm tired (speaker is a woman); तुम कैसी हो? *tum kaisī ho?* "you how-F are" = how are you (to a woman) | no | **lon** and **me** carry no gender; gender is a modifier "stated once when it matters", and there is no natural slot to state it for I/you. A Hindi listener learns the speaker's and addressee's gender from every verb; Apu only by an explicit statement (**lon gun futa**). |
| 1.3 | Kin terms fixing lineage (father's vs mother's side) and relative age | habitual | चाचा *chāchā* "father's younger brother", ताऊ *tāū* "father's elder brother", मामा *māmā* "mother's brother"; दादी *dādī* "father's mother" vs नानी *nānī* "mother's mother" | partly | Composed with **pi**: **faba lowu pi wede** "male sibling of father", **mole pi wede** "mother of father" vs **mole pi mole**. Relative age needs a comparative: **faba lowu ke pani pi wede** "older brother of father" (5 words for a 2-syllable kin term). The term can't be used as a one-word address ("Chacha!"). |
| 1.4 | Teknonymy and name avoidance (a wife not saying her husband's name; referring to elders via a child) | habitual (traditional families) | मुन्ने के पापा आ गए *munne ke pāpā ā gae* "Munna's papa come went-PL" = (my husband) is home | yes | Same strategy is available: **wede pi en muna obu** "the father of Munna came". |
| 1.5 | Solidarity vocative / filler **yār** (also mild exasperation) | habitual | यार, छोड़ ना *yār, chhoṛ nā* "friend, leave NA" = come on man, let it go | partly | **sonu** "friend" can be said as an address before a pause, but it's a literal content word; the solidarity filler and exasperated tone are lost. |
| 1.6 | Coaxing **nā** after an imperative (pleading, wheedling) | habitual | चलो ना *chalo nā* "go-FAM NA" = oh come on, let's go | partly | **so pen isi** "let's go"; **nun** is deference, not intimate pleading. No coaxing or insisting particle. |
| 1.7 | Confirmation tag **na?** | habitual | तुम आओगे न? *tum āoge na?* "you come-FUT-2 NA" = you'll come, won't you? | yes | Clause-final **min** tag: **me obu wuma, min.** |
| 1.8 | Polar question marker **kyā** at the start | habitual | क्या तुम चलोगे? *kyā tum chaloge?* "Q you go-FUT-2" = will you come along? | yes | **min me isi.** |
| 1.9 | Topic/contrast particle **to** ("as for X", implies others differ) | habitual | मैं तो जा रहा हूँ *maĩ to jā rahā hū̃* "I TO go PROG am" = well, *I'm* going (whatever you do) | partly | No topic or focus particle; contrast is carried only by an explicit parallel sentence (**lon isi. me la ...**). When the contrasting party is left unsaid, as with *to*, the implication disappears. |
| 1.10 | Exclusive/identity particle **hī** ("only", "the very", "right") | habitual | मैं ही करूँगा *maĩ hī karū̃gā* "I HI do-FUT" = I'll do it myself (no one else); वही *vahī* "that-HI" = that very one; अभी *abhī* "now-HI" = right now | partly | Exclusive sense: **na lon muge bu** "only I do it". The identity sense ("that very one", "right now") has only **kige** "same" or **ba**/**bi**, which mean other things. |
| 1.11 | Additive **bhī** ("also") | habitual | वो भी आया *vo bhī āyā* "he BHI came" = he came too | yes | **wame wan obu** "also he came". (Scalar "even" is 4.7.) |
| 1.12 | Compound verb **lenā** "take": action done for oneself / completed on one's own account | habitual | मैंने खाना खा लिया *maĩne khānā khā liyā* "I-ERG food eat take-PFV" = I've (gone ahead and) eaten | partly | **lon sin nawi pasu** gives completion; "for my own sake" only as a literal **li nen**. The light, self-directed nuance is lost. |
| 1.13 | Compound verb **denā** "give": action done outward / for another, finality | habitual | मैंने उसे बता दिया *maĩne use batā diyā* "I-ERG him tell give-PFV" = I've told him (done, settled) | partly | **lon sin iko wan**; benefit to another as a literal **li wan**. The "settled, off my hands" nuance is lost. |
| 1.14 | Compound verb **ḍālnā** "throw": thorough, violent or reckless completion, often with disapproval | sometimes | उसने सारा खाना खा डाला *usne sārā khānā khā ḍālā* "he-ERG all food eat throw-PFV" = he wolfed down all the food | partly | Thoroughness: **wan sin nawi pasu we** "ate all the food". Speaker's disapproval of the recklessness is lost. |
| 1.15 | Compound verb **baiṭhnā** "sit": did something rash, regrettable or unintended | sometimes | वो ग़लती कर बैठा *vo ġaltī kar baiṭhā* "he mistake do sat" = he went and made a mistake | no | **wan sin muge musu gagan** "he did a wrong thing" states the act only; no device marks "rashly, to his regret". |
| 1.16 | Non-volitional agent (**mujhse** "from me") vs deliberate agent (**maĩne**) | habitual (excuses, family friction) | मुझसे गिलास टूट गया *mujhse gilās ṭūṭ gayā* "me-FROM glass break went" = I broke the glass by accident vs मैंने गिलास तोड़ा *maĩne gilās toṛā* "I-ERG glass broke" = I broke it | partly | **lon sin alun ladon** covers both. Accident needs an added clause (**lon mu momu bu** "I didn't want it"), which is heavier and reads as an excuse rather than a grammatical fact. |
| 1.17 | Dative experiencer (feeling happens to you) vs agentive feeling (you do it) | obligatory (for feeling, hunger, knowing, liking) | मुझे गुस्सा आया *mujhe ġussā āyā* "to-me anger came" = anger came over me vs मैंने गुस्सा किया *maĩne ġussā kiyā* "I-ERG anger did" = I got angry (showed it, chose to) | partly | **lon feme bonko** / **lon gun bonko** "I feel / am angry". The came-over-me vs I-did-it contrast is lost. |
| 1.18 | Echo reduplication "X and that sort of thing" (casual vagueness) | habitual | खाना-वाना खाओगे? *khānā-vānā khāoge?* "food-ECHO eat-FUT-2" = want to eat something or other? | partly | **min me momu nawi pasu lu musu eni** "food or another thing". Meaning survives; the casual, offhand tone doesn't. |
| 1.19 | Full reduplication: intensity, gradualness, distributive, "which all" | habitual | गरम-गरम चाय *garam-garam chāy* "hot-hot tea" = piping hot tea; धीरे-धीरे *dhīre-dhīre* "slowly-slowly" = gradually; कौन-कौन आया? *kaun-kaun āyā?* "who-who came" = who all came? | partly | Doubling is reserved for listed words (**yo yo**, **mu mu**, **nun nun**, **nen nen**). Paraphrase: **bi tikun** "very hot", **ewo yu la obu** "which persons came". "Gradually" has no device (**gake** is just "slow"). |
| 1.20 | Terms of endearment, incl. gender-crossing **beṭā** "son" to a daughter | habitual | जान *jān* "life" = darling; बेटा, खाना खा लो *beṭā, khānā khā lo* "son, food eat take" = eat, sweetheart (to a girl) | partly | Content words can be used as address (**mala pi lon** "my heart", **tiwu** "child"), but there are no conventional endearments; each one is a fresh literal phrase. |
| 1.21 | Interjections with fixed emotional values | habitual | अरे *are* (surprise, attention), हाय *hāy* (dismay, longing), उफ़ *uf* (exasperation), छी *chhī* (disgust), वाह *vāh* (admiration) | partly | **ba** + a feeling word (**ba bebu** "ugh", **ba upe** "great"); outside the system only **mm** and **huh?**. Distinct conventional cries are lost. |
| 1.22 | Present habitual vs present progressive | obligatory | वो पढ़ता है *vo paṛhtā hai* "he read-HAB is" = he reads / studies vs वो पढ़ रहा है *vo paṛh rahā hai* "he read PROG is" = he's reading now | partly | Progressive: **wan le lilu**. Habitual has no marker; bare **wan lilu** covers both. |
| 1.23 | Subject and object drop, fragment answers | habitual | कहाँ जा रहे हो? — बाज़ार। *kahā̃ jā rahe ho? — bāzār.* "where go PROG are? — market" | yes | Rule 11: **isi tun kemu yu. — pu bika.** "going where? — market". |

## Register 2: hierarchical (boss, elder, official)

| # | Distinction | Obligatory/habitual/sometimes | Example (original + meaning) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Respectful second person **āp** with plural verb agreement | obligatory | आप कहाँ जा रहे हैं? *āp kahā̃ jā rahe haĩ?* "you-HON where go PROG-PL are-PL" = where are you going, sir/ma'am? | partly | **nun me le isi tun kemu yu.** **nun** marks deference, but per utterance and optionally; Hindi repeats respect on every pronoun and verb, and can't drop it. **nun** also marks the politeness of the act, so respect for the person and softness of the request can't be told apart. |
| 2.2 | Honorific plural for a respected third person (**ve**, plural verb; very formal third-person **āp**) | obligatory | पिताजी आ गए हैं *pitājī ā gae haĩ* "father-JI come went-PL are-PL" = Father has arrived; वे कहते हैं *ve kahte haĩ* "they say" = he (respected) says | partly | A role or kin word can be named once (**wede pi lon sin liwon**), but **wan** is the same for anyone, and **nun** is defined on the speech act, not on a referent. Respect for an absent person isn't carried past the first mention. |
| 2.3 | Honorific **-jī** on names, kin terms and titles | habitual | शर्मा जी *Sharmā-jī*; माताजी *mātā-jī* "mother-JI"; गुरुजी *guru-jī* | partly | Role word + name: **no tofo en mala** "Teacher Mara", plus **nun**. A plain surname with no role gets only **en** + name; there's no general honorific to attach. |
| 2.4 | Polite yes/no and polite "pardon?" | habitual | जी हाँ *jī hā̃* "JI yes"; जी नहीं *jī nahī̃*; जी? *jī?* = yes? / sorry? | partly | No yes/no; answers echo the verb, with **nun** first: **nun sin muge** "done (politely)". **huh?** has no polite form. |
| 2.5 | Graded request and imperative forms (**-iye**, deferred **-iyegā**, **kṛpayā** / **meharbānī karke** "please") | obligatory | बैठिए *baiṭhiye* "sit-HON"; बैठिएगा *baiṭhiyegā* "sit-HON-FUT" = do please sit; कृपया बैठें *kṛpayā baiṭhẽ* "please sit-SUBJ" | yes | **so goyu** / **so nun goyu** / **so nun nun goyu**; **tin nun**. The deferred-future flavour of *-iyegā* folds into politeness. |
| 2.6 | Minimizers that shrink the imposition (**zarā**, **thoṛā**, **ek minaṭ**) | habitual | ज़रा देखिए *zarā dekhiye* "a-little look-HON" = could you just take a look | partly | **so nun miya nin foke** "please look for a moment" is literal; there's no conventional minimizer, and **to** "rather" grades a property, not a request. |
| 2.7 | Permission questions | habitual | क्या मैं अंदर आ सकता हूँ? *kyā maĩ andar ā saktā hū̃?* "Q I inside come can-M am" = may I come in? | yes | **min nun lon gun efon inwa.** "am I allowed to enter, please?" |
| 2.8 | Honorific verbs (separate verbs used only of the respected person) | habitual (formal hosting, Urdu especially) | तशरीफ़ रखिए / تشریف رکھیے *tashrīf rakhiye* "honour place-HON" = please be seated; पधारिए *padhāriye* (Sanskritic) = please come; फ़रमाइए / فرمائیے *farmāiye* "command-HON" = please say | partly | **so nun nun goyu** carries the deference; the exalting vocabulary itself doesn't exist (one root per meaning). |
| 2.9 | Self-humbling and other-exalting vocabulary | sometimes (habitual in Lucknowi Urdu, formal letters) | ग़रीबख़ाना / غریب خانہ *ġarībḳhāna* "poor-house" = my home vs दौलतख़ाना / دولت خانہ *daulatḳhāna* "wealth-house" = your home; ख़ाकसार / خاکسار *ḳhāksār* "dust-like" = I (humbly); बंदा *bandā* "servant" = I | no | **guyi pi lon** vs **guyi pi me**: no humble/exalted pairs, no humble first person. |
| 2.10 | Titles of respect without a name or specific role | habitual | साहब *sāhab*, हुज़ूर / حضور *huzūr*, जनाब / جناب *janāb*, महोदय *mahoday* (Sanskritic), सर *sar* | partly | Only role words (**no wungu** "leader") plus **nun**. A generic "sir/madam" with no role has no word. |
| 2.11 | Fictive kinship for strangers and juniors, encoding relative age and gender | habitual | भैया, यहीं रोक दो *bhaiyā, yahī̃ rok do* "elder-brother, here-HI stop give" = (to a driver) stop right here; दीदी *dīdī*, आंटी *āṇṭī*, बेटा *beṭā* (to any younger person) | partly | **faba, so nun bamo** "sibling, please stop": **faba** has no relative age or gender, and the convention that kin address = friendly respect to a stranger doesn't exist. |
| 2.12 | Greetings graded by deference and signalling community | habitual | प्रणाम *praṇām* (to elders, often touching feet); नमस्ते *namaste*; आदाब / آداب *ādāb*; अस्सलामु अलैकुम *assalāmu alaikum*; farewell ख़ुदा हाफ़िज़ *ḳhudā hāfiz* vs फिर मिलेंगे *phir milẽge* "again we'll meet" | partly | **nun upe**, **upe leso**, **upe isi**: deference survives; the elder-specific greeting and the religious/community signal are lost. |
| 2.13 | Elder-to-junior blessing formulas (the reply to *praṇām*) | habitual | जीते रहो *jīte raho* "living stay" = live long; ख़ुश रहो *ḳhush raho* "happy stay" | partly | **lon ganin wun me sogi** "I hope you're happy" is a literal wish; no blessing formula, and no downward register for the elder's voice. |
| 2.14 | Soft refusal or deferral to a superior | habitual | देखते हैं *dekhte haĩ* "see-HAB are" = we'll see; कोशिश करूँगा *koshish karū̃gā* "try I-will-do" | yes | Same strategy: **de walo** "I'll try"; a clear refusal with **nun** is also available. |
| 2.15 | Subjunctive-conditional deference ("if you'd permit…") | habitual | अगर आप इजाज़त दें तो मैं चलूँ *agar āp ijāzat dẽ to maĩ chalū̃* "if you permission give-SUBJ then I go-SUBJ" = if you'll allow me, I'll take my leave | yes | **don me efon, de isi.** with **nun** as needed. |
| 2.16 | Apology as a preface to disagreeing or interrupting | habitual | माफ़ कीजिए, लेकिन… *māf kījiye, lekin…* "forgive do-HON, but" = excuse me, but…; क्षमा कीजिए *kṣamā kījiye* (Sanskritic) | yes | **nun sepi, un lon pume musu eni** "sorry, but I think another thing". |

## Register 3: written for strangers (instructions, technical and legal text)

| # | Distinction | Obligatory/habitual/sometimes | Example (original + meaning) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Polite impersonal directive (subjunctive **-ẽ**) | obligatory (signs, manuals) | कृपया दरवाज़ा बंद करें *kṛpayā darvāzā band karẽ* "please door closed do-SUBJ.PL" = please close the door | yes | **tin nun tapi beya.** |
| 3.2 | Negator chosen by mood: **na** (polite or subjunctive prohibition), **mat** (direct prohibition), **nahī̃** (statement) | obligatory | धूम्रपान न करें *dhūmrapān na karẽ* "smoking not do-SUBJ" = no smoking; मत छुओ *mat chhuo* "don't touch-FAM"; वह नहीं छूता *vah nahī̃ chhūtā* "he doesn't touch" | yes | One **mu**, with the mood carried by the speech-act particle: **tin nun mu sanne** vs **tin mu sanne** vs **wan mu sanne**. |
| 3.3 | Prohibited/permitted as a standing state | habitual | प्रवेश वर्जित है *praveś varjit hai* (Hindi) / داخلہ ممنوع ہے *dāḳhila mamnū hai* (Urdu) "entry forbidden is" | yes | **go mu gun efon inwa** "no one is allowed to enter". |
| 3.4 | Deontic future ("shall") in rules and law | obligatory (legal style) | प्रत्येक आवेदक फ़ॉर्म जमा करेगा *pratyek āvedak form jamā karegā* "each applicant form deposit will-do" = each applicant shall submit the form | yes | **no mido we keya sege mabo tan.** **keya** separates obligation from prediction, which the Hindi future doesn't. |
| 3.5 | Agentless passive (**jānā** passive) | obligatory (formal, legal, official) | आवेदन भेजा जाएगा *āvedan bhejā jāegā* "application sent will-go" = the application will be sent | yes | Drop the subject (rule 11) or state the result: **mabo gun sege** "the paper is sent". |
| 3.6 | Agent phrase in a passive (**dvārā** Sanskritic / **ke zariye** Urdu) | habitual | पुलिस द्वारा गिरफ़्तार *pulis dvārā giraftār* "police by arrested" | yes | Make the agent the subject: **palisu (loan) yidu wan.** |
| 3.7 | Conditions and limits: **yadi** / **agar** "if", **basharte ki** "provided that", **jab tak … nahī̃** "unless / until" | habitual | यदि भुगतान नहीं हुआ तो… *yadi bhugtān nahī̃ huā to…* "if payment not happened then" | yes | **don**, **don mu** "unless", **ya** "before / until". |
| 3.8 | Relative–correlative ("whoever …, that person …") | habitual (legal) | जो कोई भुगतान नहीं करेगा, वह दंडित होगा *jo koī bhugtān nahī̃ karegā, vah daṇḍit hogā* "who any payment not will-do, that-one punished will-be" | yes | Relative clause + the comma rule: **ewo man mu unfu, keya ...**; no resumptive "that one", but rule 15 does the job. |
| 3.9 | Step sequencing (**-kar** "having done", **uske bād** "after that") | obligatory | बटन दबाकर दो सेकंड रुकें *baṭan dabākar do sekaṇḍ rukẽ* "button press-having two seconds stop-SUBJ" = press the button, then wait 2 s | yes | **tin nun dandi batan (loan), wo, sefu sekandu sa.** |
| 3.10 | Purpose (**ke liye**, **tāki** + subjunctive) | habitual | सुरक्षा के लिए *surakṣā ke liye* "safety for"; ताकि कोई गिरे नहीं *tāki koī gire nahī̃* "so-that anyone fall-SUBJ not" | yes | **li** + verb: **li mu dosi**. |
| 3.11 | Mandatory vs recommended | obligatory (technical docs) | अनिवार्य / لازمی *anivārya* / *lāzmī* "mandatory" vs सलाह दी जाती है *salāh dī jātī hai* "advice given goes" = it is recommended | yes | **keya** vs **fon nun**. |
| 3.12 | Vocabulary stratum: Sanskritic (*tatsam*) vs Perso-Arabic vs English, signalling formality, field, and Hindi vs Urdu audience | obligatory (every formal content word picks one) | निर्देश *nirdesh* / ہدایت *hidāyat* / *instruction*; विद्युत *vidyut* / बिजली *bijlī* / *electricity*; शासन *shāsan* (Sanskritic) vs सरकार / حکومت *sarkār* / *hukūmat* (Perso-Arabic) "government" | no | One stratum: native roots plus English loans. A text can't signal formality, field or community through word choice. |
| 3.13 | Legal terms of art with fixed senses | habitual (legal) | अभियुक्त *abhiyukt* / ملزم *mulzim* "accused" vs दोषी *doṣī* / مجرم *mujrim* "convicted, guilty" | partly | Composed each time: **ewo man go oyi wun wan sin bunsi** "person someone says stole" vs **fen** / **kon yawu**; no fixed terms, so every clause grows. |
| 3.14 | Number words: lakh/crore grouping; fraction words **savā** 1¼, **ḍeṛh** 1½, **ḍhāī** 2½, **paune** "¼ less than" | habitual | ढाई लाख रुपये *ḍhāī lākh rupaye* "two-and-a-half lakh rupees" = ₹250,000 | yes | Exact via digits: **lupi (loan) 250 000**, **sa mimi te** 2.5. The one-word units are gone but the meaning is exact. |
| 3.15 | Object specificity with **-ko** (definite or specific object vs any) | habitual (obligatory for human objects) | किताब पढ़ो *kitāb paṛho* "book read" = read (a book, books) vs किताब को पढ़ो *kitāb ko paṛho* "book KO read" = read the book (the one we mean) | partly | No articles. **tan**/**ben** can point (**tin lilu ingo ben**), but a specific-not-pointed-at object ("the book") is unmarked. |
| 3.16 | Locatives: **mẽ** "in", **par** "on (in contact)", **tak** "up to", **ke andar** "inside" | obligatory | डिब्बे में रखें *ḍibbe mẽ rakhẽ* "box in put-SUBJ" vs डिब्बे पर रखें *ḍibbe par rakhẽ* "box on put-SUBJ" | partly | **mon** "at" + space nouns: **yaga bu mon bese pi yufa** "at the inside of the bag". "On (touching)" has no word: **bige** is "above". **tun** covers "up to". |
| 3.17 | Definition / restatement (**arthāt**, **yānī**) | habitual | यानी *yānī* "that is"; अर्थात् *arthāt* (Sanskritic) | yes | **tan yofu ...** "this means …". |

## Register 4: persuasive (argument, opinion, complaint)

## Top gaps

In order of how often speakers need them:

1. **Graded second person (tū / tum / āp) with matching agreement** (registers 1 and 2, rows 1.1, 2.1). Every addressed utterance in Hindi–Urdu fixes the speaker's relation to the listener. Apu has one **me**: no intimate end at all, and the respectful end is the optional, per-utterance **nun**, which also marks politeness of the act.
2. **Gender in agreement** (registers 1 and 5, rows 1.2, 5.11). Every finite verb shows the gender of the speaker, the addressee or the third person, which also tracks characters in narrative. Apu has no slot for it on **lon**/**me** and relies on topic-based **wan**/**yi** for third persons.
3. **Topic and focus particles to / hī / scalar bhī** (registers 1 and 4, rows 1.9, 1.10, 4.6, 4.7). Among the most frequent words in speech; they carry contrast, exclusivity, "exactly" and "even". Apu has only **na** "only" and **wame** "also"; contrast needs a parallel sentence, and scalar "even" has no device.
4. **Compound (vector) verbs: lenā, denā, ḍālnā, baiṭhnā, paṛnā, uṭhnā** (registers 1 and 5, rows 1.12–1.15, 5.5). They mark for-whom, finality, recklessness, regret and suddenness on a large share of everyday verbs. Apu keeps only "done" (**sin**) and "start" (**tuko**).
5. **Habitual aspect** (registers 1 and 5, rows 1.22, 5.1). Hindi forces habitual vs progressive vs perfective in every clause. Apu marks progressive (**le**) and done (**sin**) but leaves habitual unmarked and merged with the bare verb.
6. **Respect for third persons** (registers 2 and 5, rows 2.2, 2.3, 2.10, 5.12). Honorific plural, **-jī**, untied titles (**sāhab**, **huzūr**) and "the late". Apu can name a role once, but **wan** and **nun** don't carry respect for someone absent.
7. **Volitionality: accidental vs deliberate agent; feeling that happens to you vs feeling you do** (registers 1 and 4, rows 1.16, 1.17). Central to excuses and blame. Apu has one construction for both; the difference needs an added clause.
8. **Vocabulary strata and humble/exalting vocabulary** (registers 2 and 3, rows 2.8, 2.9, 3.12). Sanskritic vs Perso-Arabic choice signals formality, field and Hindi vs Urdu audience; Urdu courtesy depends on paired humble/exalted words. Apu has one stratum and one root per meaning.
