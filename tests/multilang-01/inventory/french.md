# French: register inventory against Apu Pi We

Source language: French (metropolitan standard, with common colloquial forms).
Grammar checked against: `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv`, `data/compounds.tsv` only.

Ratings: **yes** = the meaning can be said with existing grammar and roots; **partly** = the core meaning gets through but something French marks is lost; **no** = no way to carry it beyond a long paraphrase, or no way at all.
Frequency: **obligatory** = the grammar forces a choice; **habitual** = speakers mark it in most relevant utterances of this register; **occasional** = a marked choice speakers reach for sometimes.

Glosses: 2SG/2PL = second person singular/plural, SBJV = subjunctive, COND = conditional, IMPF = imperfect, PS = passé simple, F = feminine, M = masculine, DEF/INDEF = definite/indefinite article, CL = clitic pronoun.

Totals: 116 items. 60 yes, 45 partly, 11 no.

## 1. Close and familiar (friends, family)

| # | distinction | obligatory/habitual/occasional | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | comment, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | *tu* as a marker of intimacy and solidarity (not just "you") | obligatory | *Tu viens ?* "you.2SG come?" | partly | **min me obu.** "me" is "you" to anyone; politeness can be added with **nun** but there is no way to mark closeness itself. A standing relationship becomes a per-utterance choice, and the solidarity signal of *tu* is lost. |
| 1.2 | *on* = "we" in speech | obligatory (replaces *nous*) | *On y va ?* "one there goes?" = shall we go? | yes | **so pen isi.** "let's go". Apu Pi We is more precise: it forces inclusive **pen** vs exclusive **din**, which *on* leaves open. |
| 1.3 | *vous* plural vs *tu* singular | obligatory | *Vous venez, les gars ?* "you.2PL come, the guys?" | yes | **min me la obu.** "me la" = you (plural). |
| 1.4 | Casual grammar variants as register signals: dropped *ne*, intonation questions, *ça* for *cela*, *t'as* | habitual | *J'sais pas.* "I know not" (no *ne*) | no | **lon mu lugo.** There is one grammar for all registers, so the sentence is identical in casual and formal speech. The "relaxed" signal is lost. |
| 1.5 | Left/right dislocation: *moi, je...*, *ton frère, il...* (topic and contrast) | habitual (very frequent) | *Moi, je reste.* "me, I stay" | partly | **me isi. lon piyi.** Contrast falls out of parallel sentences (rule: no focus device yet). No way to promote a topic without fronting it as a full noun, and a bare noun can't be fronted (only scene-setting phrases can open a sentence). |
| 1.6 | Clitics *en*, *y*, *le/la* (partitive, locative, anaphoric) | obligatory | *J'en veux deux.* "I CL.of-it want two" | yes | **lon momu sa.** Rule 11 drops what context supplies; **bu**, **wan**, **tan/ben** cover the rest. |
| 1.7 | Gender of referents: *il/elle*, agreement that reveals the speaker's or referent's gender | obligatory | *Elle est contente.* "she is happy.F" | partly | **wan gun sogi.** Gender is an optional modifier (**futa**, **lowu**), so it is lost unless stated. *Je suis contente* reveals the speaker is a woman; Apu Pi We never does this unprompted. |
| 1.8 | Tag *hein ?*, *non ?* (seeking agreement) | habitual | *C'est bon, hein ?* "it is good, eh?" | yes | **ben gun upe, min.** Clause-final **min** is a tag. |
| 1.9 | Utterance-final *quoi* (sums up, "you know what I mean", vagueness) | habitual | *C'était nul, quoi.* "it was rubbish, what" | no | **ben sin yomo.** Nothing marks "that's the gist, I won't elaborate". |
| 1.10 | *du coup* (consequence, discourse transition) | habitual | *Du coup, on fait quoi ?* "of-the blow, one does what?" = so, what do we do? | yes | **gi tan, pen muge yu.** "because of this, we do what" |
| 1.11 | *genre* (approximation, quotative, hedge) | habitual among young speakers | *Il y avait genre trente personnes.* "it there had kind thirty persons" | partly | **gun ewo fu ka yawu.** "about" with numbers is **fu**. The quotative use (*il m'a fait genre « non »* "he me made kind 'no'") has no particle: quotes need a say-verb, and the "roughly this" tone of the quote is lost. |
| 1.12 | Turn-starters and hesitation: *bah*, *ben*, *bon*, *euh*, *eh bien* (resignation, mild objection, holding the floor) | habitual (very frequent) | *Bah, je sais pas.* "bah, I know not" | no | **lon mu lugo.** Only **mm** "heard you" and **huh?** "say again" exist outside the system. Resigned, objecting or floor-holding openers have no form. |
| 1.13 | *en fait* (correcting an expectation) | habitual | *En fait, c'est pas lui.* "in fact, it is not him" | partly | **un, ben mu gun wan.** "but" or **kon yawu** can carry the correction; no counter-expectation (mirative) marker. |
| 1.14 | *tu vois*, *tu sais* (appeal to shared ground) | habitual | *C'est compliqué, tu vois.* "it is complicated, you see" | partly | **ben gun gofa, min me lafi.** Spelled out as a real question, it asks rather than assumes the shared ground. |
| 1.15 | *bref*, *voilà* (wrapping up, skipping ahead, "that's it") | habitual | *Bref, j'ai démissionné.* "brief, I have resigned" | no | **lon sin kusa doku.** No word marks "to cut it short" or "and that's that". |
| 1.16 | Emotive interjections: *aïe*, *ouf*, *beurk*, *bof*, *oh là là* | habitual | *Beurk !* "yuck" | partly | **ba bebu.** "(exclaim) disgust", **ba wekun** "ouch". Works where a root exists, but there are no conventional one-word cries, and relief (*ouf*) or indifference (*bof*) have no root. |
| 1.17 | Expletives and swearing: *putain*, *merde* | habitual (in some groups) | *Putain, j'ai raté le bus !* "whore, I have missed the bus" | partly | **ba bi yomo, ensu sin kusa.** "(exclaim) very bad, the bus left". The emotion gets through with **ba**/**bi**; the taboo-breaking force can't, since no word may sound like an offensive word. |
| 1.18 | Familiar vocabulary, slang, verlan, clipping: *bouffer*, *mec*, *boulot*, *resto*, *ouf* (= fou) | habitual | *J'ai bouffé avec mon pote.* "I have scoffed with my mate" | no | **lon nawi gan sonu pi lon.** One root per meaning, no register doublets. |
| 1.19 | Terms of endearment: *mon chéri*, *ma puce*, *mon cœur* | habitual (couples, parents) | *Ça va, ma puce ?* "it goes, my flea?" | partly | **min me upe.** Can be described (**ewo man lon tana** "person I like") but there is no affectionate address form. Nicknames as names with **en** work. |
| 1.20 | Familiar kin address: *maman*, *papa*, *mamie*, *tonton* | habitual | *Mamie arrive !* "granny arrives" | partly | **mole** and **wede** serve as address. Grandparents, uncles and aunts need phrases (**mole pi mole** "mother of mother"), which also force a choice of side French doesn't make; no affectionate forms. |
| 1.21 | Informal greetings and leave-takings: *salut*, *coucou*, *à plus*, *bisous* | habitual | *Coucou ! ... Bisous, à plus !* "peekaboo ... kisses, at more" | partly | **upe. ... upe isi, nagu me wo.** Greeting and farewell exist, but one form for every register; *bisous* (affectionate sign-off) has no form. |
| 1.22 | Short echo fragments: *moi aussi*, *moi non plus*, *pas moi* | habitual | *Moi non plus.* "me not more" = me neither | yes | **lon wame mu.** Rule 11 allows fragments; **wame** "also" works like **na**. |
| 1.23 | *si* vs *oui* (yes, contradicting a negative question) | obligatory | *Tu viens pas ? — Si !* "you come not? — yes(contra)!" | yes | **min me mu obu. — obu.** Echo answers remove the ambiguity, just as *si* does. |
| 1.24 | Teasing, irony: *je rigole*, *c'est ça, oui* | habitual | *Je te déteste... je rigole.* "I you hate... I joke" | yes | **a lon mu tana me.** **a** = not literal; **a a** = the whole turn. More explicit than French. |
| 1.25 | Colloquial intensifiers: *trop*, *grave*, *vachement*, *super* | habitual | *C'est trop bien !* "it is too good" | yes | **ba ben gun bi upe.** The colloquial colour is lost (one **bi** for all registers). |
| 1.26 | Suggestions and invitations: *ça te dit de...*, *on se fait un...* | habitual | *Ça te dit d'aller manger ?* "that you says of to-go eat?" | yes | **min me momu isi nawi.** or **so pen isi nawi.** |
| 1.27 | Request softeners: *s'te plaît*, *un peu*, *donc* (*viens voir un peu*) | habitual | *Passe-moi le sel, s'te plaît.* "pass-me the salt, if-it-you-pleases" | yes | **so nun neke lon bunwu.** *un peu* and *donc* as friendly softeners collapse into **nun**, which also means deference. |

## 2. Hierarchical (to a superior, an elder, an official)

| # | distinction | obligatory/habitual/occasional | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | comment, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | *vous* of respect (singular) | obligatory | *Vous venez, Madame ?* "you.2PL come, madam?" | partly | **min nun me obu.** Deference is carried by **nun**, but per utterance. French marks a fixed relationship on every verb and pronoun; leaving out **nun** once means nothing in particular. |
| 2.2 | Asymmetric *tu/vous* and the switch (*on se tutoie ?*) | habitual | *On peut se tutoyer ?* "one can each-other tu-say?" | partly | **pen mu wupi apu "nun".** "we don't need the word 'nun'". Can be negotiated in words, but there is no pronoun to switch, and asymmetric power (boss says *tu*, employee *vous*) has no marker. |
| 2.3 | Honorific *Monsieur/Madame* (+ surname or alone), gendered | obligatory | *Bonjour, Madame Mala.* "good-day, madam Mala" | partly | **upe, en mala.** A name with **en**, plus **nun**. There is no general honorific address word; *Madame* alone ("excuse me, madam") has no form. |
| 2.4 | Office and professional titles: *Monsieur le Directeur*, *Docteur*, *Maître* | habitual | *Bonjour, Monsieur le Directeur.* "good-day, sir the director" | yes | **upe, no wungu en mala.** A role word before the name, as in **no tofo en mala**; titles are built with **no** + verb. |
| 2.5 | Polite conditional: *je voudrais*, *pourriez-vous* | habitual | *Je voudrais parler au directeur.* "I would-want speak to-the director" | yes | **nun, lon momu kegu gan no wungu.** **nun** carries the politeness. The hypothetical distancing is gone, by design. |
| 2.6 | Softening imperfect: *je voulais vous demander* | habitual | *Je voulais vous demander un service.* "I wanted.IMPF you ask a favour" | yes | **nun, lon momu wun me yema lon.** Same as 2.5; the softening goes into **nun**. |
| 2.7 | Indirect request as a question: *est-ce que vous pourriez...* | habitual | *Est-ce que vous pourriez m'aider ?* "is-it that you could me help?" | yes | **min nun me lenu yema lon.** Allowed (watch list: questions used as politeness). |
| 2.8 | Formal imperative: *veuillez*, *je vous prie de* | habitual (writing, announcements) | *Veuillez patienter.* "want.IMP.2PL wait" | yes | **tin nun sefu.** or **so nun nun sefu.** |
| 2.9 | Deferential performatives: *je me permets de*, *j'ai l'honneur de*, *je vous saurais gré de* | habitual (formal letters) | *Je me permets de vous écrire...* "I myself permit of you write" | partly | **nun nun, lon tusi tun me...** The politeness level gets through; the formula of self-effacement (asking permission to speak) doesn't. |
| 2.10 | Graded closing formula in letters | obligatory (formal letter) | *Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.* "I you pray of accept, madam, the expression of my greetings distinguished" | partly | **nun nun nun, upe isi.** The grading maps onto doubled **nun**, but there is no fixed closing, and a French reader expects one. |
| 2.11 | Vocative opening by office: *Monsieur le Maire,* | obligatory (letter) | *Monsieur le Maire, ...* "sir the mayor" | yes | **no wungu en mala, ...** Role word + name. |
| 2.12 | Graded thanks: *merci infiniment*, *je vous remercie par avance* | habitual | *Je vous remercie par avance.* "I you thank by advance" | yes | **nun, lon lota me ya.** **ya** alone = beforehand; **bi** for degree. |
| 2.13 | Apology for imposing: *excusez-moi de vous déranger* | habitual | *Excusez-moi de vous déranger.* "excuse.2PL-me of you disturb" | yes | **nun nun, sepi.** There is no root for "disturb", but the act (apology, with deference) gets through. |
| 2.14 | Reply to thanks: *je vous en prie*, *de rien*, *avec plaisir* | habitual | *Je vous en prie.* "I you of-it pray" | partly | **ben gun upe.** "that's good" can do the job, but there's no routine; the grammar lists greeting, farewell, thanks and sorry only. |
| 2.15 | Asking permission: *puis-je*, *permettez-moi* | habitual | *Puis-je entrer ?* "can-I enter?" | yes | **min nun lon gun efon inwa.** "May" = **gun efon**, kept apart from ability. |
| 2.16 | Politeness hedges: *il me semble que*, *sauf erreur de ma part* | habitual | *Sauf erreur de ma part, j'ai payé.* "save error of my part, I have paid" | yes | **nun, kon mape lon sin unfu.** Works, but using **kon** for politeness is the drift risk on the watch list. |
| 2.17 | Formal vocabulary doublets: *demeurer* for *habiter*, *se rendre* for *aller*, *effectuer* for *faire* | habitual | *Je demeure à Londres.* "I dwell at London" | no | **lon nume mon en landan.** Same sentence as the everyday one; the formal colour is lost. |
| 2.18 | Formal grammar variants: subject inversion, keeping *ne*, *nous* not *on*, *cela* not *ça* | habitual | *Puis-je ?* vs *Je peux ?* "can-I?" vs "I can?" | no | **min lon lenu.** One form; the register signal is lost. |
| 2.19 | Respectful reference to a third person: *Monsieur le Directeur vous recevra* | habitual | *Monsieur le Directeur vous recevra demain.* "sir the director you will-receive tomorrow" | partly | **no wungu en mala nagu me wuma.** The role word gives some respect, but **nun** is sentence-level and aimed at the listener; nothing honours the person talked about. |
| 2.20 | Subjunctive in wish and necessity clauses | obligatory | *Je souhaite que vous veniez.* "I wish that you come.SBJV" | yes | **nun, lon momu wun me obu.** The verb **momu** already says "not real yet"; the French subjunctive adds little meaning here. |
| 2.21 | Softened disagreement with a superior: *je ne suis pas tout à fait d'accord* | habitual | *Je ne suis pas tout à fait de votre avis.* "I not am not all at fact of your opinion" | yes | **nun, lon to mu tayu.** "(polite) I rather disagree". |

## 3. Writing for a stranger (instructions, technical and legal texts)

| # | distinction | obligatory/habitual/occasional | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | comment, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Infinitive instructions with no addressee | habitual (genre norm) | *Agiter avant emploi.* "shake.INF before use" | yes | **tin kobi bu ya bone.** No subject needed after **tin**; **ya** takes a verb naming the act. ("shake" becomes **kobi** "move".) |
| 3.2 | Addressing the reader (*vous*, imperative) vs impersonal infinitive | habitual | *Ouvrez la porte* vs *Ouvrir la porte* "open.2PL the door" vs "open.INF the door" | yes | **tin benbi beya.** vs **me keya benbi beya.** |
| 3.3 | Impersonal constructions: *il faut*, *il est interdit de*, *il convient de* | habitual | *Il est interdit d'entrer.* "it is forbidden of enter" | yes | **go mu gun efon inwa.** "anyone is not allowed to enter". **go keya...** = *il faut*. |
| 3.4 | Legal present with the force of obligation | habitual (legal) | *Le locataire paie le loyer.* "the tenant pays the rent" (= must pay) | yes | **no sanwa keya unfu.** More explicit than French, which leaves "pays" vs "must pay" to convention. |
| 3.5 | Graded duty: *doit*, *peut*, *est autorisé à*, *est tenu de* | obligatory (legal) | *Le locataire peut résilier.* "the tenant can terminate" | yes | **no sanwa gun efon bamo sanwa.** Ability (**lenu**), permission (**gun efon**) and duty (**keya**) are kept apart; French *pouvoir* blurs the first two. |
| 3.6 | Recommendation vs obligation: *il est recommandé de* vs *obligatoire* | habitual | *Il est recommandé de se laver les mains.* "it is recommended of self wash the hands" | yes | **fon pensi kiti.** vs **tin pensi kiti.** |
| 3.7 | Passive and *se*-passive (focus on the patient) | habitual | *Le formulaire doit être rempli.* "the form must be filled"; *Se conserve au frais.* "self keeps at-the cool" | partly | **go keya tusi mon mabo tan.** There is no passive: the patient can't become the subject. Resulting state with **gun** + verb works (**gun tapi** "is closed"); event passives need **go** or a dropped subject. |
| 3.8 | Generic *on* | habitual | *On mélange ensuite la farine.* "one mixes then the flour" | yes | **go...** or an instruction with no subject. |
| 3.9 | Nominalized actions: *la mise en service*, *après réception du paiement* | habitual | *après réception du paiement* "after reception of-the payment" | yes | **wo dunu ge unfu** "after getting what is paid". Any verb serves as a noun by position. |
| 3.10 | Articles: definite/indefinite/partitive/generic (*le client*, *un client*, *du sucre*) | obligatory | *Un client a appelé.* "a.INDEF customer has called" | partly | **no bika sin yebin.** "(a/the) customer called". **tan/ben**, **in** "some" and **la** exist, but nothing marks new vs known referent, or generic vs specific. |
| 3.11 | Defined terms and legal anaphora: *ci-après « le Locataire »*, *ledit*, *le présent contrat* | habitual (legal) | *ledit locataire* "the-said tenant" | partly | **no sanwa tan** "this tenant". A defined label can only be set up with a sentence (**suso pi wan gun "no sanwa"**); no compact *ci-après* / *ledit*. |
| 3.12 | Conditions: *si*, *à défaut de*, *le cas échéant* | obligatory | *À défaut de paiement, le bail est résilié.* "at default of payment, the lease is terminated" | yes | **don no sanwa mu unfu, sanwa fugi.** *le cas échéant* = **don ben muwo** "if that happens". |
| 3.13 | Exceptions: *sauf*, *hormis*, *à l'exception de* | habitual | *Tous sauf les enfants.* "all except the children" | partly | **ewo we, un mu tiwu la.** There's no "except"; **don mu** covers *sauf si*, but a noun exception needs a clause. |
| 3.14 | *et/ou* vs exclusive *soit... soit* | habitual | *soit par écrit, soit par téléphone* "either by written, either by phone" | yes | **lu tusi lu yebin** (one, not both); plain **lu** allows both. |
| 3.15 | Concessive: *bien que* (+ subjunctive), *toutefois*, *néanmoins* | habitual | *Bien qu'il pleuve, ...* "well that it rain.SBJV" = although it rains | partly | **gun gegi, un pen isi tun aban.** Only **un** "but"; the concessive frame can't be put first as a subordinate clause, so "although" and "but" are not told apart. |
| 3.16 | Clarifying connectors: *c'est-à-dire*, *notamment*, *par exemple* | habitual | *notamment les enfants* "notably the children" | partly | **tan yofu wun ...** "this means that ..." covers *c'est-à-dire*; there is no "for example" or "in particular". |
| 3.17 | Time limits: *dans un délai de 30 jours à compter de* | habitual (legal) | *dans un délai de trente jours* "in a delay of thirty days" | yes | **tin unfu nin dili ka yawu wo dunu ge tusi.** |
| 3.18 | Gender-generic reference (*l'utilisateur*, inclusive writing *les candidat·e·s*) | obligatory (some choice must be made) | *les candidat·e·s* "the candidates.M·F" | yes | **no waka la.** Neutral by default; the French debate doesn't arise. |
| 3.19 | Warnings: *Attention*, *Danger*, *Important* | habitual | *Attention : surface chaude.* "attention: surface hot" | yes | **yo, musu tan gun tikun.** **yo yo** for danger; **bawe** "important". |
| 3.20 | Numbered references, cross-references and layout: *art. 3*, *ci-dessus*, colon, semicolon, parentheses | habitual | *voir l'art. 3 ci-dessus* "see the art. 3 here-above" | partly | **nagu wabun pi ka.** Labels work (**pi** + number). Only `, . " "` are allowed, so lists, parentheticals and abbreviations need words; *ci-dessus* only as **ya** "beforehand". |
| 3.21 | Quantity bounds: *au moins*, *au plus*, *tout*, *aucun* | obligatory (legal) | *âgé d'au moins 18 ans* "aged of at-the least 18 years" | yes | **ewo man nume pa fawu yawu kefi.** **pa**, **mu ke se**, **we**, **fa**. |
| 3.22 | Salutation to an unknown reader: *Madame, Monsieur,* | obligatory (letter) | *Madame, Monsieur,* "madam, sir" | partly | **nun, upe.** Gender doesn't need covering, but there is no fixed letter salutation. |
| 3.23 | Specific vs non-specific (subjunctive in a relative clause) | occasional | *Nous cherchons quelqu'un qui sache cuisiner.* "we seek someone who know.SBJV cook" | partly | **din walo geno ewo man lenu siten.** Whether such a person exists is left open. **bo** "unspecified" exists, but its use inside a noun phrase isn't defined. |
| 3.24 | Role nouns in contracts: *le bailleur*, *le preneur*, *l'acheteur* | obligatory (legal) | *le bailleur et le preneur* "the lessor and the lessee" | yes | **no ibe guyi yen no sanwa.** "one who has the house and one who rents". |

## 4. Persuasive (argument, opinion, complaint)

| # | distinction | obligatory/habitual/occasional | example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | comment, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Opinion markers: *à mon avis*, *je trouve que*, *selon moi* | habitual | *À mon avis, c'est une erreur.* "at my opinion, it is a mistake" | yes | **lon pume wun ben gun gagan.** |
| 4.2 | Belief vs doubt in the verb's mood (*je pense que* + indicative vs *je ne pense pas que* + subjunctive) | obligatory | *Je ne pense pas qu'il vienne.* "I not think not that he come.SBJV" | yes | **kon da wan obu.** or **lon mu pume wun wan obu.** The **kon** scale grades doubt more finely. |
| 4.3 | Epistemic adverbs: *peut-être*, *sans doute* (= probably), *certainement* | habitual | *Il a sans doute raison.* "he has without doubt reason" = probably right | yes | **kon oma wan oyi feko.** Fixes the French trap where *sans doute* means "probably", not "certainly". |
| 4.4 | Hearsay and inference: *il paraît que*, *apparemment* | habitual | *Il paraît qu'il démissionne.* "it appears that he resigns" | yes | **fen wan kusa doku.** **san** for inference. |
| 4.5 | Concession then counter: *certes..., mais*; *il est vrai que..., mais* | habitual | *Certes, c'est difficile, mais c'est possible.* "certainly, it is difficult, but it is possible" | yes | **kon yawu ben gun gofa, un pen lenu muge ben.** |
| 4.6 | Rhetorical question | habitual | *Tu crois vraiment que je vais payer ?* "you believe truly that I go pay?" | partly | **a min me pume wun lon unfu.** **a** flags the question as non-literal, but how **a** combines with **min** isn't specified. |
| 4.7 | Emphatic negation: *pas du tout*, *absolument pas*, *jamais de la vie* | habitual | *Absolument pas !* "absolutely not" | yes | **lon mu mu tayu.** Doubled **mu**; *jamais* = **mon muyi fa**. |
| 4.8 | Intensifiers and emphasis: *vraiment*, *absolument*, *tout à fait* | habitual | *C'est absolument inacceptable.* "it is absolutely unacceptable" | yes | **ba ben gun bi yomo.** |
| 4.9 | Contrastive focus and clefts: *c'est lui qui...*, *ce que je veux, c'est...* | habitual | *C'est lui qui a commencé.* "it is him who has started" | partly | **ewo man tuko, gun wan.** A pseudo-cleft works, with a pause; there's no focus particle (none yet, by design), so it's heavier than French. |
| 4.10 | *quand même* (insisting despite something) | habitual (very frequent) | *C'est quand même bizarre.* "it is when same weird" = it IS weird, though | no | No word for "despite everything, still"; **le** "still" is time only. |
| 4.11 | *de toute façon*, *en tout cas* (whatever the case) | habitual | *De toute façon, c'est trop tard.* "of all manner, it is too late" | partly | **don me tayu lu mu tayu, ben sin fugi.** "whether or not" has to be spelled out; no single "anyway". |
| 4.12 | Adding an argument: *d'ailleurs*, *en plus*, *de plus* | habitual | *En plus, il ment.* "in more, he lies" | yes | **wame, wan oyi ge mu feko.** "also, he says untrue things" (no root for "lie"). |
| 4.13 | *puisque* (cause both sides already accept) vs *parce que* (new cause) | habitual | *Puisque tu sais tout, réponds.* "since you know all, answer" | partly | **gi me lugo musu we, tin febo.** One **gi**; the sarcastic "given that, as we all know" is lost. |
| 4.14 | Reproach in the past conditional: *tu aurais pu/dû...* | habitual | *Tu aurais pu me prévenir !* "you would-have could me warn" | partly | **ba, me sin lenu iko lon. me mu iko.** Counterfactual exists only inside **don kon po**; the one-clause reproach must be split into two. |
| 4.15 | Indignation: indignant conditional, *quel culot !* | habitual | *Et il faudrait que je paie, moi ?* "and it would-need that I pay.SBJV, me?" | partly | **a min lon keya unfu.** Disbelief via **a** + question; no indignant mood. |
| 4.16 | Exhortation and assumption: *soyons clairs*, *admettons que* | habitual | *Admettons que tu aies raison.* "let's-admit that you have.SBJV reason" | yes | **don me oyi feko, ...**; **so pen oyi feko.** "let's speak truly". |
| 4.17 | Formal complaint formulas: *je me vois dans l'obligation de*, *force est de constater* | habitual (written complaint) | *Je me vois dans l'obligation de résilier.* "I myself see in the obligation of terminate" | partly | **nun, lon keya bamo sanwa.** The content gets through; the formal-indignant tone, which tells the reader "this is serious", doesn't. |
| 4.18 | Ultimatum: *faute de quoi*, *sans quoi* | habitual (complaints) | *Faute de quoi, je saisirai la justice.* "fault of which, I will-seize the justice" | yes | **don me mu unfu, de iko no wungu.** "(if you don't pay, I will tell the leader)". There is no root for courts. |
| 4.19 | Understatement (litotes): *c'est pas faux*, *je ne suis pas contre* | habitual | *C'est pas faux.* "it is not false" | yes | **ben mu gun gagan.** Said literally; the understated agreement still works as an inference. |
| 4.20 | Insults and labels: *crétin*, *abruti* | occasional | *C'est un crétin.* "it is a cretin" | partly | **wan gun bi mu gole.** Description, not insult; no word carries offence. |
| 4.21 | Agreement responses: *tout à fait*, *bien sûr que si*, *pas d'accord* | habitual | *Bien sûr que si !* "well sure that yes(contra)" | yes | **ba, obu.** / **tayu.** / **mu tayu.** |
| 4.22 | Inclusive "we" in argument: *on ne peut pas accepter ça* | habitual | *On ne peut pas accepter ça.* "one not can not accept that" | yes | **pen mu lenu tayu gan ben.** |

| 5.1 | *passé simple* (written, literary) vs *passé composé* (oral) | obligatory (genre choice) | *Il entra.* "he entered.PS" vs *Il est entré.* "he is entered" | no | **wan inwa.** The literary register signal is lost. |

| 5.4 | Earlier past (*plus-que-parfait*) | obligatory | *Quand je suis arrivé, il était parti.* "when I am arrived, he was left" | yes | **nin lon liwon, wan sin kusa.** **sin** = done. |
| 5.5 | Future in the past (*conditionnel temporel*) | obligatory | *Il ne savait pas qu'il ne reviendrait jamais.* "he not knew not that he not would-return never" | yes | **wan mu lugo wun mon muyi fa, wan sigo.** Tense is optional; **win** is available. |

## Top gaps

Ranked by how often French speakers need them.

1. **Discourse particles and turn-starters** (*bah, ben, bon, euh, quoi, bref, voilà*; register 1, items 1.9, 1.12, 1.15): no. They appear in nearly every casual turn, and only **mm** / **huh?** exist.
2. **Topic dislocation and contrastive focus** (*moi, je...*; *c'est lui qui...*; registers 1 and 4, items 1.5, 4.9): partly. Very frequent in spoken French. There is no topic slot or focus device; contrast relies on parallel sentences.
3. **A standing relationship marker, *tu/vous*** (registers 1 and 2, items 1.1, 2.1, 2.2): partly. Every second-person reference marks it in French. **nun** marks politeness per utterance, but not intimacy, distance or a power imbalance.
4. **Definiteness: articles** (*un/le/du*; registers 3 and 5, items 3.10, 5.12): partly. Forced on every noun in French. New vs known and generic vs specific are left to context.
5. **Gender of referents** (*il/elle*, agreement; registers 1 and 5, items 1.7, 5.13): partly. Obligatory in French and used for keeping track of referents; here it is optional, and tracking falls to **wan/yi**.
6. **Register-marking variants** (familiar/formal vocabulary, dropped *ne*, inversion, *passé simple*; registers 1, 2 and 5, items 1.4, 1.18, 2.17, 2.18, 5.1): no. The same sentence serves every register, so "casual" and "official" can't be signalled through form.
7. **Imperfective background, habitual and recent past** (*imparfait*, *venir de*; register 5, items 5.2, 5.3, 5.20): partly. **le** covers ongoing only; habitual and "just" must be spelled out.
8. **Passive (patient as subject)** (registers 3 and 5, items 3.7, 5.11): partly. Common in instructions and news; only the resulting state (**gun** + verb) or a generic **go** subject is available.
