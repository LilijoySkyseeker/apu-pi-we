# Spanish: register inventory against Apu Pi We

Sources read: `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv`, `data/compounds.tsv`. Apu Pi We (APW) examples use only those words, plus loan/name adaptation where marked.

Varieties: ES = Spain, MX = Mexico, RP = Río de la Plata (Argentina, Uruguay), CO = Colombia, CR = Costa Rica, CL = Chile, VE = Venezuela, AM = the Americas generally.

Glosses: IND indicative, SBJV subjunctive, IPFV imperfect, PFV preterite, COND conditional, FUT future, INF infinitive, IMP imperative, DIM diminutive, AUG augmentative, PEJ pejorative, DAT dative clitic, REFL reflexive/se, F feminine, FORM usted-form, INF.2SG tú-form, VOS vos-form.

Status column: **obligatory** = the grammar forces a choice; **habitual** = speakers mark it routinely in that register; **occasional** = available, used for effect.

APW column: **yes** = the meaning can be said; **partly** = some of it can be said, and the last column says what's lost; **no** = no device carries it.

## 1. Close and informal (friends, family)

| # | Distinction | Obl./hab./occ. | Example (original + gloss) | APW | How, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | tú as the default between intimates; every 2sg verb and pronoun shows it | obligatory | *¿Tú vienes?* 'you.INF come.2SG' (vs *¿Usted viene?*) | partly | **min me obu.** **me** is "you" to anyone. Leaving out **nun** reads as plain, not as close. There's no grammar for the positive signal of closeness or solidarity, or for the step of switching to tú (*tutear*, *¿nos tuteamos?*). |
| 1.2 | Voseo (RP, Central America, parts of CO, VE, CL), with its own verb forms; marks informality plus regional identity | obligatory (in those varieties) | *Vos sabés.* 'you.VOS know.2SG.VOS'; CL *¿Cómo estái?* 'how be.2SG.VOS' | partly | **me lugo.** The addressee is covered. The in-group and regional identity is lost, because APW has no dialect grammar. |
| 1.3 | vosotros (informal plural) vs ustedes (ES); AM uses ustedes for both | obligatory (ES) | *¿Vosotros venís?* 'you.PL.INF come.2PL' | partly | **min me la obu.** The plural is there. The formality split shows only through **nun**. |
| 1.4 | Overt subject pronoun vs dropped pronoun, used for contrast | habitual | *Yo no fui, fue ella.* 'I not went.1SG, went.3SG she' | partly | **lon mu sin isi. wan sin isi.** Dropping is allowed (rule 11) and contrast comes from parallel sentences. There's no focus particle and no stressed pronoun. |
| 1.5 | Affective diminutive -ito/-ita (-ico CR/CO/Caribbean, -illo Andalusia, -ín Asturias): warmth, and making a request feel small | habitual (very frequent in AM) | *Espérame un momentito.* 'wait.IMP-me a moment.DIM' | partly | **so sefu nin foke fi.** 'REQUEST wait during moment one'. **meli** means literal size only. The warmth and the shrinking of the imposition are lost. **nun** adds deference, not warmth. |
| 1.6 | Vague, diminutivised time adverbs: *ahorita* (MX "in a bit, maybe never"; CR/CO "right now"), *ahora mismo*, *luego* (MX "later on") | habitual | *Ahorita voy.* 'now.DIM go.1SG' | partly | **de obu wo.** 'INTEND come after'. **eda** is now and **wo** is later. The deliberate vagueness of *ahorita* has to be stated (**kon bo**) or it's lost. |
| 1.7 | Augmentative and pejorative suffixes (-ote, -ón, -azo, -ucho, -ejo, -uzo); -azo also means "blow with" (*portazo* door slam) | habitual | *un perrazo* 'a dog.AUG'; *una casucha* 'a house.PEJ' | partly | **totu bi kudu** 'very big dog'; **guyi meli yomo** 'small bad house'. The tone (admiring, scary or scornful) needs an extra word. The "blow with X" derivation doesn't exist. |
| 1.8 | Ethical dative: the speaker's emotional stake in an event they take no part in | habitual (colloquial) | *No me llores.* 'not me.DAT cry.2SG.SBJV'; *El niño no me come.* 'the child not me.DAT eats' | no | **tin mu nowa** loses the "for my sake" layer. No slot exists for a person who is affected without being a participant. It can only be said as a separate clause about feelings. |
| 1.9 | Dative of an affected possessor or victim | habitual | *Se me murió el perro.* 'REFL me.DAT died the dog'; *Le robaron la cartera.* 'him.DAT stole.3PL the wallet' | partly | **totu pi lon kono.** 'dog of me died'. Possession is kept. The sense that it happened *to me* is lost unless stated (**lon bayi**). |
| 1.10 | Accidental se: an event befalls someone, with no blame | habitual | *Se me cayó el vaso.* 'REFL me.DAT fell the glass' (vs *Tiré el vaso* 'I threw/knocked the glass') | partly | **ladon dosi se kiti pi lon.** 'cup fell from hand of me' vs **lon gitin ladon.** The choice of an intransitive verb keeps blame out. The "it happened to me" dative is lost. |
| 1.11 | Aspectual/expressive se: completion, eating something up, personal involvement; *irse* vs *ir* | habitual | *Se comió toda la pizza.* 'REFL ate.3SG all the pizza'; *Me voy* 'REFL go.1SG' (I'm leaving) | partly | **wan sin nawi pasu we.** 'done eat food all'. Leaving is **kusa**, going is **isi**. The expressive "polished it off" colour is lost. |
| 1.12 | Gender agreement that reveals the speaker's and addressee's gender | obligatory | *Estoy lista.* 'am.ESTAR ready.F' | yes | **lon gun wonwe.** Gender isn't marked. It's stated once with a modifier when it matters (**no wungu futa**). Spanish forces this information; APW neither forces it nor loses it. |
| 1.13 | ser vs estar: trait vs current state or perception, plus location and resulting states | obligatory | *Es guapa / Está guapa.* 'is.SER pretty / is.ESTAR pretty' (is pretty / looks pretty today); *Es aburrido / Está aburrido* (boring / bored) | partly | There is one copula, **gun**. Lexical pairs are separate roots (**gole** clever / **wonwe** ready for *listo*). Location (**gun mon guyi**) and resulting state (**gun tapi**) are covered. Trait vs passing state for the *same* adjective needs a time word: **wan gun sesa mefa** 'she is beautiful today'. |
| 1.14 | Confirmation tags *¿no?*, *¿verdad?*, *¿eh?*, *¿vale?* (ES), *¿sí?*, *¿cierto?* (CO, CL) | habitual | *Vienes mañana, ¿no?* 'come.2SG tomorrow, no' | yes | **me obu wuma, min.** A clause-final **min** is a tag. |
| 1.15 | *Pues*: links to what was just said, hesitates before an unwelcome answer, or intensifies (*pues claro*) | habitual (ES, MX, Andes) | *Pues no sé.* 'well not know.1SG' | partly | **gi tan** covers "so, therefore". The hesitant "well..." use has no word. |
| 1.16 | Acceptance and topic-closing: *bueno*, *vale* (ES), *dale* (RP), *órale/sale/va* (MX), *listo* (CO) | habitual | *—¿Vamos? —Dale.* 'go.1PL — give.IMP-it' | partly | Acceptance is **de** + verb (**de isi** "I'll go") or **tayu** "agree". The topic-closing "bueno, ..." has no word. |
| 1.17 | Reformulation: *o sea*, *es decir*, *digo* | habitual | *Es difícil, o sea, no puedo.* 'is difficult, or be.SBJV, not can.1SG' | yes | **ben gun gofa, lon yofu, lon mu lenu.** **lon yofu** = "I mean". *O sea* used as a filler falls under 1.18. |
| 1.18 | Fillers that hold the floor: *este...* (AM), *esto...* (ES), *eh...*, *mmm* | habitual | *Es que... este... no sé.* 'is that... this... not know.1SG' | no | The only defined interjections are **mm** "heard you" and **huh?** "say again". There's no hesitation word. |
| 1.19 | Attention-getters and turn-openers: *oye/oiga*, *mira/mire*, *fíjate*, *escucha*, *¿sabes?*, *¿viste?* (RP) | habitual | *Oye, ¿tienes hora?* 'hear.IMP, have.2SG hour' | partly | **so doma** "please listen", **so miya** "look". These are full requests, heavier than the worn-down markers. The tú/usted split (*oye/oiga*) is lost. |
| 1.20 | Camaraderie vocatives: *tío/tía*, *macho* (ES), *güey* (MX), *che*, *boludo* (RP), *parce* (CO), *pana* (VE), *mano*, *hermano* | habitual | *¿Qué pasa, tío?* 'what happens, uncle' | partly | Address by name (**en ...**) or by **sonu** "friend". The in-group and regional flavour is lost, and so is the warmth of mock insults (*boludo*). |
| 1.21 | Terms of endearment: *cariño*, *mi amor*, *mi vida*, *mi cielo*, *mijo/mija* (AM), *gordo/a*, *flaco/a*, *negro/a* (AM, affectionate), *corazón* | habitual | *Ven, mi vida.* 'come.IMP, my life' | partly | **so obu, tiwu pi lon** works only for a real child of the speaker. There are no conventional endearments. **mala pi lon** "my heart" would be a fresh metaphor needing **a**. |
| 1.22 | Emotional interjections: *¡ay!* (pain, pity), *¡uf!* (relief, fatigue), *¡vaya!*, *¡anda!* (ES), *¡híjole!* (MX), *¡guau!*, *¡puaj!* (disgust), *¡ojo!* (watch out), *¡bah!* | habitual | *¡Ay, qué dolor!* 'ay, what pain' | partly | **ba** + a content word: **ba wekun** "(exclaim) pain", **ba bebu** "disgust". **yo** covers *¡ojo!*. Apart from **mm** and **huh?** there are no short conventional interjections. |
| 1.23 | Expletives, for frustration, emphasis or group identity: *joder*, *hostia*, *coño* (ES), *chingado*, *no mames* (MX), *carajo*, *mierda*, *la puta madre* | habitual (in many groups) | *¡Joder, qué frío!* 'fuck, what cold' | partly | **ba** + a taboo-area word (**ba desin**) is possible. No conventionalised expletive exists, and no word carries taboo force. |
| 1.24 | Intensifiers: *súper*, *re-* (RP *re lindo*), *mazo* (ES slang), *bien* (MX *bien bonito*), *-ísimo*, *un montón* | habitual | *Está rebueno.* 'is.ESTAR re-good' | yes | **bi**, **ba**, and doubling to strengthen: **ben gun bi upe.** |
| 1.25 | Hyperbole, irony, teasing | habitual | *Te lo he dicho un millón de veces.* 'you.DAT it have.1SG said a million of times' | yes | **a lon iko ben tun me gimu tonen.** **a** = not literal; **a a** = the whole turn. |
| 1.26 | Repair and echo questions: *¿Qué?*, *¿Cómo?*, *¿Mande?* (MX), *¿Que si vienes?* | habitual | *¿Que si vienes?* 'that if come.2SG' (I said, are you coming?) | yes | **huh?** = say again. Repeating the question: **lon mido wun min me obu.** |
| 1.27 | Greetings and farewells: *¿Qué tal?*, *¿Qué onda?* (MX), *¿Quiubo?* (CO), *chao*, *nos vemos*, *hasta luego*, *cuídate* | habitual | *Nos vemos mañana.* 'us see.1PL tomorrow' | yes | **upe**; **upe isi**; **nagu me wuma**; "how are you?" = **min me feme fu yu.** |
| 1.28 | Many-sided *ya*: *¡Ya voy!* (coming!), *Ya, ya* (ES, sceptical), *¡Ya está!* (done), *Ya verás* (you'll see: threat or promise) | habitual | *¡Ya voy!* 'already go.1SG' | yes | Each sense has its own form: **de obu** (I'll come), **sin** (done), **a** (sceptical), **win** (about to). |
| 1.29 | Vague-category extenders: *y eso*, *y tal*, *y todo*, *o algo así*, *y cosas así* | habitual | *Compré pan y eso.* 'bought.1SG bread and that' | yes | **lon bika sowe yen musu eni.** 'and other things'; "or something" = **lu musu bo**. |
| 1.30 | Hedges: *como que*, *medio* (AM *medio raro*), *un poco*, *algo*, *tipo* (RP/ES slang) | habitual | *Está medio raro.* 'is.ESTAR half strange' | yes | **ben gun to gagan.** **to** = rather. |
| 1.31 | Imperatives (the negative takes the subjunctive) and requests put as questions | obligatory (form) | *¡Ven! / ¡No vengas!* 'come.IMP / not come.SBJV'; *¿Me pasas la sal?* 'me.DAT pass.2SG the salt' | yes | **tin obu.** / **tin mu obu.** / **so neke lon bunwu.** |
| 1.32 | Ambiguous *vamos (a)*: "let's" vs "we're going to" | habitual | *Vamos a comer.* 'go.1PL to eat' | yes | **so pen nawi** (let's eat) vs **pen win nawi** (we're about to eat). APW separates them. |
| 1.33 | Wishes with *ojalá*: present subjunctive (possible) vs imperfect subjunctive (unlikely or contrary to fact) | habitual | *Ojalá vengas / Ojalá estuvieras aquí.* 'may.God come.SBJV / may.God were.SBJV here' | yes | **lon ganin wun me obu.** / **lon momu wun kon po me gun mon kemu tan.** |

## 2. Hierarchical (boss, elder, official)

| # | Distinction | Obl./hab./occ. | Example (original + gloss) | APW | How, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | usted, with 3rd-person agreement, for deference or social distance | obligatory | *¿Usted viene mañana?* 'you.FORM come.3SG tomorrow' | partly | **min nun me obu wuma.** **nun** marks deference, and **nun nun** grades it more finely than Spanish. Usted also marks plain social distance without special respect (a stranger, a same-age colleague) and keeps that as a standing relationship. APW has no channel for that. |
| 2.2 | Marked switches: *ustedeo* among intimates (CO, CR, rural AM: parents and children, couples), and switching to usted to signal coldness or to scold | occasional (regionally habitual) | *¡Usted se calla!* 'you.FORM REFL shut.up.3SG' (parent to child) | no | **nun** only adds respect. There's no way to show warmth through usted, or chill by withdrawing tú. |
| 2.3 | Titles with names: *señor/señora/señorita*, *don/doña* + first name; AM professional titles *licenciado*, *ingeniero*, *doctor*, *maestro* | habitual | *Buenos días, don Ramón.* 'good days, DON Ramón' | partly | Role word + name: **no wungu en lamon**, **no tofo en mala**. Nothing matches *don/doña* (respect for an older person combined with first-name familiarity), *señorita* (young or unmarried), or degree titles. A role noun must be built, or the title loaned. |
| 2.4 | Speaking to the hearer in the third person: *¿El señor desea algo más?* (waiters, shops); *su merced* (CO), *su señoría* (judge), *su excelencia* | occasional | *¿El señor desea algo más?* 'the sir wishes something more' | partly | A role word as vocative + **nun**: **min nun, no wungu, me momu musu eni.** Third-person reference to the listener isn't possible (**wan** never means the listener), so this distancing strategy is lost. |
| 2.5 | Conditional of courtesy | habitual | *¿Podría ayudarme?* 'could.COND.3SG help.INF-me' | yes | **so nun yema lon.** or **min nun me lenu yema lon.** **nun** carries the politeness instead of the tense. |
| 2.6 | Imperfect of courtesy | habitual | *Quería pedirle un favor.* 'wanted.IPFV.1SG ask.INF-you.FORM.DAT a favour' | yes | **nun lon momu mido me yema.** The past tense as softener is gone; **nun** does its job. |
| 2.7 | Courtesy formulas: *por favor*, *con permiso* (when passing or leaving), *disculpe/perdone* (to get attention), *muy amable*, *de nada / no hay de qué*, *a la orden* (CO, VE), *para servirle* (MX) | habitual | *Con permiso.* 'with permission' | partly | **so nun** = please, **sepi** = sorry (also to get attention), **lota** = thanks. *Con permiso* = **min nun lon gun efon isi.** There's no conventional reply to thanks and no service formula. |
| 2.8 | Graded apologies and thanks | habitual | *Mil disculpas.* 'thousand apologies'; *Le agradezco muchísimo.* 'you.FORM.DAT thank.1SG very.much' | yes | **sepi**, **sepi bi**, **nun nun lon sepi bi**; **nun lon lota me bi**. |
| 2.9 | Wish formulas in the subjunctive: *Que le vaya bien*, *Que tenga buen día*, *Buen provecho*, *Que se mejore*, *Que en paz descanse*, *¡Salud!* (after a sneeze) | habitual | *Que se mejore.* 'that REFL improve.SBJV.3SG' | partly | **upe isi** "go well" is the only fixed form. The others must be said as plain hopes: **nun lon ganin wun me mu simi wuma.** The formulas, and their being expected at set moments, are lost. |
| 2.10 | Hedging a claim to a superior | habitual | *Me parece que hay un error.* 'me.DAT seems that there.is an error' | yes | **nun kon oma gun batu.** 'polite, 7 in 10, there is a problem'. |
| 2.11 | Suggesting instead of ordering: *¿Y si...?*, *Convendría que...*, *Yo que usted...* | habitual | *¿Y si lo dejamos para mañana?* 'and if it leave.1PL for tomorrow' | yes | **fon nun pen muge bu wuma.** **fon** = advise (the decision stays with the listener). |
| 2.12 | Polite disagreement and refusal; the excuse frame *es que...* | habitual | *Se lo agradezco, pero no puedo.* 'you.DAT it thank.1SG, but not can.1SG' | yes | **nun lota, un lon mu lenu.** / **nun nun lon mu tayu.** *Es que* ≈ a **gi** clause giving the reason. |
| 2.13 | Asking permission: *¿Me permite...?*, *¿Puedo...?*, *¿Se puede?* (while knocking) | habitual | *¿Se puede?* 'REFL can.3SG' | yes | **min nun lon gun efon inwa.** |
| 2.14 | Indirect requests by question or hint | habitual | *¿Tendrá un momentito?* 'have.FUT.3SG a moment.DIM' | yes | **min nun me ibe foke fi.** The direct polite route **so nun** also exists. Hinting is on the watch list. |
| 2.15 | Euphemism and softer synonyms: *falleció* vs *murió*, *pasar a mejor vida*, *el servicio* (toilet), *persona mayor* vs *viejo* | habitual | *Su padre falleció.* 'your.FORM father passed.away' | partly | One root per meaning: **wede pi me kono.** Softening comes only from **nun** or a non-literal phrase marked **a**. No gentler synonym exists. |
| 2.16 | Formal-register vocabulary (*solicitar* vs *pedir*, *efectuar* vs *hacer*, *abonar* vs *pagar*, *domicilio* vs *casa*) | habitual (official settings) | *Debe abonar el importe.* 'must.3SG pay.FORMAL the amount' | partly | **me keya unfu nimo.** One word per meaning (**mido**, **muge**, **unfu**, **guyi**). Formality can come only from **nun**; the "official voice" is lost. |
| 2.17 | Modest self-reference and hospitality formulas: *un servidor* (= I), *mi humilde opinión*, *está usted en su casa*, MX *su casa* when giving one's address | occasional | *Aquí tiene usted su casa.* 'here have.3SG you.FORM your house' | partly | Only **lon**. Humility must be said outright, or a claim lowered with **kon**. The *su casa* hospitality formula has no equivalent. |
| 2.18 | Modest/authorial "we" for a single speaker | occasional | *Creemos que...* 'believe.1PL that' (the speaker alone) | partly | **din** (we, not you) would be heard as a real group, so using it for "I" misstates. The modesty can only come from **nun** or **kon**. |
| 2.19 | Respectful reply to being called: *¿Mande?* (MX), *¿Sí, señora?*, *Dígame* (ES, on the phone) | habitual (MX) | *¿Mande?* 'command.IMP.FORM' | yes | **mm nun, no tofo.** or **huh?** for repair. |
| 2.20 | Greetings by time of day, also used as farewells | habitual | *Buenas tardes.* 'good afternoons' | yes | **upe leso**, **upe kegin**, **upe yami**. *Tarde* maps to **kegin**; there's no separate "afternoon". |
| 2.21 | Letter and email openings and closings: *Estimado/a*, *Muy señor mío*, *A quien corresponda*, *Atentamente*, *Reciba un cordial saludo*, *Quedo a la espera de su respuesta* | habitual (written) | *Atentamente,* 'attentively' | partly | Open with **nun** + role or name; close with **lota** or **upe isi**. No conventional letter formulas exist. "To whom it may concern" has to be built (**tun ewo bo**). |
| 2.22 | Respectful words for older or unknown adults: *señor/señora*, *caballero*, *joven* (MX), *abuelo/abuela* (AM, to elders), *jefe/maestro* (ES/MX, to tradespeople) | habitual | *Pase, joven.* 'come.in.IMP.FORM, young.man' | partly | **ewo** + **nun**. Kin words (**mole**, **wede**) would be taken literally. No conventional respectful age terms exist. |
| 2.23 | Depersonalised requests to officials | habitual (written) | *Solicito que se me expida un certificado.* 'request.1SG that REFL me.DAT issue.SBJV a certificate' | yes | **so nun, sege mabo tun lon.** The request act + **nun**, with the subject left out. |

## 3. Written for a stranger (instructions, technical, legal)

| # | Distinction | Obl./hab./occ. | Example (original + gloss) | APW | How, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Instruction forms: infinitive, impersonal se, usted imperative, tú imperative | obligatory (one must be chosen) | *Agitar antes de usar.* 'shake.INF before of use.INF' | yes | **tin kobi bu ya bone.** 'INSTRUCT move it before use'. |
| 3.2 | How a text addresses an unknown reader: usted (official, legal) vs tú (ads, apps, many ES manuals). The choice signals officialdom or closeness | habitual | *Descarga la app* vs *Descargue la aplicación* 'download.IMP.2SG / download.IMP.FORM the app' | partly | **tin** vs **tin nun** gives plain vs polite. A brand's deliberate closeness (*tuteo*) can't be signalled. |
| 3.3 | Impersonal or generic subject: impersonal se, *uno*, *hay que*, 3pl | habitual | *Aquí se paga antes de entrar.* 'here REFL pays before of enter.INF' | yes | **go keya unfu ya inwa.** **go** = anyone. |
| 3.4 | Passive: *ser* + participle with a *por* agent, or the se-passive | habitual (technical, legal, news) | *La norma fue aprobada por el consejo.* 'the rule was approved.F by the council' | partly | There's no passive voice. The agent stays the subject: **funti man wungu sin efon wabun duwa.** An unknown agent is **ewo bo** or **go**; a resulting state is **gun** + verb. Promoting the patient to subject and topic is lost. |
| 3.5 | Anticausative: an event with no agent | habitual | *Se rompió la máquina.* 'REFL broke the machine' | yes | **du doku sin alun.** 'tool for work done break' (verbs can be intransitive). |
| 3.6 | Resulting state (*estar* + participle) vs event (*ser* + participle) | habitual | *La puerta está cerrada / fue cerrada.* 'the door is.ESTAR closed / was.SER closed' | yes | **beya gun tapi** (state) vs **ewo bo sin tapi beya** (event). |
| 3.7 | Graded obligation: *deber*, *tener que*, *haber de*, *es obligatorio*, the legal future *pagará*, *queda obligado a* | obligatory (contracts) | *El arrendatario pagará la renta.* 'the tenant pay.FUT.3SG the rent' | yes | **no sanwa keya unfu nimo.** **keya** must, **wupi** need. |
| 3.8 | Permission and prohibition: *se prohíbe*, *queda prohibido*, *podrá*, *se permite* | habitual | *Prohibido el paso.* 'prohibited the passage' | yes | **go mu gun efon inwa.** |
| 3.9 | Probability vs obligation in *poder/deber* (*puede contener...*; *debe de estar* vs *debe estar*, often blurred by speakers) | habitual | *Puede contener trazas de frutos secos.* 'can.3SG contain traces of nuts' | yes | Probability = **kon**, ability = **lenu**, permission = **gun efon**, obligation = **keya**: **kon fi pasu tan ibe tanun meli.** APW keeps them apart more strictly than Spanish. |
| 3.10 | Conditions: *si* + IND, *en caso de que* + SBJV, *siempre que* (provided that), *a menos que / salvo que* (unless), the archaic future subjunctive *si no lo hiciere* | habitual | *Salvo que el inquilino pague, deberá marcharse.* 'unless that the tenant pay.SBJV, must.FUT.3SG leave' | yes | **don mu no sanwa unfu, wan keya kusa.** **don** = if, **na don** = only if, **don kon po** = counterfactual. |
| 3.11 | Exceptions: *salvo*, *excepto*, *a excepción de* | habitual | *Todos pueden entrar, excepto los menores.* 'all can.3PL enter, except the minors' | partly | **ewo we gun efon inwa, mu tiwu la.** There's no "except" word. It's built with **mu**, and the listener has to work out the scope of the exception. |
| 3.12 | Free-choice relatives with the subjunctive | habitual (legal) | *El que encuentre el error, que avise.* 'the who find.SBJV the error, that notify.SBJV' | yes | **go man geno batu, tin iko.** |
| 3.13 | Specific vs non-specific indefinite, shown by the mood of a relative clause | habitual | *Busco un piso que tiene / tenga terraza.* 'seek.1SG a flat that has.IND / has.SBJV terrace' | partly | **bo** "unspecified" can mark the "any one that fits" reading (**lon walo geno guyi bo man ...**). The grammar doesn't define **bo** for that, so it may be read as "a particular one I'm not naming". |
| 3.14 | Definitions and stipulated terms | habitual | *Se entenderá por "usuario"...* 'REFL understand.FUT.3SG by user' | yes | **mon wabun tan, "no bone" yofu ewo man ...** |
| 3.15 | Cross-reference and legal anaphora: *el artículo 3*, *el citado*, *dicho*, *el mismo* | habitual | *dicho contrato* 'said contract' | yes | Labels with **pi** + number (**wabun pi ka**); repeat the noun with **tan**/**ben** or **kige** "same". |
| 3.16 | Relative clauses: *que / el cual / quien / cuyo*; commas separate restrictive from non-restrictive | habitual | *los alumnos, que estaban cansados,* vs *los alumnos que estaban cansados* 'the students (,) who were tired' | partly | **man** always attaches to the nearest noun. *Cuyo* needs **pi wan** inside the clause. The restrictive/non-restrictive difference isn't marked (the comma is only the pause rule). *El cual* (choosing a farther antecedent by gender agreement) has no equivalent. |
| 3.17 | Inclusive vs exclusive "or": *y/o*, *o bien... o bien* | habitual | *o bien en efectivo o bien con tarjeta* 'or well in cash or well with card' | yes | **lu** (both allowed); **lu X lu Y** (one, not both). |
| 3.18 | Quantifiers and the scope of negation: *no todos*, *ninguno*, *al menos*, *como máximo*, *ni... ni* | habitual | *No todos los usuarios...* 'not all the users' | yes | **kin** some-not-all, **fa** none, **pa** at least, **mu ke se** X at most. |
| 3.19 | Deadlines and intervals: *en un plazo de*, *a partir de*, *hasta el día 5 inclusive* | habitual | *en un plazo de 30 días* 'in a period of 30 days' | yes | **nin dili ka yawu**; **se** X **tun** Y. Like English "to", *inclusive* must be spelled out. |
| 3.20 | Approximation | habitual | *unos 30 kilos* 'some 30 kilos' | yes | **fu** + number; **pa** = at least. |
| 3.21 | Gender in generic reference: generic masculine (*los usuarios*), doublets (*los usuarios y las usuarias*, *el/la solicitante*), inclusive forms (*todes*, *-x*; contested) | obligatory | *los usuarios y las usuarias* 'the users.M and the users.F' | yes | **no bone la** covers everyone. Gender is stated only when it matters. |
| 3.22 | Nominalisation | habitual | *la construcción del edificio* 'the construction of.the building' | yes | **selo pi guyi**. Position makes the noun; **ge/no/du/pu** name roles. |
| 3.23 | Formal connectors: *por consiguiente*, *no obstante*, *sin embargo*, *asimismo*, *en virtud de*, *sin perjuicio de*, *a tal efecto* | habitual | *No obstante, el plazo podrá ampliarse.* 'not hindering, the period can.FUT REFL extend' | partly | **gi tan** (therefore), **un** (but, however), **wame** (also), **gi** (by virtue of), **li** (for that purpose). The shades of concession collapse into **un**, and "without prejudice to" needs a whole clause. |
| 3.24 | Purpose with a different subject: *para que* + SBJV, *a fin de que* | habitual | *Cierre la puerta para que el perro no salga.* 'close.IMP.FORM the door for that the dog not go.out.SBJV' | yes | **tin tapi beya li totu mu kusa.** |
| 3.25 | Warnings and notes: *¡Atención!*, *Peligro*, *Precaución*, *Advertencia*, *Nota* | habitual | *Peligro: fuego.* 'danger: fire' | yes | **yo, lumi.**; **yo yo** = emergency; **fon** = advice or note. |

## 4. Persuasive (argument, opinion, complaint)

| # | Distinction | Obl./hab./occ. | Example (original + gloss) | APW | How, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Belief commitment: *creo que* + IND vs *no creo que* + SBJV | habitual | *No creo que tenga razón.* 'not believe.1SG that have.SBJV.3SG reason' | yes | **lon mu pume wun wan gun denku.** More precisely: **kon da wan gun denku.** |
| 4.2 | Graded probability: *seguro que*, *sin duda*, *probablemente*, *a lo mejor* (+IND), *quizá(s)/tal vez* (+IND/SBJV), *puede que*, *igual* (ES colloquial) | habitual | *Igual llueve mañana.* 'equal rains tomorrow' (maybe it'll rain) | yes | **kon da gegi muwo wuma.** Tenths are finer than the Spanish scale. |
| 4.3 | Emotive and evaluative subjunctive over facts | habitual | *Me alegra que vengas.* 'me.DAT gladdens that come.SBJV.2SG' | yes | **lon sogi gi me obu.** |
| 4.4 | Concession: *aunque* + IND (granted fact) vs *aunque* + SBJV (even if, doesn't matter) | habitual | *Aunque llueva, vamos.* 'although rain.SBJV, go.1PL' | partly | Granted fact: **gegi muwo, un pen isi.** Hypothetical: **don gegi muwo, pen isi.** The "even if, regardless" force is lost (see 4.5). |
| 4.5 | Scalar particles: *incluso*, *hasta* ('even'), *ni siquiera*, *encima*, *para colmo* | habitual | *Ni siquiera me saludó.* 'not even me.DAT greeted.3SG' | no | **wan mu oyi upe tun lon** says only "he didn't greet me". **wame** (also) and **na** (only) cover addition and restriction, but nothing marks the far end of a scale. **ba** adds feeling, not a scale. |
| 4.6 | Contrastive focus: clefts, focus by word order | habitual | *Fue Juan quien lo rompió.* 'was Juan who it broke'; *Eso no lo dije yo.* 'that not it said I' | partly | There's no focus particle. Parallel sentences carry it (**lon mu oyi ben. wan oyi ben.**), or a relative frame (**ewo man alun bu gun en wan**). Fixed SVO means word order can't do it. |
| 4.7 | Verum focus and insistence: *¡Sí que vino!*, *¡Que sí!*, *¡Claro que sí!*, *Por supuesto* | habitual | *¡Sí que vino!* 'yes that came.3SG' | yes | **ba wan sin obu.** or **kon yawu wan sin obu.** |
| 4.8 | Emphatic negation: *¡Qué va!*, *¡Ni hablar!*, *Para nada*, *¡Ni loco!* | habitual | *¡Ni hablar!* 'nor talk.INF' | yes | **mu mu.** (emphatic NOT), e.g. **mu mu isi.** |
| 4.9 | Exclamatives of degree: *¡Qué...!*, *¡Cuánta...!*, *¡Lo... que es!*, *-ísimo* | habitual | *¡Lo difícil que es!* 'the difficult that is' | yes | **ba ben gun bi gofa.**; **ba ewo bi feli.** |
| 4.10 | Rhetorical questions | habitual | *¿Y quién te lo pidió?* 'and who you.DAT it asked' | yes | Said as what it means, **ewo fa mido me.**, or as a non-literal question, **a min ewo yu mido me.** |
| 4.11 | Irony and sarcasm: *¡Qué bien!* (meaning bad), *Sí, claro* (disbelief), *Ya, ya* | habitual | *¡Qué bien, otra vez tarde!* 'what good, other time late' | yes | **a ben gun bi upe.**; **a a** = the whole turn. |
| 4.12 | Mitigators: *un poco*, *medio*, *como que*, *digamos*, *más o menos*, *por así decirlo* | habitual | *Es un poco injusto.* 'is a little unfair' | yes | **ben gun to yomo.**; also **fu** "like", **kon**. |
| 4.13 | Opinion frames: *en mi opinión*, *a mi parecer*, *me da la impresión de que*, *para mí que* | habitual | *Para mí que miente.* 'for me that lies.3SG' | yes | **lon pume wun ...** + **kon**; **san** = it seems (inferred). |
| 4.14 | Distancing reported opinion: *según él*, *dizque* (AM, doubtful hearsay), *supuestamente*, *eso dicen* | habitual | *Dizque es muy listo.* 'says.that is very clever' | yes | **fen kon ka wan gun bi gole.** **fen** = told; a low **kon** = doubt. |
| 4.15 | Generalisation and common ground: *todo el mundo sabe*, *la gente*, self-including *uno* | habitual | *Uno trabaja y trabaja y...* 'one works and works and' | yes | **go doku yen doku, ...**; **ewo we lugo wun ...** |
| 4.16 | Persuasive "we" (Spanish doesn't say whether the listener is included) | habitual | *Tenemos que cambiar esto.* 'have.1PL that change.INF this' | yes | **pen keya kubo tan** (with you) vs **din** (without you). APW is sharper here. |
| 4.17 | Counterfactual reproach | habitual | *Si me hubieras escuchado, no estaríamos aquí.* 'if me had.SBJV.2SG listened, not be.COND.1PL here' | yes | **don kon po me doma lon, pen mu gun mon kemu tan.** |
| 4.18 | Reproach for a past omission: *¡Haberlo dicho!*, *Deberías haber llamado* | habitual | *¡Haberlo dicho!* 'have.INF-it said' (you should have said so!) | partly | **me sin keya oyi ben.** gives the past obligation. The conventional "you didn't, and I blame you" construction is only implied. |
| 4.19 | Complaint formulas: *¡Ya está bien!*, *¡No hay derecho!*, *¡Hasta cuándo!*, *¡Es el colmo!*, *¡Manda narices!* (ES) | habitual | *¡No hay derecho!* 'not there.is right' | partly | **ba ben gun bi yomo.** works as plain content. The fixed formulas, and the stock indignation that comes with them, are lost. |
| 4.20 | Dismissive reference: postposed demonstrative *el tipo ese*, *un tal Pérez*, *fulano*, article + name *la Mari* (ES/CL) | habitual | *un tal Pérez* 'a such Pérez' | partly | *Un tal* ≈ **ewo man go yebin en pelesu** "someone people call Pérez". **ben** after the noun is the normal position, so the sneer of *el tipo ese* is lost unless said (**yomo**). |
| 4.21 | Insults and name-calling: *idiota*, *imbécil*, *pendejo* (MX), *gilipollas* (ES), *boludo* (RP), *cabrón* | habitual (in quarrels) | *¡Eres un idiota!* 'are.2SG an idiot' | partly | Descriptive only: **me gun ewo mu gole**, **no nuta** (coward). No conventional insult words. |
| 4.22 | Agreement backchannels: *vale*, *claro*, *exacto*, *eso*, noncommittal *ya* (ES), *pues no*, *¿tú crees?* | habitual | *—Exacto.* 'exact' | yes | **tayu** / **mu tayu**, an echo (**gun feko**), **mm** for noncommittal "heard", **min me pume wun ...** |
| 4.23 | Challenges and suggestions in argument: *¿Por qué no...?*, *¿Y si...?*, *A ver si...*, *Fíjate que...* | habitual | *¿Por qué no lo intentas?* 'for what not it try.2SG' | yes | **fon me walo.**; *a ver* = **so pen miya**. |
| 4.24 | Justification frames: *es que...*, *lo que pasa es que...* | habitual | *Es que no tengo tiempo.* 'is that not have.1SG time' | yes | **gi lon mu ibe muyi.** |
| 4.25 | Topic framing: *en cuanto a*, *respecto a*, *lo de ayer* (*lo de* = the matter of), *hablando de* | habitual | *Lo de ayer fue un error.* 'the of yesterday was an error' | partly | Only time or place may open a sentence. The topic has to fill a role: **ben man muwo afe gun gagan.** The topic-then-comment packaging is lost. |

| # | Distinction | Obl./hab./occ. | Example (original + gloss) | APW | How, or what's lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Preterite vs imperfect: bounded foreground vs ongoing background | obligatory | *Llovía cuando llegué.* 'rain.IPFV.3SG when arrive.PFV.1SG' | yes | **nin gegi le dosi, lon liwon.** **le** = ongoing, **sin** = done. Aspect is optional in APW; when it's left out, context carries the contrast. |
| 5.2 | Habitual imperfect and *soler* | habitual | *De niño jugaba en la calle.* 'of child play.IPFV.1SG in the street'; *Solía venir los lunes.* 'used.to.3SG come the Mondays' | partly | There's no habitual marker. | 5.3 | Verbs whose meaning shifts with aspect: *conocí/conocía* (met / knew), *supe/sabía* (found out / knew), *quise/quería* (tried / wanted), *no quise* (refused) | obligatory | *Supe la verdad.* 'knew.PFV.1SG the truth' (found out) | yes | Separate verbs or **lewe**: **lon lewe lugo musu feko.** **walo** = tried, **mu tayu** = refused. |

## Top gaps

Ranked by how often Spanish speakers need the distinction. The ranking is judgment, not corpus counts.

1. **Definite vs indefinite articles** (5.14; also registers 1, 3 and 4). Partly. Spanish marks new vs known on nearly every noun phrase; APW leaves it to context unless a speaker adds **fi**, **bo** or **tan/ben**.
2. **tú / vos / usted as a standing relationship** (1.1–1.3, 2.1–2.2). Partly. **nun** carries deference. Closeness, solidarity, neutral distance, and switching register for warmth or coldness have no channel.
3. **Habitual past (imperfect, *soler*)** (5.2). Partly. Every past habit is marked in Spanish. APW has no habitual aspect, so frequency must be counted out (**gimu feli**).
4. **Fillers and light discourse markers** (*este*, *eh*, *pues*, *bueno*) (1.15, 1.16, 1.18). No/partly. Only **mm** and **huh?** exist; nothing holds the floor, softens the start of a reply, or closes a topic.
5. **Affective diminutives and augmentatives** (1.5–1.7, 5.24). Partly. **meli/kudu** give size only; the warmth, pity, scorn and request-softening are lost.
6. **Topic and focus packaging** (clefts, left dislocation, verb-before-subject order) (4.6, 5.16, 5.17). Partly. Fixed SVO and no focus particle; only parallel sentences or relative frames carry contrast.
7. **Datives of affectedness and accidental se** (1.8–1.10). No/partly. There's no slot for a person who is affected by an event but takes no part in it, or for an event that befalls someone without blame.
8. **Scalar "even"** (*incluso*, *hasta*, *ni siquiera*) (4.5, and 4.4's *aunque* + SBJV). No. Nothing marks the far end of a scale.
