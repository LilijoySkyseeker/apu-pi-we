# Bengali (Bangla): register inventory against Apu Pi We

Sources read for Apu Pi We: `docs/language.md`, `data/grammar.tsv`, `data/lexicon.tsv`, `data/compounds.tsv` only. Verdicts judge what those files make expressible. Apu Pi We examples are built only from words in those files.

**Transcription.** Standard Colloquial Bengali (*cholito bhasha*, Kolkata/Dhaka standard). Romanization is broad: ṭ ḍ ṛ are retroflex, *sh* is [ʃ], *chh* is aspirated, *ã ẽ* are nasal vowels; vowel length is not marked.

**Gloss abbreviations.** INTIM / FAM / HON = the three second-person (and verb) levels *tui / tumi / apni*; 3HON / 3NH = honorific / non-honorific third person; CL classifier; FOC focus clitic *-i*; ADD additive/scalar clitic *-o*; PRT discourse particle; CONJ conjunctive participle *-e*; IMP imperative; FUT future; PST past; PERF perfect; PROG progressive; HAB habitual past; INF infinitive; VN verbal noun; OBJ object case *-ke*; GEN genitive; LOC locative; NEG.EX existential negator *nei*; ECHO echo-word.

**What Bengali does not mark** (so not listed as gaps): grammatical gender (pronouns *she, o, tini* are sex-neutral), an inclusive/exclusive "we" (*amra* covers both; Apu Pi We's **pen/din** is finer), and articles separate from classifiers.

**Frequency column.** *Obligatory* = grammar forces a choice in every relevant clause; *habitual* = speakers make the distinction nearly every time the situation arises, though grammar allows omitting it; *occasional* = available and meaningful, used in some fraction of cases.

## Register 1: intimate and informal (friends, family)

| # | Distinction | Obl./hab./occ. | Example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Three-level second person with verb agreement: *tui* (intimate / to juniors) vs *tumi* (familiar) vs *apni* (polite) | obligatory | তুই কী করছিস? *tui ki korchhis?* you.INTIM what do.PROG.2INTIM<br>তুমি কী করছ? *tumi ki korchho?* you.FAM what do.PROG.2FAM<br>"What are you doing?" | no | **min me muge yu.** is the only form for all three. **nun** marks deference upward only; there is nothing for intimacy or talking down, and leaving **nun** out is neutral, not intimate. The tui/tumi line (closeness) cannot be drawn at all. |
| 1.2 | Subject drop recovered from the verb ending (person + honorific level) | obligatory | কাল আসবি? *kal ashbi?* tomorrow come.FUT.2INTIM "Coming tomorrow?" (ending says: you, intimate) | partly | **min wuma obu.** Rule 11 allows the drop, but the verb carries no person, so who is meant comes only from context, and the social level is gone. |
| 1.3 | Polar question particle *ki* (optional; intonation also works) | habitual | তুমি কি আসবে? *tumi ki ashbe?* you Q come.FUT.2FAM "Will you come?" | yes | **min me obu wuma.** |
| 1.4 | *to* seeking confirmation, with an expectation the answer is yes | habitual | আসবি তো? *ashbi to?* come.FUT.2INTIM PRT "You will come, right? (I'm counting on it)" | yes | Clause-final tag: **me obu wuma, min.** The "counting on you" warmth rides on intonation. |
| 1.5 | *naki* as a surprised / incredulous question | habitual | তুই যাবি নাকি? *tui jabi naki?* you.INTIM go.FUT.2INTIM PRT "You're actually going, then?" | partly | **min me isi.** with surprised pitch (pitch is free for emotion). No word carries "contrary to what I assumed". |
| 1.6 | *naki* as hearsay ("apparently, so I hear") | habitual | ও নাকি বিয়ে করছে। *o naki biye korchhe.* s/he PRT marriage do.PROG.3 "Apparently she's getting married." | yes | **fen wan win lewe ofin.** (told: she about-to become spouse). |
| 1.7 | Intimate particle *re* (to equals/juniors; warmth, urging) | habitual | চল রে, দেরি হচ্ছে। *chol re, deri hochchhe.* go.IMP.INTIM PRT, late become.PROG.3 "Come on, mate, we're getting late." | no | **so pen isi.** keeps the content; the intimate, chummy tone has no outlet (**nun** points the other way). |
| 1.8 | Affectionate particle *go* (warm address, often to elders or by women) | habitual | কী গো, কেমন আছ? *ki go, kemon achho?* what PRT, how be.2FAM "Well dear, how are you?" | no | **min me feme fu yu.** Affection is unmarked. |
| 1.9 | Coaxing *na* on imperatives (pleading, "go on") | habitual | বল না! *bol na!* tell.IMP.INTIM PRT "Oh go on, tell me!" | partly | **so oyi.** is a request, but the wheedling insistence is lost; adding **nun** would turn it into deference, a different thing. |
| 1.10 | Attention-getting vocatives *ei, ore, ogo* | habitual | এই, শোন! *ei, shon!* hey, listen.IMP.INTIM "Hey, listen!" | partly | Only a name or role word can call someone: **tin doma, en lina.** (the reference doesn't say where an address word goes). No nameless "hey". |
| 1.11 | Focus clitic *-i* ("X and no other", "exactly, very") | habitual | আমিই করব। *ami-i korbo.* I-FOC do.FUT.1 "*I'll* do it (me, nobody else)." / এখনই *ekhon-i* "right now" | partly | **na lon muge bu.** ("only I do it") covers exclusive focus. "Right now / that very one" has no device; the reference says emphasis and contrast have no special device yet. |
| 1.12 | Additive/scalar clitic *-o* ("also" and "even") | habitual | আমিও যাব। *ami-o jabo.* I-ADD go.FUT.1 "I'll go too." / একটাও নেই। *ekṭa-o nei.* one.CL-ADD NEG.EX "There isn't even one." | partly | "Also" = **lon wame isi.** "Even" has no word: **gun ingo fa.** ("there are no books") loses the scalar "not even one". |
| 1.13 | Classifier marks count and definiteness: *ekṭa boi* "a book", *boi-ṭa* "the book", bare *boi* "books, a book in general" | obligatory | বইটা কোথায়? *boi-ṭa kothay?* book-CL where "Where's the book?" vs একটা বই দাও *ekṭa boi dao* one-CL book give.IMP.FAM "Give me a book." | partly | Count yes (**ingo fi**), "this/that" yes (**ingo tan**, **ingo ben**). There is no definite/indefinite marking, so bare **ingo** cannot show "the book we both know" vs "some book". |
| 1.14 | Classifier choice carries affect: *-ṭi* (tender, delicate, also formal) vs *-ṭa* (neutral, can be dismissive) | habitual | মেয়েটি *meye-ṭi* girl-CL.tender vs মেয়েটা *meye-ṭa* girl-CL "the girl" | no | **tiwu tan** "this child" (gender as a modifier if needed). No affective classifier; the tenderness/neutrality contrast disappears. |
| 1.15 | Classifier *-khana* (the whole, solid thing; admiring or ironic emphasis) | occasional | মুখখানা দেখো! *mukh-khana dyakho!* face-CL look.IMP.FAM "Just look at that face of hers!" | no | Size/wholeness words (**kudu**, **minwe**) give a different, literal meaning. |
| 1.16 | Human classifier *-jon* (respectful counting of people) vs *-ṭo* | habitual | দুজন লোক *du-jon lok* two-CL.HUM person "two people" vs দুটো ছেলে *du-ṭo chhele* "two boys (casual)" | partly | **ewo sa** "two people" keeps the count; the respect carried by the classifier choice is lost. |
| 1.17 | Approximate-quantity morphology: *-khanek* "or so", paired numerals | habitual | ঘণ্টাখানেক *ghonṭa-khanek* hour-about "an hour or so"; দশ-বারোটা *dosh-baro-ṭa* ten-twelve-CL "ten or twelve" | yes | **awuwa fu fi** "about one hour"; **se yawu tun yawu sa** "from 10 to 12". |
| 1.18 | Echo-word reduplication ("X and that sort of thing", casual vagueness) | habitual | খাবার-টাবার কিছু আছে? *khabar-ṭabar kichhu achhe?* food-ECHO some exist "Is there any food or anything?" | partly | **min gun pasu lu musu eni.** ("food or other thing?") gets the sense; the light, offhand vagueness of a one-word echo is lost. |
| 1.19 | Fine-grained interjections: *ish* (pity/regret/embarrassment), *uff* (exasperation), *dhur* (dismissal), *baba re* (shock/fear), *aha* (sympathy) | habitual | ইশ, জামাটা ভিজে গেল! *ish, jama-ṭa bhije gelo!* INTJ, shirt-CL get.wet.CONJ go.PST.3 "Oh no, the shirt got soaked!" | partly | **ba** + content word: **ba bayi** (sad), **ba bebu** (disgust), **ba wekun** (pain). Each shade needs a choice of root, and blends like *ish* (pity + embarrassment) fall between them. |
| 1.20 | Kin terms split elder/younger and maternal/paternal side; no neutral "uncle/aunt/sibling" | obligatory | মামা *mama* mother's brother, কাকা *kaka* father's younger brother, জেঠু *jeṭhu* father's elder brother, মাসি *mashi* mother's sister, পিসি *pishi* father's sister, দাদা *dada* elder brother, ভাই *bhai* younger brother | partly | Composable: **faba lowu pi mole** "brother of mother"; elder brother **faba lowu man gun ke pani se lon**. Precise but long (one Bengali word becomes 4-9), and none works as a short form of address. |
| 1.21 | Endearment address terms (*shona* "gold", *babu*, *mamoni*) | habitual | সোনা, খেয়ে নাও। *shona, kheye nao.* gold, eat.CONJ take.IMP.FAM "Darling, eat up." | partly | Any word can be used to address someone (**tin nawi, tiwu pi lon.** "eat, my child"), but there are no conventional endearments. |
| 1.22 | Compound verb *phela* "throw": completed, often inadvertent or regretted | habitual | গ্লাসটা ভেঙে ফেলেছি। *glas-ṭa bhenge phelechhi.* glass-CL break.CONJ throw.PERF.1 "I've gone and broken the glass." | partly | **lon sin alun ladon.** gives completion; the "oops / couldn't help it" colouring is lost (adding **mu momu** "not wanting" changes it into a claim). |
| 1.23 | Compound verb *dewa* "give": action done for someone else; also softens requests | habitual | আমাকে একটু লিখে দে। *amake ekṭu likhe de.* me.OBJ a.little write.CONJ give.IMP.INTIM "Write it out for me, will you." | partly | **so tusi bu li lon.** ("for me") gives the beneficiary; the completive and the request-softening disappear. |
| 1.24 | Compound verb *newa* "take": action done for oneself | habitual | নামটা লিখে নে। *nam-ṭa likhe ne.* name-CL write.CONJ take.IMP.INTIM "Write the name down (for yourself)." | yes | **tin tusi suso li nen.** |
| 1.25 | Agentive vs non-agentive event (blame-relevant): "it fell from my hand" vs "I dropped/threw it" | habitual | আমার হাত থেকে পড়ে গেল। *amar hat theke poṛe gelo.* my hand from fall.CONJ go.PST.3 "It slipped from my hand." vs আমি ফেলে দিলাম। *ami phele dilam.* "I threw it down." | yes | Verb choice: **bu dosi se kiti pi lon.** vs **lon gitin bu.** |
| 1.26 | Four negators: verbal *na*, past/perfect *-ni*, copular *noy*, existential *nei* | obligatory | টাকা নেই। *ṭaka nei.* money NEG.EX "There's no money." / আমি যাইনি। *ami jaini.* "I didn't go / haven't gone." | yes | **gun nimo fa.** / **lon mu isi.** / **lon mu sin isi** "not yet". |

## Register 2: hierarchical (boss, elder, official)

| # | Distinction | Obl./hab./occ. | Example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Polite second person *apni* with *-en* agreement on every verb | obligatory | আপনি কি খাবেন? *apni ki khaben?* you.HON Q eat.FUT.2HON "Will you eat (something)?" | partly | **min nun me momu nawi.** **nun** (and **nun nun**) carries deference. Lost: it is optional and per sentence, not built into every reference to the person, and its absence doesn't distinguish *tumi* from *tui*. |
| 2.2 | Referent honorific: respected third persons take *uni/tini* and *-en* verbs, even when they're absent | obligatory | বাবা বাড়িতে নেই, উনি অফিসে গেছেন। *baba baṛi-te nei, uni office-e gechhen.* father home-LOC NEG.EX, he.3HON office-LOC go.PERF.3HON "Father isn't home, he's gone to the office." | no | **wede mu gun mon guyi. wan sin isi tun pu pi mabo.** **nun** is about the speech act toward the listener; nothing marks respect for the person spoken about, and **wan** is the same for a father and a child. |
| 2.3 | Polite imperative *-un* and polite future imperative *-ben* ("do it later, please") | obligatory | বসুন। *boshun.* sit.IMP.HON "Please sit." / কাল আসবেন। *kal ashben.* tomorrow come.FUT.IMP.HON "Please come tomorrow." | yes | **tin nun goyu.** / **so nun obu wuma.** (the time word does what the future imperative does). |
| 2.4 | Formulaic "please": *doya kore* ("doing mercy"), formal *onugroho kore* | habitual | দয়া করে দরজাটা বন্ধ করুন। *doya kore dorja-ṭa bondho korun.* mercy doing door-CL closed do.IMP.HON "Please close the door." | yes | **so nun tapi beya.** |
| 2.5 | Minimizer *ekṭu* "a little" as politeness ("it's only a small thing") | habitual | একটু দেখবেন? *ekṭu dekhben?* a.little see.FUT.2HON "Could you take a quick look?" | partly | **min nun me lenu miya bu.** keeps the politeness. The minimizing strategy is gone; literal **to** "rather" would change the meaning. |
| 2.6 | Conditional politeness formula "if you don't mind" | habitual | যদি কিছু মনে না করেন, … *jodi kichhu mone na koren, …* if anything mind-LOC not do.2HON "If you don't mind, …" | yes | **don bu mu yenwu me, …** "if it doesn't offend you, …". |
| 2.7 | Request as an ability question | habitual | আমাকে একটু সাহায্য করতে পারবেন? *amake ekṭu shahajjo korte parben?* me.OBJ a.little help do.INF can.FUT.2HON "Would you be able to help me a bit?" | yes | **min nun me lenu yema lon.** |
| 2.8 | Softening by tense shift ("there was a word") | habitual | স্যার, একটা কথা ছিল। *shyar, ekṭa kotha chhilo.* sir, one-CL word was "Sir, I had something to say." | yes | The function is carried by **nun**: **nun lon momu oyi musu fi tun me.** The tense-shift device itself has no equivalent, which the reference intends. |
| 2.9 | Kin terms as address to non-kin, ranking by age (*dada, didi, kaku, mashima, apa, bhai*) | habitual | দাদা, একটু সরুন। *dada, ekṭu shorun.* elder.brother, a.little move.IMP.HON "Excuse me (brother), could you move a bit?" (to a stranger) | partly | **so nun kobi, faba lowu.** is possible, but **faba** has no "elder" in it, and the reference's address words are names and role words. The "you are older than me" ranking is lost. |
| 2.10 | Seniority suffixes and titles on names: *-da / -di* (elder), *-babu*, *shaheb*, *-ji* | habitual | সুমনদা *Suman-da* Suman-elder.brother; করিম সাহেব *Karim shaheb* "Mr Karim" | partly | Role word + name: **no tofo en suman**. No word for "senior to me" or a general honorific title. |
| 2.11 | Nameless deferential address: *shyar* "sir", *madam*, *huzur* | habitual | স্যার, ফাইলটা রেখেছি। *shyar, phail-ṭa rekhechhi.* sir, file-CL keep.PERF.1 "Sir, I've put the file there." | partly | A role word (**no wungu** "commander, leader") or the name + **nun**. No generic "sir/madam". |
| 2.12 | Name-avoiding vocative for a spouse or elder (*ogo*) | occasional | ওগো, শুনছ? *ogo, shunchho?* VOC, listen.PROG.2FAM "Dear, are you listening?" | partly | **min me doma.** or **ofin** as address. No vocative particle; the custom of not naming is not encoded. |
| 2.13 | Greetings that signal respect and community: *nomoshkar* (Hindu), *assalamu alaikum* (Muslim), *adab* (neutral), *pronam* (touching elder's feet) | habitual | নমস্কার। *nomoshkar.* / আসসালামু আলাইকুম। *assalamu alaikum.* | partly | **nun upe.** One neutral greeting plus politeness. Community affiliation and the rank-specific *pronam* are lost (arguably by design). |
| 2.14 | Honorific / euphemistic verbs for respected people (death, eating) | habitual | উনি প্রয়াত হয়েছেন। *uni proyat hoyechhen.* he.3HON deceased become.PERF.3HON "He has passed away." vs মারা গেছে *mara gechhe* "died" | partly | **wan sin kono.** is neutral; **a, wan sin isi.** ("not literal: he has gone") gives a euphemism. A respectful register for the deceased is lost. |
| 2.15 | Respectful acknowledgement *ji, accha* / *ajñe* "yes sir" | habitual | জি, আচ্ছা। *ji, accha.* yes.HON, okay "Yes, sir, will do." | yes | **de nun muge.** (intend, politely: do). |
| 2.16 | Self-humbling expressions (*gorib-khana* "my poor house", *adhom* "this lowly one") | occasional | আমার গরিবখানায় একবার আসবেন। *amar gorib-khanay ekbar ashben.* my poor-house-LOC once come.FUT.2HON "Do visit my humble home sometime." | no | **nun** raises the listener; nothing lowers the speaker. Saying "small house" literally is a claim, not modesty. |

## Register 3: written for strangers (instructions, technical, legal)

| # | Distinction | Obl./hab./occ. | Example (original + gloss) | Apu Pi We | How, or what is lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Literary variety *sadhu bhasha* (full verb and pronoun forms) vs colloquial *cholito* | occasional (older legal and official texts, some deeds) | তিনি আসিয়াছিলেন। *tini ashiyachhilen.* (sadhu) vs তিনি এসেছিলেন। *tini eshechhilen.* (cholito) "He had come." | no | **wan sin obu ya.** One variety; the document-genre signal is lost. |
| 3.2 | Formal Sanskrit-derived (*tatsama*) vocabulary vs everyday words | habitual | প্রবেশ নিষেধ। *probesh nishedh.* entry forbidden (sign) vs ঢুকবেন না। *ḍhukben na.* enter.FUT.2HON not "Don't come in." | no | **tin mu inwa.** for both. One root per meaning, so formal written register can't be signalled by word choice. |
| 3.3 | Verbal-noun (nominal) style for notices | habitual | ধূমপান নিষেধ। *dhumpan nishedh.* smoke-drinking forbidden "No smoking." | yes | Position turns verbs into nouns; the prohibition is **tin mu …** ("tin mu inwa." No entry). |
| 3.4 | Agentless obligation: infinitive + *hobe* "will be (needed)" | habitual | ফর্মটি পূরণ করতে হবে। *form-ṭi puron korte hobe.* form-CL fill do.INF will.be "The form must be filled in." | yes | **go keya tusi mabo tan.** (generic **go** as subject), or drop the subject. |
| 3.5 | Passive with agent phrase (*kortṛk / dwara*) putting the object first | habitual | আইনটি সংসদ কর্তৃক পাস হয়েছে। *ain-ṭi songshod kortṛk pash hoyechhe.* law-CL parliament by pass become.PERF.3 "The law was passed by parliament." | partly | Agentless state: **wabun tan gun waka.** ("this law is chosen"); with the agent, only the active: **no wungu la sin waka wabun tan.** No construction keeps the law as subject and also names the agent. |
| 3.6 | Formal collective plurals *-gon, -brindo, -shomuho* (vs *-ra, -gulo*) | habitual | নাগরিকগণ *nagorik-gon* citizen-PL.formal; রাস্তাসমূহ *rasta-shomuho* road-PL.formal | partly | **la** "plural" keeps the number; the formality is lost. |
| 3.7 | Public instructions default to the honorific *apni* imperative | obligatory | বই ফেরত দিন। *boi pherot din.* book return give.IMP.HON "Please return books." | yes | **tin nun …**, e.g. **tin nun tapi beya.** |
| 3.8 | Future as legal "shall" (often with honorific *-ben* for a party) | habitual | ভাড়াটিয়া প্রতি মাসের ৫ তারিখের মধ্যে ভাড়া পরিশোধ করবেন। *bhaṛaṭiya proti mash-er 5 tarikh-er moddhe bhaṛa porishodh korben.* tenant every month-GEN 5 date-GEN within rent pay do.FUT.3HON "The tenant shall pay the rent by the 5th of each month." | yes | **no sanwa keya unfu nimo pi sanwa ya dili pi te, mon dunan we.** **keya** is unambiguous obligation. |
| 3.9 | Definitional formula "X means …" | habitual | এই আইনে "ভাড়াটিয়া" অর্থ … *ei ain-e "bhaṛaṭiya" ortho …* this act-LOC "tenant" meaning … "In this Act, 'tenant' means …" | yes | **mon wabun tan, apu "no sanwa" yofu ewo man sanwa guyi.** |
| 3.10 | Exception and proviso: *byatito* "except", *tobe shorto thake je* "provided that" | habitual | শিশু ব্যতীত সকলে প্রবেশ করতে পারবেন। *shishu byatito shokol-e probesh korte parben.* child except all-NOM entry do.INF can.FUT.3HON "Everyone except children may enter." | partly | No "except": **ewo we gun efon inwa, un tiwu mu gun efon.** (all may enter, but children are not allowed). Workable but two clauses; scope of the exception is less compact. |
| 3.11 | Relative-correlative "whoever …, he …" | habitual | যে ব্যক্তি এই নিয়ম ভঙ্গ করবেন, তাকে জরিমানা দিতে হবে। *je byakti ei niyom bhongo korben, ta-ke jorimana dite hobe.* REL person this rule break do.FUT.3HON, him-OBJ fine give.INF will.be "Whoever breaks this rule must pay a fine." | yes | **ewo man alun wabun tan, keya unfu nimo.** (comma after a subject containing a clause, rule 15). |
| 3.12 | Object case *-ke* on human/definite objects | obligatory | শিক্ষককে জানান। *shikkhok-ke janan.* teacher-OBJ inform.IMP.HON "Inform the teacher." | yes | Word order does the work: **tin nun iko no tofo.** |
| 3.13 | Instrument (*diye*) vs companion (*shonge*) | obligatory | কাঁচি দিয়ে কাটুন। *kãchi diye kaṭun.* scissors with.INSTR cut.IMP.HON "Cut with scissors." vs বন্ধুর সঙ্গে যান *bondhu-r shonge jan* "Go with a friend." | partly | **gan** "with" covers both (**fonsu bu gan yetu** / **isi gan sonu**). The instrument reading can be forced with **bone** "use": **tin bone yetu li fonsu bu.** |
| 3.14 | Locative/genitive/ablative case suffixes and postpositions | obligatory | ব্যাগে রাখুন। *bag-e rakhun.* bag-LOC keep.IMP.HON "Put it in the bag." | yes | **tin nun yaga bu mon yufa.**; **pi**, **se**, **tun** for genitive, from, to. |

## Top gaps

Ordered by how often Bengali speakers need the distinction.

1. **Three-level second person (tui / tumi / apni) built into every verb** (R1, R2; 1.1, 1.2, 2.1, 4.10). Obligatory in every clause addressed to someone. **nun** covers only the upward end; intimacy and talking down have no marker, and pronoun switching as a social act is impossible.
2. **Discourse particles to, na, re, go, naki (surprise)** (R1, R4; 1.5, 1.7–1.9, 4.1). Present in most casual turns. They carry warmth, coaxing, shared-knowledge appeal and surprise; Apu Pi We has speech-act particles but no stance or intimacy particles.
3. **Referent honorifics for third persons (uni/tini + -en)** (R2, R5; 2.2, 5.16). Obligatory whenever a parent, elder, official or public figure is mentioned; **nun** only marks politeness toward the listener.
4. **Classifiers: definiteness and affect (-ṭa / -ṭi / -jon / -khana)** (R1, R4, R5; 1.13–1.16, 4.11). "The book" vs "a book" vs "books" has no marker, and the tender/neutral/contemptuous colouring of a classifier is lost.
5. **Age-ranked kin terms and kin-based address (dada, didi, mama, kaka, -da/-di)** (R1, R2; 1.20, 2.9, 2.10). Used daily, to relatives and strangers. Composable, but 4-9 words long and unusable as short address; the "elder than me" ranking has no word.
6. **Focus -i and scalar -o ("exactly", "even")** (R1, R4; 1.11, 1.12, 4.2, 4.3). **na** and **wame** cover "only" and "also"; "even" and "that very one" have no device.
7. **Compound-verb nuances (phela, uṭha, poṛa, bosha, dewa)** (R1, R4, R5; 1.22, 1.23, 4.15, 5.8, 5.9). Inadvertence, suddenness, rashness and doing-for-someone colour a large share of past-tense verbs; only completion (**sin**) and the beneficiary (**li**) survive.
8. **Formal written register (tatsama vocabulary, sadhu forms, -gon plurals)** (R3; 3.1, 3.2, 3.6). Every notice, form and legal text signals formality by word choice; with one root per meaning, that signal is gone (the content survives).
