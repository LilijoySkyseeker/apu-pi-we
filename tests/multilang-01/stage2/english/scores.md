## Other losses, in turns scored 2

- **Name drift (t10, 11, 19, 22):** Denise → **en danisu** → "Janice". The loan phonology maps both names to the same form, and the back-translator flagged it as a guess (but listed Janice, Dennis and Danice, not Denise). "She" was recovered only because the Janice reading happens to be female (**wan** is genderless). Mostly L (lossy loan mapping), partly B.
- **t1:** Turn-opening "Okay" lost. The outrage in "our *own* lot" survives only as an exclamation.
- **t2:** The incredulity in "Why *would* they" was flattened to a plain "why".
- **t3:** "That's what I said!" (A had asked the same thing at the time) came back as the idiom "That's what I'm saying!". The past-tense report is softened (B; the Apu Pi We has **sin**). The filler "like" was lost.
- **t4/t5 onward:** "sticker" and "permit" merged into one word (**du efon**). The physical sticker is gone.
- **t5:** The dismissive "September or whatever" became confident "I'm pretty sure… September". The vagueness and indifference were lost (T: **lu musu fu ben** "or something like that", used in t9, was available).
- **t7:** "literally" is reduced to exclamation. Register shift of "valid" lost.
- **t9:** Officialese "Lot management requested it" became "The lot manager requested the tow". The institutional subject is now one person.
- **t11:** The "by the way" aside lost; it foreshadowed A's last line about the Uber. "my own car back": "own" lost.
- **t12:** "Oh man" (commiserating) → "That's awful!" (slightly more evaluative).
- **t16:** The legal register "may be subject to removal" / "vehicles" became plain "may be towed".
- **t17:** The contrast "a normal person" vs a lawyer weakened to generic "you". "this weekend" → "end of the week".
- **t18:** "Yeah, no" (agree, then soften) reduced to "Mm".
- **t19:** "a mess" → "a problem" (weaker). "admit" was recovered by the back-translator even though the gloss says "agree".
- **t20:** "Actually" lost (B moving from sceptic to supporting A). "van" → "big car" (**tale kudu**). "yelling" → "talking loudly", which loses Henderson's anger.
- **t22:** "actually" lost (C conceding after doubting in t18). The imperative "Get the Hendersons…" softened to "Maybe get…" (**fon** advice, a translator choice).

## Summary

Every turn kept its full propositional meaning, including who did what to whom, reported speech, the logophoric "they'd tow people", the verum "did send" and the "may" hedge. So the text scores 46/46 with no L/T/B/E losses at the turn level. What Apu Pi We dropped is the predicted surface layer: counter-expectation "actually", asides and fillers, legal vs casual register, the anger in "yelling", and the identity of the borrowed name Denise. Each is minor alone, but together they flatten the speakers' voices and stance shifts.
