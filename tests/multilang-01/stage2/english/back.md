# Back-translation

Setting: three neighbours in the same apartment building, talking casually. A's car was towed from the building's lot. A is angry and venting, B is a bit sceptical and plays devil's advocate, and C is sympathetic and practical.

## 1. Translation

A: You're not going to believe this. Somebody towed my car. Right out of our lot!
B: Hang on. The building's lot? Why'd they tow you from there?
A: That's what I'm saying! This morning at 7:15 I go downstairs, already running late, and there's just an empty space. An oil stain and nothing else.
C: Did you have your permit on the car?
A: Yes, I had it. But it was the old permit. I'm pretty sure the new permits came out in September. But mine is still good through the end of this month. I checked.
B: Hold on. So you didn't put the new permit on the car.
A: No, I didn't. But that's not the point. The point is that the old permit is still valid. It says October! In big numbers!
C: Who towed your car? What company?
A: Some place way out by the highway. Rick's Recovery or something like that. I had to call the number on the sign, and the guy on the phone said, "The lot manager requested the tow." That's all he said. Like he was reading off a menu.
B: Did you call the manager, Janice?
A: Oh, I called Janice all right! She didn't answer. I left her two messages. Then I took an Uber out to that place. I paid 31 dollars for that. And the tow people want 280 dollars to give me my car back.
C: 280? That's awful!
A: 280, cash or card, and it's more if you pay by card. And the guy's telling me it's my own fault, because there was an email. What email? Nobody sent me an email.
B: Well, management did send something out. I got one. It said everybody had to switch permits before the start of the month.
A: Mm. But did it say they'd tow people? Because if all it says is "Please renew your permit," that's not the same as "We're going to tow your car."
B: I think it said something like "Vehicles without a new permit may be towed." Something like that.
A: "May." See? "May" isn't a warning. That's lawyer-speak. You read that and think, "Mm, I'll get to it at the end of the week." And the old permit still says October! How was I supposed to know they meant the start of the month?
C: Mm, I get it. It's confusing. But I don't know if you'd win. They'll just point to the email.
A: I'm not trying to win in court. I'm trying to get my money back. I want Janice to admit this was a problem. I also want her to pay for the tow. I'd bet half the lot still hasn't switched yet. Did they tow anyone else?
B: Yes, they did. I'm pretty sure the Hendersons' big car is gone too. I saw Mr. Henderson outside. He was on the phone, talking loudly.
A: See? So it's not just me being dumb. If two of us got towed in one night, the email didn't work. That's on them, not us.
C: Mm, that's a pretty good point. Maybe get the Hendersons to go with you. If one person complains, Janice doesn't listen. If it's three or four, she has to do something.
A: I'll do that. And I'll keep the receipt for the tow, and the Uber one too. 31 dollars. She's paying for that Uber!

## 2. Turns I was unsure about

- **Turn 9 (A), "en likusu likabali":** a company name borrowed through the sound rules. "likabali" fits "Recovery" well. "likusu" could be Rick's, Rix or Lucas, so the exact name is a guess.
- **Turns 10, 11, 18, 19, 22, 23, "en danisu":** I read this as Janice (j → d). It could also be Dennis or Danice. The pronoun **wan** has no gender, so "she" depends on the Janice reading.
- **Turn 7 (A), "gan wifu kudu":** literally "with big numbers." I took it as "the October date is printed in big numbers on the permit." It might instead be a more general "in plain sight."
- **Turn 13 (A), "lowu ben le iko lon":** "that man is telling me." It's unclear whether this is the man from the phone call in turn 9 or someone A met at the tow yard. I left it as "the guy."
