# multilang-01 stage 2: English source in Apu Pi We

Each block: English, Apu Pi We, word-by-word gloss. Grammar words are glossed in capitals with the labels of `data/grammar.tsv`.

Phrases built for this text (word-building rules, no new roots):
- **nunya** "pull" = tow; **no nunya** "one who tows" = the tow company.
- **pu pi tale** "place of cars" = parking lot.
- **du efon** "thing for allowing" = permit, used for both "sticker" and "permit".
- **du wuye** "thing for showing" = sign; **mabo pi pasu** "paper of food" = menu; **mabo pi unfu** "paper of paying" = receipt.
- **ge oyi** "what is said" = message; **ge bodu** "what dirtying makes" = stain.
- **no lugo wabun** "one who knows rules" = lawyer; **sola kudu** "big road" = highway; **tale kudu** "big car" = van.
- **fugi pi nelon** "end of the week" = weekend.
- Loans and names (`tools/names.py`): Denise **en danisu**, Henderson **en endalusan**, Rick's Recovery **en likusu likabali**, Uber **ubalu**, email **imeyilu**, court **kolutu**.

## 1

```
A: Okay, you're not gonna believe this. They towed my car. From our own lot.
A: a me mu pume wun tan gun feko. ewo bo sin nunya tale pi lon. ba se pu pi tale pi pen.
A: NONLITERAL you NOT think THAT.comp THIS BE true. person UNSPEC DONE pull car OF I. EXCLAIM FROM PLACE OF car OF WE.incl.
```

- "Okay" as a turn opener has no word; left out.
- "You're not gonna believe this" is hyperbole, so it gets **a** (not literal).
- "They" with no known referent is **ewo bo** "some unspecified person", by analogy with **ewo yu** "who". No rule says this directly.
- "our" is **pen** (inclusive), since B and C live in the same building. That's a distinction English doesn't make.
- "own" (outrage emphasis): no device. Its force goes into **ba**.

## 2

```
B: Wait, the building lot? Why would they tow you from there?
B: tin sefu. min pu pi tale pi guyi. nunya me se kemu ben gi yu.
B: INSTRUCT wait. Q PLACE OF car OF house. pull YOU FROM place THAT BECAUSE WH.
```

- Subject "they" is left out: the towers are clear from context.
- The incredulity in "would" isn't carried.

## 3

```
A: That's what I said! I come down this morning, seven fifteen, I'm already late, and there's just, like, an empty spot. Oil stain and nothing.
A: ba lon sin oyi tan. mon leso tan, mon oma fi te, lon isi tun deba. lon sin gun wo muyi. gun na kemu tapun. ge bodu pi [oil] yen musu fa.
A: EXCLAIM I DONE say THIS. AT morning THIS, AT 7 1 5, I go TO below. I DONE BE AFTER time. BE ONLY place empty. RESULT dirty OF [oil] AND thing NONE.
```

- "late" = **gun wo muyi** "be after the time"; "already" = **sin**. Judgement call.
- The filler "like" isn't carried.
- "and there's" lost its "and", because **gun** means "there is" only at the very start of a sentence.
- "oil": no root. **gimen** is body fat only, and oil isn't a technical/international word, so it stays bracketed.

## 4

```
C: Did you have the sticker on?
C: min me ibe du efon mon tale.
C: Q YOU have TOOL allow AT car.
```

- "sticker" = **du efon** "permit". The physical-sticker sense is lost, and later "sticker" and "permit" become the same word.

## 5

```
A: Yes, I had the sticker. Well, I had the old sticker. The new ones came out in September or whatever, but mine's still good till the end of the month, I checked.
A: ibe, lon ibe bu. un lon ibe du efon pani. kon oma du efon duwa la obu mon dunan pi mape. un du efon pi lon le gun upe tun fugi pi dunan tan. lon sin miya.
A: have, I have IT. BUT I have TOOL allow old. CONF 7 TOOL allow new PL come AT month OF 9. BUT TOOL allow OF I ONGOING BE good TO end OF month THIS. I DONE look.
```

- "Yes" is the echo **ibe**.
- "Well" (self-correction) is carried only as **un** "but".
- "or whatever" is rendered as confidence **kon oma**. Its dismissive tone ("doesn't matter") is lost.
- "I checked" is kept as a clause rather than the evidential **o**, because English asserts the act of checking.

## 6

```
B: Hang on, so you didn't put the new one on.
B: tin sefu. san me mu yaga du efon duwa mon tale.
B: INSTRUCT wait. INFER YOU NOT put TOOL allow new AT car.
```

- "so" (drawing a conclusion) = the inferred-source marker **san**.

## 7

```
A: No, but that's not the point. The point is the old one is still valid. It literally says October on it. In big numbers.
A: mu yaga. un tan mu gun musu bawe. musu bawe gun wun du efon pani le gun upe. ba bu oyi dunan pi yawu. gan wifu kudu.
A: NOT put. BUT THIS NOT BE thing important. thing important BE THAT.comp TOOL allow old ONGOING BE good. EXCLAIM IT say month OF 10. WITH number big.
```

- "valid" = **gun upe**, the same as "good" in turn 5. The shift to an official register is lost.
- "literally" (intensifier) is carried only by **ba**.

## 8

```
C: So who towed it? Like which company?
C: ewo yu nunya tale pi me. funti yu.
C: person WH pull car OF YOU. group WH.
```

- "So" (back to the main thread) and "Like" (narrowing the question) aren't carried.
- "it" is named (**tale pi me**) because the sticker had become the topic.
- "company" = **funti** "group". Judgement call: borrowing "company" was possible, but a native root exists.

## 9

```
A: Some place out by the highway, Rick's Recovery or something. I had to call the number on the sign, and the guy on the phone goes, "Lot management requested it." Just like that. Like he's reading a menu.
A: kemu in man gun lini, mon gelu pi sola kudu. en likusu likabali lu musu fu ben. lon keya yebin wifu man gun mon du wuye. yen lowu man gun mon liga, oyi "no wungu pi pu pi tale sin mido nunya". wan oyi na tan. fu wan le lilu mabo pi pasu.
A: place SOME WHO.rel BE far, AT near OF road big. NAME likusu likabali OR thing LIKE THAT. I must call number WHO.rel BE AT TOOL show. AND man WHO.rel BE AT phone, say "AGENT lead OF PLACE OF car DONE ask pull". HE/SHE say ONLY THIS. LIKE HE/SHE ONGOING read paper OF food.
```

- "or something" = **lu musu fu ben** "or a thing like that".
- "goes" (quotative) = **oyi**.
- "Just like that" = "he said only this", which gives its meaning (no explanation), not its idiom.
- The officialese register of "requested" isn't carried.

## 10

```
B: Did you call the manager? Denise?
B: min me yebin no wungu, en danisu.
B: Q YOU call AGENT lead, NAME danisu.
```

- "Denise?" is an afterthought apposition inside one question, not a second question.

## 11

```
A: Of course I called Denise. She didn't pick up. I left two messages. Then I got an Uber out there, which was thirty-one dollars, by the way, and they wanted two hundred and eighty to give me my own car back.
A: ba lon yebin en danisu. wan mu febo. lon sege ge oyi sa. wo, lon isi tun kemu ben gan ubalu. lon unfu dola ka yawu fi li ben. yen no nunya la momu dola sa loki kefi yawu li neke lon tale pi lon.
A: EXCLAIM I call NAME danisu. HE/SHE NOT answer. I send RESULT say 2. AFTER, I go TO place THAT WITH Uber. I pay dollar 3 10 1 FOR THAT. AND AGENT pull PL want dollar 2 100 8 10 FOR give I car OF I.
```

- "Of course" is carried only by **ba**. Using **kon yawu** for it would be emphasis through confidence, which is on the watch list.
- "by the way" (aside) isn't carried. The relative clause became its own sentence.
- "own" and "back" aren't carried.

## 12

```
C: Two eighty? Oh man.
C: min sa loki kefi yawu. ba yomo.
C: Q 2 100 8 10. EXCLAIM bad.
```

## 13

```
A: Two eighty, cash or card plus a fee for the card. And the guy's telling me it's my fault because there was an email. What email? Nobody sent me an email.
A: sa loki kefi yawu, gan nimo lu mise, yen go unfu ke nimo gan mise. yen lowu ben le iko lon wun ben gun gagan pi lon, gi gun imeyilu. imeyilu yu. ewo fa sin sege lon imeyilu.
A: 2 100 8 10, WITH money OR card, AND GENERIC pay MORE money WITH card. AND man THAT ONGOING tell I THAT.comp THAT BE wrong OF I, BECAUSE BE email. email WH. person NONE DONE send I email.
```

- "a fee for the card" = "one pays more money with a card".
- "fault" = **gagan pi lon** "my wrong".
- Rule 15 puts the clause-final **gi** clause on the innermost clause, which is the right reading (the guy's reason).

## 14

```
B: I mean, they did send something. I got one. About switching stickers by the first.
B: un no wungu la sin sege musu in. lon dunu fi. bu oyi wun go keya kubo du efon ya tuko pi dunan.
B: BUT AGENT lead PL DONE send thing SOME. I get 1. IT say THAT.comp GENERIC must change TOOL allow BEFORE start OF month.
```

- "I mean" (gentle correction) is carried only as **un**.
- The emphatic "did" (verum focus) is carried only by the contrast with A's "nobody sent" and by **sin**.
- "they" is named as management (**no wungu la**): with no gendered or institutional pronoun, **wan la** could be the tow company.
- "by the first" = "before the start of the month". **dili pi fi** alone would mean Monday, and English names no month.
- The fragment "About…" was expanded into "it said that…".

## 15

```
A: Okay, but did it say they'd tow people? Because if it just said "please update your sticker," that's not the same as "we're gonna take your car."
A: mm. un min bu oyi wun sun la nunya ewo la. gi don bu na oyi "so nun muge duwa du efon pi me", ben mu gun kige fu "de din yidu tale pi me".
A: mm. BUT Q IT say THAT.comp LOGOPHOR PL pull person PL. BECAUSE IF IT ONLY say "REQUEST POLITE make new TOOL allow OF YOU", THAT NOT BE same LIKE "INTEND WE.excl take car OF YOU".
```

- "Okay, but" = **mm** (heard you) + **un**. Most of the concession is carried.
- "they" (the email's authors) = **sun la**, the logophoric pronoun for the source of reported words. Judgement call: the "speaker" here is a document.
- "would" (future-in-past) is unmarked.
- "please" = **so nun**. "we're gonna" = **de** (intent) with exclusive **din**.

## 16

```
B: I think it said vehicles without the new permit "may be subject to removal." Something like that.
B: kon oma bu oyi "kon bo tale la man mu ibe du efon duwa, gun yidu". musu fu ben.
B: CONF 7 IT say "CONF UNSPEC car PL WHO.rel NOT have TOOL allow new, BE take". thing LIKE THAT.
```

- "I think" = **kon oma**.
- English quotes only "may be subject to removal". Apu Pi We can't splice a quoted predicate into a reported clause, so the whole email sentence is quoted.
- "may" is ambiguous in English (permission vs possibility). The language forces a choice. **kon bo** "unknown how likely" fits A's reply ("not a warning"), and **gun efon** "is allowed" was rejected.
- "vehicles" and "removal" (legal register) become plain **tale** and **gun yidu**.

## 17

```
A: "May be." See, "may be" is not a warning, that's a lawyer thing. A normal person reads that and goes, okay, I'll do it this weekend. And the old sticker still says October! How was I supposed to know they meant early?
A: "kon bo". tin nagu. "kon bo" mu gun "yo". ben gun apu pi no lugo wabun. go lilu ben yen oyi "mm, de muge mon fugi pi nelon tan". yen ba du efon pani le oyi dunan pi yawu. lon keya lugo fu yu wun wan la yofu tuko pi dunan.
A: "CONF UNSPEC". INSTRUCT see. "CONF UNSPEC" NOT BE "WARN". THAT BE language OF AGENT know rule. GENERIC read THAT AND say "mm, INTEND do AT end OF week THIS". AND EXCLAIM TOOL allow old ONGOING say month OF 10. I must know LIKE WH THAT.comp HE/SHE PL mean start OF month.
```

- "warning" has no content root, so A names the warning particle itself: "'kon bo' is not 'yo'". A native speaker would plausibly say exactly this.
- "See," = **tin nagu** as its own sentence, because a particle covers its whole sentence.
- "a normal person" = generic **go**. The contrast "normal person vs lawyer" is only implied.
- "okay, I'll do it" = **mm** + **de muge** (the "will do" acknowledgment).
- "supposed to" = **keya** (obligation). The rhetorical "I couldn't have known" reading is left to context.
- "early" = "the start of the month".

## 18

```
C: Yeah, no, I get it, it's confusing. But I don't know if you're gonna win that one. They'll just point at the email.
C: mm, lon lafi. ben gun gofa li lafi. un lon mu lugo wun min me yiyin. wan la na wuye imeyilu.
C: mm, I understand. THAT BE difficult FOR understand. BUT I NOT know THAT.comp Q YOU win. HE/SHE PL ONLY show email.
```

- "Yeah, no" (agree, then soften) isn't carried. Only **mm** remains.
- "confusing" = "hard to understand".
- "point at" = **wuye** "show".

## 19

```
A: I'm not trying to win in court, I'm trying to get my money back. I want Denise to admit it was a mess and pay for the tow. Half the lot probably didn't switch yet. Did they tow anyone else?
A: lon mu walo yiyin mon kolutu. lon walo dunu nimo pi lon moti. lon momu wun en danisu tayu wun ben sin gun batu. lon momu wame wun wan unfu nimo pi nunya. kon oma timon pi pu pi tale mu sin kubo. min nunya ewo eni.
A: I NOT try win AT court. I try get money OF I again. I want THAT.comp NAME danisu agree THAT.comp THAT DONE BE problem. I want also THAT.comp HE/SHE pay money OF pull. CONF 7 half OF PLACE OF car NOT DONE change. Q pull person other.
```

- Contrast "not X, but Y" is carried by the parallel sentences.
- "admit" = **tayu** "agree". The sense of conceding against one's interest is lost.
- "a mess" = **batu** "problem".
- The want-sentence is split in two. In one sentence, "and pay" after a nested **wun** clause would attach to the inner clause ("it was a mess and paid").
- "probably" = **kon oma**. "not yet" = **mu sin**.

## 20

```
B: Actually, yeah, I think the Hendersons' van was gone too. I saw him out there on the phone yelling.
B: nunya. kon oma wame tale kudu pi yonyo en endalusan gun kusa. lon nagu lowu en endalusan mon aban. wan le oyi dibon mon liga.
B: pull. CONF 7 also car big OF family NAME endalusan BE leave. I see man NAME endalusan AT outside. HE/SHE ONGOING say loud AT phone.
```

- "Actually" (contrary to expectation) isn't carried. "yeah" is the echo **nunya**.
- "the Hendersons" = **yonyo en endalusan** and "him" = **lowu en endalusan** "Mr Henderson", using the role-word-before-name pattern (**no tofo en mala**).
- English "him" picks out the man by gender alone. Here he has to be named.
- "yelling" = **oyi dibon** "say loudly".

## 21

```
A: See! So it's not just me being dumb. If two of us got caught in one night, the email didn't work. That's on them, not us.
A: tin nagu. gi tan, mu na lon gun mu gole. don sa pi pen gun nunya nin yami fi, imeyilu mu yema. ben gun gagan pi wan la, mu pi pen.
A: INSTRUCT see. BECAUSE THIS, NOT ONLY I BE NOT smart. IF 2 OF WE.incl BE pull WHILE night 1, email NOT help. THAT BE wrong OF HE/SHE PL, NOT OF WE.incl.
```

- "got caught" = "were towed". The metaphor is lost.
- "didn't work" (fail to function) = **mu yema** "didn't help". **doku** is labour only.
- "on them" = their **gagan** "wrong", matching "my fault" in turn 13.

## 22

```
C: Okay, that's actually a decent point. Get the Hendersons to go in with you. One person complaining, she ignores. Three or four, she's gotta do something.
C: mm, ben gun gason to upe. fon muge isi yonyo en endalusan gan me. don ewo fi iko batu, en danisu mu doma. don ka lu da, wan keya muge musu in.
C: mm, THAT BE idea RATHER good. ADVISE make go family NAME endalusan WITH YOU. IF person 1 tell problem, NAME danisu NOT listen. IF 3 OR 4, HE/SHE must do thing SOME.
```

- "actually" (C conceding against their earlier doubt) isn't carried.
- "decent" = **to upe** "rather good".
- The imperative "Get…" is **fon** (advice, the decision stays with A) rather than **tin**. Judgement call.
- English "she" finds Denise by gender alone. Here the first "she" has to be named.
- "complain" = **iko batu** "tell a problem". "ignore" = **mu doma**.

## 23

```
A: That's what I'm gonna do. And I'm keeping the receipt, and the Uber receipt too. Thirty-one dollars. She's paying for that Uber.
A: de muge tan. yen de kuwi mabo pi unfu pi nunya, yen wame ben pi ubalu. dola ka yawu fi. ba wan unfu li ubalu ben.
A: INTEND do THIS. AND INTEND keep paper OF pay OF pull, AND also THAT OF Uber. dollar 3 10 1. EXCLAIM HE/SHE pay FOR Uber THAT.
```

- "the receipt" is specified as the tow's receipt, which is implicit in English.
- A's determination in "She's paying" is carried by **ba**, not by confidence.

## Rules I found ambiguous

1. **gun** "there is" applies "only at the very start of a sentence". It's unclear whether that still holds after a linker (**yen gun …**) or at the start of a **gi** clause (**gi gun imeyilu**, turn 13).
2. **Impersonal or unknown "they".** No rule covers it. Can **bo** modify a noun (**ewo bo** "someone"), by analogy with **ewo yu**? **go** is generic ("one"), not existential.
3. **Dropped subjects** (rule 11): nothing says how a dropped subject is read when context supplies no referent (a default "I"? an unspecified agent?).
4. **sun** when the reported "speaker" is a document (an email), and whether **sun la** is a valid plural.
5. **Days of the month:** **dili pi** + 1–7 alone is a weekday, so "the first" (of an unnamed month) can't be said without naming the month.
6. **Verb + verb:** the list of verbs that take a verb object ends in "…". Is **mido nunya** "ask to tow"? Does **li** + an action noun mean "for X" or "in order to X"?
7. **de with an explicit non-"I" subject** (**de din yidu …**): allowed? And do speech-act particles work inside quotes the same way?
8. **Commas after a bare linker before a particle** (**un min …**, **yen ba …**). The only example (**gi tan, tin**) has a linker phrase.
9. **Scope of la and pi chains:** **pu pi tale pi pen** ((place of car) of us, or place of (car of us)?), and whether **la** after a derived noun (**no lugo wabun la**) pluralizes the whole noun or just its last word.
10. **Manner modifier vs a wun object:** rule 15 makes a clause-final **fu yu** attach to the inner clause, so placing "how" before a **wun** object (**lugo fu yu wun …**) is forced, but object-then-manner is the stated order.
11. **wame** before a subject noun to mean "X too" (**wame tale kudu …**): only object positions have examples.
12. **Quoting a word without a say-verb** (**"kon bo".**) vs rule 12, which says quotes are framed by a say-verb.
13. **Clock times before 10:00:** read with or without a leading zero (**oma fi te** vs **po oma fi te**)?
14. **Role word before a name** for family names: **yonyo en endalusan** "the Hendersons" and **lowu en endalusan** "Mr Henderson". Only **no tofo en mala** is given.
15. **Confidence on echo answers:** where **kon** goes when a one-word echo answer is itself uncertain.
