# economy-01-context: translation v2

Translated from `source.txt` following `docs/language.md` as of 2026-10-10. Names: Marcus → **en malukusu**, Jess → **en desu** (r → l, added u after stranded consonants). Gaps in the vocabulary are in [brackets].

## 1

```
A: Hey, are you guys still coming Saturday or what?
upe. min me la le obu mon dili pi ufo.
good. Q you PL ONGOING come AT day OF 6.
```

"Hey" is the greeting **upe**. "Or what" adds no content beyond impatience and is left out; **min … lu …** would make it a real either/or question.

## 2

```
B: Yeah, I think so. What time again?
kon oma obu. muyi yu, moti.
CONF 7 come. time WH, again.
```

No word for yes: the echo **obu**, with "I think so" as confidence. I use **kon oma** for every "I think" / "probably" in this text (one favourite value, as the doc expects).

## 3

```
A: Like seven-ish. Maybe seven thirty, I gotta clean first.
fu fi mape po po. kon te fi mape ka po. lon keya muge inan ya.
like 1 9 0 0. CONF 5 1 9 3 0. I must make clean before.
```

"Like … -ish" both mean "about", so one **fu**. "Clean" is the causative **muge inan**; its object (the house) is left out. **ya** alone = "first".

## 4

```
C: Wait, is this the thing at your place or at Marcus's?
tin sefu. tan gun mon guyi pi me lu mon guyi pi en malukusu yu.
INSTRUCT wait. this BE AT house OF you OR AT house OF NAME Marcus WH.
```

"Wait" kept as an instruction (**tin sefu**), since it's the literal act of holding the conversation. It's a which-question, so **… lu … yu**, not **min**. "The thing" (the party) is an event that is here-and-now in the conversation: **tan**.

## 5

```
A: Mine. Marcus bailed, his landlord's doing something with the pipes.
guyi pi lon. en malukusu mu lenu. no ibe guyi pi wan, muge musu bo gan du dinyo osa.
house OF I. NAME Marcus NOT can. AGENT have house OF he/she, do thing UNSPEC WITH TOOL carry water.
```

- "Mine" echoes the choice. "Bailed" is rendered by what it means here, "can't (host)".
- Marcus is now the person being talked about, so "his" is **wan**.
- Landlord = **no ibe guyi pi wan** "the one who has his house". Pipes = **du dinyo osa** "thing for carrying water" (word building with **du**; I didn't borrow "pipe" because it isn't clearly one of the technical/international classes).
- Comma after the subject: it contains a verb phrase, and without the pause **wan muge** could run on as "him doing".

## 6

```
C: Oh no, again? Didn't that happen last month too?
ba yomo. min moti. ben muwo wame ya dunan fi, min.
EXCLAIM bad. Q again. that happen also BEFORE month 1, Q.
```

- The negative question is really asking for confirmation, so it's a statement with the tag **min** ("…, right?"), not **mu**.
- "That" = the event A reported: **ben** (see ambiguity 1).
- "Last month" = **ya dunan fi** "one month before", by analogy with the doc's **wo nelon fi** "next week".

## 7

```
A: Yeah, same thing. His whole kitchen was flooded, it was bad.
muwo, musu kige. osa muge kete pu siten pi wan. tan gun yomo.
happen, thing same. water make full PLACE cook OF he/she. this BE bad.
```

- "Yeah" = the echo **muwo**.
- "Whole kitchen was flooded" became "water filled his kitchen" (**muge kete**), because **minwe** "whole" has no clean slot after **pu siten pi wan**.
- "It was bad" = the situation A has just described, so **tan**.

## 8

```
B: Honestly he should just move out at this point.
eda, wan wupi kusa.
now, he/she need leave.
```

- "Honestly" and "just" are left out as stance words with no rule-given slot.
- "Should" = **wupi** "need to", the milder obligation word; **fon** is advice to the *listener* only.
- The object of "leave" (his place) is left out because context supplies it.

## 9

```
A: I know, right? I told him that, he just said he can't find anything cheaper.
lon lugo. lon iko ben tun wan. wan oyi wun sun mu lenu geno guyi pi sanwa ke meli.
I know. I tell that TO he/she. he/she say THAT.comp LOGOPHOR NOT can find house OF rent more small.
```

- "Right?" here isn't a question; it's left out.
- **lon iko ben tun wan** is the doc's own example.
- Inside the reported speech, Marcus is **sun**.
- "Anything cheaper" = "a house of smaller rent". There's no word for price or cheap, so I used **sanwa** "rent" as a noun with the comparative **ke meli**.

## 10

```
C: I mean, fair. Rent's insane right now.
denku. eda, ba sanwa gun bi kudu.
correct. now, EXCLAIM rent BE very big.
```

- "I mean" is left out. "Fair" is a one-word follow-up, **denku** "correct".
- "Insane" is an exaggeration for "very high". I rendered the meaning with **ba** + **bi** rather than marking it non-literal with **a**.
- **eda** is fronted as a scene-setter, which may precede the particle.

## 11

```
B: Okay, so should I bring anything?
gi tan, min lon wupi dinyo musu bo.
BECAUSE this, Q I need carry thing UNSPEC.
```

"Okay, so" = the linker **gi tan**, which may precede **min**.

## 12

```
A: Uh, maybe chips or something? Or drinks. Whatever, it's not a big deal.
fon [chips] lu musu bo. lu ge koye. musu bo gun upe. tan mu gun bawe.
ADVISE [chips] OR thing UNSPEC. OR RESULT drink. thing UNSPEC BE good. this NOT BE important.
```

- **fon [chips]** follows the doc's example: the "maybe" is a suggestion, so it's advice and not confidence.
- **bo** follows its noun, as **yu** does (**musu bo** "some unspecified thing"), by analogy with **ewo yu**.
- Drinks = **ge koye** "what is drunk".

## 13

```
C: I can bring that dip, the one I made at Jess's birthday.
lon lenu dinyo [dip] man lon siten mon [birthday] pi en desu.
I can carry [dip] WHO.rel I cook AT [birthday] OF NAME Jess.
```

- "That … the one" is carried by the relative clause.
- The final **mon** phrase attaches to the innermost clause (made at the birthday), which is the meaning wanted (rule 15).
- "Can" is kept as ability (an offer), not turned into **de**.

## 14

```
B: Oh, the spinach one? Yes, please, that was so good.
min gan [spinach]. so nun dinyo. ba bu gun bi upe.
Q WITH [spinach]. REQUEST POLITE carry. EXCLAIM it BE very good.
```

- A follow-up of just the new part: "with spinach?"
- "Yes, please" = a polite request, **so nun dinyo**, with the object left out.
- The dip is now the thing being talked about: **bu**.

## 15

```
C: Ha, okay. I gotta remember to buy the stuff for it though.
de dinyo. un lon keya imu bika musu li siten bu.
INTEND carry. BUT I must remember buy thing FOR cook it.
```

- "Ha" can't be written (there is no h) and is left out.
- "Okay" = **de** + verb, "will do".
- "The stuff for it" = "things to make it", using the purpose **li** + verb.

## 16

```
A: Is Jess coming, by the way? I texted her but she didn't answer.
min en desu obu. lon tusi tun wan, un wan mu sin febo.
Q NAME Jess come. I write TO he/she, BUT he/she NOT DONE answer.
```

- "By the way" is left out.
- Jess becomes the topic, so she is **wan**. **wan** is kept after **un** so the subject isn't read as "I".
- "Texted" = **tusi tun** "wrote to".
- "Didn't answer" = **mu sin** "not yet".

## 17

```
B: She's working Saturday, I think. She said maybe after, like ten?
kon oma doku mon dili pi ufo. wan oyi wun kon te wo, fu sa sa po po.
CONF 7 work AT day OF 6. he/she say THAT.comp CONF 5 after, like 2 2 0 0.
```

- The subject is left out in the first sentence: it answers "is Jess coming?".
- The second sentence follows the doc's **kon te wo, fu sa sa po po**, framed with **wan oyi wun** so the "maybe" is Jess's and not B's.
- No **min**: the rising "?" is uncertainty, not a question.

## 18

```
A: Okay, cool, that's fine. We'll probably still be up.
ben gun upe. kon oma pen le gun unda.
that BE good. CONF 7 we.incl ONGOING BE wake.
```

- "Okay, cool" is folded into **ben gun upe** (the doc's example).
- "We" is the people at the party, B and C included: **pen**.
- "Be up" = **gun unda** (the resulting state).

## 19

```
C: Wait, do I need to park on the street or is there room in the driveway?
tin sefu. lon wupi yaga tale mon sola, lu lenu yaga mon pu yaga tale pi me yu.
INSTRUCT wait. I need put car AT road, OR can put AT PLACE put car OF you WH.
```

- Park = **yaga tale** "put the car".
- Driveway = **pu yaga tale pi me** "your place for putting the car". It drops "driveway" specifically but keeps what the question is about.
- "Is there room" = "can (I) put (it)".
- It's a which-question over two clauses: **… lu … yu**. The comma is there so **sola lu lenu** isn't read as a noun list.

## 20

```
A: Street's fine, just don't block the neighbor's thing, he gets really weird about it.
sola gun upe. tin mu tapi musu pi no nume gelu. wan lewe bi bonko.
road BE good. INSTRUCT NOT close thing OF AGENT live near. he/she become very angry.
```

- Don't = **tin mu**.
- Block = **tapi** "close (off)".
- Neighbour = **no nume gelu** "one who lives near".
- "Weird" here means "upset", so **bonko**. That's slightly stronger, but B's reply (he yelled) confirms it.
- The neighbour is now the person being talked about: **wan**, not **yi** (Jess has dropped out of the talk).
- "About it" is left out.

## 21

```
B: Ha, yeah, he yelled at me last time.
wan oyi dibon tun lon ya muyi fi.
he/she say loud TO I BEFORE time 1.
```

- "Ha, yeah" is left out (h can't be written). The sentence itself is the agreement.
- Yell = **oyi dibon** "say loudly".
- "Last time" = **ya muyi fi** "one occasion before", on the anchor-plus-offset pattern.

## 22

```
C: Okay, noted. See you guys Saturday.
de mu tapi. nagu me la mon dili pi ufo.
INTEND NOT close. see you PL AT day OF 6.
```

- "Noted" acknowledges A's instruction, so it's **de** + verb, "will do (won't block)". The second sentence is the doc's example.

## Rules I found ambiguous

1. tan vs ben for events. "tan = nearer (just said, or here and now), ben = further." Is "just said" measured by time, by speaker, or by how far back the event itself is? The doc's own examples use **ben** for "I told him that" and "that's fine", both about something the other person just said. I used **tan** for a speaker's own just-said description or the present situation (turns 4, 7, 12), and **ben** for what someone else said or a past event (turns 6, 9, 18).
2. wan vs yi for a newly introduced person. **yi** is for someone "newly brought in", but a newly introduced person who becomes the subject of the talk (the neighbour, turn 20) is also "the person being talked about". It's unclear when the new person takes over **wan**.
3. Position of bo. It's listed with the operators, but operators "cover what follows". **kon bo** and **ewo yu** suggest it follows its noun. I wrote **musu bo** "something / anything"; the doc never shows it with a noun.
4. Interjections and discourse markers ("hey", "wait", "okay", "honestly", "I mean", "by the way", "ha"). There's no rule for which of these get a word, an act particle or nothing. "Ha" can't even be spelled. "Okay" got three different treatments by function (dropped, **de** + verb, folded into **ben gun upe**).
5. "should" about a third person. **fon** is advice to the listener only, **keya** is "must", **wupi** is "need to". There's no stated word for a weaker "ought to" about someone else.
6. "Last month" / "last time". The doc gives **wo nelon fi** "next week" but no past example. **ya dunan fi** could mean "a month ago" rather than "in the previous calendar month".
7. Which-questions over clauses. **… lu … yu** is shown only with nouns. With two full clauses (turn 19), it's unclear where **yu** goes and whether a comma is needed before it.
8. muge + one word with the object dropped (**muge inan**, turn 3). The result/manner order rule needs both words to decide between "make clean" and "do cleanly". Here both readings mean the same thing.
