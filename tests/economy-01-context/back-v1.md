# Back-translation

## 1. Translation

1. A: Hi. Are you guys still coming on Saturday?
2. B: Probably, yeah. Remind me what time we're supposed to come?
3. A: Around seven. Maybe more like seven-thirty. I have to clean the house first.
4. C: Wait, is this happening at your place or at Marcus's?
5. A: At mine. Marcus can't do it. His landlord is still doing some work on the water pipes.
6. C: Ugh, that's bad. Again? That same thing happened a month ago too, didn't it?
7. A: It did. Same problem. His whole kitchen was full of water. It's a real mess.
8. B: So now he has to move out of his own place.
9. A: I know. I told him the same thing, but he said he can't find a place that costs less.
10. C: I get it. Rent is so high these days!
11. B: Mm. So, do I need to bring anything?
12. A: You could bring chips or something like that. Or drinks. Whatever you pick is fine, it doesn't matter.
13. C: I can bring that dip I made at Des's birthday.
14. B: The spinach dip? Oh, please bring it. That dip is really good.
15. C: Will do. But I have to remember to buy the stuff for it.
16. A: Is Des coming? I texted them, but they didn't answer.
17. B: They're probably working Saturday. They said they might come after work, around ten.
18. A: Mm. Great! We'll most likely still be up.
19. C: Wait, do I park on the street, or in your parking spot?
20. A: The street's fine. Just don't park in front of the neighbour's place, because the neighbour gets really angry.
21. B: He yelled at me once before.
22. C: I'll remember. See you guys Saturday.

## 3. Unsure turns

- Turn 20: "musu pi no nume gelu" is literally "the neighbour's thing". I read it as the neighbour's place (house or driveway), but it could be their car, mailbox, or some specific spot; the text doesn't say what.
- Turn 21: "ya muyi fi" could be "once before" (one occasion in the past) or "the time before" (the last visit). The reference of wan follows the rule cleanly (the neighbour), but it's a little surprising that B, a guest, was yelled at; the meaning is roughly the same either way.

Minor note: the name "en desu" could be Des, Dez or Desi; its gender isn't given, so I used "they".
