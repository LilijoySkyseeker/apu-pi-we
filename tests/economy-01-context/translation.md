# economy-01-context: translation

Source: `source.txt`. Each block: English, Apu Pi We, gloss.
Names: Marcus → **en malukasu**, Jess → **en desu** (adapted by hand under the
Words rules: r → l + added u, schwa → a as in **landan** / **kulisutafalu**, j → d).

## 1. A

```
Hey, are you guys still coming Saturday or what?
upe. min me la le obu mon dili pi ufo.
hello. Q you PL ONGOING/still come at day OF 6.
```

Note: "or what" dropped. **min … lu …** would make it a question about an
"or"; the English tag adds only impatience, which has no word.

## 2. B

```
Yeah, I think so. What time again?
kon oma obu. so iko moti lon, wun din obu mon muyi yu.
CONF 7 come. REQUEST tell again I, THAT we.excl come at time WH.
```

Note: "yeah" = echo answer **obu** (rule 4); "I think so" = confidence
(**kon oma**), not **pume**. "What time again?" turned into a request to be told
again, so **moti** has a verb to modify. The pause is the recipient/long-object
pause (rule 15).

## 3. A

```
Like seven-ish. Maybe seven thirty, I gotta clean first.
mon fu fi mape po po. kon te mon fi mape ka po. lon keya muge inan guyi ya.
at about 1 9 0 0. CONF 5 at 1 9 3 0. I must make clean house beforehand.
```

Note: Clock times use 24-hour digit reading (rule 9), so "seven" in the evening
is **fi mape po po**. "-ish" is **fu** + number (Quantities). "First" is **ya**
used alone, at the end of the sentence.

## 4. C

```
Wait, is this the thing at your place or at Marcus's?
tin sefu. musu tan gun mon guyi pi me lu mon guyi pi en malukasu yu.
INSTRUCT wait. thing this BE at house OF you or at house OF NAME Marcus WH.
```

Note: Since C wants to know *which*, this uses **… lu … yu**, not
**min … lu …**. **bu** would point to "house" from line 3, so the party is
**musu tan**.

## 5. A

```
Mine. Marcus bailed, his landlord's doing something with the pipes.
mon guyi pi lon. en malukasu mu lenu. no ibe guyi pi wan, le muge musu in mon du dinyo osa.
at house OF I. NAME Marcus NOT can. AGENT have house OF he, ONGOING do thing SOME at TOOL carry water.
```

Note: Echo answer to a choice. "Bailed" is rendered as "can't".
Landlord = **no ibe guyi pi wan** "the one who has his house" (**no sanwa**
would be the tenant, by the doc's own example). Pipes = **du dinyo osa** "thing for
carrying water". The comma follows the pause rule: without it, **wan le muge**
could be heard as "he is doing". **wan** = Marcus (the most recent third-person
subject).

## 6. C

```
Oh no, again? Didn't that happen last month too?
ba yomo. min moti. batu ben wame muwo ya dunan fi, min.
EXCLAIM bad. Q again. problem that also happen before month 1, Q(tag).
```

Note: The negative question is the clause-final tag **min** ("…, right?").
"Last month" = **ya dunan fi**: no anchor, so the anchor is now. "That" is
**batu ben**, because **bu** would mean the pipes.

## 7. A

```
Yeah, same thing. His whole kitchen was flooded, it was bad.
muwo. batu kige. pu siten minwe pi yi gun kete gan osa. batu gun yomo.
happen. problem same. PLACE cook whole OF OBV BE full with water. problem BE bad.
```

Note: Here "his" is **yi**, not **wan**. The landlord (line 5) is the most recent
person-subject, and Marcus is the one before him. Past time was already set by
**ya dunan fi**, so the verb is unmarked. "It was bad": **bu** would be the
water, so the subject is **batu**. Kitchen = **pu siten** (pattern of
**pu wopo**).

## 8. B

```
Honestly he should just move out at this point.
eda, yi wupi kusa guyi pi nen.
now, OBV need leave house OF self.
```

Note: The landlord is still the most recent person-subject, so Marcus is again **yi**.
"Should" = **wupi** "need to". **fon** advises the listener, and Marcus isn't
listening. "Honestly" and "just" are dropped as fillers. After this
sentence, Marcus is the most recent subject.

## 9. A

```
I know, right? I told him that, he just said he can't find anything cheaper.
lon lugo. lon sin iko wan musu kige, un wan oyi wun sun mu lenu geno guyi man wupi nimo ke uyo.
I know. I DONE tell he thing same, but he say THAT LOG NOT can find house WHO.rel need money more few.
```

Note: The rhetorical "right?" is dropped, because a tag **min** would really
ask for confirmation. Both **wan** = Marcus (the subject of line 8). This assumes
**lon** as a subject doesn't reset **wan**. **sin** on the first verb sets the
past for this little narrative. "Cheaper" = "a house that needs less money";
there is no word for cheap. **sun** is the one reported.

## 10. C

```
I mean, fair. Rent's insane right now.
lon lafi. eda, ba nimo pi sanwa gun bi kudu.
I understand. now, EXCLAIM money OF rent BE very big.
```

Note: "Insane" is translated by its meaning (very big, exclaimed), so it doesn't
need **a** (non-literal).

## 11. B

```
Okay, so should I bring anything?
mm. gi tan, min lon wupi dinyo musu in.
mm. because this, Q I need carry thing SOME.
```

## 12. A

```
Uh, maybe chips or something? Or drinks. Whatever, it's not a big deal.
fon me dinyo [chips] lu musu fu bu. lu ge koye. waka gun mu bawe.
ADVISE you carry [chips] or thing like it. or RESULT drink. choice BE NOT important.
```

Note: The hedged suggestion is **fon** (advice: "the decision stays with you"),
not a low **kon** or a question. Rule 11 lets only instructions and requests
drop the subject, so **me** is said. **bu** = [chips]. "Drinks" = **ge koye**
"what is drunk". "Whatever, it's not a big deal" = "the choice isn't important".

## 13. C

```
I can bring that dip, the one I made at Jess's birthday.
lon lenu dinyo [dip] ben man lon sin siten mon [birthday] pi en desu.
I can carry [dip] that WHO.rel I DONE cook at [birthday] OF NAME Jess.
```

Note: Here **sin** is needed. Unmarked, the relative clause could read as
habitual ("the dip I make at her birthdays"). "Birthday" is bracketed:
**dili pi pata** or a "day her life started" clause would mean the birth date,
not the yearly party.

## 14. B

```
Oh, the spinach one? Yes, please, that was so good.
min [dip] pi [spinach]. so nun dinyo. [dip] ben gun bi upe.
Q [dip] OF [spinach]. REQUEST POLITE carry. [dip] that BE very good.
```

Note: **pi** is the only relation that can hang on a bare noun. "Yes, please" =
echo the verb as a request, with "please" as **nun**. The object is left out,
as in rule 4's echo answers. In the last sentence, **bu** would be [spinach], so
the dip is named again.

## 15. C

```
Ha, okay. I gotta remember to buy the stuff for it though.
de dinyo. un lon keya imu bika musu la li bu.
INTEND carry. but I must remember buy thing PL for it.
```

Note: "Okay" here accepts the request, so it's **de** + verb ("will do",
rule 4). "Ha" is dropped. **bu** = the dip. **lon** is kept because the new
sentence starts with **un**. Whether the subject of **de dinyo**, which is only
understood, licenses dropping is unclear.

## 16. A

```
Is Jess coming, by the way? I texted her but she didn't answer.
min en desu obu. lon sin tusi tun wan gan liga, un wan mu febo.
Q NAME Jess come. I DONE write to she with phone, but she NOT answer.
```

Note: "By the way" is dropped. "Texted" = "wrote to her with the phone".
Both **wan** = Jess, the most recent third-person subject.

## 17. B

```
She's working Saturday, I think. She said maybe after, like ten?
kon oma wan doku mon dili pi ufo. wan oyi wun kon te sun obu wo doku, mon fu sa sa po po.
CONF 7 she work at day OF 6. she say THAT CONF 5 LOG come after work, at about 2 2 0 0.
```

Note: The "maybe" is Jess's, so **kon te** sits inside her reported clause.
"After" needs an object, so it's **wo doku** "after working" (rule 8:
preposition + verb naming the act).

## 18. A

```
Okay, cool, that's fine. We'll probably still be up.
mm. ba upe. kon kefi pen le gun unda.
mm. EXCLAIM good. CONF 8 we.incl still BE wake.
```

Note: **pen**, because A means everyone at the party, listeners included.
**gun unda** = the state "awake" (rule 7).

## 19. C

```
Wait, do I need to park on the street or is there room in the driveway?
tin sefu. lon yaga tale mon sola lu mon pu yaga tale pi me yu.
INSTRUCT wait. I put car at road or at PLACE put car OF you WH.
```

Note: The two English clauses are merged into one *which* choice, as in line
4. Park = **yaga tale** "put the car". Driveway = **pu yaga tale pi me** "your
place for putting the car".

## 20. A

```
Street's fine, just don't block the neighbor's thing, he gets really weird about it.
sola gun upe. tin mu yaga tale mon sinye pi musu pi no nume gelu, gi no nume gelu lewe bi bonko.
road BE good. INSTRUCT NOT put car at front OF thing OF AGENT live near, because AGENT live near become very angry.
```

Note: The neighbour has to be named twice. He was only mentioned, never a
subject, and **wan** here would mean Jess, the most recent third-person subject
(line 17). "Block" = "put the car in front of". "Weird about it" = **bonko**
"angry", which line 21 supports. Neighbour = **no nume gelu** "one who lives
near".

## 21. B

```
Ha, yeah, he yelled at me last time.
wan oyi dibon tun lon ya muyi fi.
he say loud to I before time 1.
```

Note: Here **wan** = the neighbour (the subject in line 20). "Yell" =
**oyi dibon** (manner modifier, like **oyi fanse**). "Last time" =
**ya muyi fi** "one occasion ago", using **muyi** as the occasion unit
(**muyi sa** "twice") in the anchor + offset pattern. "Ha, yeah" is dropped;
nothing was asked, so there's nothing to echo.

## 22. C

```
Okay, noted. See you guys Saturday.
de imu. lon nagu me la mon dili pi ufo.
INTEND remember. I see you PL at day OF 6.
```

Note: "Noted" = **de** + verb ("will remember"). The farewell is a plain
statement. The routine **upe isi** wasn't said in the English.

## Ambiguous rules

1. **wan and first/second-person subjects.** Rule 6 says **wan** is "the most
   recent person … that was the subject of a clause". It doesn't say whether
   **lon**, **me**, **pen** and **din** as subjects count. I assumed they don't.
   Otherwise **wan** after "I texted her" (line 16) could point to the speaker.
2. **Mentioned vs subject.** A person mentioned many times but never a subject
   (the neighbour in line 20) can't be **wan**. An older subject (Jess) takes it.
   That forces renaming in casual speech; the rule may intend this.
3. **Dropped subjects after a subjectless echo.** Rule 11 allows dropping after
   **un** "when the subject repeats". It's unclear whether that applies when the
   earlier clause is a subjectless **de** + verb answer (line 15).
4. **Advice with no subject.** Rule 11 licenses dropping the subject only for
   instructions and requests, not **fon** (line 12). I kept **me**.
5. **Objects in echo answers.** Rule 4's echo answers have no object
   (**so nun dinyo**, line 14). Nothing says whether an object may be dropped
   elsewhere.
6. **Modifiers inside derived phrases.** In **pu siten minwe pi yi** (line 7),
   **minwe** could modify the verb base **siten** ("cook wholly") or the
   whole **pu** phrase. Principle 4 places it before **pi** but doesn't settle
   which it modifies.
7. **Clock times with "about".** Where **fu** goes relative to **mon** and a
   digit-read clock time (**mon fu fi mape po po**) is not specified. The
   **fu** + number rule is written for counts.
8. **"Last time".** The anchor + offset rule names calendar units. Using
   **muyi** "occasion" as the unit (**ya muyi fi**) is my extension.
9. **Name adaptation.** `tools/names.py` (run, not read) prints only a demo list.
   Its outputs (Christopher → kilisatafala) don't match the doc's
   added-**u** rule (kulisutafalu), so names here follow the doc.
