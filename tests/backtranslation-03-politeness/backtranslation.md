# Back-translation: hospital scene (P1–P10)

## P1. Nurse: "Upe LEso, ku no YEma SImi en OKAfolu."
- **Gloss:** good morning, POLITE AGENT-help sick NAME Okafolu
- **Reading:** "Good morning, Doctor Okafor." (A polite, respectful greeting.)
- **Notes:** *no YEma SImi* = "one who helps the sick". It is a role word placed before the name, so here it is a title: "Doctor". *OKAfolu* looks like an adapted *Okafor* (r → l, plus an added u).

## P2. Doctor: "Upe LEso. so ku GOyu."
- **Gloss:** good morning. REQUEST POLITE sit
- **Reading:** "Good morning. Please, have a seat." (A friendly, polite request.)

## P3. Nurse: "ku SEpi. yo ku: MAla pi Ewo SImi le GAke."
- **Gloss:** POLITE sorry. WARN POLITE: heart OF person sick ONGOING slow
- **Reading:** "Sorry. But I need to warn you: the patient's heart is slowing down." (She politely apologises, perhaps for not sitting or for interrupting, then gives a clear warning.)
- **Notes:** *Ewo SImi* "sick person" = the patient. *le GAke* = "is going slow / slowing". This could mean a dropping heart rate.

## P4. Doctor: "LOta. tin NEke Osa tun wan Eda."
- **Gloss:** thanks. INSTRUCT give water TO him/her now
- **Reading:** "Thanks. Give him fluids, now." (A direct instruction with some urgency.)
- **Notes:** *wan* refers to the patient. The subject of the last clause was the heart, which isn't a person, so *wan* falls back to the most recent person mentioned. *Osa* is literally "water". In a hospital it probably means fluids or a drip.

## P5. Nurse: "fon ku ku: lon PUme wun pen WUpi BAmo FONsu."
- **Gloss:** ADVISE POLITE POLITE: I think THAT we(incl.) need stop cut
- **Reading:** "If I may, Doctor, with all respect: I think we need to stop the operation." (Advice, very deferential. The decision is left to the doctor.)
- **Notes:** *BAmo FONsu* is literally "stop cutting". In this setting it most likely means stopping the surgery or procedure. *pen* (we, including you) makes it "we", i.e. the team.

## P6. Nurse: "yo yo! MAla pi wan sin BAmo!"
- **Gloss:** WARN WARN! heart OF him/her DONE stop!
- **Reading:** "Emergency! His heart has stopped!" (An urgent alarm. Leaving out the politeness word is normal in an emergency, not rude.)

## P7. Doctor: "SEpi. me gun FEko."
- **Gloss:** sorry. you BE true
- **Reading:** "I'm sorry. You were right." (The doctor apologises and admits the nurse was right.)
- **Notes:** Tense is unmarked. Literally it is "you are right", but in context it means "you were right (about stopping)".

## P8. Doctor: "so ku TApi BEya."
- **Gloss:** REQUEST POLITE close door
- **Reading:** "Please close the door." (A polite request.)

## P9. Nurse: "ku lon mu LAnu MUge tan."
- **Gloss:** POLITE I NOT can do this
- **Reading:** "I'm sorry, I can't do that." (A polite refusal.)
- **Notes:** *ku* marks politeness only. The refusal itself is plain, with no reason given. She may need to stay with the patient.

## P10. Doctor: "LOta. Upe Isi; lon NAgu me WUma."
- **Gloss:** thanks. good go; I see you tomorrow
- **Reading:** "Thank you. Take care, see you tomorrow." (Thanks, a farewell and a friendly sign-off.)
- **Notes:** *Upe Isi* "go well" is the standard farewell.
