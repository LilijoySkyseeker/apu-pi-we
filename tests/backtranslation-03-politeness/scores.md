## Questions

**(a) Did politeness ever blur the act?** No. P3 was read as an explicit warning ("I need to warn you"), not a hint; the `yo` marker made the act unmistakable despite `ku`. P5 was read as advice/recommendation with the exact proposition "we need to stop the operation". The note "the decision is left to the doctor" describes deference, not weakening; the original leaves the decision to the doctor too. The double `ku` raised deference slightly but did not turn the recommendation into a suggestion or hint.

**(b) Was P6's lack of politeness read as urgency rather than rudeness?** Yes, explicitly: "Leaving out the politeness word is normal in an emergency, not rude." The one loss in P6 is content (the "Stop!" order), not tone.

**(c) Were P2 and P8 read as polite requests?** Yes, both. P2 ("Please, have a seat") is an exact match. P8 ("Please close the door") is a polite request, but it lost the extra indirectness of "Would you mind…" (Politeness 1).
