# Back-translation: two contract sentences

Source context line: "Two sentences from a rental contract."

Derived words used below (built with the word-building markers):

- **no SANwa** "one who rents" = the tenant (contrasted with the next word, so not the landlord)
- **no KUwi GUyi** "one who keeps the house" = the landlord / owner
- **no MIya** "one who looks" = the inspector
- **ge TAyu** "what is agreed" = the agreement / lease
- **ge Alun** "what is broken" = damage
- **ge SElo** "what is built" = the building
- **du BENbi** "thing for opening" = a key (**la** = plural: keys)
- **pu YONnu NImo** "place for holding money" = a bank (or account)
- **MUyi pi FUgi** "time of the end" = the deadline / due date

---

```
           UNfu li ge Alun we

                     no MIya GEno
                        [WHILE-clause (attaches to GEno)
                           nin no SANwa KUsa ] ] ]
  ,
  [BEFORE-clause (attaches to main verb KEya UNfu)
     ya go NEke wan du BENbi la
                       [REL-e (attaches to "du BENbi la"; subject gap)
                          man BENbi ge SElo ] ]

```

- REL-a ends where REL-b ends, at **SEge**: **KEya** cannot continue REL-b ("the landlord sent must…" makes no sense, and **SEge** is not a verb that takes a verb as its object), so **KEya** is the main verb of the sentence.
- REL-c ends at the comma before **ya**. The before-clause belongs to the main verb.

### Reading

"A tenant who has signed the agreement that the landlord sent must first pay a sum of money. That money covers all damage the inspector finds when the tenant moves out. The tenant must pay it before anyone gives them the keys that open the building."

As one contract sentence: "A tenant who has signed the agreement sent by the landlord must pay in advance a sum that covers all damage found by the inspector at the tenant's move-out, before the tenant is handed the keys to the building."

In short, the tenant pays a damage deposit, and only then gets the keys.

## Notes: uncertain boundaries, attachments and alternative parses

### L1

1. **Where the subject's relative clauses end (REL-a / REL-b) and where the main verb is.** No relative clause has an end marker, so **no SANwa man … man no KUwi GUyi sin SEge KEya UNfu NImo ya** could in principle run on. I put the main verb at **KEya**, because "the landlord has sent must pay" is not a possible continuation. Alternatives I considered:
   - REL-b attaches to **SUso** ("the name that the landlord sent") or to **no SANwa** ("the tenant whom the landlord sent"). I rejected both: the nearest noun is **ge TAyu**, and a second relative clause on the tenant would have to sit inside REL-a anyway.
   - **SEge KEya** as a verb chain ("sent that [someone] must pay"). Rejected: **SEge** is not in the class of verbs that take a verb as object, and that reading would leave **no SANwa** with no main verb.
2. **What REL-c "man UNfu li ge Alun we…" attaches to. This was the hardest call.** It comes after the adverb **ya** and a comma, not straight after a noun.
   - **(chosen) NImo, "money that pays for all damage"**, i.e. a deposit. I take **ya** to have been placed before the relative clause on purpose: since nothing may follow a relative clause in its phrase, the adverb had to come first or the relative clause would swallow it.
   - **(alternative) no SANwa**, "the tenant…, who pays for all damage…". This is semantically possible: the tenant must pay money up front *and* is the one who pays for damage. But the antecedent is far away.
   - It cannot be **ge TAyu**, since an agreement doesn't pay.
   - Either way the gap is the subject of **UNfu**: dropped subjects aren't allowed here, so it can't be "money that [the tenant] pays for damage".
3. **The first "ya" vs the final "ya"-clause.** **ya** before the comma reads as bare "beforehand / first" (rule 8). The sentence then also ends with **ya go NEke…** "before anyone gives…". I read these as one idea said twice: "pay first, … before getting the keys". Alternatively, the first **ya** could be a "before" whose clause was moved to the end of the sentence (heavy goes last), with the relative clause in between. The meaning comes out the same.
4. **What the while-clause "nin no SANwa KUsa" attaches to.**
   - **(chosen) GEno**: the inspector finds the damage during move-out.
   - **(alternative) UNfu** in REL-c: the money pays for damage at move-out.
   - **(alternative) main KEya UNfu**: rejected, because it contradicts **ya** "beforehand".

   I also considered **nin** + noun phrase "during the leaving tenant". I rejected it because **no SANwa KUsa** reads as subject + verb, a clause.
5. **What the final "ya"-clause attaches to.** Because REL-c and REL-d have no end marker, **ya go NEke…** could grammatically belong to the deepest clause: "the tenant leaves before anyone gives him keys", or "the inspector finds … before giving keys". I attached it to the main verb (pay before key handover). The comma and the sense both point that way, but this is exactly the kind of place the no-end-marker rule leaves open.
6. **"wan" in the before-clause.** By the reference rule, **wan** is the most recent person who was the subject of a clause, other than its own clause's subject (**go**). That is **no SANwa** in **nin no SANwa KUsa**, so the tenant gets the keys. The landlord or inspector would only be a reading if the rule weren't applied mechanically.
7. **Lexical choices.**
   - **no SANwa** "one who rents" could be the lessor in principle. Because **no KUwi GUyi** is a separate party, I read it as the tenant.
   - **no KUwi GUyi** "house keeper" could be a caretaker or property manager rather than the owner. I chose "landlord".
   - **TUsi SUso tun** "write name onto" = sign.
   - **ge SElo** "building" could also be "premises".

### L2

1. **Scope of the because-clause.** **gi pu YONnu NImo…** sits inside the first if-clause (before the comma). So the condition is "cannot pay *because the bank hasn't released the money*", not "the landlord may extend because…". The alternative (the because-clause giving the landlord's reason) is ruled out by the comma placement.
2. **NOT + because.** **mu** directly before **LAnu** covers that one word ("cannot"), so the reading is "cannot pay, because…". The other English reading, "not [able to pay because X]", is excluded, because **mu** doesn't start the clause.
3. **"pu YONnu NImo pi wan".**
   - Read as "the place that holds his money": **pi wan** can attach to **NImo** ("his money") or to the whole **pu**-phrase ("his bank"). The meaning is the same either way.
   - **wan** = the tenant, the most recent person subject.
   - **pu YONnu NImo** could also be "account" or "escrow" rather than "bank".
4. **"bu" in "MUge ENpa bu".** **bu** = the most recent thing mentioned: **NImo**, the money, not the bank. So "has not released / freed the money". **MUge ENpa bu** is a causative, result before object: "make it free" = release, unfreeze.
5. **"MUge NEtu MUyi pi FUgi" and what "FUgi" ends.** Causative "make the end-time long" = extend the deadline. I took it to be the payment deadline, since this sentence is about not being able to pay. The alternative is the end date of the lease.
6. **What "mu ke se DIli SA NObi SA" attaches to.**
   - **(chosen)** the size of the extension: "by no more than 14 days".
   - **(alternative)** a limit on **FUgi** itself: "a deadline no later than 14 days [from some unstated point]".

   **mu** covers **ke se …**, so "not more than 14" = at most 14. The number: **SA NObi SA** = 2·6 + 2 = 14 (decimal).
7. **The second "don"-clause.** It attaches to the main verb: the landlord's power to extend depends on written notice. The text says **don** "if", not "only if". A contract reader would probably take it as a requirement ("provided that"), but strictly it's just a condition.
   - **wan** here = the landlord. It can't be the subject of its own clause (the tenant), so it falls to the most recent other subject, **no KUwi GUyi**.
   - **gan TUsi** "with writing" = in writing.
8. **"LAnu"** = "can". In a contract the landlord's **LAnu** reads as "may" (permission). The tenant's **mu LAnu** reads as inability.
