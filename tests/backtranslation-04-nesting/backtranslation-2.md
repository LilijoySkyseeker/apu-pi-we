# Back-translation: C1–C5

Sources used: language.md, grammar.tsv, lexicon.tsv, compounds.tsv, numbers.tsv only.

Key rules applied (language.md, rule 15 and related):
- A relative clause (**man …**) attaches to the nearest noun before it.
- A time/reason/condition clause at the **start** of a sentence belongs to the main clause; at the **end** it belongs to the innermost clause before it.
- A comma ends a fronted or embedded clause.
- A prepositional phrase attaches to its clause's main verb, never to a modifier (rule 8).
- Two objects: verb + recipient + thing (rule 14). **ya** alone = "first, beforehand" (rule 8).

## C1

> ya go NEke no SANwa du BENbi la man BENbi ge SElo, no SANwa man sin TUsi SUso tun ge TAyu man no KUwi GUyi sin SEge KEya UNfu NImo ya, man UNfu li ge Alun we man no MIya GEno nin no SANwa KUsa.

**Gloss:**
ya (BEFORE) go (anyone) NEke (give) no SANwa (AGENT rent = renter/tenant) du BENbi (TOOL open = key) la (PL) man (WHO.rel) BENbi (open) ge SElo (RESULT build = building), | no SANwa (tenant) man (WHO.rel) sin (DONE) TUsi (write) SUso (name) tun (to) ge TAyu (RESULT agree = the agreement/contract) man (WHO.rel) no KUwi GUyi (AGENT keep house = landlord/house-keeper) sin (DONE) SEge (send) | KEya (must) UNfu (pay) NImo (money) ya (beforehand), | man (WHO.rel) UNfu (pay) li (for) ge Alun (RESULT break = breakage/damage) we (all) man (WHO.rel) no MIya (AGENT look = inspector/the one who looks) GEno (find) nin (while) no SANwa (tenant) KUsa (leave).

**Attachments:**
- Relative clauses:
  - R1 **man BENbi ge SElo** "that open the building" → **du BENbi la** (the keys).
  - R2 **man sin TUsi SUso tun ge TAyu** "who has written (their) name to the agreement" = has signed the contract → **no SANwa** (the tenant, subject of the main clause).
  - R3 **man no KUwi GUyi sin SEge** "that the landlord sent" → **ge TAyu** (the agreement). R3 is nested inside R2.
  - R4 **man UNfu li ge Alun we** "that pays for all damage" → **NImo** (the money). (The noun nearest before it; **ya** is not a noun.)
  - R5 **man no MIya GEno** "that the inspector finds" → **ge Alun we** (all the damage). R5 is nested inside R4.
- Time clauses:
  - **ya go NEke …, ** (fronted, sentence start) → belongs to the **main clause**: the paying must happen before the keys are handed over.
  - **ya** (bare, after **NImo**) = "beforehand", an adverb in the main clause (must pay money beforehand).
  - **nin no SANwa KUsa** (sentence end) → belongs to the innermost clause before it, **R5** (the inspector *finds*): the finding happens while the tenant is leaving / at the tenant's departure.

**Reading:**
Before anyone gives the tenant the keys that open the building, the tenant — the one who has signed the contract that the landlord sent — must pay money in advance. That money pays for all the damage that the inspector finds when the tenant is moving out (the inspection happens at move-out).

**Notes / doubts:**
- Where R2/R3 end: no comma separates **SEge** from **KEya**. Read strictly, "a comma ends an embedded clause" would extend R3 up to the comma, leaving the main clause with no verb. I take **KEya** as the main verb ("sent must pay" makes no sense, and the main clause needs a verb). A listener has to infer this boundary; the grammar gives no explicit marker.
- **no SANwa du BENbi la** could in principle be one agent noun with a verb-phrase base ("one who rents keys"). I read it as recipient + thing (rule 14: verb + recipient + thing), which gives "give the tenant the keys".
- R4 after the comma: the comma might suggest a non-restrictive or extraposed clause, but rule 15 attaches it to the nearest noun, **NImo**, not to the tenant.

## C2

> no YONnu sin NAgu LOwu man YIdu TAle nin GEgi.

**Gloss:** no YONnu (AGENT hold = the holder/guard, one who holds back) sin (DONE) NAgu (see) LOwu (man) man (WHO.rel) YIdu (take) TAle (car) nin (during) GEgi (rain).

**Attachments:**
- Relative **man YIdu TAle** "who took a car" → **LOwu** (the man).
- **nin GEgi** "during the rain" is at the sentence end, so it belongs to the innermost clause before it, the relative clause (**YIdu TAle**). It is not "the car in the rain": a prepositional phrase attaches to its clause's verb.

**Reading:** The holder (guard) saw a man who had taken a car during the rain. The car was taken while it was raining. The sentence doesn't say when the seeing happened.

## C3

> nin GEgi, no YONnu sin NAgu LOwu man YIdu TAle.

**Gloss:** nin (during) GEgi (rain), | no YONnu (holder/guard) sin (DONE) NAgu (see) LOwu (man) man (WHO.rel) YIdu (take) TAle (car).

**Attachments:**
- **nin GEgi,** is fronted (sentence start, closed by a comma), so it belongs to the **main clause** (**NAgu**, saw).
- Relative **man YIdu TAle** → **LOwu** (the man).

**Reading:** During the rain, the holder (guard) saw a man who had taken a car. The seeing happened while it was raining. The sentence doesn't say when the car was taken.

## C4

> en MAla sin NAgu SOnu pi no TOfo man YUyun.

**Gloss:** en (NAME) MAla (Mara) sin (DONE) NAgu (see) SOnu (friend) pi (of) no TOfo (AGENT teach = teacher) man (WHO.rel) YUyun (sing).

**Attachments:**
- Relative **man YUyun** "who sings" → **no TOfo** (the teacher), the nearest noun before it. It does not attach to **SOnu** (the friend).

**Reading:** Mara saw the friend of the teacher who sings. In other words, Mara saw a friend of the singing teacher. The teacher is the one who sings.

## C5

> lon sin NEke YUfa tun SOnu pi en MAla, man YUyun.

**Gloss:** lon (I) sin (DONE) NEke (give) YUfa (bag) tun (to) SOnu (friend) pi (of) en (NAME) MAla (Mara), | man (WHO.rel) YUyun (sing).

**Attachments:**
- Relative **man YUyun** "who sings" → **en MAla** (Mara), the nearest noun before it. The comma doesn't change the attachment, so it does not go to **SOnu** (the friend) or **YUfa** (the bag).

**Reading:** I gave the bag to Mara's friend; Mara is the one who sings. In other words, I gave the bag to a friend of Mara, and Mara sings.
