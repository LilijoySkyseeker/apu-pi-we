# The goal of Apupiwe

## What it is for

- **P1.** A universal human language: fundamentally the best language that can be built with what humanity and AI understand today. A thought experiment.
- **P2.** Its job is to let humans exchange meaning.
- **P3.** It is designed for native speakers, children who grow up with it, using the answers the world's languages each found independently.
- **P4.** Its speakers live among today's languages and cultures, and use it in speech and in writing, including online. "Speakers" means everyone who uses it, in either.

## What "best" means

Best is whatever exchanges meaning best, at a cost its speakers will pay.

- **B1.** Meaning gets through.
- **B2.** Words are hard to mishear.
- **B3.** A precise, unambiguous way to say anything exists, even when speakers don't use it.
- **B4.** Writing carries the same meaning as speech.
- **B5.** It is built from what many languages share, not from one.
- **B6.** It carries whatever its speakers need to say, modern life included.
- **B7.** It is predictable: words are made and connected by rule, with little to memorise.
- **B8.** It is brief.
- **B9.** It survives its speakers changing it. It doesn't try to control how it evolves.
- **B10.** Any adult can learn it.

## How the criteria fit together

- **C1.** B1 is the aim itself; every other criterion serves it. The rest are not a ranking. They are of three kinds:
  - **Guarantees, which must hold:** B2, B3, B4, B6, B9 and B10.
  - **Costs, minimised together:** a lifetime speaker's everyday effort (B8) plus learning effort (B7). Nothing arbitrary unless it pays for itself in everyday saving.
  - **The method:** building from what many languages share (B5). Convergence across unrelated languages is evidence of what works, and it keeps the language fair to every culture.
- **C2.** The language's insides are built for native speakers, who learn it once and use it for life. Today's speakers are protected by the guarantees and by coexistence: it carries the conventions of the cultures it lives in, and where those conflict and one must be chosen, it follows the international standard.
- **C3.** When two forms cost about the same, prefer the one that doesn't sound like a common offensive word in a major language. This never overrides a cheaper or clearer form.
- **C4.** Agreement among languages doesn't count where it rests on a belief about the world now known to be wrong (the sun "rises"). There, the language describes what is observed instead, without building today's theories into its basic words either.

## Hard limits

- **L1.** It is for humans, not designed around machines or AI.
- **L2.** It coexists with existing cultures and carries their conventions (C2).
- **L3.** Where an international standard exists for a field, formal or de facto, the language follows it; for example, it is written in the Latin alphabet.
- **L4.** The language's own vocabulary borrows a word only when the world already shares its form. Its speakers borrow freely; that is use, not design (N4).

## What it does not try to do

- **N1.** Be a fantasy language, or have a signed form.
- **N2.** Favour adult learners over native speakers.
- **N3.** Weight cultures by how many people they have today.
- **N4.** Define everything. Style, idiom and change come from real use.
- **N5.** Aim for beauty of sound; its expressiveness is in meaning.
- **N6.** Be tailored to the projects that use it. Built well on its own, it will fit them.
