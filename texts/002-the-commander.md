## Measurements

- **Length:** 414 syllables against about 337 in the English (contractions expanded), so 23% more syllables (001 was 16%). At the syllable rates of languages with simple syllables (about 7.8 per second against English 6.2, from memory), speaking time comes out about equal again: about 53 against 54 seconds.
- **Where the extra length comes from:**
  - Possession: "my" is **on e**, two syllables, nine times.
  - Frequent compounds: commander is **NIte GOni**, four syllables, nine times.
  - The explicit causative **Avu**.

  Real languages shorten frequent compounds into new roots over time. A story with a commander in every scene would clip **NIte GOni** quickly. That's healthy evolution, not a design flaw, so no fix is adopted.

## What worked

- **Obviation did English's animacy work.** English separates the commander ("she") from her reaction ("it") by animacy. Here the commander is **lu** and everything else is **tan**, with no gender or animacy needed.
- **Echo answers:** "No, I—" is **na SEfo, e—** ("not dreaming, I—"). The denial says *what* is denied.
- **Reflexives come free:** **e SIdo e** "I held myself back". With obviation, a repeated third-person pronoun is always the same person.
- **Questions compose from parts:** why = **win mi** "because what", how = **wo mi** "like what", what = **mi**.
- **An in-world oath grew from the curse system:** "Godfire" is **dun VOnnu NOwi**, EXCLAIM god fire. This is how a taboo vocabulary seeds itself.
- **The hiss** became **GAka wo BOzen**, "say like a snake".

## Findings and fixes

| # | Finding | Fix | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where do question words go? | In place (**vun ZIzi yo e GAka mi?** "you want me to say what?"). Moving them to the front would break "role from position", the noise-robustness argument for SVO. | adopted |
| 2 | Indirect questions ("I don't know what I did") | **mi** inside a **yo** clause is an indirect question | adopted |
| 3 | Tag questions ("It was them, wasn't it?") | A clause-final **yun** = "right?" A clause-initial **yun** stays an open question. | adopted |
| 4 | "Standalone **san.**" relied on hearing a pause, which is fragile | **san** = this sentence; **san san** = the whole turn. Doubling widens or strengthens, as with **ze ze**. | adopted, 001 updated |
| 5 | "seven days" or "the seventh day"? **ONo AYon** was both | Noun + number = count (**ONo AYon** seven days). Noun + **on** + number = label or order (**PEpa on DONma** step zero). | adopted, 001 updated |
| 6 | "You offended [whoever taught you that panic] angry": a relative clause has no end marker, so anything after it gets swallowed | Causative order is **Avu** + result + object: **Avu OPun vun** "make angry you". Heavy phrases go last. | adopted |
| 7 | Passive / resulting state ("was locked") | **la** + verb = the resulting state: **la FEson** "is closed" | adopted |
| 8 | **KOnu VUge** would mean "a fatherly mother" | Noun + noun is always modifier; coordinate with **gu**: **KOnu gu VUge** parents | adopted |
| 9 | Dropped subjects | Allowed only for the same subject after **gu** / **fen**, and for the addressee of an instruction or request | adopted |
| 10 | "So …" | **win va** "because this". No new word. | adopted |
| 11 | "So, get rid of…": a linker before the speech-act particle | A linker to the previous sentence may come first | adopted |
| 12 | Duration ("for a week") | **zan** + noun phrase = during | adopted |
| 13 | A verb after a preposition ("from retreating") | It names the act: **gen FOta** | adopted |
| 14 | Names with foreign sounds ("Lilith") | A foreign sound maps to the native sound listeners most often hear it as (Miller & Nicely: th → f). A final consonant gets an echo vowel. Lilith → **LIlifi**. | adopted |
| 15 | Two content verbs in a row ("meditate" = **KApo Ife**) | Already covered: any content word can modify the one before it ("sit thinking") | observation |
| 16 | Metaphors (face darkened, froze, glass wall) | Translated literally. Whether they land is culture, not grammar. | observation |
